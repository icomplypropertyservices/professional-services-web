## What happens at a first meeting about estate administration

When you meet a local wills and probate specialist for the first time about estate administration, they will usually start by asking to see the will and any paperwork you already have, such as bank statements or a list of assets. They will explain the steps involved in administering the estate and what your role as executor requires, including the fact that executors can be personally liable for mistakes. You can expect the specialist to talk you through how they keep clear accounts of everything coming in and going out of the estate, and how they will communicate with beneficiaries. Use this meeting to judge whether you feel comfortable with the person and whether their answers are clear. It is also a chance to ask how often you will hear from them and what you need to do next.

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity in England and Wales, so some will writers are not regulated. Solicitors (SRA) and chartered legal executives (CILEx) are regulated, insured and covered by the Legal Ombudsman. Many specialists are also members of STEP (the Society of Trust and Estate Practitioners) or Solicitors for the Elderly. Applying for a grant of probate on someone else's behalf for a fee is a reserved activity.

When choosing a provider, ask whether they are regulated, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance and where your original will is stored. Scotland has its own rules on succession and confirmation, so make sure your adviser practises in the right jurisdiction.

## Understanding regulation and local help

In England and Wales, applying for a grant of probate on someone else's behalf for a fee is a reserved legal activity, so the person you instruct should be regulated and hold professional indemnity insurance. Solicitors are regulated by the SRA and chartered legal executives by CILEx, and both are covered by the Legal Ombudsman.

Scotland has its own rules on succession and confirmation, so if the deceased lived in Scotland or owned property there, make sure your adviser practises in the right jurisdiction. When you search for estate administration near me, you are likely looking for someone you can meet or speak to easily, but it is still worth checking their regulatory status and insurance before you proceed.

## FAQs: estate administration

### Do I have to use a professional to administer an estate?

No, as an executor you can administer the estate yourself if it is straightforward. Many people choose professional help for complex estates, or where they are unsure about debts, tax or legal responsibilities.

### What does estate administration near me mean in practice?

### How do I know if someone is qualified to administer an estate?

Ask whether they are regulated, for example as a solicitor with the SRA or a chartered legal executive with CILEx, and whether they hold professional indemnity insurance. Membership of STEP or Solicitors for the Elderly can also indicate specialist experience, but you should still check their regulatory status.

### What should I bring to a first meeting about estate administration?

Bring the original will if you have it, along with any paperwork about the deceased's assets and debts, such as bank statements, share certificates or mortgage details. Also bring identification and any correspondence from HMRC or other organisations. This helps the specialist give you accurate guidance from the start.

### How is estate administration different from probate?

Probate is the legal process of proving the will and getting the authority to act as executor, while estate administration is the wider work of collecting assets, paying debts and taxes, and distributing what remains. A specialist may help with both, but the terms are not interchangeable.

### Do I need a solicitor for probate?

Not always. Executors can apply for probate themselves for simpler estates. Many choose professional help where there is property, inheritance tax, overseas assets or family tension.
