## Qualifications and registration to look for

Many surveyors are members of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), and RICS-regulated firms must follow professional standards, hold insurance and offer a complaints process. Chartered surveyors use the letters MRICS or FRICS. Some specialist roles have their own bodies, such as the Faculty of Party Wall Surveyors or the Property Care Association for damp and timber specialists.

Check the RICS directory, ask which report type you are getting and confirm whether the surveyor is independent of the sale. For asbestos work, look for organisations accredited by UKAS for surveying.

## What happens when a surveyor visits

When you have found a surveyor near you and booked a visit, they will usually arrange access and walk around the property themselves, looking at the structure, roof, walls, floors and services and noting anything that concerns them. It helps to tell them in advance about the specific worries that prompted your search, so they spend time on those rather than only giving a general once-over.

A surveyor's visit is not the same as a builder's quote or a lender's valuation. The surveyor is there to report on condition and flag problems, not to carry out repairs or tell you what work will cost, so expect a written report afterwards rather than a price for fixing things. Reading that report carefully and asking follow-up questions is part of getting your money's worth.

If the report raises something specialist, such as damp, timber decay or asbestos, the surveyor may suggest a further inspection by someone with that expertise. That is normal and not a sign the original survey was wasted, so ask how any specialist findings should be handled before you decide on next steps.

## Surveyor Near Me: FAQs

### How do I find a surveyor near me?

You can check the RICS directory for members in your area, and ask about their experience with the type of property you have. We can also introduce you to a suitable UK surveyor who will quote you directly.

### Do I need a survey if my lender is doing a valuation?

A lender's valuation is for the lender's purposes, not a full picture of condition for you. If you want to know what you are buying, ask a surveyor which report suits the property.

### What is the difference between a valuation and a condition survey?

A valuation focuses on what the property is worth, while a condition survey reports on its state and any problems. Tell the surveyor what you need so they recommend the right report.

### What should I have ready before the surveyor visits?

Have any plans, previous reports or details of work done available, and note the specific concerns you want looked at. Mention access arrangements and anything the surveyor should know about the property.

### Will the surveyor come inside the property?

Yes, a condition survey normally involves going into the property and looking at its structure and services, as well as the outside. Tell the surveyor about access arrangements and any areas you especially want examined when you book.

### Can I be there during the survey?

Some surveyors are happy for you to attend, while others prefer to work alone and talk you through the report afterwards. Ask when you enquire, and if you do attend, save detailed questions for the follow-up so the surveyor can concentrate on the inspection.

### Does the surveyor's report include repair costs?

A condition report describes what the surveyor found and flags problems, but it is not usually a builder's estimate of repair costs. If you need figures for work, ask the surveyor what the report covers and speak to a builder separately.

### Which survey do I need when buying a home?

For a conventional property in reasonable condition, an RICS Level 2 survey is often enough. Older, larger or altered properties often justify a Level 3 building survey. A surveyor can recommend the right level.
