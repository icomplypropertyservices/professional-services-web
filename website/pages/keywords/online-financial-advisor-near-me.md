## Getting ready for your first online meeting

Advice by video call or phone works best when you have your paperwork to hand. Before the first meeting, gather recent statements for any pensions, savings accounts and investments, plus details of any protection policies you already hold. You do not need to send anything in advance, but having it nearby means the adviser can talk through your actual situation rather than generalities.

Expect the first conversation to be as much about you as about money. A good adviser will ask about your goals, your timescales and how much risk you are comfortable with, and will explain whether their advice is independent or restricted and how they charge. This is also your chance to check they are authorised on the FCA register and to ask about their qualifications, so have those questions ready.

Because the meeting is online, think about the practicalities beforehand: a quiet room, a reliable connection and enough time without interruptions. If you would rather have a family member or friend listen in, say so when you arrange the call. Anything you are unsure about can be followed up in writing afterwards, and you should ask for the key points and any fees to be confirmed that way.

## What to understand about online financial advice

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Before you proceed, check that the adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. This matters whether advice is given face to face or online.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. They should also agree any ongoing service with you in writing. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications, which you can ask about.

## How online appointments work

## Questions to ask the financial adviser

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you authorised by the FCA, or an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and what is your firm's reference number?
- Will you provide independent advice considering the whole market, or restricted advice limited to certain products or providers?
- How will you charge for your advice, and will you set this out in writing before any work starts?
- How do you usually deliver advice online, and what technology will we use for our meetings?
- What ongoing service, if any, do you offer after the initial advice, and how is that agreed and charged?
- What qualifications do you hold, such as Chartered status or CFP certification, and how do you keep your knowledge up to date?

## Online Financial Advisor Near Me: FAQs

### Can I get financial advice entirely online?

Yes, many financial advisers offer advice by phone, video call or secure online messaging. The important thing is that the adviser is authorised by the FCA and that you understand whether their advice is independent or restricted, and how they charge.

### How do I check an adviser is regulated?

You can check the FCA register to confirm that the adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

### What does 'near me' mean if advice is online?

It usually means you'd like an adviser who understands your local area or is close enough for an occasional in-person meeting, even if most advice is delivered online. When you enquire, tell iComply your location and how you'd like to meet so we can introduce you to a suitable adviser.

### Will I know the cost before I commit?

Advisers must explain how they charge, and quotes are price on application. You should ask for a written explanation of fees before any work begins, and make sure you're comfortable with both the cost and the service you'll receive.

### Do I need to share sensitive documents before an online meeting?

No. At the enquiry stage you only need to describe broadly what you want help with. Personal documents and account details should only be shared with the adviser once you have checked their FCA authorisation and are comfortable to proceed.

### Is online advice different from face-to-face advice?

The regulation is the same either way: the adviser must be authorised by the FCA, must explain whether their advice is independent or restricted, and must set out how they charge. What changes is the delivery, so ask how meetings will run and how you will receive and discuss any written recommendations.

### Can I switch to meeting in person if I prefer?

That depends on the individual adviser, so it is worth asking when you enquire. If being able to meet locally matters to you, say so, and iComply can take that into account when introducing you to a suitable UK financial adviser.

### What is the difference between independent and restricted advice?

Independent advisers consider products from across the market. Restricted advisers focus on certain products or providers. Both can give good advice; they must tell you which they offer.

### Can everything be done online?

Often much of it can. Some steps may need a visit, depending on the service. The practice will tell you what can be done remotely.
