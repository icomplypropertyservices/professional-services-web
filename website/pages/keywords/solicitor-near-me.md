## Checking solicitors are qualified and regulated

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

## When to think twice

Most professionals are honest and competent, but it is worth knowing the warning signs:

- Guarantees about the outcome of a dispute
- Pressure to sign immediately without time to read terms
- Requests to send money to an account that changes by email
- A firm or individual you cannot find on the relevant register

## What happens once you have found a local solicitor

Once you have a shortlist of solicitors near you, the next step is usually a first conversation, often free of charge, where you describe your matter and they tell you whether it is something they handle. This is your chance to ask how they would approach your particular issue and who in the firm would actually do the work.

At that first appointment, take along anything relevant: contracts, letters, court papers or correspondence about the matter. The solicitor will explain your options in plain terms and set out likely costs, and you should ask them to confirm the fee arrangement in writing before you instruct them. If your matter is urgent, say so at the outset so they can tell you whether they can act in time.

It also helps to be clear about how you want to work. Some people prefer to visit a local office in person, while others are happy with phone and email contact, and many solicitors now offer both. Ask about this early, along with anything you need such as accessible premises, an interpreter or out-of-hours contact, so there are no surprises later.

## Costs and quotes for a solicitor

Legal fees depend on the type of matter, its complexity and who does the work. Firms commonly charge hourly rates, fixed fees for defined stages, or (in some claims) conditional fee arrangements. Regulated firms in England and Wales must publish pricing information for certain services and give you clear costs information at the outset. The firm will give you a POA quote.

Ask for a written estimate and for an explanation of what is not included, such as court fees, barristers' fees or experts. Ask too whether legal expenses insurance or legal aid might cover some or all of the cost.

## FAQs about solicitor near me

### How do I check that a solicitor is regulated?

In England and Wales, you can check an individual or firm on the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA) register. In Scotland, check with the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland with the Law Society of Northern Ireland.

### What should I do if I have a complaint about a solicitor?

First, use the firm's complaints procedure. If that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

### Can I use a solicitor who is not near me?

Yes, many solicitors work remotely or by phone and email, but some matters such as court hearings may require attendance in person. It is worth asking about this when you enquire.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a barrister?

Solicitors usually deal with clients directly, give advice, prepare documents and may instruct a barrister for specialist advocacy in court. Barristers are usually instructed by solicitors rather than directly by the public.

### Will a local solicitor always be cheaper than one further away?

Not necessarily. Fees depend on the type of work, the solicitor's experience and how the firm is structured, not simply on location. Ask each solicitor you speak to for a clear estimate and compare like with like.

### Can I change solicitors if I am not happy with the one near me?

Yes, you can usually move your matter to another solicitor, though you may be charged for work done so far and the firm must hand over your file. It is worth raising your concerns with the firm first, as many issues can be resolved without changing.

### Do I need to see a solicitor in person for every matter?

No. Many matters can be handled by phone, email or post, and some documents can be signed and witnessed remotely where the law allows. For court hearings or certain original documents you may still need to attend in person, so ask when you enquire.

### Can I get legal aid?

Legal aid is still available for some criminal, family (especially where there is domestic abuse), housing and immigration matters, subject to means and merits tests. A matched firm can tell you whether you are likely to qualify.
