## Understanding regulation and choice when searching locally

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA register. In Scotland, solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

## Costs and quotes for a solicitor

Legal fees depend on the type of matter, its complexity and who does the work. Firms commonly charge hourly rates, fixed fees for defined stages, or (in some claims) conditional fee arrangements. Regulated firms in England and Wales must publish pricing information for certain services and give you clear costs information at the outset. The firm will give you a POA quote.

Ask for a written estimate and for an explanation of what is not included, such as court fees, barristers' fees or experts. Ask too whether legal expenses insurance or legal aid might cover some or all of the cost.

## FAQs: a solicitor

### How do I check that a solicitor is properly regulated?

In England and Wales, you can check a solicitor or firm on the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA) register. In Scotland, check with the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland with the Law Society of Northern Ireland. This helps confirm they are authorised to practise.

### What should I do if I have a complaint about a solicitor?

First, use the firm's own complaints procedure. If that does not resolve the issue, in England and Wales you can take service complaints to the Legal Ombudsman. The SRA also handles conduct matters.

### Can I use a solicitor who is not local to me?

Yes, you can instruct a solicitor anywhere, but many people prefer one nearby for face-to-face meetings and local knowledge. iComply can introduce you to a suitable solicitor based on your needs, wherever they are.

### What information should I have ready when I first contact a solicitor?

Have a clear summary of your legal issue, any relevant documents, key dates and details of other parties involved. Also think about your budget and whether you prefer fixed fees or hourly rates.

### How can I compare local solicitors fairly?

Ask each firm the same set of questions, such as whether they specialise in your type of matter, what their fees are, and how they will keep you informed. Compare their answers side by side rather than relying on impressions alone.

### What should I bring to my first meeting with a solicitor?

Bring any letters, contracts, court papers or other documents related to your matter, along with a written summary of what has happened and any important dates. Also note down your questions and what you want to achieve.

### Is the first meeting with a solicitor usually free?

Not always. Some firms offer a free initial consultation, while others charge for the first meeting. It is best to ask when you book so you know what to expect.

### Is a solicitor the same as a lawyer?

"Lawyer" is a general word that covers solicitors, barristers, chartered legal executives and others. A solicitor is a specific regulated professional who can advise you, run your case and instruct a barrister if needed.
