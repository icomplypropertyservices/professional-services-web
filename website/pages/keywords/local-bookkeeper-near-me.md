## Questions to ask the bookkeeper

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the bookkeeper is right for you:

- Which body supervises you for anti-money-laundering purposes, and do you hold professional indemnity insurance?
- Are you a member of the Institute of Certified Bookkeepers or the Association of Accounting Technicians?
- How will we share documents each month, and how often will you reconcile my bank accounts?
- Will you prepare and submit my VAT returns and payroll, or do you work with someone else for that?
- Where does your work stop and an accountant's start, for example on year-end accounts and tax advice?
- Can you explain your fees clearly and what is included in your quote?

## Choosing between local bookkeepers

When several local bookkeepers are within reach, the useful comparison is not who claims the most but who fits how you actually work. Ask each one how they prefer to receive paperwork, how often they will reconcile your bank accounts, and whether they will handle VAT returns and payroll or pass those to someone else. That tells you more about day-to-day fit than any wording on a leaflet.

Fees are usually quoted on application, so ask each bookkeeper to set out what their quote covers and what would be charged separately, such as catching up on overdue records or dealing with payroll. A quote that looks modest can grow once extras are added, so compare like with like. It is also fair to ask how they would keep you informed if your cash flow starts to look tight.

Before you commit, check that the bookkeeper is supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by HMRC or by a professional body, and ask whether they hold professional indemnity insurance. Membership of the Institute of Certified Bookkeepers or the Association of Accounting Technicians shows training to a recognised standard and agreement to a code of conduct. None of this makes one bookkeeper the best for you; it simply gives you solid ground for your own judgement.

## FAQs: a bookkeeper

### Do I need a bookkeeper who is based near me?

Not necessarily, but a local bookkeeper can be easier to meet in person and to hand over documents to. If you prefer to work remotely, that can work too, as long as you agree how to share information securely.

### Is bookkeeping a regulated profession in the UK?

Bookkeeping is not a regulated title, but anyone providing bookkeeping services for a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by HMRC or by a professional body. Membership of the Institute of Certified Bookkeepers or the Association of Accounting Technicians shows a bookkeeper has trained to a recognised standard and agreed to a code of conduct.

### What should I have ready before I speak to a bookkeeper?

It helps to have your business records, bank statements and any VAT or payroll information to hand. Be ready to explain how you currently record sales and purchases and whether you work with an accountant.

### Can a local bookkeeper work alongside my accountant?

Yes, and it is common. A bookkeeper can keep your sales, purchase and bank records accurate through the year, while your accountant handles year-end accounts and tax advice. Ask each of them where their work stops so nothing falls between the two.

Expect them to ask about your business, how you currently record sales and purchases, and how far behind your books are. They may look at a sample of your records and explain how they would work with you month to month. You should leave with a clear idea of what they would do and what they would charge.

Not at all. Many people want a bookkeeper nearby so they can meet face to face if needed, while still sharing records securely online. Agree the method that suits you both, and confirm how often you will pass things over.
