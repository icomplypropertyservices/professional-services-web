## Understanding IR35 and finding the right help

IR35 is the set of rules that determine whether a contractor working through an intermediary, such as a limited company, is effectively an employee for tax purposes. The rules hinge on concepts like control, substitution, and mutuality of obligation, which can be subtle and are not always easy to apply on your own. Online tools can give an indication, but they rarely capture the full picture of your working practices.

When looking for the best help, it's worth checking a professional's qualifications and experience. 'Tax adviser' is not a protected title, so ask whether they hold a recognised qualification such as those from the Chartered Institute of Taxation or the Association of Taxation Technicians, or whether they are an accountant or solicitor with tax expertise. Also ask about their supervision for anti-money-laundering purposes and whether they have professional indemnity insurance.

## How to check you are in safe hands

"Tax adviser" is not a protected title in the UK. The Chartered Institute of Taxation (CTA qualification) and the Association of Taxation Technicians (ATT) are the main specialist bodies, and many accountants and solicitors also advise on tax. Members of these bodies follow Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation, a shared code covering honesty, confidentiality and dealing with HMRC.

Tax advisers who provide services for a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes. HMRC has also been tightening standards for paid tax agents, so it is reasonable to ask about qualifications, supervision and professional indemnity insurance before you go ahead.
