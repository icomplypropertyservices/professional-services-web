## Questions to ask the broker

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority, and can you give me your firm reference number so I can check the register?
- How will you be paid for arranging this policy, and will you disclose any commission or fees in writing?
- Will you check my sums insured and business interruption period to make sure they still fit my business?
- Which insurers will you approach for my trade, and what makes your recommended package suitable for my risks?
- What exclusions should I be aware of, and how would the policy respond if I needed to claim?
- If I have a complaint that we cannot resolve, how do I take it to the Financial Ombudsman Service?

## Costs and quotes for a business insurance broker

Brokers are often paid commission by the insurer and sometimes charge a broker fee; they must disclose how they are paid if you ask. Premiums are set by insurers based on risk. The broker quotes you, POA.

Compare cover, excesses and exclusions, not just the premium. The cheapest policy is not always the best value when you need to claim.

## How to check you are in safe hands

Insurance brokers and intermediaries in the UK must be authorised by the FCA, or be appointed representatives of an authorised firm. Check them on the FCA register. Many brokers belong to the British Insurance Brokers' Association (BIBA), and some hold Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute.

Brokers must understand your demands and needs, explain key policy features and exclusions, and tell you how they are paid. Complaints that are not resolved can go to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

A broker works on your behalf to find suitable products from providers. Ask how many providers they compare and how they are paid.

## FAQs: a business insurance broker

### Do I need a broker near me, or can I use one further away?

A local broker can be convenient for face-to-face meetings and may know local risks, but distance is not a requirement. What matters is that the broker understands your trade and is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority.

### How do I check that a business insurance broker is legitimate?

Search the Financial Conduct Authority register for the firm's name or reference number. You can also ask whether they are a member of the British Insurance Brokers' Association or hold Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute.

### Will a broker find me cheaper cover than going direct?

A broker can compare packages from several insurers and may find a better fit for your risks, but cheaper is not always better. Ask what is included and excluded, and check that the sums insured and business interruption cover are adequate.

### What information will a broker need from me?

Expect to provide details of your trade, turnover, premises, stock, vehicles, staff and any claims history. The broker must understand your demands and needs before recommending a policy.

### Is a broker more expensive than buying direct?

Not necessarily. Brokers can access insurers and schemes not available directly, and help with claims. The right choice depends on how complex your needs are.
