## Tracking down old pensions before you consolidate

Searching for a pension consolidation adviser near you often means part of the problem is that you are not entirely sure what you have. Old workplace schemes from previous employers can be easy to lose touch with, especially if you moved house or changed name, and an adviser can only assess what is actually there. The government's Pension Tracing Service is free to use and can help you find contact details for schemes you have lost track of, so it is worth doing this before your first meeting.

When you do meet an adviser locally, a useful first appointment is mostly about gathering facts rather than making a decision. Expect to go through each scheme's paperwork, statements and any letters about charges or exit fees, and to talk about what you want your retirement to look like. Nothing should be recommended on the day, and a careful adviser will want to check the detail of every pot before saying whether combining them helps or harms you.

It also helps to think about what consolidation can and cannot do. Bringing pots together may make them simpler to keep an eye on and could reduce charges, which can help growth over time, but it will not turn a poor-value pension into a good one on its own. If one of your schemes carries a promised annuity rate or other safeguarded feature, the adviser should flag this early, because those protections can be lost once you transfer.

## How to check you are in safe hands

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Some pension decisions carry special rules. Advice on transferring out of a defined benefit (final salary) scheme above a set value must come from an adviser with pension transfer specialist permissions, and safeguarded benefits deserve particular care. Free, impartial guidance is available from Pension Wise for people over 50 with defined contribution pensions.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## FAQs about pension consolidation advisor near me

### Do I need a local adviser for pension consolidation?

Not necessarily, but many people prefer to meet in person. A local adviser can look at your pensions face to face and explain whether consolidation is right for you, while still being regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority.

### Will consolidating my pensions lose me any protections?

It can. Some older pensions carry valuable protections, such as a promised annuity rate, that are lost on transfer. An adviser will check for these before making a recommendation.

### Can I consolidate a defined benefit pension?

Transferring out of a defined benefit (final salary) scheme above a set value requires advice from an adviser with pension transfer specialist permissions. Safeguarded benefits deserve particular care, and free guidance is available from Pension Wise for people with defined contribution pensions who have reached the age when it applies.

### How do I check an adviser is properly authorised?

You can check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

### I have lost track of an old workplace pension. Can an adviser find it for me?

An adviser can help you work through what you remember, but the Pension Tracing Service is the free starting point for finding contact details of schemes you have lost track of. Once you have the scheme details, a pension consolidation adviser can assess charges, exit fees and any protections before recommending whether to combine it with your other pots.
