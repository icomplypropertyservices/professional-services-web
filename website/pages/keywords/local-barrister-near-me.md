## Checking barristers are qualified and regulated

Barristers in England and Wales are regulated by the Bar Standards Board (BSB). You can check the Barristers' Register to see whether someone holds a current practising certificate and whether they are authorised to accept instructions directly from the public. In Scotland the equivalent professionals are advocates, regulated through the Faculty of Advocates.

Barristers usually work from chambers, with clerks who manage diaries and fees. Many barristers can be instructed through a solicitor in the traditional way, and those registered under the Public Access scheme can be instructed directly by members of the public for suitable matters.

## FAQs: a barrister

### Can I instruct a local barrister directly without going through a solicitor?

Some barristers are registered under the Public Access scheme, which allows them to accept instructions directly from members of the public for certain types of work. You can check the Barristers' Register to see whether a barrister is authorised to do this.

### How do I check that a barrister is properly regulated?

Barristers in England and Wales are regulated by the Bar Standards Board. You can use the Barristers' Register to confirm that someone holds a current practising certificate and to see their practising details.

### Will a local barrister charge me for an initial conversation?

Barristers usually agree fees for a defined piece of work, and some may charge for an initial consultation while others may not. It is best to ask about fees upfront when you make contact.

### What should I bring to my first meeting with a barrister?

It helps to bring any relevant documents, such as court papers, correspondence, and a summary of the key facts. Being organised can help the barrister understand your situation quickly.

### Does a barrister have to be based near my local court?

Not necessarily. Barristers can appear at courts across England and Wales, and some hearings are held remotely. Being local can make meetings and attendance easier, but the right specialism matters more than the postcode.

### How do I compare two local barristers when both seem suitable?

Ask each about their experience in cases like yours, their fees for the defined piece of work, and how they would approach it. You can also check both on the Barristers' Register to confirm their practising details and whether they accept direct instructions.

### Can I meet a local barrister before deciding whether to instruct them?

Many barristers offer an initial consultation, in person or remotely, so you can discuss your situation and ask about fees. Ask when you make contact whether there is a charge for that first conversation.

### Can I go straight to a barrister without a solicitor?

Often yes, through the Public Access scheme, if the barrister is registered and the matter is suitable. Some cases still need a solicitor, and the barrister will tell you if so.
