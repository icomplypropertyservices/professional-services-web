## Understanding care advice and regulation

In England, care homes and home care services are registered with and inspected by the Care Quality Commission (CQC), and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own regulators. Local councils assess care needs and finances and must provide information and advice. An independent care advisor can help you understand these options, but you should ask whether their advice is regulated.

Some care fees advice involves financial products, such as immediate needs annuities, and that part is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). The Society of Later Life Advisers (SOLLA) accredits specialist advisers. Knowing this helps you judge whether the person you speak to is giving regulated financial advice or general guidance.

## FAQs: a care adviser

### Do I need a care advisor if I can contact the council myself?

You can ask your local council for a needs assessment directly, and they must provide information and advice. A care advisor can help you prepare for that assessment and understand the options that follow, which some families find useful.

### How do I check whether a care advisor is regulated?

Ask whether any financial advice they give is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). If they are a specialist in later-life care fees, they may be accredited by the Society of Later Life Advisers (SOLLA). General care advice itself is not regulated in the same way.

### Will a care advisor help me choose a care home near me?

Care advisors can help you understand what to look for and how to compare local care homes, but you should always visit homes yourself before deciding. In England, you can check a home's registration and inspection reports with the Care Quality Commission (CQC).

### What should I have ready for a first conversation with a care advisor?

It helps to have a clear picture of the person's daily needs, any existing assessments, and whether funding is a worry. Notes about what you have already tried and what matters most to the family will make the conversation more useful.

### Who pays for care?

It depends on needs, income and assets. Councils carry out financial assessments, and some people qualify for NHS Continuing Healthcare. An adviser can explain how the rules apply to you.
