## Understanding the rules around dermal fillers

Dermal fillers are classed as devices rather than medicines, and the rules are tightening. The government has set out plans for a licensing scheme for non-surgical cosmetic procedures in England, but until that is in force you need to choose a clinic carefully yourself.

Practitioners on registers such as Save Face or the JCCP, and doctors and nurses registered with the GMC or NMC, offer extra reassurance. Clinics run by regulated healthcare providers may also be registered with the CQC. A good clinic should use CE or UKCA-marked products and be able to show that its practitioners are trained in anatomy and managing complications.

## FAQs: a dermal fillers clinic

### Are dermal fillers regulated in the UK?

Dermal fillers are treated as devices rather than medicines, and the rules are tightening. The government has set out plans for a licensing scheme for non-surgical cosmetic procedures in England, so until that is in force it is worth checking a practitioner's training and registration carefully.

### What should I look for in a clinic near me?

Look for a practitioner who can show relevant training in anatomy and complication management, and who uses CE or UKCA-marked products. Registers such as Save Face or the JCCP, and registration with the GMC or NMC for doctors and nurses, offer extra reassurance.

### Why does it matter whether hyaluronidase is on site?

Hyaluronidase is used to help dissolve hyaluronic acid filler if there is a problem. Asking whether it is kept on site is a sensible question when you are choosing a clinic for dermal fillers.
