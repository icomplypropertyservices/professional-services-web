## Checking aesthetic practitioners are qualified and regulated

Anti-wrinkle injections use a prescription-only medicine, so a prescriber such as a doctor, nurse prescriber, dentist or pharmacist prescriber must assess you, normally face to face. Dermal fillers are devices rather than medicines, and the rules are tightening: the government has set out plans for a licensing scheme for non-surgical cosmetic procedures in England.

Until licensing is in force, choose a clinic carefully. Practitioners on registers such as Save Face or the JCCP, and doctors and nurses registered with the GMC or NMC, offer extra reassurance. Clinics run by regulated healthcare providers may also be registered with the CQC.

## Key things to know about dermal fillers

A few points come up again and again with dermal fillers:

- Hyaluronic fillers can be dissolved.
- Vascular complications are rare but serious.
- Ask about emergency protocols.

Raise any of these with the aesthetic practitioner you are introduced to; they should be happy to explain.

## FAQs about fillers near me

### Are dermal fillers the same as anti-wrinkle injections?

No. Dermal fillers are devices rather than medicines, and the rules around them are tightening.

### Can fillers be reversed if I don't like the result?

Hyaluronic acid fillers can be dissolved, which is one reason they are widely used. Ask the practitioner about this before treatment so you know where you stand.

### What should I check before choosing a clinic near me?

Look at the practitioner's registration and the clinic's arrangements for emergencies.

### Is there a licensing scheme for these treatments yet?

The government has set out plans for a licensing scheme for non-surgical cosmetic procedures in England, but it is not yet in force. Until then, choosing a clinic carefully matters all the more.

### Are under-18s allowed to have these treatments?

In England it is illegal to give Botox-type injections or dermal fillers for cosmetic purposes to under-18s.
