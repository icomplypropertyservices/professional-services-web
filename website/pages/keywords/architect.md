## What the first appointment with an architect involves

When you first sit down with an architect, expect a conversation about your site, your ideas and how you want to use the building rather than a sales pitch. They will usually ask about your budget and any constraints such as a listed building, a conservation area or a party wall, and they may want to visit the property before putting anything in writing. It helps to bring any plans, deeds or correspondence from the council you already have.

The appointment should then be set out in writing, and this is where the RIBA stages are useful as a shared language. The stages run from early feasibility and concept design through to technical drawings for building regulations and on to construction and handover, and a clear appointment says which of these the architect is responsible for and what each part covers. Some people only need help getting through planning permission; others want the practice to stay involved on site.

Fees are normally quoted on application once the scope is known, because the work varies so much between a small domestic alteration and a full new build. Ask how the fee is built up, what is excluded, and whether planning application fees and building regulations fees are separate from the architect's own charge. Getting the appointment in writing before work begins protects you and gives you a clear basis for comparison.

## The protected title of architect

In the UK, the title "architect" is protected by law. Only people on the Architects Registration Board (ARB) register can call themselves architects, and they must follow the ARB code of conduct and hold professional indemnity insurance.

Many practices are also RIBA Chartered Practices, which means they meet certain standards. Architectural designers and technologists can do excellent work too, but they are not registered architects, so it's worth knowing which you are hiring.

## FAQs about architect

### Do I need an architect for a small extension?

Not always. Some small projects can be handled by an architectural designer or technologist, but if you want the assurance of a regulated professional, an architect is a good choice. They can also help navigate planning and building regulations.

### What is the difference between an architect and an architectural designer?

An architect is registered with the Architects Registration Board (ARB) and has completed rigorous training and is bound by a code of conduct. Architectural designers may have relevant qualifications and experience but are not registered architects, so they cannot call themselves architects.

### How do architects charge for their services?

Architects typically charge a percentage of the construction cost, a fixed fee, or an hourly rate. The fee should be agreed in writing before work starts, and it's important to clarify what is included.

### Can an architect help with planning permission?

Yes, architects can prepare and submit planning applications and building regulations drawings on your behalf. They can also liaise with the local authority and other professionals as needed.

### Do I need an architect or will a structural engineer do?

They do different jobs. An architect designs the building and prepares drawings for planning and building regulations, while a structural engineer works out how loads are carried, which matters if you are removing walls or altering a structure. Many projects need both, and your architect can usually advise when a structural engineer should be brought in.

### Can an architect help if my plans have already been refused planning permission?

Yes. An architect can review the refusal and the officer's reasons, then advise on whether to revise the scheme, submit a fresh application or appeal. Bringing one in early, before you resubmit, is often more useful than waiting until a second refusal.

### Is an architect the same as a building surveyor?

No. A building surveyor inspects a property and reports on its condition, which is what you would commission before buying. An architect designs and prepares drawings for your project, so the two roles can sit side by side but are not interchangeable.

### Do I need an architect for an extension?

Not legally, but a registered architect brings design skill, planning experience and accountability. For simple work, an architectural technologist or designer may be enough. We can match you to the right level of help.
