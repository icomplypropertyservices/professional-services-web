## Questions to ask the project management surveyor

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the surveyor is right for you:

- What experience do you have managing projects similar to mine, such as domestic extensions or refurbishments?
- How often will you visit the site and what reporting will I receive?
- Will you handle the appointment of contractors and agree the contract terms on my behalf?
- How do you monitor budget and programme, and what happens if costs start to rise?
- Are you a member of RICS, and is your firm regulated by RICS?
- What is your complaints procedure if I'm not happy with the service?

## Why local knowledge and clear agreements matter

Choosing a project management surveyor near you means they can visit the site regularly without long travel, which helps with keeping an eye on progress and quality. Many surveyors are members of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), and RICS-regulated firms must follow professional standards, hold insurance and offer a complaints process. Chartered surveyors use the letters MRICS or FRICS after their name.

Before you appoint anyone, make sure you understand what they will do and how often they will report to you. Clear contracts reduce risk, and regular site meetings keep control. Agree reporting frequency and the scope of their project management role in writing so there are no surprises.

## Checking surveyors are qualified and regulated

Chartered surveyors use the letters MRICS or FRICS. Some specialist roles have their own bodies, such as the Faculty of Party Wall Surveyors or the Property Care Association for damp and timber specialists.

Check the RICS directory, ask which report type you are getting and confirm whether the surveyor is independent of the sale. For asbestos work, look for organisations accredited by UKAS for surveying.

## FAQs: a project management surveyor

### What does a project management surveyor do?

A project management surveyor runs building projects on your behalf, managing contractors, programme, quality and budget. They act as your representative to help keep the project on track and to your specification.

### How do I find a project management surveyor near me?

You can search the RICS directory for chartered surveyors in your area, or ask iComply to introduce you to a suitable local professional. It's worth checking that they have experience with projects like yours and that you understand their scope of work.

### Do I need a project management surveyor for a small building project?

For smaller projects you might not need full project management, but a surveyor can still help with contracts and quality checks. It depends on the complexity of the work and how much time you have to oversee it yourself.

### What should I agree with the surveyor before work starts?

You should agree the scope of their role, how often they will visit the site, what reports you'll receive and how they will manage the budget and programme. Clear contracts reduce risk, so make sure everything is in writing.

### Is a mortgage valuation the same as a survey?

No. A mortgage valuation is carried out for the lender. A survey is carried out for you and looks at the property's condition.
