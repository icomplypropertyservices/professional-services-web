## FAQs about purchase only conveyancer near me

### What does 'purchase only' mean in conveyancing?

It means the conveyancer acts solely for you as the buyer and does not also act for the seller. This avoids any conflict of interest and ensures your interests are the only ones they represent.

### Do I need a conveyancer who is local to the property?

Not necessarily, but a conveyancer familiar with the area can be helpful for local searches and knowing the local market. Many conveyancers now work remotely, so location is often less important than expertise and communication.

### How do I check if a conveyancer is qualified?

In England and Wales, conveyancers must be either solicitors regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority or licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. You can check their registration on the relevant regulator's public register.

### What should I do if my lender requires a conveyancer on their panel?

Ask the conveyancer directly whether they are on your lender's panel before you instruct them. If they are not, you may need to choose a firm that is, or your lender may allow you to use your chosen firm under certain conditions.

### When should I instruct a conveyancer?

As soon as your offer is accepted, or before you list a property for sale. Instructing early helps you avoid delays.
