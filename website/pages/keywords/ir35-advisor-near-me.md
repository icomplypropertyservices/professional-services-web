## Why working practices matter as much as the contract

For IR35, HMRC looks at the reality of how you work, not just what the contract says. Things like who controls your work, whether you can send a substitute, and how you're integrated into the client's business all matter. An adviser will want to understand your day-to-day arrangements to give you a realistic view.

For medium and large clients, the client makes the status decision and should provide a Status Determination Statement. If you disagree, you can raise a dispute with the client. An adviser can help you frame that challenge and explain your options.

## How to check you are in safe hands

"Tax adviser" is not a protected title in the UK. The Chartered Institute of Taxation (CTA qualification) and the Association of Taxation Technicians (ATT) are the main specialist bodies, and many accountants and solicitors also advise on tax. Members of these bodies follow Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation, a shared code covering honesty, confidentiality and dealing with HMRC.

Tax advisers who provide services for a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes. HMRC has also been tightening standards for paid tax agents, so it is reasonable to ask about qualifications, supervision and professional indemnity insurance before you go ahead.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## What happens at a first IR35 appointment

A first meeting with an IR35 adviser is mostly about gathering facts rather than giving a verdict on the spot. Expect them to read your contract closely, then ask how the engagement actually runs: who decides what you do and when, whether you can send someone else, how you're treated alongside the client's own staff, and whether you take on financial risk. Bring the contract, any Status Determination Statement and a plain description of your working week.

The adviser will also want to know who the client is, because for medium and large clients the status decision sits with them and should come with reasons you can examine. If you're the client rather than the contractor, the conversation shifts to how you reached your determination and whether your records would stand up if HMRC asked. Either way, the adviser should explain the reasoning behind their view, not just hand you an answer.

You may not leave with a final conclusion at that first appointment, especially if the adviser needs to see more paperwork or speak to the end client. Ask what happens next, what further information they need and how their charges work, so there are no surprises. It's also fine to say if you'd rather meet in person locally or work through things remotely.

## IR35 Advisor Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need an IR35 advisor if I'm a contractor?

If you work through your own limited company and provide services to clients, IR35 may apply. An adviser can go through your contracts and working practices to help you understand your position and what to do next.

### What is a Status Determination Statement?

For medium and large clients, the client must give you a Status Determination Statement setting out its decision and the reasons. An adviser can help you understand the statement and challenge it if needed.

### How do I choose an IR35 advisor near me?

Look for a tax adviser with relevant experience and qualifications, and ask about their approach to looking at working practices. It's reasonable to ask about their supervision for anti-money-laundering purposes and professional indemnity insurance before you go ahead.

### Is an IR35 adviser the same as an accountant?

Not necessarily. Many accountants advise on IR35, and so do solicitors and specialist tax advisers, but "tax adviser" isn't a protected title in the UK. Ask what qualifications and IR35 experience they hold, and whether they're supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, before you go ahead.

### Can I just rely on an online check instead of an adviser?

An online tool can only work with what you type in, and IR35 turns on the reality of your working practices as well as the contract wording. An adviser can ask about the details a checklist won't capture, such as control, substitution and how you're integrated into the client's business. ### Should I reply to HMRC before speaking to an adviser?

Note the deadline on the letter, but avoid sending a detailed reply before you have advice if the matter is complex. An adviser can often ask HMRC for more time where it is reasonable.
