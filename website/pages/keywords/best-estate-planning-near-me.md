## Checking wills and probate specialists are qualified and regulated

Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity in England and Wales, so some will writers are not regulated. Solicitors (SRA) and chartered legal executives (CILEx) are regulated, insured and covered by the Legal Ombudsman. Many specialists are also members of STEP (the Society of Trust and Estate Practitioners) or Solicitors for the Elderly. Applying for a grant of probate on someone else's behalf for a fee is a reserved activity.

When choosing a provider, ask whether they are regulated, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance and where your original will is stored. Scotland has its own rules on succession and confirmation, so make sure your adviser practises in the right jurisdiction.

Before you meet anyone, it helps to write down what you actually want to achieve, such as providing for a partner, protecting a child's inheritance or making sure the right person can act for you. ## FAQs about best estate planning near me

### Do I need a solicitor for estate planning?

Not always, because writing a will is not a reserved legal activity in England and Wales. However, solicitors and chartered legal executives are regulated, insured and covered by the Legal Ombudsman, which gives you more protection if something goes wrong.

### What is the difference between a will writer and a solicitor?

A will writer may not be regulated, while a solicitor is regulated by the SRA and a chartered legal executive by CILEx. Both solicitors and chartered legal executives are insured and covered by the Legal Ombudsman.

### How often should I look at my estate plan again?

It is sensible to go over your estate plan regularly, especially after major life events such as marriage, divorce, a birth or a significant change in your finances. Your adviser can tell you what is appropriate for your circumstances.

### Can estate planning help with inheritance tax?

Yes, estate planning can bring together wills, trusts and gifts to help manage inheritance tax. Because this often overlaps with financial advice, it is worth checking that your estate planner will coordinate with your financial adviser.

### What should I bring to a first estate planning appointment?

Bring a list of your assets and liabilities, details of any existing will or powers of attorney, and the names of the people you want to provide for. If you have a financial adviser, bring their details too, so the specialist can coordinate.

### Will the first appointment cost me anything?

### How do I judge which estate planning specialist is best for me?

Rather than relying on a label, compare whether they are regulated, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance and how clearly they explain your options. Ask where your original will is stored and how their advice will fit with your financial adviser.

### Do I need a solicitor for probate?

Not always. Executors can apply for probate themselves for simpler estates. Many choose professional help where there is property, inheritance tax, overseas assets or family tension.
