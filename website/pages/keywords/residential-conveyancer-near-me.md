## Questions to ask the conveyancer

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority or the Council for Licensed Conveyancers?
- Do you have experience with properties like mine, for example leasehold or new build?
- Are you on my lender's panel, and will you act for them as well as for me?
- How will you keep me updated on progress, and who will be my main point of contact?
- What are your fees and disbursements, and when will I need to pay them?
- How do you handle local searches, and what might affect the searches in my area?

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity. It can be carried out by solicitors regulated by the SRA or by licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers (CLC). Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money. In Scotland, property transactions are handled by solicitors under Scottish law.

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme (CQS) is a common accreditation. Check that your chosen firm is on your lender's panel before you instruct them.

## Choosing between local conveyancers and checking what 'near me' really means

## FAQs about residential conveyancer near me

### Do I need a conveyancer who is local to the property?

Not necessarily, as much of the work can be done remotely, but a local conveyancer may have useful knowledge of local issues that affect searches. It can also be easier to visit their office if you prefer face-to-face contact.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a licensed conveyancer?

Both are qualified to handle residential conveyancing in England and Wales, but solicitors are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority and licensed conveyancers by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers.

### How do I check if a conveyancer is on my lender's panel?

You can ask the conveyancer directly, or check with your lender, as many lenders publish a list of approved conveyancers. It is important to confirm this before you instruct them, as otherwise your lender may not accept their work.

### What is the Conveyancing Quality Scheme?

The Conveyancing Quality Scheme is an accreditation run by the Law Society for conveyancing firms in England and Wales. It indicates that a firm meets certain standards, but you should still check that the firm is on your lender's panel and ask about their experience with your property type.

### Will a conveyancer near me definitely know about local searches in my area?

Not automatically, so it is worth asking directly. A local conveyancer may have useful familiarity with issues that commonly affect searches in the area, but you should still ask how they will handle searches and what they would do if something came up.

### Can I use a conveyancer who is not near the property?

Yes, in most cases much of the work can be handled remotely, and location is not a requirement for acting on a residential transaction. What matters more is regulation, experience with your property type and whether they are on your lender's panel.

### What should I have ready before I speak to a conveyancer?

It helps to know whether you are buying, selling, remortgaging or transferring equity, the property type and location, and your lender's name if you have a mortgage. Having these details to hand means the conveyancer can quote you more accurately and tell you what they need from you.
