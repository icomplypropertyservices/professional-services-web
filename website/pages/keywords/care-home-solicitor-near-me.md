## Checking solicitors are qualified and regulated

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

## Understanding care home contracts and funding

A care home contract is a legal agreement, and once signed it can be difficult to change. It is worth having a solicitor read it before you commit, so you can ask questions about fees, notice periods and what happens if your needs change. They can also explain how NHS Continuing Healthcare might affect what you pay.

If someone lacks mental capacity and has no lasting power of attorney, the Court of Protection may need to appoint a deputy to make decisions about their care and finances. A solicitor can guide you through that process. Always check that the person you speak to is a solicitor or another authorised professional, as the word "lawyer" alone is not protected.

## FAQs: a care home solicitor

### Do I need a solicitor to sign a care home contract?

You are not legally required to use a solicitor, but a contract is a binding legal agreement. A solicitor can check the terms and explain what they mean before you sign, which can help you avoid problems later.

### Can a solicitor help if I disagree with the local authority about care funding?

Yes, a solicitor can advise you on your rights and help you challenge a decision about care funding or needs. They can also assist with disputes about the care provided by a home.

### What is the difference between a lasting power of attorney and deputyship?

A lasting power of attorney is a legal document you set up in advance to let someone you trust make decisions for you if you lose capacity. Deputyship is when the Court of Protection appoints someone to make decisions for a person who has already lost capacity and has no power of attorney in place.

### How can I check that a solicitor is properly regulated?

In England and Wales, solicitors are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland, check with the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland with the Law Society of Northern Ireland.

### Can I get legal aid?

Legal aid is still available for some criminal, family (especially where there is domestic abuse), housing and immigration matters, subject to means and merits tests. A matched firm can tell you whether you are likely to qualify.
