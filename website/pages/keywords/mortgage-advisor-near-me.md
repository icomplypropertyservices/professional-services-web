## Understanding who you are dealing with

Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and you can check this on the FCA register. Advisers typically hold a qualification such as CeMAP.

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders, and how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

## What happens once you have a local adviser in mind

When you search for a mortgage advisor near me, the word "near" often matters more than people admit. You may want to drop documents in by hand, sit opposite someone while they explain lender criteria, or simply have a short journey if the process needs more than one meeting. It is worth saying early whether you want face-to-face meetings or are happy with phone and video, because many advisers work across a wider area than their office suggests.

At a first appointment, expect the adviser to go through your income, outgoings, deposit or equity, and plans for the property. They should explain whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel, and set out how they are paid before you commit to anything. Bring identification, proof of income and details of any existing mortgage or credit commitments so the conversation can be useful rather than general.

A common misunderstanding is that a local adviser will automatically have access to deals nobody else can see. In reality, the value lies in how they match your circumstances to lenders' criteria and explain the trade-offs, such as a lower initial rate against higher arrangement costs. It also helps to ask how they will keep in touch after completion, for example when your current deal nears its end.

## FAQs about mortgage advisor near me

### How do I check that a mortgage advisor is properly authorised?

Mortgage advice is regulated by the FCA, and any broker or adviser should be authorised or an appointed representative of an authorised firm. You can check this yourself on the FCA register before you proceed.

### What does it mean if an advisor only uses a limited panel of lenders?

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel. A limited panel means they can only recommend from a set group of lenders, which may still suit your circumstances, but you should know this upfront.

### How will I know what the advice will cost?

Brokers must explain how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. Ask for this in writing before you commit, and check the total cost alongside any lender arrangement fees.

### Can I get advice about protection at the same time as my mortgage?

Yes, it is sensible to ask about protection such as life cover or income protection while discussing your mortgage. The adviser can explain the options that fit your circumstances and budget.

### Does using a local mortgage adviser cost more than one further away?

There is no rule that local advice costs more or less. Brokers must tell you how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both, so ask for the total cost in writing and compare it with what another adviser would charge for the same circumstances.

### Can I meet a mortgage adviser in person before deciding to use them?

Many advisers offer an initial conversation by phone or in person so you can judge whether they understand your situation. Ask what the first meeting covers and whether there is any charge before you agree to proceed.

### What should I bring to a first appointment with a mortgage adviser?

Bring identification, proof of income such as payslips or accounts if you are self-employed, and details of your deposit, savings and any existing debts. Having these ready helps the adviser give you a realistic picture rather than a rough estimate.

### Is a broker better than going to my bank?

A broker can compare many lenders and may find options your bank does not offer. Your bank can only offer its own products. Either can suit you; a broker gives you a wider view.
