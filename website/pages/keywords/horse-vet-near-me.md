## Questions to ask the vet

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Do you visit yards in my area, and how quickly can you usually get here in an emergency?
- Are you registered with the RCVS, and is the practice part of the RCVS Practice Standards Scheme?
- Do you have experience with horses of my horse's age, breed or discipline?
- What is your approach to routine care such as vaccinations, dental checks and worming?
- How do you handle out-of-hours emergencies, and who covers when you are unavailable?
- Can you provide a written estimate for any planned treatment or procedure?

## Checking vets are qualified and regulated

Every vet practising in the UK must be registered with the Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons (RCVS), and you can search the RCVS register by name before you book. Veterinary nurses have their own RCVS register too. Many practices also join the voluntary RCVS Practice Standards Scheme, which inspects premises, equipment and clinical processes.

Some vets hold further qualifications such as an RCVS certificate, advanced practitioner status or specialist status in a particular species or discipline. If your animal has a complex or unusual condition, it is reasonable to ask whether the vet treating it holds one of these, or whether they refer to someone who does.

## FAQs: a horse vet

### How do I check that a horse vet is properly registered?

Veterinary nurses also have their own RCVS register, so you can check them too.

### What should I have ready for an emergency vet visit?

Keep a basic first aid kit at the yard and your vet's emergency number to hand. It also helps to have your horse's passport and insurance details accessible, and to know your yard's postcode and access instructions.

### Can I share a vet visit with other horse owners at my yard?

Many vets are happy to see several horses on the same visit if you arrange it in advance, which can be more convenient and may reduce call-out costs. Agree routine visit days with others on your yard and ask the vet about booking multiple appointments.

### What if my horse needs a specialist?
