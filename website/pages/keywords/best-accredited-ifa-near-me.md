## Understanding 'best' and 'accredited' for independent financial advice

The word 'best' is personal: the right adviser for you depends on your circumstances, how you like to communicate and the type of advice you need. There is no official ranking of independent financial advisers, so you should judge for yourself by checking independence, charges and qualifications. 'Accredited' is not a single official label; instead, look for recognised qualifications such as Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, or certifications like CFP, and confirm the firm's FCA authorisation.

Independent financial advice means the adviser considers products from across the market, while restricted advice is limited to certain products or providers. Advisers must tell you which type they offer and how they charge. Always check the adviser or firm on the FCA register, and remember that if something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

## Checking accreditation before you choose

Accreditation means a practice or professional has met standards set by a regulator or professional body. It is worth checking both the individual's registration and any practice-level accreditation, as they cover different things.

Ask which accreditations apply, what they require and how you can verify them online. Accreditation is not a guarantee of the outcome, but it does mean there are standards, a code of conduct and a route for complaints.
