## Questions to ask a chiropractor

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you registered with the General Chiropractic Council, and what is your GCC registration number?
- What does the evidence say about chiropractic treatment for my specific problem?
- What will the first appointment involve, and will you explain any risks before treatment?
- How many sessions do you expect I might need, and will you check my progress as we go?
- Do you offer a treatment plan that I can pay for session by session rather than upfront?
- What should I do if my symptoms get worse after treatment, and how can I contact you between appointments?

## Chiropractors: FAQs

### What does a chiropractor do?

Chiropractors assess and treat problems with the spine, joints and muscles, often using spinal manipulation alongside exercise and lifestyle advice. They focus particularly on back and neck pain, where the evidence for manual therapy is strongest.

### How do I check if a chiropractor is properly qualified?

All chiropractors in the UK must be registered with the General Chiropractic Council (GCC) to use the title. You can search the GCC register online to confirm registration. Many also belong to professional bodies such as the Royal College of Chiropractors or the British Chiropractic Association.

### Is chiropractic treatment safe?

Chiropractic treatment is generally considered safe when carried out by a registered practitioner, but like any physical treatment it carries some risks, such as temporary soreness. A careful chiropractor will explain these risks and check your medical history before treating you. If you have concerns, ask about the evidence for your specific condition.

### Will I have to pay for a long course of treatment upfront?

You should be cautious about any practitioner who asks for a large prepayment before you've had a proper assessment. A responsible chiropractor will usually suggest a short trial of treatment and check your progress before recommending more. Quotes are price on application, so you can ask about costs and payment options before you commit.

### Is treatment painful?

Some techniques can feel uncomfortable, and you may feel sore for a day or two. Your practitioner should explain what to expect and adapt treatment to your comfort.
