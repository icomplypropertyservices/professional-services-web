## How to check you are in safe hands

"Counsellor" and "psychotherapist" are not protected titles in the UK. To protect yourself, choose a practitioner on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS. Practitioner psychologists, who use titles such as clinical or counselling psychologist, must be registered with the HCPC.

Accredited registers require training, supervision, a code of ethics and a complaints procedure. A good therapist will explain their approach, confidentiality and its limits in the first session.

## FAQs: a couples counsellor

### Do we both need to attend every session?

Couples counsellors usually work with both partners together, but some also offer individual sessions alongside. It is worth asking the counsellor how they structure sessions when you first make contact.

### How do we choose a couples counsellor near us?

Look for someone with relationship-specific training and check whether they are on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS. A first conversation can help you see whether you both feel comfortable with their approach.

### Can we have couples counselling online if we cannot find someone nearby?

Many counsellors offer online sessions as well as in-person work, so it is worth asking. Online counselling can be useful if one of you travels or if local availability is limited.

### What if my partner is reluctant to come?

It is common for one partner to be more hesitant, and a counsellor can discuss this with you. You can ask whether they offer an initial conversation to help your partner understand what is involved.

### What is the difference between counselling and psychotherapy?

The terms overlap. Counselling often focuses on a specific problem over a shorter period, while psychotherapy may explore deeper patterns over a longer time. A good practitioner will explain their approach.
