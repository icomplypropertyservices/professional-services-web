## Qualifications and registration to look for

Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity. It can be carried out by solicitors regulated by the SRA or by licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers (CLC). Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money. In Scotland, property transactions are handled by solicitors under Scottish law.

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme (CQS) is a common accreditation. Check that your chosen firm is on your lender's panel before you instruct them.

"Lawyer" is a general term. In England and Wales the person handling your matter will usually be a solicitor, chartered legal executive or barrister, each regulated by their own body.

## FAQs: a conveyancing solicitor

### Do I need a solicitor or a licensed conveyancer?

Both can carry out conveyancing in England and Wales. Solicitors are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority and licensed conveyancers by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers, and both must hold professional indemnity insurance. Your choice may depend on whether you also need advice on wider legal issues.

### How do I check if a conveyancer is on my lender's panel?

Ask the conveyancer directly whether they are on your lender's panel, and confirm this with your lender before you instruct them. Many lenders publish a list of approved conveyancers, so it is worth checking that too.

### What does CQS accreditation mean?

The Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme (CQS) is a common accreditation for conveyancing firms. It shows that a firm has met certain standards set by the Law Society, but it is not a mark of quality on its own, so it is still worth asking about the firm's experience and who will handle your file.

### Can a local conveyancer help if I am buying in another part of the country?

Yes, conveyancers can act for you on property anywhere in England and Wales, and many work remotely. A local firm may still be convenient if you prefer to drop off documents or speak in person, but it is not essential for the legal work.

### Is a local conveyancer better than one further away?

Not necessarily. The legal work can be done from anywhere in England and Wales, so a firm further away may suit you just as well. A local firm can be convenient if you prefer to hand over documents in person or speak face to face, so it comes down to how you like to work.

### Why do quotes for the same property vary between firms?

Firms set their own fees and may include different things in a quote, such as searches, leasehold work or dealing with a lender. Ask each one what is included and what might be charged extra so you are comparing like with like. Price is on application, so it is worth getting the detail in writing.

### Should I choose a solicitor or a licensed conveyancer?

Both can carry out conveyancing in England and Wales, and both are regulated and must hold professional indemnity insurance. A solicitor may be the better fit if you also need advice on wider legal issues linked to the move, such as a boundary question or a restriction on the title.

### When should I instruct a conveyancer?

As soon as your offer is accepted, or before you list a property for sale. Instructing early helps you avoid delays.
