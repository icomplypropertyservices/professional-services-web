## Qualifications and registration to look for

Every vet practising in the UK must be registered with the Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons (RCVS), and you can search the RCVS register by name before you book. Veterinary nurses have their own RCVS register too. Many practices also join the voluntary RCVS Practice Standards Scheme, which inspects premises, equipment and clinical processes.

Some vets hold further qualifications such as an RCVS certificate, advanced practitioner status or specialist status in a particular species or discipline. If your animal has a complex or unusual condition, it is reasonable to ask whether the vet treating it holds one of these, or whether they refer to someone who does.

## Questions to ask the vet

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you open now, and will a vet be on site throughout the night?
- Do you monitor overnight patients with a vet or nurse on site all night?
- What are your out-of-hours consultation fees, and how are they charged?
- Can you tell me whether my pet needs to be seen immediately or can wait until morning?
- Do you have the equipment to deal with my pet's specific problem, or would you refer us elsewhere?
- Will you share information with my usual vet so they have a full record?

## Warning signs to watch for

Be cautious if you notice any of the following:

- Refusal to provide a written estimate or prescription when asked
- Pressure to agree to expensive treatment without a clear explanation of alternatives
- No clear plan for follow-up after surgery or treatment
- Vague answers about who covers emergencies at night and at weekends

### Can I check if a vet is registered?

Every vet practising in the UK must be registered with the Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons (RCVS), and you can search the RCVS register by name before you book. Veterinary nurses have their own RCVS register too.

If your pet has a life-threatening emergency, call the nearest open practice immediately. For general advice when your practice is closed, you can call NHS 111 for human health queries, but for animal health you should speak to a vet.
