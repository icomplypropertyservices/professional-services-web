## Qualifications and registration to look for

In the UK, the word "accountant" on its own is not a protected title, so anyone can use it. Titles such as Chartered Accountant (ICAEW, ICAS or Chartered Accountants Ireland), Chartered Certified Accountant (ACCA) and Chartered Management Accountant (CIMA) are protected, and members are bound by their body's ethics, training and complaints rules. Only registered auditors can sign statutory audit reports.

Every practice offering accountancy services must also be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by a professional body or by HMRC. It is reasonable to ask which body supervises a firm, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance, and how complaints are handled.

## FAQs: a corporation tax accountant

### When is corporation tax usually due?

Corporation tax is usually due nine months and one day after your company's year end. The return itself has its own filing deadline, so it is worth confirming both dates with your accountant early.

### Should I ask about R&D relief?

Yes, it is worth asking whether your company's work could qualify for R&D relief, as it can affect the tax you pay. Your accountant can look at your activities and tell you whether a claim is possible.

### What is marginal relief?

Marginal relief applies between certain profit thresholds, so the rate of corporation tax rises gradually rather than jumping straight to the higher rate. Your accountant can work out whether it affects your company.

### How do I check an accountant is properly supervised?

Ask which professional body or HMRC supervises the practice for anti-money-laundering purposes, and whether they hold professional indemnity insurance. You can also check whether any protected titles they use, such as Chartered Accountant, are held with the relevant body.
