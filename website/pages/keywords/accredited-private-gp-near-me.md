## Checking that a private GP is properly accredited

Every doctor practising in the UK must be registered and hold a licence to practise with the General Medical Council (GMC). GPs also appear on the GMC's GP register, and you can search the GMC's online register by name to confirm a doctor's registration and licence. This is the clearest way to check that a doctor is properly accredited.

Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects services and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA. You can check a clinic's registration and any inspection findings with the relevant regulator before you book.

A private GP does not replace your NHS GP. Good private clinics will ask for your consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice, so your records stay complete and medication is prescribed safely.

### How can I check that a private GP is accredited?

You can search the General Medical Council's online register by the doctor's name to confirm registration and licence to practise. For the clinic, check registration with the Care Quality Commission in England, Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, Healthcare Improvement Scotland, or the RQIA in Northern Ireland, depending on where you are.

### Can a private GP see me for the same problems as my NHS GP?

### What should I expect to pay for a private GP appointment?

### Does an accredited private GP replace my NHS GP?

No. A private GP does not replace your NHS GP, and good private clinics will ask for your consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice so your records stay complete and medication is prescribed safely. It is worth keeping your NHS registration even if you see a private GP for convenience.

### What should I bring to my first private GP appointment?

Bring a list of your symptoms and how long you have had them, any medication you take, and details of relevant past treatment. If you have seen your NHS GP about the same problem, say so, and mention whether you are happy for notes to be shared between the two.

### Can a private GP near me see me if I am not registered anywhere locally?

Private GPs generally see people who are not registered with a local NHS practice, though you should confirm this when you enquire. It is still sensible to remain registered with an NHS GP, because private care is not a substitute for NHS primary care.

### Will a private GP share my notes with my NHS GP?

With your consent, most do, and it is safer for you. Joined-up records help avoid duplicate tests and medication clashes.
