## Questions to ask the professional

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Do you hold British Menopause Society accreditation, and what does your menopause clinic involve?
- How long is the appointment, and will there be time to go through all of my symptoms?

- Will you share your consultation notes with my NHS GP, and how does that work?
- Can HRT be continued through my NHS GP after the private consultation?
- What should I do if my symptoms change before my next appointment?

## Menopause Clinic Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need a referral to book a menopause clinic appointment?

Private GP clinics that run menopause clinics usually let you book directly without a referral from your NHS GP. It is still helpful to bring any relevant history, such as recent test results or a list of your medication, to the appointment.

### How should I prepare for a menopause appointment?

Tracking your symptoms for a few weeks beforehand, noting what you experience and when, gives the clinician a much clearer picture. It also helps to write down your questions and any treatments you have already tried.

### Can I keep seeing my NHS GP as well?

Yes. A private GP does not replace your NHS GP, and the two can work alongside each other. A good clinic will ask for your consent to share notes with your NHS practice so your records stay complete and any prescriptions are handled safely.

### How do I check that a clinic is properly registered?

You can look up the doctor by name on the General Medical Council's online register to confirm they are registered and licensed to practise. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must also register with the Care Quality Commission, which publishes inspection ratings, and equivalent regulators apply in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland.

### Is a menopause clinic different from seeing a private GP?

A menopause clinic is usually a private GP service with a particular focus on perimenopause and menopause, so the appointment is built around assessing your symptoms and discussing treatment options such as HRT. A general private GP appointment can cover the same ground, but may be shorter or less specialised. It is worth asking how the clinic structures its menopause appointments before you book.

### What happens at a first menopause clinic appointment?

The clinician will usually take a full history of your symptoms, your periods and any relevant health background, then talk through options such as HRT and what might suit you. Bringing a symptom diary and a list of your current medication helps the appointment go smoothly. You can also ask about sharing notes with your NHS GP so your records stay joined up.

### Can a private GP prescribe medication?

Yes, a GMC-registered doctor can issue private prescriptions. You pay the clinic's prescription fee and the pharmacy's price for the medicine.
