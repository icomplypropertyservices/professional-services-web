## How to check you are in safe hands

In the UK, the word "accountant" on its own is not a protected title, so anyone can use it. Titles such as Chartered Accountant (ICAEW, ICAS or Chartered Accountants Ireland), Chartered Certified Accountant (ACCA) and Chartered Management Accountant (CIMA) are protected, and members are bound by their body's ethics, training and complaints rules. Only registered auditors can sign statutory audit reports.

Every practice offering accountancy services must also be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by a professional body or by HMRC. It is reasonable to ask which body supervises a firm, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance, and how complaints are handled.

## Questions worth asking the accountant

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Which professional body are you a member of, and can I check your entry on its directory?
- Which body supervises the firm for anti-money-laundering purposes?
- Will a partner look over my work, and who will I deal with day to day?
- Do you hold professional indemnity insurance?
- How do you handle complaints if I am unhappy with the service?
- Are you authorised to carry out a statutory audit if my company needs one?

## When to think twice

Most professionals are honest and competent, but it is worth knowing the warning signs:

- Reluctance to say which professional body or AML supervisor they use
- No engagement letter or written scope before work begins
- Pressure to sign up to schemes you do not understand
- No clear answer about who handles your file day to day

## Choosing between the chartered accountants close to you

Once you have a shortlist of chartered accountants near you, the useful comparison is not who sounds most impressive but who fits how you actually work. A practice a few streets away may suit you best if you like dropping paperwork in by hand, while one further out may be the better match if your work is unusual and you are happy to deal with most things by phone and email. Distance matters less than whether the person handling your affairs understands your trade or profession.

Ask each practice the same set of questions so the answers sit side by side: who will be your main contact, whether a partner reviews the work, what they would need from you and when, and how they set their fees. Fees are usually quoted as price on application because the work varies so much, so ask what is included and what would be charged separately. It also helps to ask how they prefer to keep in touch, since some practices are set up for remote working and others expect you to come in.

A first meeting is really a two-way conversation. Take along your last set of accounts or tax return if you have one, a note of your year end, and any correspondence from HMRC, so the accountant can see the shape of your affairs rather than guess at them. Use the time to judge whether they explain things in a way you follow, because you will be relying on that plain speaking for as long as you work together.

## Chartered Accountant Near Me: FAQs

### Is a chartered accountant different from an accountant?

Yes. Anyone can use the word accountant, but Chartered Accountant is a protected title held by members of ICAEW, ICAS or Chartered Accountants Ireland. Those members are bound by their body's ethics, training and complaints rules.

### How do I check that someone is really chartered?

Ask which institute they belong to and look them up on that institute's directory. It is a quick check and worth doing before you share any financial information.

### Can you tell me who is the best accountant near me?

We do not rank or rate professionals, and we would be wary of anyone who claims to be the best. Instead, ask about their experience in your sector, who will handle your work, and how they charge, then judge for yourself.

### What if I need an audit rather than ordinary accounts?

Only registered auditors can sign statutory audit reports, so mention this when you enquire. We can introduce you to a practice that is authorised for audit work where that applies.

### Should I pick a chartered accountant in my own town or is further afield fine?

It depends on how you like to work. If you want to hand over records in person or sit down face to face, someone local is easier, but if you are comfortable with phone and email, a practice further away may suit your needs better. What matters most is that they understand your sector and explain things clearly.

### What should I bring to a first appointment with a chartered accountant?

Bring your last set of accounts or tax return if you have them, a note of your business year end, and any letters from HMRC. If you are thinking of changing accountants, mention it early, because records will need to be handed over from your current one. The more they can see, the more useful the conversation will be.

### Why are fees shown as price on application rather than a fixed figure?

Accountancy work varies widely depending on the size of your business, the records you keep and whether an audit is involved, so a single figure would mislead. Ask each practice what their quote covers and what would be charged separately, then compare the answers directly.

### Do I need a chartered accountant?

Not always. For simple Self Assessment a qualified, supervised accountant may be enough. For audits, complex group structures or specialist tax work, a chartered or certified firm is often the safer choice. We can match you to either.
