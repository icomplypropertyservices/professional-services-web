## What to understand about sale only conveyancing

Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity, so it must be carried out by a solicitor regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority or a licensed conveyancer regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money. In Scotland, property transactions are handled by solicitors under Scottish law.

If you have a mortgage, your lender may require the sale to be handled by a conveyancer on its panel. The Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme is a common accreditation, so it's worth checking that any firm you're considering is on your lender's panel before you instruct them.

## Sale Only Conveyancer Near Me: FAQs

### What does a sale only conveyancer do?

A sale only conveyancer acts for you as the seller, preparing the contract pack, answering enquiries from the buyer's solicitor and redeeming your mortgage when the sale completes. They don't handle a purchase for you, which keeps the work focused on the sale.

### Do I need a conveyancer if I'm selling without buying?

Yes, in England and Wales conveyancing is a reserved legal activity, so the legal work of selling must be done by a regulated solicitor or licensed conveyancer. Even if you're not buying, you'll need one to transfer the legal title and deal with your mortgage.

### How do I check a conveyancer is properly regulated?

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and licensed conveyancers by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. You can check the relevant register to confirm their status before instructing them.

### What should I have ready before instructing a sale conveyancer?

It helps to have your ID, details of your mortgage, and any certificates for works done on the property, such as building regulations completion certificates or warranties. Completing the property forms promptly will also help avoid delays.

### When should I instruct a conveyancer?

As soon as your offer is accepted, or before you list a property for sale. Instructing early helps you avoid delays.
