## Questions to ask a funeral plan adviser

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the funeral director is right for you:

- Can we visit the person who has died?
- If you sell funeral plans, are you FCA authorised?

## Warning signs to watch for

Most professionals are honest and competent, but it is worth knowing the warning signs:

- Unclear third-party costs
- Funeral plans sold by firms not authorised by the FCA
- Poor communication
- Pressure to buy extras

## Checking funeral directors are qualified and regulated

Funeral directors are not licensed in England, but many belong to the National Association of Funeral Directors (NAFD) or the National Society of Allied and Independent Funeral Directors (SAIF), which have codes of practice. Scotland has a statutory code of practice for funeral directors. Since 2022, providers of pre-paid funeral plans must be authorised by the FCA.

The Competition and Markets Authority requires funeral directors to display standardised price lists, so you can compare costs clearly.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.
