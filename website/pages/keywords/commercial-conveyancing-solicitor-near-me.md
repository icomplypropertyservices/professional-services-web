## How to check you are in safe hands

Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity. It can be carried out by solicitors regulated by the SRA or by licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers (CLC). Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money. In Scotland, property transactions are handled by solicitors under Scottish law.

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme (CQS) is a common accreditation. Check that your chosen firm is on your lender's panel before you instruct them.

## FAQs: a commercial conveyancing solicitor

### Do I need a solicitor who is local to the property?

Not necessarily, but a local solicitor may have useful knowledge of the area and be able to attend the property if needed. Many transactions are now handled remotely, so convenience and expertise are often more important than proximity.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a licensed conveyancer?

Both are qualified to carry out conveyancing. Solicitors are regulated by the SRA, while licensed conveyancers are regulated by the CLC.

### Why do environmental searches matter in commercial conveyancing?

Commercial properties can have a history of industrial use, which may mean potential contamination. Environmental searches help identify risks that could affect the property's value or your liability, and your lender may require them.

### How can I check if a solicitor is on my lender's panel?

You can ask the solicitor directly, or check with your lender. Many lenders publish a list of approved conveyancers on their panel. It's important to confirm this before you instruct a solicitor to avoid delays.

### Should I choose a commercial conveyancing solicitor in my own town or near the property?

Either can work. What matters is that the solicitor understands commercial property of your kind and, if you have a lender, is on its panel. Proximity helps if site visits or face-to-face meetings are likely, but it is not essential for the legal work itself.

### Can the same solicitor act for me and my lender?

Lenders often instruct their own conveyancer, or require yours to act for them as well under panel arrangements. Ask early whether the firm can act for your lender too, because if not, a separate appointment may be needed and that can affect timing and cost.

### I am taking on a business lease rather than buying. Is that still commercial conveyancing?

Yes. Lease work is a core part of commercial conveyancing, covering the terms, repairing obligations, break clauses and any rent deposit. Tell us it is a lease when you enquire so we can introduce you to a professional suited to that work.
