Insurance brokers and intermediaries in the UK must be authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), or be appointed representatives of an authorised firm. ## FAQs: home insurance

### Do I need home insurance by law?

Home insurance is not a legal requirement, but if you have a mortgage, your lender will usually require buildings insurance. Contents insurance is optional but protects your belongings.

### What is the difference between buildings and contents insurance?

Buildings insurance covers the structure of your home, while contents insurance covers the items inside. Many policies combine both, but you can buy them separately.

### How does flood risk affect my home insurance?

Flood risk can increase your premium, and some policies may have exclusions or higher excesses for flood damage. It is important to tell your broker about any flood risk so they can find suitable cover.

### What does unoccupancy limit mean?

Most home insurance policies have a limit on how long your home can be left unoccupied, often around thirty days, before cover is reduced or you need to inform your insurer. Check your policy for the exact terms.

### Can I use a local broker if I live in a different area?

Yes, you can use a broker who is not strictly local, but a local broker may have useful knowledge of local risks such as flood patterns or typical property types. It is worth asking about their experience with your area.

### How do I check a broker is authorised?

You can check whether a broker is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority by looking on the FCA register. Brokers must be authorised or be appointed representatives of an authorised firm.

### Is a broker more expensive than buying direct?

Not necessarily. Brokers can access insurers and schemes not available directly, and help with claims. The right choice depends on how complex your needs are.
