## Checking private GPs are qualified and regulated

Every doctor practising in the UK must be registered and hold a licence to practise with the General Medical Council (GMC). GPs also appear on the GMC's GP register. You can search the GMC's online register by name. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

A private GP does not replace your NHS GP. Good private clinics will ask for consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice, so your records stay complete and medication is prescribed safely.

## Local Private Doctor Near Me: FAQs

### Will using a private doctor affect my NHS care?

No, a private GP does not replace your NHS GP. Good private clinics will ask for your consent to share notes with your NHS practice so your records stay complete. It is sensible to keep your NHS GP informed about any private treatment you receive.

### How do I check that a private doctor is properly qualified?

You can search the GMC's online register by name to confirm a doctor's registration and GP status. In England, private clinics providing regulated healthcare must also register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings.

### What should I expect to pay for a private GP appointment?

Fees vary between clinics and depend on what is included, such as the consultation length and any tests. iComply does not set fees; the private doctor will quote you directly. Ask what is included in the fee and whether there are extra costs for tests or prescriptions.

### Can a private GP prescribe medication?

Yes, a private GP can prescribe medication if it is clinically appropriate. They may need to contact your NHS GP to ensure your records are up to date and that any prescription is safe. Always tell the private GP about any medicines you are already taking.

### Can I see a private GP if I already have an NHS GP?

Yes. A private GP works alongside your NHS GP rather than replacing them, and good clinics will ask for your consent to share notes so your records stay complete. It is sensible to tell your NHS practice that you have also seen a private doctor.

### Is a private doctor near me the same as an NHS walk-in centre?

No. A private GP sees you on a self-pay or insured basis and typically offers shorter waits, whereas an NHS walk-in centre is part of NHS services. If you are unsure whether you need urgent NHS help, NHS 111 can advise you.

### What should I bring to a first private GP appointment?

Bring a list of any medicines you take, details of any allergies, and any relevant past results or letters. It also helps to note down your main concern and any questions, so you make the most of the time with the doctor.

### Will a private GP share my notes with my NHS GP?

With your consent, most do, and it is safer for you. Joined-up records help avoid duplicate tests and medication clashes.
