## Checking financial advisers are qualified and regulated

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Questions to ask a financial adviser

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority, and what is your firm's reference number?
- Is your advice independent or restricted, and what does that mean for the products you can recommend?
- How do you charge for your advice, and will you set out all costs in writing before I commit?
- What ongoing service do you provide after the initial advice, and what does it cost?
- Can you explain your qualifications and experience in the areas I need help with?
- What happens if I'm unhappy with your advice, and how do I make a complaint?

## Comparing several advisers before you choose

Searching for the best financial advisors near me, in the plural, suggests you want to weigh up more than one option rather than settle on the first name you come across. That is a sensible approach with financial advice, because two advisers can both be properly authorised yet differ in the areas they specialise in, the way they charge and how they prefer to work with clients.

When comparing, ask each one the same set of questions so the answers sit side by side: whether their advice is independent or restricted, how their fees are structured, and what ongoing service they offer after the initial recommendation. It also helps to ask what kind of client they usually work with, since an adviser who mostly deals with retirement income may not be the natural fit for someone wanting to start investing.

Bear in mind that 'best' is personal here. The adviser who suits you is the one whose specialism matches your situation, whose explanations you can follow, and whose charges you understand before you commit. Being near you is a practical bonus for meeting in person, but it should not outweigh those fundamentals.

Most professionals are honest and competent, but it is worth knowing the warning signs:

- Not on the FCA register, or a clone firm using a real firm's name
- Pressure to transfer pensions or invest quickly
- Unclear or hidden charges
- Guaranteed high returns

## FAQs about best financial advisors near me

### How do I check if a financial adviser is properly authorised?

You can check the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register to see if an adviser or firm is authorised or is an appointed representative. The FCA regulates financial advice in the UK, and using an authorised firm gives you access to the Financial Ombudsman Service and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme if things go wrong.

### What's the difference between independent and restricted financial advice?

Independent advisers consider the whole market when making recommendations, while restricted advisers are limited to certain products or providers. Advisers must tell you which type of advice they offer, so you can decide what suits you.

### Will a local adviser charge for an initial meeting?

Many advisers offer a free initial consultation, but some may charge. It's best to ask upfront about any fees for the first meeting and how they will charge for advice, so there are no surprises.

### Can I get financial advice if I don't have a large sum to invest?

Yes, financial advisers can help with a range of situations, from small savings to complex retirement planning. Some may have minimum investment requirements, so it's worth asking what their typical client looks like and whether they can help with your circumstances.

### Should I speak to more than one financial adviser before deciding?

It can be worthwhile, especially if your situation is complex or you are unsure what kind of advice you need. Speaking to a few lets you compare how they explain things, what they charge and whether their specialism fits your goals.

### How can I compare advisers fairly when their fees are quoted differently?

Ask each adviser to set out their charges in writing in the same way, so you can see what you would pay for the initial advice and for any ongoing service. That makes it easier to compare like with like rather than judging on a single figure.

### Does an adviser need to be local to help me?

Not necessarily. Many people prefer a nearby adviser for face-to-face meetings, but advice can also be given by phone or video. What matters most is that the firm is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority and that you are comfortable with how they work.

### What is the difference between independent and restricted advice?

Independent advisers consider products from across the market. Restricted advisers focus on certain products or providers. Both can give good advice; they must tell you which they offer.
