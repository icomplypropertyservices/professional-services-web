## Getting the most from a surveyor close to home

Searching for surveyors near me often means you want someone who can get to the property without a long journey, which matters when a report needs to be done quickly or when you may want a follow-up visit. Local knowledge can also help, because a surveyor who works in your area will be familiar with the kinds of buildings and ground conditions common there. It is still worth asking how far they travel and whether they have worked on properties like yours.

When you have more than one name to consider, compare them on the same points rather than on price alone. Ask each one which report they would recommend for your property, what it includes, whether they are independent of the sale, and what their availability looks like. A written quote that sets out the scope makes it much easier to weigh one against another, and remember that the cheapest quote is not always the one that tells you what you need to know.

At the first appointment, the surveyor will usually arrange access and inspect the property, taking notes and photographs as they go. They may raise anything urgent with you straight away, but the full findings normally come in the written report afterwards. It helps to have your questions ready beforehand, particularly about any cracks, damp or alterations you have noticed, so the report addresses what worries you most.

## How to check you are in safe hands

Many surveyors are members of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), and RICS-regulated firms must follow professional standards, hold insurance and offer a complaints process. Chartered surveyors use the letters MRICS or FRICS. Some specialist roles have their own bodies, such as the Faculty of Party Wall Surveyors or the Property Care Association for damp and timber specialists.

Check the RICS directory, ask which report type you are getting and confirm whether the surveyor is independent of the sale. For asbestos work, look for organisations accredited by UKAS for surveying.

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

## Choosing the right surveyor for your property

Surveyors work in different specialisms, so it helps to match the person to your job. A building surveyor reports on condition, while a valuation surveyor focuses on market value. If you have damp or timber problems, you may need a specialist. Chartered surveyors use the letters MRICS or FRICS after their name.

Before you instruct anyone, ask which report type you are getting and confirm whether the surveyor is independent of the sale. You can check the RICS directory to find members, but always ask about their experience with the specific issue you face.

## FAQs: a surveyor

### How do I find a surveyor near me?

You can search the RICS directory for members in your area, or ask us to introduce you to a suitable surveyor. We'll ask about your property and needs, then introduce you to a professional who can quote you directly.

### What's the difference between a valuation and a survey?

A valuation is an assessment of a property's market value, often for mortgage purposes. A survey is a more detailed inspection of the property's condition. Tell us what you need so we can introduce you to the right person.

### Do I need a survey if I'm buying a new build?

Even new builds can have snagging issues, so a survey can be worthwhile. A professional can check the workmanship and flag anything that needs fixing. Ask the surveyor what they'll cover for a new property.

### Can a surveyor help with a party wall dispute?

Yes, some surveyors specialise in party wall matters and can act for you in a dispute. The Faculty of Party Wall Surveyors is one body that represents specialists in this area. Tell us about the dispute so we can introduce you to someone with the right experience.

### Can I ask a surveyor to look at just one issue rather than the whole property?

Sometimes a surveyor can focus on a specific concern, such as damp or structural movement, rather than carrying out a full building survey. Ask them directly what they can offer for your situation and what the report would cover. Being clear about your concern helps them tell you whether a targeted inspection is appropriate.

### Should I use the same surveyor as the buyer or seller?

It is worth confirming whether a surveyor is independent of the sale, as this affects the advice you receive. If you are the buyer, your own surveyor acts for you, and if you are the seller responding to concerns, your surveyor acts for you too. Ask about independence before you instruct anyone.

### What if I need a surveyor urgently?

Tell us about your deadline when you enquire, whether it is an exchange of contracts or a mortgage offer, so we can introduce you to a surveyor who may be able to help in time. Availability varies between practices, so it helps to ask early. A written quote will also confirm what can be done and when.

### Is a mortgage valuation the same as a survey?

No. A mortgage valuation is carried out for the lender. A survey is carried out for you and looks at the property's condition.
