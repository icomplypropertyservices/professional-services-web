## Qualifications and registration to look for

Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and you can check this on the FCA register. Advisers typically hold a qualification such as CeMAP.

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders, and how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## FAQs about mortgage adviser near me

### How do I check that a mortgage adviser is properly authorised?

You can check the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register to see if an adviser or broker is authorised or an appointed representative of an authorised firm. It's a good idea to do this before you share any personal or financial information.

### What is the difference between a mortgage adviser and a mortgage broker?

The terms are often used interchangeably. A mortgage adviser recommends suitable mortgages based on your circumstances, while a broker may search the market and handle the application. Both should be authorised by the FCA.

### Will a mortgage adviser charge me a fee?

Advisers and brokers must tell you how they are paid—whether by lender commission, a fee from you, or both. You should ask for this in writing before you proceed so you understand any costs involved.

### Can a mortgage adviser help if I'm self-employed?

Yes, many advisers have experience with self-employed applicants and can help you prepare evidence of income and find lenders that accept your circumstances. It's worth mentioning your employment status when you enquire.

### Does a mortgage adviser near me have to meet me in person?

No. Many advisers work by phone or video as well as face to face, and some handle the whole process remotely. It is worth asking how they prefer to work before you decide whether travelling to see them is necessary.

### Is a local mortgage adviser better than one further away?

Not necessarily. Being close by can make meetings easier, but what matters most is that the adviser understands your circumstances and that you are comfortable with how they work. Compare how each one searches the market, how they are paid, and how they communicate.

### What should I have ready for a first meeting with a local adviser?

It helps to bring proof of identity and address, details of your income and employment, and information about any deposit, savings or existing mortgage. Having these to hand means the adviser can give you a clearer picture of what may be available to you.

### Is a broker better than going to my bank?

A broker can compare many lenders and may find options your bank does not offer. Your bank can only offer its own products. Either can suit you; a broker gives you a wider view.
