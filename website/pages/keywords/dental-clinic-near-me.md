## Checking private dentists are qualified and regulated

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Specialist titles such as orthodontist or endodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process. The Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

## What happens at a first appointment at a dental clinic near you

A first visit to a private dental clinic near you usually begins with a check-up rather than treatment. The dentist will look at your teeth, gums and mouth, ask about your medical history and any medicines you take, and discuss anything that has been bothering you. It helps to arrive a little early so you can complete any registration forms without feeling rushed.

From there, the dentist should explain what they have found and set out your options, including what each involves and what it costs. You can ask for the plan and the quote in writing so you have time to think it over, and you are free to go away and consider it rather than deciding on the spot. If you need more complex care, ask whether the clinic handles it in-house or would refer you on.

It is worth checking a few practical things before you book, since clinics differ in ways that matter day to day. Ask about opening hours, whether emergency slots are kept free for people who are not already registered, and how appointments work for children or for anyone who finds dental visits difficult. These details often shape whether a clinic near you is genuinely convenient, not just close.

## FAQs about dental clinic near me

### How do I check if a dental clinic near me is properly registered?

You can check that the dentists and other dental professionals are registered with the General Dental Council (GDC) by searching the GDC register online. In England, you can also check whether the clinic is registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC) and view its inspection rating. In Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, the respective regulators are Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, Healthcare Improvement Scotland and the RQIA.

### What should I do if I have a dental emergency and need a clinic near me?

If you have a serious dental emergency, such as uncontrolled bleeding or a facial swelling that affects your breathing or swallowing, call 999 or go to A&E. For urgent dental advice, you can call NHS 111. For private care, look for a local clinic that advertises emergency slots, and ask about availability when you contact them.
