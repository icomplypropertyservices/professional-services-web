## Understanding accreditation and regulation

"Architect" is a protected title in the UK, so anyone calling themselves an architect must be on the Architects Registration Board (ARB) register and follow its code of conduct. Many practices are also RIBA Chartered Practices. Conservation architects often hold additional accreditation from the AABC (Architects Accredited in Building Conservation), which shows they have specialist knowledge in historic buildings.

Before you appoint anyone, check the ARB register and ask for a written appointment that sets out services, fees and responsibilities. Building regulations approval, planning permission and party wall matters are separate processes that your architect can help you navigate.

## Conservation Architect Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need a conservation architect for a listed building?

Not always legally required, but a conservation architect has specialist knowledge of historic fabric and can help you avoid damaging the building or falling foul of consent rules. They can also guide you through planning and listed building consent processes.

### What does AABC accreditation mean?

AABC stands for Architects Accredited in Building Conservation. It is a recognised accreditation that shows an architect has demonstrated experience and competence in building conservation.

### Are grants available for historic building repairs?

Grants may be available from organisations such as Historic England or local authorities, but eligibility and availability vary. A conservation architect can advise on potential funding sources for your project.

### How do I check if someone is a registered architect?

You can check the Architects Registration Board (ARB) register online. Only those on the register can call themselves architects, and they must follow the ARB code of conduct and hold professional indemnity insurance.

### How do I compare conservation architects near me?

Start by checking their accreditation and ARB registration, then ask about their experience with buildings like yours. It also helps to discuss their approach to repairs and materials, and to compare written appointments that set out services and fees.

### What should I have ready for a first meeting with a conservation architect?

Gather any information you have about the building, such as its age, listing status and any previous surveys or consents. It is also useful to have a clear idea of what you want to achieve and any constraints, such as budget or timescales.

### Will an architect guarantee planning permission?

No one can guarantee planning permission. A good architect will explain local policy, the risks and how to give your application the best chance.
