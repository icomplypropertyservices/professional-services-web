## How to check you are in safe hands

In the UK, the word "accountant" on its own is not a protected title, so anyone can use it. Titles such as Chartered Accountant (ICAEW, ICAS or Chartered Accountants Ireland), Chartered Certified Accountant (ACCA) and Chartered Management Accountant (CIMA) are protected, and members are bound by their body's ethics, training and complaints rules. Only registered auditors can sign statutory audit reports.

Every practice offering accountancy services must also be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by a professional body or by HMRC. It is reasonable to ask which body supervises a firm, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance, and how complaints are handled.

## FAQs: an accountant for your company

### Do I legally need an accountant for my limited company?

Directors are responsible for keeping proper records and filing statutory accounts, a corporation tax return and a confirmation statement on time. Many directors use a company accountant to prepare and file these, but the legal duties remain with the directors.

### What is the difference between an accountant and a registered auditor?

An accountant prepares accounts and tax returns, while only a registered auditor can sign statutory audit reports. Most small companies are not required to be audited, but if you are told you need one, ask who will sign the report.

### How can I check an accountant's credentials?

Ask which professional body they belong to and which body supervises the practice for anti-money-laundering purposes. Protected titles such as Chartered Accountant and Chartered Certified Accountant mean the member is bound by that body's ethics, training and complaints rules.

### What should I have ready before I speak to a company accountant?

It helps to have your company registration details, recent bank statements, records of income and expenses, and any correspondence from your previous accountant. Tell iComply these details when you enquire so the introduction is useful from the start.

### Do I need a chartered accountant?

Not always. For simple Self Assessment a qualified, supervised accountant may be enough. For audits, complex group structures or specialist tax work, a chartered or certified firm is often the safer choice. We can match you to either.
