## How to check you are in safe hands

"Physiotherapist" and "physical therapist" are protected titles in the UK. Anyone using them must be registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC), and you can check the HCPC register online. Most physiotherapists are also members of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy (CSP), shown by the letters MCSP.

Sports therapists, massage therapists and personal trainers can be helpful for some needs, but they are not physiotherapists. For an injury, post-surgery rehabilitation or a neurological condition, check that you are booking a HCPC-registered physiotherapist.

## FAQs: carpal tunnel physiotherapy

### Can physiotherapy help carpal tunnel syndrome?

Physiotherapy can help manage carpal tunnel symptoms through splinting, nerve gliding exercises and activity changes. If symptoms persist, your physiotherapist may recommend a medical review.

### Do I need a referral to see a physiotherapist for carpal tunnel syndrome?

You can often see a physiotherapist without a referral, but some clinics may ask for one. It's best to check when you enquire.

### What happens during a physiotherapy appointment for carpal tunnel syndrome?

The physiotherapist will assess your hand and wrist, discuss your symptoms and daily activities, and may provide splinting, exercises and advice. They will also explain what to do if symptoms don't improve.

### How do I check if a physiotherapist is qualified?

You can check that your physiotherapist is registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) by searching the online register. Many are also members of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy (CSP), shown by MCSP after their name.

### Do I need a GP referral for private physio?

No, you can usually book directly as a self-paying patient. Your insurer may require a referral, so check your policy.
