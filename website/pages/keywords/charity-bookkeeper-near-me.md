## Checking bookkeepers are qualified and regulated

Bookkeeping is not a regulated title in the UK, but anyone providing bookkeeping services for a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by HMRC or by a professional body. Membership of the Institute of Certified Bookkeepers (ICB) or the Association of Accounting Technicians (AAT) shows a bookkeeper has trained to a recognised standard and agreed to a code of conduct.

Ask whether a bookkeeper holds professional indemnity insurance and which body supervises them. Good bookkeepers are also clear about where their work stops and an accountant's starts, for example on year-end accounts and tax advice.

## Why charity bookkeeping is different

Charity bookkeeping isn't just ordinary bookkeeping with a different label. Charities have to separate restricted funds, given for a specific purpose, from unrestricted funds, and keep a clear record of donations and Gift Aid so the picture is accurate for trustees and for independent examination. A bookkeeper who works with charities will understand this from the start.

Yes, a bookkeeper who works with charities will keep restricted and unrestricted funds separate so your records show clearly what each pot of money is for. It's worth asking about this directly, because it's central to charity bookkeeping.

A charity bookkeeper can keep your Gift Aid declarations organised and help make sure the records behind claims are in order. They'll be clear about what falls within bookkeeping and what needs an accountant.
