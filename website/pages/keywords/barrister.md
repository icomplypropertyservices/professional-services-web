## Understanding how barristers work

Barristers in England and Wales are regulated by the Bar Standards Board (BSB). You can check the Barristers' Register to see whether someone holds a current practising certificate and whether they are authorised to accept instructions directly from the public. In Scotland, the equivalent professionals are advocates, regulated through the Faculty of Advocates.

Barristers usually work from chambers, with clerks who manage diaries and fees. Many barristers can be instructed through a solicitor in the traditional way, and those registered under the Public Access scheme can be instructed directly by members of the public for suitable matters.

## Choosing the right barrister for your case

Searching simply for a barrister suggests you have not yet settled on a particular area of law, which is a sensible place to start. Barristers specialise, so the most useful next step is to pin down what kind of matter you have — a criminal hearing, a family dispute, an employment or immigration tribunal, a property issue, or a written opinion on a contract, for example — and then look for someone whose practice covers that field. A barrister who spends their working life in your type of case will understand the procedure and the likely arguments far better than a generalist.

When you compare barristers, ask about their experience in matters like yours rather than relying on a single impressive-sounding title. It is reasonable to ask how often they handle this kind of case, whether they will be the person appearing for you, and how they prefer to work with you — in person, in writing, or remotely. You can check the Barristers' Register to confirm a current practising certificate and whether the barrister is authorised to accept instructions directly from the public.

Bear in mind that a barrister's fees are usually quoted for a defined piece of work, such as a hearing, a written opinion or drafting a document, rather than as a single overall figure for the whole matter. Ask the clerk to set out what the quote covers and what might fall outside it, so there are no surprises later. If you are unsure whether your matter is suitable for direct instruction, it is worth raising that question at the outset.

## Costs and quotes for a barrister

Barristers usually agree a fee for a defined piece of work, such as a written advice, a conference or attendance at a hearing, often negotiated through their clerk. Fees reflect seniority, specialism and the time needed. The chambers will quote you, POA.

Ask clerks to confirm the fee in writing, what it covers and what happens if the hearing runs longer or is adjourned. Under public access, you may need to handle some administrative tasks a solicitor would normally do.

## Questions to ask the barrister

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the barrister is right for you:

- What is your experience in cases like mine?
- Are you authorised to accept instructions directly from the public under the Public Access scheme?
- How will your fees be structured for the work I need?
- What information or documents do you need from me to get started?
- Will you be the person representing me at the hearing, or might that be another barrister?
- How will you keep me informed about the progress of my case?

## Barrister: FAQs

### Can I instruct a barrister directly without going through a solicitor?

Yes, if the barrister is registered under the Public Access scheme and your matter is suitable for direct instruction. You can check the Barristers' Register to see whether a barrister is authorised to accept instructions directly from the public.

### How do I know if a barrister is properly regulated?

You can check the Barristers' Register to confirm that someone holds a current practising certificate.

### What is the difference between a barrister and a solicitor?

Solicitors often handle the preparatory work and may instruct a barrister on your behalf, but you can also instruct a barrister directly for suitable matters.

### How are barristers' fees usually agreed?

Fees are usually agreed for a defined piece of work, such as a hearing or a written opinion. The barrister's clerk will discuss the likely cost with you before any work begins.

### Do I need to know which area of law my problem falls under before I enquire?

No, but it helps. Describe the situation in plain terms — what has happened, what you want to achieve and any court or tribunal involved — and we can introduce you to a barrister whose specialism fits.

### Can a barrister take on the whole of my case from start to finish?

Barristers focus on advocacy, advice and drafting, and their involvement is usually defined around specific pieces of work. For matters needing ongoing case management, a solicitor may be involved alongside them, though direct instruction under Public Access is possible for suitable matters.

### What should I have ready for a first conversation with a barrister?

Gather any court or tribunal papers, correspondence, contracts or notices connected to the issue, along with a short summary of what has happened so far. Having key dates and documents to hand helps the barrister assess your matter quickly and quote accurately.

### Can I go straight to a barrister without a solicitor?

Often yes, through the Public Access scheme, if the barrister is registered and the matter is suitable. Some cases still need a solicitor, and the barrister will tell you if so.
