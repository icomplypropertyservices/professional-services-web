## Checking financial advisers are qualified and regulated

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## FAQs: a financial adviser

### How do I check that a financial adviser is properly authorised?

You can check the FCA register online or by contacting the Financial Conduct Authority. The adviser or firm should be listed as authorised, or as an appointed representative of an authorised firm. This gives you access to the Financial Ombudsman Service and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme if things go wrong.

### What is the difference between independent and restricted advice?

Independent advisers consider the whole market when making recommendations, while restricted advisers are limited to certain products or providers. Advisers must tell you which type of advice they offer before you engage them. Neither is necessarily better, but it helps to know so you can judge whether their offering suits your needs.

### Will I have to pay for the first meeting?

Many financial advisers offer an initial consultation at no cost, but this varies. You should ask upfront whether there is a charge for the first meeting and how any fees will be calculated. Any costs should be confirmed in writing before you proceed.

### Can I get financial advice if I don't have a lot to invest?

Some advisers specialise in helping people with modest savings or who are just starting to plan. It is worth being upfront about your situation when you enquire, so you can be introduced to an adviser who works with clients like you. Advice can still be valuable even if your pot is small, as it can set you on the right path.

### Do I need to bring anything to a first meeting with a financial adviser?

It helps to bring recent statements for your savings, investments and pensions, plus details of any protection policies or debts you hold. Having these to hand means the adviser can understand your situation properly and you get more out of the conversation. If you do not have everything together, you can still meet and send documents later.

### Can a local adviser come to my home?

Some advisers offer home visits, particularly for people who find it hard to travel, but this varies between firms. Ask when you enquire whether home appointments are possible, and say if you would prefer them. Otherwise, a nearby office is often the simplest way to meet face to face.

### Is a local adviser better than one you speak to only by phone?

Neither is automatically better; it depends on how you prefer to work and what you need help with. Meeting locally can make it easier to build a relationship and go through paperwork together, while remote advice may suit you if you travel or have limited time. Ask how reviews would work before you decide.

### Is the first meeting free?

Many firms offer a free initial consultation to explain their service and charges. Formal advice is then charged as agreed in writing.
