## What to understand about freehold conveyancing and choosing a local firm

Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity, so it must be carried out by a solicitor regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority or by a licensed conveyancer regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money. In Scotland, property transactions are handled by solicitors under Scottish law.

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and the Law Society’s Conveyancing Quality Scheme is a common accreditation. Before you instruct a firm, check that it is on your lender’s panel if you are taking out a mortgage. ## FAQs: a freehold conveyancer

### Do I need a conveyancer who specialises in freehold properties?

All conveyancers deal with freehold transactions, but some have more experience with issues like restrictive covenants, boundary disputes and estate rentcharges. It is worth asking about their experience with these points if your property has them.

### Can I use a conveyancer who is not near me?

Yes, you can instruct a conveyancer anywhere in England or Wales, but many people prefer a local firm they can visit or speak to in person. If you search for a freehold conveyancer near me, you will likely find firms that understand the local area and can meet face to face if needed.

### What is an estate rentcharge and why does it matter?

An estate rentcharge is a periodic payment charged on a freehold property, often to cover maintenance of shared areas or facilities. It can affect your ownership and future obligations, so your conveyancer should check the title and explain any rentcharge that applies.

### How do I check if a conveyancer is regulated?

You can check whether a solicitor is regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority or a licensed conveyancer by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. Both regulators keep public registers of the professionals they regulate.

### Does my freehold conveyancer have to be in the same town as the property?

No. A conveyancer can act on a freehold purchase or sale anywhere in England or Wales, so being near you is about convenience rather than the law. Many people still prefer a local firm they can visit, but a firm further away can do the same legal work if they are properly regulated.

### Why might a freehold property need more checking than I expected?

Owning the building and land outright does not mean the title is simple. Restrictive covenants can limit what you do with the property, boundaries on the title plan may be unclear, and an estate rentcharge can create ongoing payment obligations, so your conveyancer should check and explain each of these.

### What should I have ready before I speak to a conveyancer near me?

It helps to have the property address, whether you are buying or selling, any title documents or enquiries you have already received, and details of your mortgage lender if you are borrowing. With those to hand, a conveyancer can tell you more clearly what the work involves and what their quote covers.

### When should I instruct a conveyancer?

As soon as your offer is accepted, or before you list a property for sale. Instructing early helps you avoid delays.
