## Qualifications and registration to look for

In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. ## FAQs about defamation solicitor near me

### Do I need a solicitor near me for a defamation matter?

Not necessarily, as defamation advice can often be given remotely. However, a local solicitor may be more convenient for meetings and court appearances if your case goes to court. iComply can introduce you to a solicitor near you who handles defamation.

### What is the time limit for making a defamation claim?

In England and Wales, defamation claims must usually be brought within one year of publication. The court does have discretion to extend this period in some circumstances, but it is best to act quickly. A solicitor can advise you on the specific time limit for your situation.

### What does 'serious harm' mean in defamation?

Serious harm is a legal threshold that requires the statement to have caused or be likely to cause serious harm to your reputation. For a body trading for profit, it means serious financial loss. A solicitor can help you assess whether the harm you have suffered meets this threshold.

### Can I sue for defamation over an online comment?

Yes, an online comment can be defamatory if it contains false statements that damage your reputation and meet the serious harm threshold. However, there are defences available, such as truth or honest opinion, so it is important to get advice. A solicitor can assess the comment and advise on the best course of action.

### Is a solicitor the same as a lawyer?

"Lawyer" is a general word that covers solicitors, barristers, chartered legal executives and others. A solicitor is a specific regulated professional who can advise you, run your case and instruct a barrister if needed.
