## How to check you are in safe hands

Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and you can check this on the FCA register. Advisers typically hold a qualification such as CeMAP.

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders, and how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

A broker works on your behalf to find suitable products from providers. Ask how many providers they compare and how they are paid.

## Questions to ask the commercial mortgage broker

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the mortgage broker is right for you:

- Are you authorised or an appointed representative of a firm authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority, and what is your FCA registration number?
- Do you search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders?
- How are you paid for arranging a commercial mortgage, and will you disclose any commission or fees in writing?
- What information will you need from me, such as business accounts or a business plan, to support my application?
- How long does a typical commercial mortgage application take, and what might cause delays?
- Can you explain the valuation process and who arranges it?

## What to understand about commercial mortgages and brokers

Commercial mortgages are not regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority in the same way as residential mortgages, but the broker you use should still be authorised or an appointed representative, and you can check this on the FCA register.

Lenders will typically want to see business accounts and may require a valuation of the property, which can take longer than a residential valuation. It's worth asking about the broker's relationships with lenders, as this can affect the options available to you.

## FAQs about commercial mortgage broker near me

### Do I need a commercial mortgage broker near me, or can I use one further away?

You can use a broker anywhere in the UK, but a local broker may have knowledge of the area and local lenders. It's a personal choice, and what matters most is that the broker understands your needs and is authorised by the FCA.

### What is the difference between a commercial mortgage and a business loan?

A commercial mortgage is secured against a property, typically for buying or remortgaging premises, while a business loan may be unsecured or secured on other assets. Commercial mortgages are usually longer-term and suited to property purchase.

### Will I need a deposit for a commercial mortgage?

Lenders typically require a deposit, but the amount varies depending on the property and your circumstances. The broker can explain what's typical for your situation.

### How can I check that a commercial mortgage broker is authorised?

You can check the Financial Conduct Authority register to see if the broker or their firm is authorised or an appointed representative. Always ask for their FCA registration number and verify it yourself.

### Is a broker better than going to my bank?

A broker can compare many lenders and may find options your bank does not offer. Your bank can only offer its own products. Either can suit you; a broker gives you a wider view.
