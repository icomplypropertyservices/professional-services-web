## How to check you are in safe hands

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## What to expect at your first meeting with a local financial adviser

When you meet a financial adviser near you for the first time, they will usually start by asking about your goals, your current financial situation and your attitude to risk. This helps them understand what you want to achieve and whether they can help. They should also explain how they work, including whether their advice is independent or restricted, and how they charge.

You should expect to discuss your income, savings, investments, pensions and any debts, as well as your plans for retirement or other major life events. ## FAQs: a financial adviser

### How do I check if a financial adviser is authorised?

You can check the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register online or by contacting the FCA. The register shows whether an adviser or firm is authorised or an appointed representative. Always do this before agreeing to any advice.

### What is the difference between independent and restricted advice?

Independent advisers consider the whole market when making recommendations. Restricted advisers focus on certain products or providers. Advisers must tell you which type they offer, so you can decide what suits you.

### Will I have to pay for the first meeting?

Many advisers offer an initial consultation without charge, but you should always ask beforehand. Any fees for advice should be explained clearly and agreed in writing before you proceed.

### What should I do if I'm unhappy with the advice I receive?

If you have a complaint, first raise it with the firm. If it isn't resolved to your satisfaction, you can take it to the Financial Ombudsman Service. The Financial Services Compensation Scheme may also protect you if an authorised firm fails.

### Do I need to prepare anything before my first meeting with a financial adviser?

It helps to gather recent statements for any savings, investments and pensions, as well as details of your income and outgoings. Also think about your goals and any concerns you want to discuss. Having this information ready can make the meeting more productive.

### Will the adviser charge for the first meeting?

Some advisers offer a free initial consultation, while others charge a fee. Always ask beforehand so you know what to expect. Any charges should be explained clearly and agreed in writing before you proceed.

### Can I bring someone with me to the meeting?

Yes, you are welcome to bring a partner, family member or friend for support. It can be helpful to have someone with you to listen and take notes. Just let the adviser know in advance so they can accommodate you.

### Is the first meeting free?

Many firms offer a free initial consultation to explain their service and charges. Formal advice is then charged as agreed in writing.
