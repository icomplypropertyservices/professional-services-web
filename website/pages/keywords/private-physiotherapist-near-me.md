## What to check before you book

In the UK, 'physiotherapist' and 'physical therapist' are protected titles. Anyone using them must be registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC). You can check the HCPC register online to confirm that the person you see is properly registered. Many physiotherapists are also members of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy (CSP), which is shown by the letters MCSP after their name.

Private physiotherapists often let you self-refer, so you can usually book directly without seeing your GP first. If you plan to use health insurance, check with your insurer before you book, as they may cap the number of sessions they cover. It is also worth asking about clinic hours and location to make sure appointments are practical for you.

## FAQs about private physiotherapist near me

### Do I need a referral from my GP to see a private physiotherapist?

In most cases, you can self-refer to a private physiotherapist without seeing your GP first. It is still a good idea to check with the clinic when you book, especially if you have complex health needs.

### How can I check that a physiotherapist is properly qualified?

You can check the HCPC register online to confirm that the physiotherapist is registered. Many also hold MCSP membership, which indicates they are a member of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy.

### Will my health insurance cover private physiotherapy?

Many health insurers cover private physiotherapy, but they may cap the number of sessions or require a referral. Check your policy and speak to your insurer before you book so you understand what is covered.

### What should I expect at my first appointment?

Your first appointment will usually involve an assessment of your condition and a discussion of your goals. The physiotherapist may also start treatment or give you exercises to do at home.

### Do I need a GP referral for private physio?

No, you can usually book directly as a self-paying patient. Your insurer may require a referral, so check your policy.
