## What people searching for a private GP usually need

Most people looking for a private GP want medical care that feels unhurried and fits their schedule.

- You need an appointment soon and cannot get one with your NHS GP as quickly as you would like.
- You want more time to talk through a problem than a standard appointment allows.
- You would prefer a video consultation for something that does not need a physical examination.
- You need a referral, a test, or a certificate and want to arrange it without a long wait.
- You want to see a GP at a time that suits your work or family commitments, such as an evening or weekend.

## What happens at a first private GP appointment

At a first appointment, expect the GP to take a full history: what has brought you in, how long it has been going on, what you have already tried, and any medication you take. Longer appointment slots mean there is usually room to cover more than one concern in a single visit, so it helps to write down your questions beforehand and decide what matters most to you.

The GP may examine you, arrange tests such as blood tests, or refer you on to a specialist if that is what your situation needs. Ask how and when you will receive results, and who will follow up on them, so nothing is left hanging between appointments.

Before you leave, ask for a summary of what was discussed and any treatment plan, and check whether the clinic will pass this on to your NHS GP with your consent. Keeping both sets of records joined up protects you if you later need NHS care, repeat prescriptions or a referral.

## What to understand about private GP care

A private GP does not replace your NHS GP. Good private clinics will ask for your consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice, so your records stay complete and any medication is prescribed safely.

Every doctor practising in the UK must be registered and hold a licence to practise with the General Medical Council (GMC). GPs also appear on the GMC's GP register, and you can search the GMC's online register by name. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

## FAQs about private GP

### Will a private GP replace my NHS GP?

No, a private GP does not replace your NHS GP. Good private clinics will ask for your consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice, so your records stay complete and medication is prescribed safely.

### How do I check that a private GP is properly qualified?

Every doctor practising in the UK must be registered and hold a licence to practise with the General Medical Council (GMC). GPs also appear on the GMC's GP register, and you can search the GMC's online register by name. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

### Can I have a video consultation with a private GP?

Many private GPs offer video consultations, which suit a range of problems where a physical examination is not needed. It is worth asking when you enquire whether a video appointment is appropriate for your situation.

### How long does a private GP appointment usually last?

Appointments of fifteen to thirty minutes are common with a private GP, giving you more time to discuss your concerns than a standard NHS appointment. You can ask about the length of appointments when you speak to the professional.

### Can a private GP prescribe medication for me?

A private GP can prescribe medication where it is clinically appropriate, and may issue a private prescription, which you pay for at a pharmacy. If you take regular medication through your NHS GP, ask the private GP to share details with your NHS practice so your records stay consistent.

### What should I bring to my first private GP appointment?

Bring a list of any medication you take, including doses, plus details of any long-term conditions and recent test results if you have them. It also helps to note down your main concerns and questions in advance, so you can make the most of the longer appointment.

### Can a private GP prescribe medication?

Yes, a GMC-registered doctor can issue private prescriptions. You pay the clinic's prescription fee and the pharmacy's price for the medicine.
