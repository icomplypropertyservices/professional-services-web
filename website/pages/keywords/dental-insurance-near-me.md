## Dental insurance or a practice plan?

If you're searching for dental insurance near you, it's worth knowing that many dental practices offer their own plans, which usually spread the cost of routine care at that practice. Insurance works differently: it's arranged through an insurer and can help towards treatment, sometimes including emergency cover abroad. Knowing which one you're actually after helps a broker point you in the right direction.

A practice plan generally ties you to that practice, while insurance may give you more flexibility about where you're treated, depending on the policy. The two can also sit alongside each other, so it's worth asking how they would interact in your case. A broker can explain the key features and exclusions of a policy and how it compares with what your practice offers.

Before you commit, think about what you actually want covered: routine check-ups, a particular course of treatment, or peace of mind while travelling. Waiting periods and yearly limits can affect how useful a policy is for your plans, so raise these early. A broker must understand your demands and needs before recommending anything.

## Understanding waiting periods and limits

Dental insurance policies often have a waiting period before you can claim, and there may be a limit on how much is covered each year. These details vary between policies, so it's worth asking about them clearly before you commit.

It can also help to compare a policy with any plan your dental practice offers, as the two work in different ways. A broker can explain the key features and exclusions, and tell you how they are paid for their service.

## Costs and quotes for dental insurance

Brokers are often paid commission by the insurer and sometimes charge a broker fee; they must disclose how they are paid if you ask. Premiums are set by insurers based on risk. The broker quotes you, POA.

Compare cover, excesses and exclusions, not just the premium. The cheapest policy is not always the best value when you need to claim.

## How to check you are in safe hands

Insurance brokers and intermediaries in the UK must be authorised by the FCA, or be appointed representatives of an authorised firm. Check them on the FCA register. Many brokers belong to the British Insurance Brokers' Association (BIBA), and some hold Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute.

Brokers must understand your demands and needs, explain key policy features and exclusions, and tell you how they are paid. Complaints that are not resolved can go to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

## FAQs about dental insurance near me

### Will dental insurance cover the full cost of my treatment?

Not usually. Policies often have a yearly limit and may not cover every type of treatment, so it's worth checking the details with the broker. They can explain what is included and what isn't before you decide.

### Can I get dental insurance if I already need treatment?

Some policies have waiting periods, and existing problems may be excluded. A broker can tell you what's possible and whether it's worth arranging cover now or waiting.

### How do I check that a broker is properly authorised?

You can check them on the FCA register, and many brokers belong to the British Insurance Brokers' Association (BIBA).

### What can I do if I'm unhappy with the policy or the service?

Brokers must explain key policy features and exclusions, and tell you how they are paid. If you have a complaint that isn't resolved, you can take it to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

### Is dental insurance the same as a plan from my dental practice?

No. A practice plan usually spreads the cost of routine care at that practice, while dental insurance is a policy arranged through an insurer that can help towards treatment costs. They work in different ways, and a broker can explain how a policy compares with your practice's plan.

### Can I use dental insurance at any practice?

That depends on the policy, so it's worth checking before you decide. Some policies may give you more choice about where you're treated, while others may not. Ask the broker to explain how this works for the cover you're considering.

### Do I need to tell the broker about my teeth and gums?

Brokers must understand your demands and needs, so they may ask about your dental history and any treatment you're expecting. Existing problems may be excluded, and waiting periods can apply. Being open helps them explain what's suitable for you.

### What is non-disclosure?

Failing to tell an insurer something relevant. It can reduce or void a claim, so answer questions fully and ask your broker if unsure.
