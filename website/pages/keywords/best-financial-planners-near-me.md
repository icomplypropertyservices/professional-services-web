## Comparing several planners without a league table

Because you are likely weighing up a few names at once, it helps to compare them on the same points rather than on impressions alone. Ask each one the same questions about authorisation, whether their advice is independent or restricted, and how they charge, then set the answers side by side. That way you are judging like for like, and the differences that matter to you become clearer.

The word best also depends on fit. A planner used to working with people building up savings may not suit someone sorting out retirement income, so ask what kind of clients they usually help and whether your situation is familiar to them. ## Checking financial advisers are qualified and regulated

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications.

iComply is not a regulator and does not provide the professional service itself. We introduce you to professionals who hold their own registrations, and we encourage you to check them on the public register.

## Costs and quotes for a financial planner

Advisers often charge for initial advice, then an ongoing fee for reviews, which may be fixed or a percentage of the money they look after. Firms must set out charges clearly before advising you. The firm quotes you, POA.

## Best Financial Planners Near Me: FAQs

### How do I check a financial planner is properly authorised?

Look up the adviser or firm on the Financial Conduct Authority register, which shows whether they are authorised or an appointed representative of an authorised firm. This is the most reliable way to confirm they are permitted to give financial advice in the UK.

### What is the difference between independent and restricted advice?

Independent advisers consider the whole market, while restricted advisers are limited to certain products or providers. Advisers must tell you which they offer and how they charge, so you can decide what suits you.

### Will a financial planner look at my whole situation?

Good financial planning takes a whole-life view, linking your goals with investments, pensions, tax and protection rather than focusing on a single product. Cash-flow models are often used to show how decisions today might affect your future.

### What can I do if I am unhappy with the advice?

You can complain to the firm first, and if that does not resolve matters you can take your complaint to the Financial Ombudsman Service. The Financial Services Compensation Scheme may also protect you if an authorised firm fails.

### Is a financial planner the same as a financial adviser?

The terms are often used interchangeably in the UK, and both describe someone who can give regulated financial advice. Financial planning tends to emphasise a whole-life view, linking your goals with investments, pensions, tax and protection, rather than focusing on a single product.

### Should I choose someone local even if I mostly deal with them remotely?

Meeting locally can make the first conversation easier, but what matters most is that the adviser is authorised and that you are comfortable with how they work and charge. Many people are happy to work remotely once they have met in person or spoken at length.

### How many planners should I speak to before deciding?

There is no set number, but speaking to more than one lets you compare how they explain things and what they charge. Give yourself enough time to weigh up the answers rather than deciding on the first conversation alone.

### Is the first meeting free?

Many firms offer a free initial consultation to explain their service and charges. Formal advice is then charged as agreed in writing.
