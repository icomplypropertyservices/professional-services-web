## What to understand before choosing a tax adviser

"Tax adviser" is not a protected title in the UK, so anyone can call themselves one. The main specialist bodies are the Chartered Institute of Taxation (for the CTA qualification) and the Association of Taxation Technicians (for the ATT qualification). Many accountants and solicitors also advise on tax, and members of these bodies follow a shared code called Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation.

Tax advisers who charge a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, and HMRC has been tightening standards for paid tax agents. It's sensible to ask about qualifications, supervision and professional indemnity insurance before you engage anyone. Getting advice in writing is also important, especially before a transaction, so you have a clear record of what was recommended.

## Tax Advisors Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need a tax adviser who is local to me?

Not necessarily. Many tax advisers work remotely and can handle your affairs by phone, email and secure online portals. However, if you prefer face-to-face meetings or have a complex situation, a local adviser may be more convenient.

### What's the difference between a tax adviser and an accountant?

All tax advisers are not necessarily accountants, and not all accountants specialise in tax. A tax adviser focuses specifically on tax matters, while an accountant may cover a broader range of financial work. Some professionals hold both roles.

### How do I know if a tax adviser is qualified?

Check whether they hold a recognised qualification such as the Chartered Tax Adviser (CTA) or Association of Taxation Technicians (ATT) designation. You can also ask about their membership of professional bodies and their experience with situations like yours.

### Should I choose a tax adviser who is close to me?

It depends on your preferences. If you value face-to-face meetings and easy access, a local adviser can be ideal. But if you're comfortable working by phone, email or video call, you can consider advisers from a wider area, which may give you more choice.

### How can I compare tax advisers near me if I don't know any?

Start by asking people you trust for recommendations, and look at the qualifications and experience of each adviser you consider. You can also ask each one how they would approach your situation and how they charge, so you can compare like for like.

### Should I reply to HMRC before speaking to an adviser?

Note the deadline on the letter, but avoid sending a detailed reply before you have advice if the matter is complex. An adviser can often ask HMRC for more time where it is reasonable.
