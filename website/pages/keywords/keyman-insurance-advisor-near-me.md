## Checking insurance brokers are qualified and regulated

Insurance brokers and intermediaries in the UK must be authorised by the FCA, or be appointed representatives of an authorised firm. Check them on the FCA register. Many brokers belong to the British Insurance Brokers' Association (BIBA), and some hold Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute.

Brokers must understand your demands and needs, explain key policy features and exclusions, and tell you how they are paid. Complaints that are not resolved can go to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Keyman Insurance Advisor Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need key person cover if the business is small?

That depends on how much the business relies on one or two people. If profits or essential knowledge would suffer badly without them, it is worth discussing with a broker. They can help you weigh up whether cover makes sense for your situation.

### Can key person cover be combined with shareholder protection?

Yes, many businesses look at the two together because they address related risks. A broker can explain how the arrangements might work alongside each other. The right structure depends on your ownership and agreements.

### How is a key person insurance payout taxed?

Tax treatment varies depending on how the policy is set up and who receives the payout. A broker can explain the general position, but you may also want to take tax advice. Do not assume a payout will be tax-free.

### How do I check that an advisor is properly authorised?

Insurance brokers and intermediaries must be authorised by the FCA or be appointed representatives of an authorised firm. You can check them on the FCA register before you proceed. Many also belong to BIBA, and some hold Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute.

### Is a broker more expensive than buying direct?

Not necessarily. Brokers can access insurers and schemes not available directly, and help with claims. The right choice depends on how complex your needs are.
