## What to understand about plantar fasciitis physiotherapy

Plantar fasciitis physiotherapy focuses on reducing heel pain through stretching, strengthening, footwear advice and load management. Morning heel pain is typical, and calf strength matters because weak calf muscles can put extra strain on the plantar fascia. A physiotherapist can assess your movement and build a plan around your specific needs.

In the UK, 'physiotherapist' and 'physical therapist' are protected titles, so anyone using them must be registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC). You can check the HCPC register online to confirm a practitioner's registration. Most physiotherapists are also members of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy (CSP), shown by the letters MCSP.

## Plantar Fasciitis Physiotherapy Near Me: FAQs

### What is plantar fasciitis and how is it treated with physiotherapy?

Plantar fasciitis is inflammation of the plantar fascia, the tissue that runs along the bottom of your foot, causing heel pain. Physiotherapy for it typically involves stretching, strengthening exercises, footwear advice and load management to reduce pain and improve function.

### Do I need a referral from my GP to see a physiotherapist for plantar fasciitis?

In the UK, you can often see a physiotherapist without a GP referral, but some services may require one. When you enquire through iComply, the professional can advise you on whether a referral is needed for their service.

### How can I check that a physiotherapist is properly registered?

You can check the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) register online to confirm that a physiotherapist is registered. The titles 'physiotherapist' and 'physical therapist' are protected, so anyone using them must be HCPC-registered.

### Will I need to do exercises at home as part of my treatment?

Yes, physiotherapy for plantar fasciitis usually involves a home exercise programme, including stretches and strengthening exercises. Consistency with these exercises is important for making progress.
