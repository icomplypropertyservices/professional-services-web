## What to know about root canal treatment

Root canal treatment removes infected or inflamed pulp from inside the tooth, cleans the canals and seals them to save the tooth. Modern anaesthetic keeps the procedure comfortable, and a crown is often needed afterwards to protect the tooth. Endodontists specialise in complex cases, so if your tooth has unusual anatomy or has had previous treatment, a dentist may suggest a referral.

Dentists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the register online. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA. If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process, and the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

## Root Canal Near Me: FAQs

### Is root canal treatment painful?

Modern anaesthetic keeps root canal treatment comfortable, and most people find it similar to having a filling. You may have some tenderness afterwards, which usually settles.

### Will I always need a crown after a root canal?

A crown is often needed afterwards to protect the tooth, especially if it is a back tooth that takes a lot of chewing. Your dentist will advise whether a crown is necessary in your case.

### How do I check a dentist's registration?

You can check the GDC register online to confirm a dentist is registered. If the practice provides regulated healthcare in England, you can also check whether it is registered with the CQC.

### What if I am unhappy with private dental treatment?

Start with the practice's complaints process. If the complaint is not resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### Do I need a check-up before treatment?

Almost always. A dentist needs to examine you, and often take X-rays, before recommending treatment.
