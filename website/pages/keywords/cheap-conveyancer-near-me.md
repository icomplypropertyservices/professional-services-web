## Spotting a conveyancing quote that only looks cheap

When a conveyancer advertises a very low fee, the figure is often for the basic freehold purchase only. Ask for the quote in writing and check whether searches, the land registry fee, VAT and bank transfer charges are included, because these are usually added on top and can change the final bill considerably.

Leasehold work is a common source of extra cost, as the conveyancer has to read the lease, obtain management information and deal with the freeholder or managing agent. If your property is leasehold, or if you are buying with a mortgage, ask specifically what those parts of the work will cost before you instruct anyone.

A local firm can be easier to reach if something needs sorting quickly, but many conveyancers work happily by phone, email and post. Weigh up the fee against how clearly the conveyancer explains the costs and how easily you can get hold of them, rather than choosing on price alone.

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity. It can be carried out by solicitors regulated by the SRA or by licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers (CLC). Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money. In Scotland, property transactions are handled by solicitors under Scottish law.

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme (CQS) is a common accreditation. Check that your chosen firm is on your lender's panel before you instruct them.

## FAQs about cheap conveyancer near me

### Is the cheapest conveyancer always the best option?

Not necessarily. A low fee might not include searches, VAT or other costs, so ask for a full breakdown. Also consider whether the firm is on your lender's panel and how they communicate, as a slightly higher fee can save you stress later.

### How can I check a conveyancer is properly regulated?

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and licensed conveyancers by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. You can check the firm's status with the relevant regulator. Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow rules on handling client money.

### Do I need a conveyancer near me, or can I use one remotely?

Many conveyancing firms work remotely by phone, email and post, so distance isn't always a barrier. However, some people prefer a local firm they can visit if they need to sign documents or discuss things face to face. It's worth asking how they handle ID checks and original documents.

### What should I do if my lender requires a conveyancer on their panel?

Tell the conveyancer your lender's name and ask if they are on that lender's panel. If not, you may need to choose a firm that is, or your lender might allow you to use your own conveyancer under certain conditions. Check with your lender before instructing.

### Why do conveyancing quotes vary so much for the same property?

Firms structure their fees differently and include different items, so one quote may exclude searches or VAT that another includes. Always ask for a full written quote with every disbursement listed, then compare like with like rather than the headline figure.

### Can I use a conveyancer who is not local to save money?

Yes, distance is not usually a barrier, as most of the work is done by phone, email and post. What matters more is that the firm is on your lender's panel if you have a mortgage, and that you are comfortable with how they will keep you updated and handle original documents and ID checks.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a licensed conveyancer?

Both are regulated and insured. Licensed conveyancers specialise in property transactions, while solicitors can also advise on wider legal issues. Either can handle a standard purchase or sale.
