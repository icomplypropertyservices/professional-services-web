## FAQs: a bridging loan

### What is a bridging loan and when is it used?

A bridging loan is short-term secured finance used to cover a gap, such as a chain break, auction purchase, or refurbishment. It is designed to be repaid quickly, often through sale or refinancing.

### Is bridging finance regulated?

Bridging finance secured on your own home is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority. If the loan is for an investment property, it may not be regulated, but the broker should still be authorised.

### How can I judge if a broker is right for me?

Check that the broker is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority and ask whether they search the whole market or a limited panel. Also ask about their experience with bridging loans and how they are paid.

### What is an exit strategy and why does it matter?

An exit strategy is your plan to repay the bridging loan, such as selling the property or refinancing. Lenders need to see a credible exit before they will agree to lend.

### Why does 'near me' matter for a bridging loan?

Bridging often involves tight deadlines and sometimes a property you need to view or value quickly, so being able to speak to your broker easily and promptly can help. Local knowledge of the area can also be useful when discussing the property and its resale prospects, though much of the work can be done by phone and email.

### Can a broker promise me the top bridging deal?

No, and you should be wary of anyone who does. Rates depend on your circumstances, the property and the lender's criteria, and a broker must tell you whether they search the whole market or a limited panel. Compare the overall cost and the speed of completion, not just the headline rate.

### Is bridging finance different from a standard mortgage?

Yes. Bridging is short-term secured finance used for chain breaks, auctions, refurbishment or quick purchases, and rates are generally higher than standard mortgages. Lenders will want a clear exit strategy showing how you plan to repay it.

### Will speaking to a broker affect my credit score?

An initial conversation does not. Formal applications usually involve a credit search, and the broker should tell you before that happens.
