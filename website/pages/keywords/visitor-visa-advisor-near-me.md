## Getting ready for a first meeting with a local visitor visa adviser

When you search for a visitor visa advisor near me, the “near me” part usually means you want to sit down with someone rather than deal with everything by email. That is a sensible instinct for a visitor visa, because the strength of an application often lies in small details: how the purpose of the visit is described, how funds are shown, and how the visitor's ties to their home country are evidenced. A face-to-face conversation makes it easier to go through those details together and spot gaps before anything is submitted.

Before that first meeting, gather what you already have: the visitor's passport details, any previous UK visa applications or refusal letters, and a rough picture of how the trip will be funded and where the visitor will stay. You do not need everything in perfect order, but bringing what exists helps the adviser see the full situation quickly. It also helps to write down the questions you most want answered, so the meeting stays focused on what matters to you.

At the meeting, expect the adviser to ask about the visitor's circumstances at home as well as the trip itself, since an application has to show both the purpose of the visit and an intention to leave the UK at the end. Ask them to explain, in plain terms, what evidence they would want and why. If anything about their answers feels vague, or they cannot tell you how they are regulated, that is a good reason to pause and consider someone else.

## The key thing to understand about visitor visa advice

It is a criminal offence to give immigration advice in the UK unless you are qualified and regulated. Advisers must be registered with the Immigration Advice Authority (IAA, formerly the OISC) at the right level for your matter, or be a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive authorised by their own regulator. Check the IAA's adviser finder or the SRA register before you share documents or pay.

IAA advisers are registered at levels that limit what they can do, from basic applications to appeals and representation. Ask what level an adviser holds and whether it covers your type of case. For a visitor visa, the adviser should be able to help you present the purpose of the visit, your funds and your ties to your home country clearly.

## When to think twice
