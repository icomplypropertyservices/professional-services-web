## Getting the most from a video appointment with a private GP

An online appointment works differently from sitting in a consulting room, and a little preparation makes the time count. Find a quiet, private spot with a steady connection, and have a list of your symptoms, when they started and what you've already tried to hand. If you can, take your temperature or blood pressure beforehand and note any readings, since the doctor can't examine you over video.

Think about what you can show on camera. A rash, a swollen joint or the colour of your skin can often be assessed if you have good light and can hold the camera steady, so have a lamp nearby and be ready to move closer. Some problems, such as chest pain, severe breathlessness or a suspected fracture, need urgent in-person care rather than a video call, so don't wait for an online slot if something feels serious.

Before you book, ask how the clinic handles anything that can't be sorted remotely. A good private GP will tell you whether they can see you in person at a clinic, refer you on, or advise you to contact NHS 111 or, in an emergency, 999. It's also worth confirming how any prescription would reach you, since online clinics vary in whether they send it electronically to a pharmacy or post it.

## What to understand about online private GP appointments

Every doctor practising in the UK must be registered and hold a licence to practise with the General Medical Council (GMC), and GPs also appear on the GMC's GP register. You can search the register by name to check anyone you're considering. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales it's Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

A private GP doesn't replace your NHS GP. Good clinics will ask for your consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice, so your records stay complete and any medication is prescribed safely. ## FAQs: a private GP

### Can an online private GP prescribe medication?

A private GP can prescribe where it's clinically appropriate, and good clinics will share notes with your NHS practice so prescribing stays safe. Some medicines, and some situations, will still need a face-to-face appointment.

### How do I check that an online private GP is properly registered?

Every doctor practising in the UK must be registered and hold a licence to practise with the General Medical Council. You can search the GMC's online register by name, and check the clinic's registration with the relevant regulator for its nation.

### Will using a private GP affect my NHS care?

A private GP doesn't replace your NHS GP, and you can still use NHS services. Sharing consultation notes with your NHS practice, with your consent, helps keep your records complete.

### What kinds of problems suit an online GP appointment?

Video consultations suit many everyday problems, and appointments of fifteen to thirty minutes are common, giving you more time to talk. Anything that needs a physical examination may need to be seen in person instead.

### Can an online private GP examine me?

A private GP can't carry out a physical examination over video, so some problems will need to be seen in person. Many everyday concerns can still be assessed by talking things through and looking at what you can show on camera, and the GP should tell you if you need to be seen face to face.

### What should I do if my symptoms feel urgent before my appointment?

If you have chest pain, severe difficulty breathing, heavy bleeding or another symptom that feels like an emergency, call 999 rather than waiting for an online appointment. For urgent advice that isn't an emergency, NHS 111 can help you work out what to do next.

### How do I know the online GP is seeing patients in my area?

Rules about where a private GP can see you vary, so it's worth asking the clinic directly whether they take patients where you live. Telling us your location when you enquire means we can introduce you to a GP set up to see patients in your area.

### Will a private GP share my notes with my NHS GP?

With your consent, most do, and it is safer for you. Joined-up records help avoid duplicate tests and medication clashes.

### Can everything be done online?

Often much of it can. Some steps may need a visit, depending on the service. The practice will tell you what can be done remotely.
