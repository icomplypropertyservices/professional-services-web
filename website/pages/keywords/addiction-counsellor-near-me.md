In the UK, the titles 'counsellor' and 'psychotherapist' are not protected, which means anyone can call themselves a counsellor. ## FAQs: an addiction counsellor

### How do I find a reputable addiction counsellor near me?

Look for a counsellor who is on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS, or who holds FDAP accreditation for addiction work. You can also ask about their training and experience with addiction before booking a session.

### What is the difference between a counsellor and a psychologist for addiction?

Counsellors typically provide talking therapy and may have specialist training in addiction, while practitioner psychologists, who use titles like clinical or counselling psychologist, must be registered with the HCPC and often have doctoral-level training. Both can help with addiction, but their approaches and qualifications differ.

### Can an addiction counsellor help with relapse prevention?

Yes, addiction counsellors are trained to help you understand triggers, develop coping strategies and plan for aftercare to reduce the risk of relapse. It's a good idea to ask specifically about their approach to relapse prevention when you first make contact.

### How do I prepare for my first session with an addiction counsellor?

Think about what you want to achieve and any questions you have about the counsellor's approach, confidentiality and experience. It can also help to note down any patterns or concerns you've noticed about your addiction so you can discuss them openly.

### Can I get therapy on the NHS?

Yes. In England you can refer yourself to NHS Talking Therapies. Waiting times vary by area, which is why some people choose private therapy.
