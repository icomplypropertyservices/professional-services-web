## What to understand about private dental care

Private dentists must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. If the practice provides regulated healthcare in England, it must also be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

Private treatment often involves costs that are agreed directly with the dentist, so ask for an itemised treatment plan before you commit. Membership plans can help spread the cost of routine check-ups and hygiene visits. If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process; the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

## Checking private dentists are qualified and regulated

## FAQs: a private dentist

### How do I check if a private dentist is properly registered?

You can check the General Dental Council (GDC) register online to confirm the dentist is registered. In England, you can also check whether the practice is registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC) and see its inspection rating.

### What should I do if I have a problem with private dental treatment?

Start with the practice's own complaints process. If the issue isn't resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### Will I know the cost before treatment starts?

A private dentist should give you an itemised treatment plan setting out the costs before you agree to treatment. It's a good idea to ask for this in writing so you can consider it carefully.

### Can a private dentist help if I can't get an NHS appointment?

Yes, private dentists offer appointments outside the NHS and may be able to see you more quickly. They will explain the costs and treatment options directly with you.

### How close does a private dentist need to be?

That's a personal choice, but think about the journeys you're most likely to make, such as an early check-up before work or an urgent visit when you're in pain. A practice a little further away with better appointment times may suit you better than the nearest one.

### Can I visit a private dentist if I already have an NHS dentist?

Yes, you can see a private dentist for some or all of your care, though it's worth telling both practices so your records stay clear. Private dentists offer appointments outside the NHS, with costs agreed directly with you.

### What happens at a first appointment with a local private dentist?

A first appointment usually involves an examination and a conversation about your dental history and what you'd like to change. Ask what the examination includes and request an itemised treatment plan before agreeing to any treatment.

### Do I need a check-up before treatment?

Almost always. A dentist needs to examine you, and often take X-rays, before recommending treatment.
