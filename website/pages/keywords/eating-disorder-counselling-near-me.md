## Questions to ask the counsellor

- What experience do you have in working with eating disorders, and which ones do you feel most equipped to support?

- Which professional register are you on, and is it accredited by the Professional Standards Authority?
- What is your approach to counselling for eating disorders, and how might it help with the thoughts and feelings behind the food?
- What are your confidentiality limits, and when might you need to share information with others?
- How do you usually work with someone who is ambivalent about change or worried about seeking help?

## What to understand about eating disorder counselling

Eating disorder counselling provides specialist support for anorexia, bulimia, binge eating and related difficulties, but it should sit alongside medical monitoring. Specialist services follow NICE guidance, and your GP can advise on the physical checks that may be needed. Beat, the UK eating disorder charity, offers support and information for people affected.

Counsellor and psychotherapist are not protected titles in the UK, so it is worth choosing a practitioner on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS. Accredited registers require training, supervision, a code of ethics and a complaints procedure. A good therapist will explain their approach and confidentiality, including its limits, in the first session.

## Checking counsellors are qualified and regulated

"Counsellor" and "psychotherapist" are not protected titles in the UK. To protect yourself, choose a practitioner on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS. Practitioner psychologists, who use titles such as clinical or counselling psychologist, must be registered with the HCPC.

Eating disorder counselling provides specialist support, but medical monitoring is essential because eating disorders can affect physical health. Counselling should work alongside any medical care you receive, and specialist services follow NICE guidance.

Counsellor and psychotherapist are not protected titles in the UK, so check whether the practitioner is on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS.
