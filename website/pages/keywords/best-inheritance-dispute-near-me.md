## Qualifications and registration to look for

Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity in England and Wales, so some will writers are not regulated. Solicitors (SRA) and chartered legal executives (CILEx) are regulated, insured and covered by the Legal Ombudsman. Many specialists are also members of STEP (the Society of Trust and Estate Practitioners) or Solicitors for the Elderly. Applying for a grant of probate on someone else's behalf for a fee is a reserved activity.

When choosing a provider, ask whether they are regulated, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance and where your original will is stored. Scotland has its own rules on succession and confirmation, so make sure your adviser practises in the right jurisdiction.

## FAQs: an inheritance dispute

### How do I judge who is the best inheritance dispute specialist for me?

There is no official ranking, so look at whether they are regulated, insured and experienced in inheritance disputes specifically. Ask about their approach to settlement and costs, and whether they belong to STEP or Solicitors for the Elderly, then decide who you feel comfortable with.

### Can I challenge a will if I was left out?

It depends on your relationship to the person who died and your circumstances. A specialist can tell you whether you may have a claim and what time limits apply, so it is worth seeking advice promptly.

### What if the estate is in Scotland?

Scotland has its own rules on succession and confirmation, so you need an adviser who practises in the right jurisdiction. Tell iComply where the estate is based so we can introduce you to someone suitable.

### Will I have to go to court?

Not necessarily. Many inheritance disputes are resolved through negotiation or mediation, and specialists often advise exploring settlement first because costs can be high. A court process may still be needed if agreement cannot be reached.

### Does the nearest inheritance dispute specialist have to be the one I choose?

Not at all. Being nearby can make meetings and paperwork easier, but experience in inheritance disputes matters more. Compare a few specialists on regulation, insurance and how they would handle your situation before deciding.

### What should I bring to a first appointment about an inheritance dispute?

Bring the will if you have it, any correspondence with the executor or other beneficiaries, and a short timeline of what has happened. A specialist can then tell you whether you may have grounds to challenge the estate and what to do next.

### What happens if I die without a will?

The intestacy rules decide who inherits, which may not reflect your wishes, especially for unmarried partners and stepchildren. A will lets you choose.
