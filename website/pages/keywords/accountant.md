## What to understand about finding an accountant

In the UK, the word "accountant" on its own is not a protected title, so anyone can use it. However, titles such as Chartered Accountant (ICAEW, ICAS or Chartered Accountants Ireland), Chartered Certified Accountant (ACCA) and Chartered Management Accountant (CIMA) are protected, and members are bound by their body's ethics, training and complaints rules. Only registered auditors can sign statutory audit reports.

Every practice offering accountancy services must also be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by a professional body or by HMRC. It is reasonable to ask which body supervises a firm, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance, and how complaints are handled.

## Questions to ask the accountant

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the accountant is right for you:

- Which professional body are you a member of, and what does that mean for the service you provide?
- Which body supervises your practice for anti-money-laundering purposes?
- Do you hold professional indemnity insurance?
- What exactly is included in your quote, and which returns and filings does it cover?
- How do you handle Making Tax Digital, and will you help me choose and set up software?
- How do you charge, and will you let me know in advance if the work is likely to cost more than quoted?

## Accountant: FAQs

### Do I need a chartered accountant?

Not necessarily, but a protected title such as Chartered Accountant or Chartered Certified Accountant shows the person is bound by a professional body's rules on ethics, training and complaints. For straightforward tax returns, a qualified accountant from any recognised body can help, but for more complex work you may want to check their specific qualifications.

### What is Making Tax Digital and does it affect me?

Making Tax Digital is HMRC's move towards digital record-keeping and filing. It already applies to VAT-registered businesses, and is being extended to other taxes over time. An accountant can explain whether it applies to you yet and help you choose compatible software.

### Can an accountant help if I am behind on my tax returns?

Yes, accountants regularly help people catch up on overdue returns and deal with HMRC. It is best to be upfront about how far behind you are so they can estimate the work involved and any penalties you may face.

### How do I know an accountant is properly supervised?

Every accountancy practice must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes by a professional body or by HMRC. You can ask which body supervises them and check the answer with that body if you wish.

### Is an accountant the same as a bookkeeper?

Not quite. A bookkeeper records day-to-day income and spending, while an accountant prepares your accounts and tax returns and can advise on tax and business decisions. Some practices offer both, so ask what is included before you agree to anything.

### What should I bring to a first meeting with an accountant?

Bring whatever records you have, such as bank statements, invoices, receipts and any correspondence from HMRC, along with a note of your business structure and how you keep records now. Being open about anything overdue helps them judge the work involved and quote accordingly.

### Can I ask an accountant to explain things without jargon?

Yes, and it is worth saying so early on. A practice that suits you should be willing to talk through what a return involves and why a particular approach is being suggested, so you can make informed decisions rather than simply signing where shown.
