## Checking opticians are qualified and regulated

Optometrists and dispensing opticians must be registered with the General Optical Council (GOC), which you can check online. Optometrists carry out eye examinations; dispensing opticians fit and supply glasses and, with extra training, contact lenses.

Some optometrists hold extra qualifications that let them manage more eye conditions or prescribe certain medicines. In many areas of the UK, community optometrists provide minor eye condition and urgent eye care services.

iComply is not a regulator and does not provide the professional service itself. We introduce you to professionals who hold their own registrations, and we encourage you to check them on the public register.

## FAQs: an eye test

### How do I find an eye test near me?

You can search for local opticians or ask iComply to introduce you to a suitable professional in your area. We will pass on your details and the professional will contact you to arrange a convenient appointment and quote you directly.

### Is an eye test free on the NHS?

Many people qualify for a free NHS eye test, including some people on certain benefits, those with diabetes or glaucoma, and people over a certain age. The optician will be able to check your eligibility when you enquire.

### What should I bring to my eye test?

Bring your current glasses or contact lenses, if you wear them, and any information about your medical history or medications. It also helps to tell the optometrist about any symptoms you have been having.

### Can I get an urgent eye test if I have a problem?

If you have sudden changes in your vision, severe eye pain, or other urgent symptoms, you should seek immediate advice from NHS 111 or your GP. For non-urgent concerns, a routine eye test with a local optometrist is appropriate.

### Do I have to buy glasses where I have my eye test?

No. You are entitled to a copy of your prescription and can buy glasses anywhere.
