## Understanding the role of a commercial building surveyor

Their work helps you understand the condition of a building and the liabilities you might be taking on. A schedule of condition, for example, records the state of the property at the start of a lease and can protect you as a tenant.

Many surveyors are members of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), and RICS-regulated firms must follow professional standards, hold insurance and offer a complaints process. When choosing a surveyor, check the RICS directory, ask which report type you are getting and confirm whether the surveyor is independent of the sale. For asbestos work, look for organisations accredited by UKAS for surveying.

## FAQs: a commercial building surveyor

### What is a schedule of condition and why might I need one?

A schedule of condition is a detailed record of the property's condition at a point in time, often used at the start of a lease. It can protect you as a tenant by limiting your liability for existing damage when the lease ends.

### How do I choose a commercial building surveyor near me?

Look for a surveyor who is a member of RICS and has experience with commercial property. You can check the RICS directory and ask about their familiarity with your area and the type of building you're dealing with.

### What is a dilapidations claim and how can a surveyor help?

A dilapidations claim is when a landlord claims against a tenant for failing to keep the property in repair. A surveyor can assess the validity of the claim and help you negotiate a fair settlement.

### Do I need a survey if I'm buying a commercial property?

A pre-acquisition survey can reveal defects and help you avoid unexpected costs after purchase. It also gives you information to negotiate the price or ask the seller to make repairs.

### Which survey do I need when buying a home?

For a conventional property in reasonable condition, an RICS Level 2 survey is often enough. Older, larger or altered properties often justify a Level 3 building survey. A surveyor can recommend the right level.
