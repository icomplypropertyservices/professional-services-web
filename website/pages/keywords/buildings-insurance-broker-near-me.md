## Understanding rebuild cost and subsidence history

When arranging buildings insurance, insurers base the sum insured on the rebuild cost of your property, not its market value. The rebuild cost is what it would take to demolish and reconstruct the building, including professional fees and site clearance. It is often lower than the market value, but getting it right is important to avoid underinsurance.

If your property has a history of subsidence, or is in an area prone to flooding, you must disclose this to the broker. Insurers may apply special terms or exclusions, and some may decline cover. A broker can approach specialist insurers and explain the implications, but you should always check the policy wording carefully.

## FAQs: a broker for buildings insurance

### Do I need a broker for buildings insurance?

You can buy buildings insurance directly from insurers, but a broker can be helpful if your property has unusual features or a history of subsidence or flooding. They can compare policies and explain exclusions, potentially saving you time and helping you find suitable cover.

### How do I check if a broker is authorised?

You can check the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register to see if a broker is authorised or an appointed representative. Many brokers also belong to the British Insurance Brokers' Association (BIBA), and some hold Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute.

### What is the difference between rebuild cost and market value?

Rebuild cost is the amount needed to reconstruct your property from scratch, including materials and labour, while market value is the price it would fetch if sold. Buildings insurance is based on rebuild cost, which is often lower than market value, so it is important to insure for the correct amount.

### Can a broker help if my property has had subsidence?

Yes, a broker can approach insurers who specialise in properties with subsidence history. They will need to disclose the history to insurers, and some may impose conditions or exclusions, but a broker can guide you through the options available.

### Does my broker have to be physically close to my property?

Not necessarily. What matters more is whether the broker understands your type of property and can access insurers who will cover it. Local knowledge can be useful for flood or subsidence risks, but a broker further away may still arrange suitable cover.

### Will a local broker visit my property?

Some brokers may visit, particularly for larger or commercial buildings, but many arrange cover from details and photographs you provide. Ask the broker directly what they need from you and whether a visit forms part of their service.

### What should I have ready before I speak to a broker?

Have the property details to hand, including its age, construction and any history of subsidence, flooding or claims. It also helps to know whether your lender has set conditions and whether you already have a rebuild cost figure.

### Is a broker more expensive than buying direct?

Not necessarily. Brokers can access insurers and schemes not available directly, and help with claims. The right choice depends on how complex your needs are.
