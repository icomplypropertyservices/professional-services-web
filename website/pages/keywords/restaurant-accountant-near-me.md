## Qualifications and registration to look for

In the UK, the word "accountant" on its own is not a protected title, so anyone can use it. Titles such as Chartered Accountant (ICAEW, ICAS or Chartered Accountants Ireland), Chartered Certified Accountant (ACCA) and Chartered Management Accountant (CIMA) are protected, and members are bound by their body's ethics, training and complaints rules. Only registered auditors can sign statutory audit reports.

Every practice offering accountancy services must also be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by a professional body or by HMRC. It is reasonable to ask which body supervises a firm, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance, and how complaints are handled.

## Getting ready for a first meeting with a restaurant accountant

When you meet a restaurant accountant for the first time, expect them to ask about the shape of your business rather than just your turnover: whether you serve eat-in, takeaway or both, how your menu splits between hot and cold items, and how tips reach your staff. Having your till reports, payroll records and recent VAT returns to hand means the conversation can get straight to the practical questions instead of waiting for paperwork.

It also helps to bring a rough picture of a typical week — what comes in, what goes out on stock and wages, and where the squeeze feels tightest. Restaurant accountants work with tight margins, so they will want to see how weekly figures could give you an early warning when food costs creep up or a shift pattern stops paying for itself. You do not need tidy books to start; you need an honest account of how the business currently runs.

Before you commit, compare more than the quote. Ask each accountant how they would handle your tronc, how they would treat the hot food and takeaway side of your VAT, and what they would actually send you each week. Two practices can quote similarly and still suit you very differently, so judge on how clearly they answer questions about your restaurant rather than on price alone.
