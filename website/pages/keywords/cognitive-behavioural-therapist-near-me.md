## Understanding CBT and checking accreditation

Cognitive behavioural therapy is a structured, evidence-based approach that focuses on the link between thoughts, feelings and behaviour. Courses are often time-limited, so it helps to ask upfront how many sessions are typical and how progress will be checked along the way. A good therapist will explain their approach and confidentiality, including its limits, in the first session.

In the UK, "counsellor" and "psychotherapist" are not protected titles, so it is worth checking that your therapist is on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS. For CBT specifically, you can also ask whether they are accredited with the British Association for Behavioural and Cognitive Psychotherapies (BABCP). Practitioner psychologists, who may use titles such as clinical or counselling psychologist, must be registered with the HCPC.

## Cognitive Behavioural Therapist Near Me: FAQs

### What is cognitive behavioural therapy and what can it help with?

CBT is a talking therapy that focuses on how your thoughts, feelings and actions affect each other. It is commonly used for anxiety, depression, OCD and PTSD, among other difficulties. A therapist will usually explain the approach and what a course of sessions involves.

### Do I need a referral from my GP to see a CBT therapist?

No, you can approach a private cognitive behavioural therapist directly without a referral. Some people choose to speak to their GP first, especially if they want to explore NHS options as well. We introduce you to a professional who will quote you directly.

### How do I check that a CBT therapist is properly qualified?

Look for accreditation with the British Association for Behavioural and Cognitive Psychotherapies (BABCP), and check that the therapist is on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS. You can also ask about their training and supervision arrangements.

### How many sessions of CBT will I need?

CBT courses are often time-limited, but the number of sessions varies depending on the difficulty and your goals. It is best to ask the therapist directly, as they can explain their typical approach and how progress is checked. They will quote you for their service.

### Can I get therapy on the NHS?

Yes. In England you can refer yourself to NHS Talking Therapies. Waiting times vary by area, which is why some people choose private therapy.
