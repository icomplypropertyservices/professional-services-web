## Understanding remortgage advice and regulation

Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and you can check this on the FCA register. Advisers typically hold a qualification such as CeMAP.

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders, and how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

## Choosing between a local adviser and a remote one

Searching for a remortgage advisor near me suggests you'd like the option of sitting down with someone, or at least dealing with an adviser whose patch you know. That's a reasonable instinct, but it's worth remembering that remortgage advice is largely about paperwork, lender criteria and timing, so a nearby adviser and a remote one will often do very similar work for you.

If being local matters, ask when you enquire whether the adviser offers face-to-face appointments and where they'd meet you. Some brokers work from home and cover a wide area by phone and email, while others have an office you can visit. Neither approach is inherently better; what matters is that you can reach them easily and understand what they tell you.

Whichever you choose, the first conversation usually covers your current lender, your deal's end date, your outstanding balance and your plans for the property. From there the adviser can compare your lender's product transfer with deals elsewhere and explain any early repayment charges that would apply if you moved now. You should leave that conversation knowing whether they search the whole market or a limited panel, and how they are paid.

## FAQs about remortgage advisor near me

### When should I start looking for a remortgage advisor?

It's usually best to start a few months before your current deal ends, so you have time to compare options and complete any paperwork. Starting early also gives you a chance to understand any early repayment charges.

### What is a product transfer and is it simpler than remortgaging?

A product transfer is when you stay with your current lender and move to a new deal with them. It can be simpler because there's often less paperwork and no need for a new valuation, but it may not be the cheapest option.

### Will I have to pay early repayment charges if I remortgage?

Early repayment charges may apply if you switch away from your current deal before it ends. These charges can be significant, so it's important to check your current mortgage terms and ask an advisor to explain them.

### How can I check that a remortgage advisor is properly authorised?

You can check on the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register to see if a broker or adviser is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm.

### Does a remortgage adviser near me have to visit my home?

Not usually. Many remortgage appointments happen by phone or video, and a home visit is rarely needed because the adviser is comparing mortgage products rather than surveying the property. If you'd prefer to meet in person, say so when you enquire so we can introduce you to someone who offers that.

### Will a local adviser know my area's property market better?

They may have a feel for local values, which can be useful context, but mortgage lenders assess your property themselves and the advice is mainly about your circumstances and the deals available. Local knowledge is a nice extra rather than the main reason to choose an adviser.

### Can I use a remortgage adviser if I'm not sure I want to switch?

Yes. Comparing your current lender's product transfer with other deals doesn't commit you to moving, and it's sensible to look before your deal ends. The adviser should set out the costs of staying and switching so you can decide.

### Will speaking to a broker affect my credit score?

An initial conversation does not. Formal applications usually involve a credit search, and the broker should tell you before that happens.
