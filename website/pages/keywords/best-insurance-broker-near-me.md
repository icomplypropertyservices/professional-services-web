## Checking insurance brokers are qualified and regulated

Insurance brokers and intermediaries in the UK must be authorised by the FCA, or be appointed representatives of an authorised firm. Check them on the FCA register. Many brokers belong to the British Insurance Brokers' Association (BIBA), and some hold Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute.

Brokers must understand your demands and needs, explain key policy features and exclusions, and tell you how they are paid. Complaints that are not resolved can go to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

A broker works on your behalf to find suitable products from providers. Ask how many providers they compare and how they are paid.

## Best Insurance Broker Near Me: FAQs

### How do I check that an insurance broker is authorised?

You can check on the FCA register to see if a broker is authorised or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm. It is a quick and free way to confirm they are allowed to give advice.

### What does 'best' mean when choosing an insurance broker?

There is no single 'best' broker because it depends on your needs. Look for one who listens, explains things clearly, and is transparent about fees. Personal recommendations and checking credentials can help you decide.

### Will a broker near me charge me a fee?

Brokers are required to tell you how they are paid, which may be through commission from the insurer, a fee from you, or both. Ask upfront so you understand the cost.

### What should I do if I have a complaint about a broker?

First, raise it with the broker directly. If it is not resolved, you can take your complaint to the Financial Ombudsman Service, which is free for consumers.

### Will a broker near me only offer policies from one insurer?

Some brokers work with a single insurer, while others compare cover from several. Ask at the start which insurers they can approach, so you understand whether you are seeing a genuine choice of options.

### Should I choose a local broker or one further away?

A nearby broker may be easier to visit in person, but distance is not the main thing. What matters is that they understand your type of insurance and explain things clearly, whether you deal with them face to face, by phone or by email.

### What happens if my circumstances change after I take out cover?

Tell your broker as soon as anything relevant changes, such as a new address or a different use of your property or vehicle. Cover can be affected if an insurer is not told, and your broker can advise on reviewing the policy each year.

### What is non-disclosure?

Failing to tell an insurer something relevant. It can reduce or void a claim, so answer questions fully and ask your broker if unsure.
