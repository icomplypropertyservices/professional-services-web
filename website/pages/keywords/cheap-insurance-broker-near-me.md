## Questions to ask the insurance broker

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- How are you paid for arranging my insurance, and will you tell me about any fees and commission in writing?
- Will you compare policies from several insurers, or do you only work with a limited panel?

- How will you help me if I need to make a claim?

## What 'cheap' really means when you're comparing brokers near you

When you type "cheap insurance broker near me", the word cheap can mean two quite different things: a broker who charges you little for their help, or a broker who finds you a low premium. These are not the same, and a broker whose own fee is modest may still arrange a policy that is poor value once the exclusions are counted. It helps to decide which of the two you actually care about before you speak to anyone.

A useful way to compare is to ask each broker to set out, in writing, their fee, any commission they receive from the insurer, and the main exclusions of the policy they are proposing. That lets you weigh the total cost of advice plus cover rather than judging on the headline figure alone. Brokers must tell you how they are paid, so this is a fair question to put to them early.

Being near you matters most if you would rather sit down and talk through a complicated situation, such as a property with a claims history or a small business with unusual activities. If your needs are straightforward, a broker further away may serve you just as well by phone, so it is worth keeping your search wider than your immediate area if the local options do not suit.

## Cheap Insurance Broker Near Me: FAQs

### How can I tell if an insurance broker is cheap or good value?

Ask the broker to explain all fees and commission clearly, then compare the total cost of advice and cover, not just the premium. A cheap premium with poor cover or large exclusions may cost you more in the long run.

### Do I need to use a broker near me?

Not necessarily. Many brokers work remotely and can advise you just as well by phone or online. A local broker can be helpful if you prefer face-to-face meetings or have complex needs, but it is not a requirement.

### What should I do if I have a complaint about a broker?

First raise it with the broker directly. If it is not resolved, you can take your complaint to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

### Can a broker help if I have been refused insurance elsewhere?

Yes, some brokers specialise in harder-to-place risks and can approach insurers that may offer cover. Be ready to explain your situation fully so they can find the right options for you.

### Is a broker with lower fees always the cheaper option?

Not necessarily. A lower fee can come with a policy that has tighter exclusions or a higher excess, which may cost you more if you claim. Compare the fee, the commission and the cover together before deciding.

### Should I choose a local broker or one further away?

A local broker can be helpful if you prefer meeting in person or your circumstances are complex. For simpler needs, a broker you deal with by phone or online may be just as suitable, so it is worth looking beyond your immediate area.

### What is non-disclosure?

Failing to tell an insurer something relevant. It can reduce or void a claim, so answer questions fully and ask your broker if unsure.
