## Knowing who you are hiring

"Architect" is a protected title in the UK. Only people on the Architects Registration Board (ARB) register can call themselves architects, and they must follow the ARB code of conduct and hold professional indemnity insurance. Many practices are also RIBA Chartered Practices. Architectural designers and technologists can do excellent work too, but they are not registered architects, so it is worth knowing which you are hiring — especially when the search brings up a mix of titles.

Interior work often looks simpler than it is. Structural changes need engineers, building regulations still apply, and lighting design transforms spaces, so a good professional will tell you early what else needs to be brought in. Before you appoint anyone, check the ARB register and ask for a written appointment that sets out services, fees and responsibilities.

## Questions to ask the professional

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you on the Architects Registration Board register, and can you confirm that in writing?
- Have you worked on interiors in buildings like mine, and can you talk me through how you approached a similar layout?
- Will you handle building regulations approval and any planning permission, or is that down to me?
- Will you bring in a structural engineer if walls need to come out, and how is that arranged?
- What exactly is included in your appointment — drawings, specifications, lighting design, site visits — and what is charged separately?
- How do you set your fees for a project like mine, and what would you need from me before quoting?

## FAQs about interior architect near me

### Do I need a registered architect for interior work?

Not always — architectural designers and technologists can do excellent interior work. But only people on the Architects Registration Board register can call themselves architects, so if that title matters to you, check the register before appointing anyone.

### Will my project need building regulations approval?

Often, yes — particularly where walls are moved, structural work is involved or the use of the space changes. Your professional can help you navigate building regulations approval, which is a separate process from planning permission.

### Can an interior architect help with lighting and finishes?

Yes. Lighting design transforms spaces, and finishes are part of how an interior is redesigned, so it is worth asking upfront what is included in the appointment and what is charged separately.
