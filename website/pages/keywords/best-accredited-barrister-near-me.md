## FAQs: a barrister

### How can I check if a barrister is accredited?

You can check the Barristers' Register on the Bar Standards Board's official register to confirm a barrister's practising certificate and whether they are authorised for Public Access. Accreditation typically refers to being regulated and in good standing.

### What does 'best' mean when choosing a barrister?

There is no official ranking of 'best' barristers. You should consider their specialism, experience, and whether they are regulated and accredited, as well as their location and fees.

### Can I instruct a barrister directly without a solicitor?

Yes, if the barrister is authorised under the Public Access scheme, you can instruct them directly for suitable matters. You should confirm this with the barrister or their clerks.

### How are barristers' fees usually charged?

Barristers typically agree fees for a defined piece of work, such as a hearing or a written opinion. Fees can vary based on the complexity and the barrister's experience, so it's best to discuss this upfront.

### Does 'accredited' mean a barrister has a special quality mark?

Not in the way you might expect. For barristers, the meaningful check is regulation by the Bar Standards Board and a current practising certificate, both visible on the Barristers' Register. Some barristers also belong to specialist associations in their field, which you can ask about directly.

### Is a barrister nearer to me always the better choice?

Not necessarily. Proximity helps for hearings and in-person meetings, but the right specialism for your case matters more. Many barristers can prepare work remotely, so a specialist a little further away can still be practical.

### How do I judge who is best when no one publishes rankings?

Ask about their experience in cases like yours, how they would handle it, and what their fees cover. Speaking to a few and comparing their answers gives you a clearer picture than any label.

### Can I go straight to a barrister without a solicitor?

Often yes, through the Public Access scheme, if the barrister is registered and the matter is suitable. Some cases still need a solicitor, and the barrister will tell you if so.
