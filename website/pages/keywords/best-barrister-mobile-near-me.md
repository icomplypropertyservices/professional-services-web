## FAQs: a barrister

### Can a barrister come to me rather than me going to chambers?

Some barristers can meet clients away from chambers, but this depends on the individual and the matter. It is best to ask the clerk directly when you enquire, explaining where you are and what you need.

### How do I check a barrister is properly regulated?

You can use the Barristers' Register to check whether someone holds a current practising certificate and whether they can accept instructions directly from the public.

### Can I instruct a barrister without a solicitor?

Barristers registered under the Public Access scheme can be instructed directly by members of the public for suitable matters. Others are usually instructed through a solicitor, so it is worth asking which route applies to your case.

### How are barrister fees worked out?

Fees are usually agreed for a defined piece of work, and the quote should set out what it covers. Ask for the fee in writing before work begins so you know where you stand.

### Does mobile mean the barrister will always travel to me?

Not necessarily. Some barristers can meet away from chambers, but whether that is practical depends on the individual, the matter and the distance involved. Ask the clerk directly and be clear about what you need, so you get a straight answer rather than an assumption.

### How can I compare barristers when I am told none is officially the best?

Look at the specialism, the experience in matters like yours, and how clearly each one explains their approach and their fee. Speaking to a few and asking the same questions gives you a fair basis for your own judgement.

### What happens at a first meeting with a barrister?

The barrister will usually want to understand the facts, see any relevant documents and explain the options open to you. It is a chance to ask about their view of your matter and what they would need from you next, so bring anything that helps them see the whole picture.

### Can I go straight to a barrister without a solicitor?

Often yes, through the Public Access scheme, if the barrister is registered and the matter is suitable. Some cases still need a solicitor, and the barrister will tell you if so.
