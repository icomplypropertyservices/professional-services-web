## Checking conveyancers are qualified and regulated

Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity. It can be carried out by solicitors regulated by the SRA or by licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers (CLC). Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money. In Scotland, property transactions are handled by solicitors under Scottish law.

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme (CQS) is a common accreditation. Check that your chosen firm is on your lender's panel before you instruct them.

## FAQs: an auction conveyancer

### What does an auction conveyancer do?

An auction conveyancer goes through the legal pack with you before you bid and then handles the legal work to complete the purchase within the auction's fixed timescale, often twenty-eight days. They also advise you on any risks in the pack so you can bid with confidence.

### Why is it important to go through the legal pack before bidding at auction?

The legal pack contains the title, searches, and special conditions that apply to the property. Going through it before bidding is essential because exchange happens at the fall of the hammer, meaning you are legally committed once you win. A conveyancer can help you understand the pack and flag any issues.

### How can I judge if an auction conveyancer is best for me?

There's no official 'best' rating, so you should judge for yourself by asking about their experience with auction purchases, their availability to go through the pack quickly, and whether they are on your lender's panel. You can also ask about their fees and timescales.

### What should I do about finance before bidding at auction?

You should arrange your finance in advance, as auction purchases require completion within a fixed timescale. Speak to your lender and make sure your conveyancer is on their panel before you bid.

### How can I tell if an auction conveyancer is right for my purchase?

Ask about their experience with auction purchases and their availability to review the legal pack quickly. Check that they are regulated by the SRA or CLC and, if you have a mortgage, that they are on your lender's panel.

### What should I have ready when I first speak to a conveyancer about an auction property?

Have the auction date and the legal pack to hand, along with details of your lender if you're using a mortgage. It also helps to know the property address and whether you're buying alone or with someone else.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a licensed conveyancer?

Both are regulated and insured. Licensed conveyancers specialise in property transactions, while solicitors can also advise on wider legal issues. Either can handle a standard purchase or sale.
