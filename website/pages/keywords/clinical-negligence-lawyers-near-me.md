## Checking solicitors are qualified and regulated

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

## FAQs: a clinical negligence solicitor

### Do I need to find a clinical negligence solicitor near me?

It can help to instruct a solicitor you can meet in person, but many firms now work remotely as well. What matters most is that the solicitor specialises in clinical negligence and is properly regulated. We can introduce you to a suitable solicitor who will quote you directly.

### How long do I have to make a clinical negligence claim?

Claims usually must start within three years of the harm occurring or of you becoming aware of it, but there are exceptions, for example for children or people who lack capacity. It is important to seek advice promptly so the time limit can be assessed for your circumstances.

### Will I have to pay for a clinical negligence solicitor upfront?

Many clinical negligence solicitors work on no win, no fee terms, but arrangements vary and you should always ask for a clear explanation of fees before instructing anyone. The solicitor will quote you directly, and prices are on application.

### What should I do if I am unsure whether I have a clinical negligence claim?

Gather any medical records and correspondence you have, and note down what happened and when. A specialist clinical negligence solicitor can then assess whether the treatment fell below an acceptable standard and caused harm. We can introduce you to one who will discuss your situation and quote you directly.

### What usually happens at a first meeting with a clinical negligence solicitor?

The solicitor will typically ask you to describe the treatment and the harm you believe it caused, and will want to know when it happened so the three-year time limit can be considered. They may explain what medical records and expert evidence would be needed, and how they would approach funding the claim. You do not need to have everything to hand before that first conversation.

### Is it worth speaking to more than one clinical negligence solicitor?

It can be, because firms differ in the type of cases they take on and in how they charge, and comparing a few answers helps you judge for yourself. Ask each the same questions about regulation, fees and who would handle your matter. There is no obligation to instruct anyone after an initial conversation.

### Can a solicitor help if the harm happened to a relative who has since died?

Yes, solicitors do advise families in these circumstances, and there are special rules about who can bring a claim. It is worth raising this early, along with any dates you know, so the solicitor can consider time limits and what evidence is available. They will explain the position for your particular situation.

### Is a solicitor the same as a lawyer?

"Lawyer" is a general word that covers solicitors, barristers, chartered legal executives and others. A solicitor is a specific regulated professional who can advise you, run your case and instruct a barrister if needed.
