## How to check you are in safe hands

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Specialist titles such as orthodontist or endodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

## Understanding bridges and your local options

A bridge replaces missing teeth by anchoring false teeth to the teeth on either side of the gap, or to dental implants. Some bridges are adhesive and need very little preparation of the neighbouring teeth, while others involve more reshaping. Your dentist will explain which type is suitable after examining your mouth.

Bridges need careful cleaning underneath to keep the supporting teeth and gums healthy. It is also worth comparing a bridge with an implant, as each has different advantages and maintenance needs. A local private dentist can talk you through both so you can decide what feels right for you.

## Questions to ask the dentist about a bridge

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- What type of bridge would you recommend for my missing tooth or teeth, and why?
- How much tooth preparation is involved with the bridge you are suggesting?
- What are the alternatives to a bridge, such as an implant or denture, and how do they compare?
- How should I clean under the bridge to keep it and my other teeth healthy?
- How long might the bridge last, and what could make it need replacing?
- What is the process from start to finish, including how many visits I will need?

## FAQs about bridge near me

### Is a bridge better than an implant?

There is no simple answer, as it depends on your mouth, your health and your preferences. A bridge is fixed and may be quicker, while an implant avoids preparing neighbouring teeth. A dentist can compare the options for your situation.

### Can I get a bridge on the NHS?

Some bridges are available on the NHS if you are eligible for NHS dental treatment and it is clinically appropriate. However, this service introduces you to private dentists, who will quote you directly for private treatment.

### How do I check a dentist is qualified?

Dentists must be registered with the General Dental Council, and you can check the register online. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must also register with the Care Quality Commission, which inspects and publishes ratings.

### What if I have a problem with private dental treatment?

Start with the practice's complaints process. If it is not resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### Do I need a check-up before treatment?

Almost always. A dentist needs to examine you, and often take X-rays, before recommending treatment.
