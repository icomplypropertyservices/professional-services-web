## Qualifications and registration to look for

"Tax adviser" is not a protected title in the UK. The Chartered Institute of Taxation (CTA qualification) and the Association of Taxation Technicians (ATT) are the main specialist bodies, and many accountants and solicitors also advise on tax. Members of these bodies follow Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation, a shared code covering honesty, confidentiality and dealing with HMRC.

Tax advisers who provide services for a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes. HMRC has also been tightening standards for paid tax agents, so it is reasonable to ask about qualifications, supervision and professional indemnity insurance before you go ahead.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Choosing between local VAT help and a general accountant

A search for a VAT advisor near me often comes from someone who already has an accountant but wants VAT looked at properly, or who is weighing up whether their existing accountant is the right person for a particular transaction. General accountants deal with VAT returns day to day, but a specialist VAT adviser spends their time on the trickier areas such as partial exemption, property VAT and HMRC disputes, so the two roles can sit alongside each other rather than compete.

When you compare local options, ask each one directly how much of their work is VAT and whether they have handled a situation like yours before, since VAT rules vary sharply between ordinary trading, property and cross-border goods. It also helps to ask who would actually do the work — the person you speak to or a colleague — and whether they will liaise with your existing accountant so nothing gets filed twice or missed. Distance matters less than it used to, but meeting locally can still be useful if you would rather go through paperwork face to face.

One common misunderstanding is that a VAT adviser will simply confirm what you have already decided; in practice the useful conversation often happens before you commit to a treatment, particularly on a property deal where the VAT position needs settling before contracts are exchanged. Another is assuming the flat rate scheme is always simpler or cheaper — its suitability depends on your costs and sector, so it is worth asking specifically whether it fits your business rather than taking it as a default.

## VAT Advisor Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need a VAT adviser if I am already an accountant?

Your accountant may already handle VAT, but if your situation is complex or you want a second opinion, a specialist VAT adviser can help. It is worth asking your accountant first about their VAT expertise.

### Can I get VAT advice remotely, or do I need to meet in person?

Many VAT advisers work remotely by phone, email or video call, which can be convenient. If you prefer to meet face to face, let iComply know and we can introduce you to a local professional.

### What should I have ready before I speak to a VAT adviser?

Gather any relevant paperwork, such as VAT returns, correspondence from HMRC, contracts, or details of the transaction you are concerned about. This will help the adviser understand your situation quickly.

### How do I know if a VAT adviser is properly qualified?

Tax adviser is not a protected title, so ask about qualifications and memberships of bodies like the Chartered Institute of Taxation or the Association of Taxation Technicians. It is also reasonable to ask about professional indemnity insurance.

### Can a VAT adviser work alongside my existing accountant?

Yes, this is common. Your accountant can keep handling routine returns while a VAT adviser looks at a specific transaction or dispute, and it is worth asking them to keep each other informed so the same issue is not dealt with twice.

### Is a local VAT adviser better than one further away?

Not necessarily. What matters most is their experience with your type of VAT issue and whether you can communicate comfortably, so a nearby adviser is convenient but not automatically the better choice for a property or import query.

### Should I ask about anti-money-laundering supervision?

It is reasonable to. Tax advisers charging a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, and HMRC has been tightening standards for paid tax agents, so asking about supervision and professional indemnity insurance is a sensible check before you go ahead.

### Is tax planning legal?

Using reliefs and allowances as Parliament intended is normal and legal. Aggressive avoidance schemes are a different matter and can carry serious risks. A qualified adviser will explain the difference clearly.
