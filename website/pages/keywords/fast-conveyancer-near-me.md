## Speed and safety go together in conveyancing

Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity, carried out either by solicitors regulated by the SRA or by licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money, so asking who regulates a firm is a fair question when you're choosing quickly.

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme is a common accreditation, so check your lender's panel before you instruct anyone. Whoever you choose, always verify bank details by phone before sending money, because fraudsters target property transactions.

## Why "fast" and "near me" pull in different directions

A conveyancer close by is not automatically the quicker choice. Much of the work is done by phone, email and post, and searches and enquiries go to the local authority and other bodies wherever the property is, so a firm in the next town can often move your file along just as briskly as one on your high street.

What usually decides the pace is the firm's current workload and how promptly it can reach you when something needs a decision. When you speak to a conveyancer, ask how they handle a file that is already under time pressure and how they will let you know the moment a search or enquiry comes back.

Being near still has its uses: it can make it easier to drop off or sign documents in person and to sit down with someone if the transaction becomes complicated. Weigh that convenience against how responsive the firm seems when you first get in touch, because that first conversation often tells you a lot.

## FAQs about fast conveyancer near me

### How quickly can a conveyancer start work?

That depends on the firm's current workload and on how quickly you can provide the documents they need. Instructing early and having your paperwork ready helps avoid delays once you've chosen.

### Does a fast conveyancer cost more?

Fees vary between firms and depend on the property and the work involved, so ask for a written quote and compare what each one covers. iComply does not set or take fees; the conveyancer quotes you directly.

### Can I use any conveyancer if I have a mortgage?

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, so check before you instruct. If your chosen firm is not on the panel, your lender may not accept them.

### How do I check a conveyancer is properly regulated?

Solicitors are regulated by the SRA and licensed conveyancers by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. You can check a firm's status with the relevant regulator before instructing them.

### Is a local conveyancer always faster than one further away?

Not necessarily. Most conveyancing is handled remotely, and the searches and enquiries depend on other organisations rather than on distance. What matters more is how organised the firm is and how quickly it responds to you.

### Can I switch conveyancers if mine is too slow?

You can change firms, but it usually means the new conveyancer has to pick up the file and may need to repeat some work, which can cost time and money. It is worth raising your concerns with your current conveyancer first and asking for a clear plan.

### What should I have ready so my conveyancer can start straight away?

Gather proof of identity and address, your mortgage offer or lender details if you have one, and any documents about the property you already hold. Having these ready when you instruct helps the legal work begin without waiting on paperwork.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a licensed conveyancer?

Both are regulated and insured. Licensed conveyancers specialise in property transactions, while solicitors can also advise on wider legal issues. Either can handle a standard purchase or sale.
