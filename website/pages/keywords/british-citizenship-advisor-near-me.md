## Checking immigration advisers are qualified and regulated

It is a criminal offence to give immigration advice in the UK unless you are qualified and regulated. Advisers must be registered with the Immigration Advice Authority (IAA, formerly the OISC) at the right level for your matter, or be a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive authorised by their own regulator. Check the IAA's adviser finder or the SRA register before you share documents or pay.

IAA advisers are registered at levels that limit what they can do, from basic applications to appeals and representation. Ask what level an adviser holds and whether it covers your type of case.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## FAQs: a British citizenship adviser

### Do I need a British citizenship advisor near me, or can I apply myself?

You can apply yourself, but an adviser can help you check residence and good character requirements and avoid common mistakes. If your case is straightforward you may feel confident to apply directly, but if you have any doubts, advice can be worthwhile.

### How do I check that an adviser is properly regulated?

Use the Immigration Advice Authority's adviser finder to check registration and level, or the Solicitors Regulation Authority register if they are a solicitor. Never share documents or pay anyone until you have checked.

### What happens after my citizenship application is approved?

If your application is approved, you will usually need to attend a citizenship ceremony and take an oath or affirmation of allegiance. Your adviser can explain what to expect and help you prepare.
