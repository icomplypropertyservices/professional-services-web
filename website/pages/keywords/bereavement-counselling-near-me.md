## How to check you are in safe hands

"Counsellor" and "psychotherapist" are not protected titles in the UK. To protect yourself, choose a practitioner on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS. Practitioner psychologists, who use titles such as clinical or counselling psychologist, must be registered with the HCPC.

Accredited registers require training, supervision, a code of ethics and a complaints procedure. A good therapist will explain their approach, confidentiality and its limits in the first session.

## FAQs: bereavement counselling

### How do I find a bereavement counsellor near me?

You can search for local counsellors, but it is important to check that they are on an accredited register such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS. iComply can introduce you to a counsellor in your area who works with bereavement, and they will quote you directly.

### Is bereavement counselling available on the NHS?

Some bereavement support may be available through the NHS, but waiting times and availability vary. You can speak to your GP about local services, or contact charities such as Cruse Bereavement Support for further help.

### What is the difference between bereavement counselling and grief counselling?

The terms are often used interchangeably, but both refer to counselling that helps you cope with the death of someone close. The focus is on supporting you through grief at your own pace.

### Can I have bereavement counselling if the death happened a long time ago?

Yes, grief has no fixed timetable, and many people seek counselling months or years after a death. A counsellor will not judge you for when you decide to seek help.

### What is the difference between counselling and psychotherapy?

The terms overlap. Counselling often focuses on a specific problem over a shorter period, while psychotherapy may explore deeper patterns over a longer time. A good practitioner will explain their approach.
