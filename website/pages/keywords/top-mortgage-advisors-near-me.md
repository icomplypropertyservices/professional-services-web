## Checking mortgage brokers are qualified and regulated

Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and you can check this on the FCA register. Advisers typically hold a qualification such as CeMAP.

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders, and how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## FAQs: a mortgage adviser

### How do I check that a mortgage adviser is properly authorised?

You can check whether a broker or adviser is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register before you proceed.

### Should I choose an adviser who is close to where I live?

Being nearby can make face-to-face meetings easier, especially if you like to discuss paperwork in person. Many people are equally happy working by phone and email, so it's worth asking how the adviser prefers to work before deciding.

### What does price on application mean for a mortgage broker?

It means the broker will set out their fees or how they are paid when they quote you directly, rather than showing a fixed price upfront. Brokers must tell you whether they are paid by lender commission, a fee, or both, so ask them to explain this clearly.

### Can an adviser help if I've been declined for a mortgage before?

Yes, an adviser can look at your circumstances and explain which lenders might consider your situation. Be open about any previous declines or credit issues so the advice you receive is suitable for you.

### Is it worth speaking to more than one mortgage adviser?

It can be, because brokers differ in whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel, and in how they are paid. Asking each the same questions lets you compare like with like before you commit to one.

### Does a local adviser have access to better mortgage deals?

Not necessarily. What matters is the scope of their search and whether the recommendation suits your circumstances. A broker must tell you whether they cover the whole of the market or a limited panel, so you can judge that for yourself.

### Will speaking to a broker affect my credit score?

An initial conversation does not. Formal applications usually involve a credit search, and the broker should tell you before that happens.
