## Checking mortgage brokers are qualified and regulated

Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and you can check this on the FCA register. Advisers typically hold a qualification such as CeMAP.

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders, and how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

iComply is not a regulator and does not provide the professional service itself. We introduce you to professionals who hold their own registrations, and we encourage you to check them on the public register.

## FAQs about mortgage advisers near me

### How do I check that a mortgage adviser is properly authorised?

You can check on the FCA register. The adviser should be authorised or an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and they should be happy to give you their firm reference number.

### Will a mortgage adviser charge me a fee?

Brokers must tell you how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. Ask for this in writing before you proceed so you know what to expect.

### Can a mortgage adviser help if I am self-employed?

Yes, many advisers have experience with self-employed income and can explain which lenders are more likely to accept your circumstances. They will need to see evidence of your income, such as tax returns or accounts.

### Should I also ask about protection when I arrange a mortgage?

It is worth discussing protection such as life insurance or income protection at the same time. A good adviser will explain the options and let you decide whether it is right for you.

### Should I contact several mortgage advisers before choosing one?

It can help to speak to a few so you can compare how they work and how clearly they explain things. Ask each whether they search the whole market or a limited panel, and how they are paid, so you are comparing like with like.

### Does a mortgage adviser need to be local to me?

Not necessarily. Much advice can be given remotely, but a nearby adviser may be preferable if you want to meet in person with your paperwork. Either way, check on the FCA register that the firm is authorised before you proceed.

### What should I have ready for a first conversation with a local adviser?

It helps to have a rough idea of your deposit or equity, how your income is earned, and any credit issues you know about. You do not need everything to hand for a first chat, but the more you can explain, the more useful the adviser's initial view will be.

### Is a broker better than going to my bank?

A broker can compare many lenders and may find options your bank does not offer. Your bank can only offer its own products. Either can suit you; a broker gives you a wider view.
