## Questions to ask the private doctor

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the private GP is right for you:

- What is included in the fee, and are there any additional costs for tests, prescriptions or follow-up?
- Will you share your consultation notes with my NHS GP, and how do you handle consent?
- Are you registered with the General Medical Council, and do you appear on the GP register if you're a GP?
- Is the clinic registered with the relevant regulator, such as the Care Quality Commission in England?
- What happens if I need a referral to a specialist or further tests?
- How do you handle urgent problems outside appointment times?

## What to understand before you book a private doctor

A private doctor works alongside the NHS, not instead of it. Your NHS GP should stay informed about any private treatment you receive, so good private clinics will ask for your consent to share notes with your NHS practice. This keeps your records complete and helps ensure any medication is prescribed safely.

Before you book, check that the doctor is registered with the General Medical Council (GMC) and, if they are a GP, that they appear on the GMC's GP register. You can search the GMC's online register by name. In England, private clinics providing regulated healthcare must be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales it's Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

## How to check you are in safe hands

Every doctor practising in the UK must be registered and hold a licence to practise with the General Medical Council (GMC). GPs also appear on the GMC's GP register. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

## What a private doctor appointment is actually like

A private GP appointment is much like an NHS consultation in how it feels: you talk through your concern, the doctor examines you if needed, and you agree a plan together. The difference is usually the setting and the pace, with longer appointment slots and more choice about when you are seen. If you are seeing a specialist privately, expect a focused assessment of that particular problem rather than a general check of everything.

It helps to arrive prepared. Bring a list of any medication you take, including doses, and note down when your symptoms started and what makes them better or worse. If you have had recent tests or hospital letters about the same issue, take copies along, as this saves repeating investigations and helps the doctor build a clear picture.

Afterwards, you will usually be sent a letter summarising what was discussed and agreed. Ask for a copy to be shared with your NHS GP so your records stay joined up, and keep the letter yourself in case you need it for insurance, work or a later appointment. If the doctor recommends tests or a referral, ask how those are arranged and what they will cost before you go ahead.

## FAQs: a private doctor

### Will a private doctor replace my NHS GP?

No, a private GP works alongside your NHS GP. Good private clinics will ask for your consent to share notes with your NHS practice so your records stay complete and medication is prescribed safely.

### How do I check that a private doctor is properly registered?

You can search the GMC's online register by name to confirm registration and, for GPs, that they appear on the GP register.

### Are private clinics regulated?

In England, private clinics providing regulated healthcare must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings.

### What should I ask about fees before I book?

Ask what is included in the fee and whether there are extra costs for tests, prescriptions or follow-up appointments. Quotes are price on application, so it's worth getting clarity on the total cost before you commit.

### Can a private doctor prescribe medication?

Yes, a private doctor can prescribe medication where it is clinically appropriate. Private prescriptions are usually dispensed by a pharmacy and paid for separately from the consultation, so ask about this before your appointment.

### What if I need to be seen urgently?

A private doctor is not an emergency service. If you feel seriously unwell, call 999 or use NHS 111 for urgent advice, and contact your NHS GP surgery for problems that cannot wait for a routine private appointment.

### Will a private GP share my notes with my NHS GP?

With your consent, most do, and it is safer for you. Joined-up records help avoid duplicate tests and medication clashes.
