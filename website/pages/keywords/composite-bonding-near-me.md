## FAQs about composite bonding near me

### Will composite bonding damage my teeth?

Composite bonding usually needs little or no drilling, so it is often described as a conservative option. Your dentist can explain what is involved for your particular tooth and whether any preparation is needed.

### Does bonding stain, and can it be polished?

The resin can stain over time, particularly with coffee, tea or smoking. Maintenance appointments help, and your dentist can advise on polishing or replacing it when needed.

### Can bonding close a gap between my front teeth?

Bonding can be used to close small gaps by adding resin to the edges of the teeth. Your dentist will assess whether bonding is suitable for the size of the gap or whether another option would work better.

### What should I do if I am unhappy with private dental treatment?

Start with the practice's own complaints process. If it is not resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### Is bonding the same as having a veneer?

No. Bonding adds tooth-coloured resin directly to your tooth, usually with little or no drilling, whereas a veneer is a thin shell fitted over the front of the tooth. Your dentist can explain which is more suitable for the change you want.

### Can I have bonding on more than one tooth at once?

Often yes, and dentists frequently bond several front teeth together so the shapes match. Whether that suits you depends on your teeth and gums, so ask the dentist to assess each tooth rather than assuming one approach fits all.

### What if the bonding chips after it is done?

Bonding can chip, and your dentist can usually repair or smooth the area. Ask at your first appointment how they handle repairs and whether there is any charge, so you know where you stand before you go ahead.

### Do I need a check-up before treatment?

Almost always. A dentist needs to examine you, and often take X-rays, before recommending treatment.
