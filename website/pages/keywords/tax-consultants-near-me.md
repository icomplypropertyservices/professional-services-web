## Knowing who you are dealing with

"Tax adviser" is not a protected title in the UK, so anyone can describe themselves that way. The Chartered Institute of Taxation, which sets the CTA qualification, and the Association of Taxation Technicians are the main specialist bodies, and their members follow Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation — a shared code covering honesty, confidentiality and dealing with HMRC. Many accountants and solicitors also advise on tax.

Tax advisers who charge a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, and HMRC has been tightening standards for paid tax agents. It is therefore reasonable to ask about qualifications, supervision and professional indemnity insurance before you go ahead. Because you are comparing several consultants, ask each the same questions so you can judge them on the same basis rather than on who sounds most confident.

## Questions to ask the consultant

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Which qualifications do you hold, and are you a member of the Chartered Institute of Taxation or the Association of Taxation Technicians?
- How will you be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, and do you hold professional indemnity insurance?
- Have you handled matters like mine before, and how would you approach it?
- Can you give me a fixed fee for this defined piece of work, and what would fall outside it?
- Will you keep my accountant informed, and how will the three of us work together?
- What will you need from me, and roughly what stages should I expect the work to go through?

## Red flags when choosing a tax consultant

Be cautious if you notice any of the following:

- Fees linked to a percentage of tax "saved" without clear terms
- Advice that relies on keeping information from HMRC
- Pressure to act quickly without written advice
- No professional body membership or AML supervision

## FAQs: a tax consultant

### Is a tax consultant the same as an accountant?

Not necessarily. Many accountants advise on tax, but tax consultants specialise in complex areas such as reliefs, restructures, international tax and disputes. Some hold the CTA or ATT qualifications, while others come from an accounting or legal background.

### How do I judge which tax consultant is right for me?

Ask each one the same questions about qualifications, supervision, insurance and experience of matters like yours, and compare the answers. A fixed fee for defined work makes it easier to compare like with like, and you should feel able to ask how they will keep your accountant informed.

### Do I need a consultant near me, or can I work remotely?

Many people prefer someone local they can meet, but much tax work can be done by phone and email. Tell iComply how far you are willing to travel and whether remote work suits you, and the introduction can reflect that.

### What should I have ready before I speak to a consultant?

Gather the documents behind the issue — correspondence from HMRC, accounts or records, and any relevant dates. A clear outline of what you are trying to achieve and any deadline helps the consultant quote and advise properly.

### Should I reply to HMRC before speaking to an adviser?

Note the deadline on the letter, but avoid sending a detailed reply before you have advice if the matter is complex. An adviser can often ask HMRC for more time where it is reasonable.
