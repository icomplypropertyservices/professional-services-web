## One key thing to understand about divorce and money

Getting divorced does not automatically end your financial claims against each other. To make a financial settlement legally binding, you need a financial order, usually made by consent if you can agree. A solicitor can explain how this works and what full financial disclosure involves.

Mediation is usually required before you can apply to court about money or children, unless an exemption applies. A local solicitor can talk you through mediation and help you decide whether it is right for your situation.

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

"Lawyer" is a general term. In England and Wales the person handling your matter will usually be a solicitor, chartered legal executive or barrister, each regulated by their own body.

## Costs and quotes for a divorce solicitor

Legal fees depend on the type of matter, its complexity and who does the work. Firms commonly charge hourly rates, fixed fees for defined stages, or (in some claims) conditional fee arrangements. Regulated firms in England and Wales must publish pricing information for certain services and give you clear costs information at the outset. The firm will give you a POA quote.

Ask for a written estimate and for an explanation of what is not included, such as court fees, barristers' fees or experts. Ask too whether legal expenses insurance or legal aid might cover some or all of the cost.

## FAQs about divorce lawyers near me

### Do I need a solicitor who is local to me?

Not necessarily, but many people prefer a solicitor they can visit in person, especially if there are documents to sign or meetings to attend. A local solicitor may also be more familiar with the local court. When you enquire, tell us your location so we can introduce you to someone nearby.

### What is the difference between a divorce lawyer and a solicitor?

The word "lawyer" is not protected, so it can be used by people who are not solicitors. Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. It is worth confirming that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor or another authorised professional.

### Will I have to go to court for my divorce?

Most divorces are dealt with by correspondence and do not require a court attendance. However, if there are disagreements about money or children, the court may become involved, and mediation is usually required first. A solicitor can explain the process for your circumstances.

### Can we sort out finances without a financial order?

You can reach an agreement, but it is not legally binding unless it is made into a financial order. A financial order can be made by consent if you both agree, and it protects you both from future claims. A solicitor can advise you on the steps to get one.

### Should I contact several local divorce solicitors before deciding?

It can help, because you get a sense of how each one explains the process and answers your questions. Many firms offer an initial conversation, so you can compare approaches before committing. Remember that quotes are on application, so ask about charges at that first contact.

### What should I bring to a first meeting with a divorce solicitor?

Bring your marriage or civil partnership certificate if you have it, any papers you have received from your spouse or the court, and a clear picture of your finances, including savings, pensions and the family home. A short list of your questions helps too, especially about financial orders and mediation.

### Does a local solicitor have to attend court with me?

Most divorces are handled by correspondence and do not involve a court attendance, so being local is often about convenience for meetings and documents. If disagreements about money or children lead to court, your solicitor can explain who will represent you and what is involved.

### Can I get legal aid?

Legal aid is still available for some criminal, family (especially where there is domestic abuse), housing and immigration matters, subject to means and merits tests. A matched firm can tell you whether you are likely to qualify.
