## Understanding your right to legal advice

If you are arrested or asked to attend a police interview, you have the right to free legal advice at the police station. It is important not to discuss the case with the police before you have received that advice. A criminal solicitor can attend the police station or advise you by telephone.

For court proceedings, legal aid may be available depending on your financial circumstances and the seriousness of the case. You can ask the solicitor about legal aid when you speak to them. Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register.

## Qualifications and registration to look for

In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

## FAQs about criminal solicitor near me

### Do I have to pay for legal advice at the police station?

No, you have the right to free legal advice at the police station. This is separate from any legal aid for court proceedings.

### Can I get legal aid for my criminal case?

Legal aid may be available for court depending on your financial circumstances and the seriousness of the case. You should ask the solicitor to assess your eligibility.

### How do I check that a solicitor is regulated?

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA). You can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register.

### What should I do if I am asked to attend a police interview?

You should not discuss the case with the police before receiving legal advice. Ask for a solicitor to be present or available by phone before the interview.

### Does the solicitor need to be based near the court or police station?

Not always, but it can help, especially if you need someone to attend a police station or a court hearing at short notice. Ask the solicitor whether they cover the specific police station or court involved in your matter.

### What should I have ready for a first conversation with a criminal solicitor?

It helps to know what you have been told so far, such as whether you have been arrested, asked to attend an interview, charged or given a court date. Have any paperwork or correspondence to hand, and be ready to explain briefly what has happened.

### Is a local solicitor likely to be cheaper?

Not necessarily. Where a solicitor is based does not tell you what they will charge, so ask each one how their fees work and whether legal aid may be available for your case. You can then compare the quotes you receive and decide what suits you.

### Can I get legal aid?

Legal aid is still available for some criminal, family (especially where there is domestic abuse), housing and immigration matters, subject to means and merits tests. A matched firm can tell you whether you are likely to qualify.
