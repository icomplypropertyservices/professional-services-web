## Why the legal pack matters so much at auction

When the hammer falls, you are committed. Exchange happens there and then, so the time to examine the legal pack is before you bid, not after. A conveyancer goes through that pack for you, flags anything that could affect the property or your plans, and explains what you would be taking on.

Completion usually follows within a fixed period set by the auction, so finance needs to be arranged in advance rather than after you win. It also helps to check early that your lender accepts the conveyancer you choose, since many lenders only work with firms on their panel. Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity, carried out by solicitors regulated by the SRA or licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers, and both must hold professional indemnity insurance.

## Checking conveyancers are qualified and regulated

Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity. It can be carried out by solicitors regulated by the SRA or by licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers (CLC). Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money. In Scotland, property transactions are handled by solicitors under Scottish law.

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme (CQS) is a common accreditation. Check that your chosen firm is on your lender's panel before you instruct them.

## Before you bid: getting the practical side in order

An auction purchase often involves a deposit payable on the day, so it is worth knowing in advance how you would pay it and having the funds ready before you raise your hand. A conveyancer can explain how the deposit and the completion money are usually handled, so there are no surprises when the hammer falls.

If you are borrowing, speak to your lender before the auction rather than after. Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and a mortgage offer needs to be in place in time for the completion date the auction sets, so confirming both early saves pressure later.

It also helps to ask what the conveyancer needs from you and when, such as identification and proof of funds, so nothing holds up the work once you have a lot in mind. Being organised before bidding opens leaves more room to focus on the legal pack itself.
