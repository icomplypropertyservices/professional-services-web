## Understanding independent mortgage advice

Independent mortgage advisers search across the market rather than being tied to a single lender. They should explain whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel, and how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. Some deals are only available directly from lenders, so it is worth asking about that too.

Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and you can check this on the FCA register. Advisers typically hold a qualification such as CeMAP. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

## Independent Mortgage Advisor Near Me: FAQs

### What does 'whole of market' mean for an independent mortgage adviser?

It means the adviser searches across the mortgage market rather than being restricted to a single lender or a small panel. You should ask them to confirm this, as some advisers only look at a limited range of lenders.

### How can I check that a mortgage adviser is properly authorised?

You can check on the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register to see if the adviser or their firm is authorised. Advisers should also hold a qualification such as CeMAP, which you can ask them about.

### Will I have to pay a fee for independent mortgage advice?

Fees vary by firm, and advisers must tell you how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. Ask for a clear explanation before you agree to proceed.

### Can an independent adviser find deals that are not available directly from lenders?

Some deals are direct-only, meaning they are only available by going straight to the lender. An adviser should explain this and help you compare those options alongside the ones they can access.

### Is it better to use an adviser near me or one further away?

There is no rule that a local adviser is better. Being nearby can help if you want to meet in person, but what matters most is that the adviser is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority, searches the market you need and explains their fees clearly.

### What should I bring to a first appointment with a mortgage adviser?

Bring identification, proof of income such as payslips or accounts if you are self-employed, and details of your deposit or current mortgage. It also helps to have a rough idea of the property value and any credit issues you want to discuss.

### Will an independent adviser charge me for the first meeting?

Fees vary by firm, and some advisers charge for an initial consultation while others do not. Ask about any charges before you book, and remember they must tell you how they are paid, whether by commission, a fee or both.

### Is a broker better than going to my bank?

A broker can compare many lenders and may find options your bank does not offer. Your bank can only offer its own products. Either can suit you; a broker gives you a wider view.
