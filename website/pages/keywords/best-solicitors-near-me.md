## Questions to ask the solicitor

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Do you specialise in this type of matter, and how often do you handle cases like mine?
- Will you be the person dealing with my case, or will it be passed to a colleague?
- Can you set out your costs at the start, including how you charge and what disbursements might arise?
- Are you regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and can I check your firm on the register?
- How will you keep me updated as the matter progresses?
- If we disagree about the service, what is your complaints procedure and what can I do if it is not resolved?

## How to check you are in safe hands

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

iComply is not a regulator and does not provide the professional service itself. We introduce you to professionals who hold their own registrations, and we encourage you to check them on the public register.

## Comparing several solicitors near you fairly

Searching for the best solicitors near me in the plural suggests you are weighing up more than one firm, which is sensible for anything as consequential as a legal matter. The difficulty is that the firms near you may look much alike from the outside, so the comparison has to be on substance rather than presentation. What separates them is how well each one knows your particular area of law and how clearly they explain their costs.

A practical approach is to narrow the field before you contact anyone. Decide which area of law your matter falls under, then shortlist firms near you whose work centres on that area, and check each one on the Solicitors Regulation Authority register in England and Wales, the Law Society of Scotland or the Law Society of Northern Ireland as appropriate. When you speak to them, ask the same questions of each so the answers can be set side by side.

It also helps to notice who you actually speak to. Ask whether the person you are talking with will handle your matter personally or whether it will be passed to a colleague, and whether that person is a solicitor or another authorised professional. A firm that answers these questions plainly, and sets out its costs at the start, gives you something concrete to compare.

## What a solicitor is likely to cost (POA)

Legal fees depend on the type of matter, its complexity and who does the work. Firms commonly charge hourly rates, fixed fees for defined stages, or (in some claims) conditional fee arrangements. Regulated firms in England and Wales must publish pricing information for certain services and give you clear costs information at the outset. The firm will give you a POA quote.

Ask for a written estimate and for an explanation of what is not included, such as court fees, barristers' fees or experts. Ask too whether legal expenses insurance or legal aid might cover some or all of the cost.
