## What people searching for a lawyer usually need

A lawyer can help you understand your position, prepare documents, negotiate on your behalf and, where necessary, run a court case.

- You're buying or selling a home and need someone to handle the conveyancing and check the title.
- You're going through a separation or divorce and need advice on finances, children or a settlement.
- You've received a letter before claim or a court form and need to know how to respond.
- You're starting or running a business and need help with a contract, a lease or a dispute.
- You're dealing with a probate or estate administration matter and need guidance on the legal steps.

## How to check you are in safe hands

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

"Lawyer" is a general term. In England and Wales the person handling your matter will usually be a solicitor, chartered legal executive or barrister, each regulated by their own body.

## How fees usually work (POA)

Legal fees depend on the type of matter, its complexity and who does the work. Firms commonly charge hourly rates, fixed fees for defined stages, or (in some claims) conditional fee arrangements. Regulated firms in England and Wales must publish pricing information for certain services and give you clear costs information at the outset. The firm will give you a POA quote.

Ask for a written estimate and for an explanation of what is not included, such as court fees, barristers' fees or experts. Ask too whether legal expenses insurance or legal aid might cover some or all of the cost.

## Working out which kind of lawyer your matter needs

Searching simply for a lawyer tells us you may not yet know whether your matter belongs with a solicitor, a chartered legal executive or another authorised professional. Legal work is divided into specialisms, so a conveyancing matter, a family separation and a business dispute will usually be handled by different people with different experience. Naming the area of law in your own words is often more useful than picking a job title.

It also helps to think about what you actually want from the person you instruct. Some people need a one-off piece of advice or a document reviewed, while others need someone to negotiate on their behalf or represent them in court. Being clear about that from the start means the professional you speak to can tell you honestly whether the work sits within their expertise.

Finally, remember that the legal system differs across the UK, so where you live affects which regulator applies and which courts or procedures are involved. Mentioning your location and the nature of the matter when you contact us lets us introduce you to someone whose work covers that area.

## Before you choose a solicitor

These practical points are worth knowing before you speak to anyone about a solicitor:

- Choose a solicitor who specialises in your type of matter.
- Ask for costs information at the start.
- Check the firm on the SRA register.

A good solicitor will talk you through each of these in plain English.

## Red flags when choosing a solicitor

Be cautious if you notice any of the following:

- Guarantees about the outcome of a dispute
- Requests to send money to an account that changes by email
- No clear costs information or client care letter
- A firm or individual you cannot find on the relevant register

## Lawyer: FAQs

### How do I check that a lawyer is properly regulated?

For solicitors in England and Wales, you can search the SRA's register online. In Scotland, check with the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland with the Law Society of Northern Ireland. This confirms whether the individual or firm is authorised to practise.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a barrister?

A solicitor usually handles the day-to-day legal work, gives advice, prepares documents and negotiates for you. A barrister is a specialist advocate, often instructed by a solicitor to represent you in court. For many matters, your first point of contact will be a solicitor.

### Will I be told how much the legal work will cost?

Regulated firms are expected to give you information about costs at the start, and you can ask for an estimate or a fixed fee where possible. Costs can change if the matter becomes more complex, so ask how you will be updated. iComply does not set or quote fees; the solicitor quotes you directly.

### Can I complain if I am unhappy with the service?

Regulated firms must have a complaints procedure, and you should ask for a copy. If the complaint is not resolved, in England and Wales you can take service complaints to the Legal Ombudsman. In Scotland and Northern Ireland, the relevant professional body can advise on the route.

### I don't know what area of law my problem falls under — can I still enquire?

Yes. Describe what has happened in plain language, such as a letter you have received, a contract you have signed or a family situation that needs sorting out. We can then introduce you to a solicitor whose work covers that kind of matter, and they can confirm whether they are the right person for it.

### Should I speak to a solicitor before I know exactly what I need?

An initial conversation is often the point at which your options become clearer, because a solicitor can explain the legal position and what steps might follow. Ask about costs at that stage so you understand what any further work would involve before you commit.

### Can I get legal aid?

Legal aid is still available for some criminal, family (especially where there is domestic abuse), housing and immigration matters, subject to means and merits tests. A matched firm can tell you whether you are likely to qualify.
