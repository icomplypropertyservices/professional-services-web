## What to expect at an urgent same-day appointment

When you contact a practice about same-day dental care, the first conversation is usually a short triage by phone or at the desk. They will ask what is wrong, how long it has been going on and whether you have swelling, bleeding or trouble swallowing or breathing. That helps them decide how quickly you need to be seen, so answer as fully as you can rather than playing down the pain.

At the appointment itself, the dentist will examine the sore area, often with a X-ray, to work out what is causing the problem before deciding on treatment. They may be able to settle the pain there and then, for example by dressing a tooth or replacing a lost filling, but sometimes the aim is to make you comfortable and plan definitive treatment for a later visit. Ask them to explain what they are doing and why as they go.

It helps to arrive with a list of any medicines you take, including blood thinners, and to mention allergies and any recent dental work abroad. Be clear about what you can afford before treatment begins, and ask for the cost of each option in writing if you are unsure. If you are nervous, say so early so the dentist can adapt how they work.

## How to check you are in safe hands

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Specialist titles such as orthodontist or endodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process. The Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

## FAQs: same-day dental treatment

### Can I get a same-day crown on the NHS?

Same-day crowns are typically offered by private dental practices, as NHS dental services vary by area and may not provide this option. You can ask the practice directly whether they offer same-day crowns and what the private fee would be.

### What should I do if I have severe toothache and can't get a same-day appointment?

If you have severe pain, swelling or bleeding that won't stop, you can call NHS 111 for advice on urgent dental care. For life-threatening emergencies, call 999.

### How do I check if a dentist is properly registered?

All dentists must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Private practices providing regulated healthcare in England must also be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC).

### What if I'm not happy with the private dental treatment I receive?

If something goes wrong, start with the practice's complaints process. If the complaint is not resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### Will the dentist definitely treat me on the same day?

Not always. A same-day appointment usually means being assessed promptly, and treatment may be limited to relieving pain or protecting a damaged tooth until a longer appointment can be arranged. The dentist will explain what can safely be done at that visit.

### Can I be seen if I am not registered with the practice?

Many private practices will see people who are not registered with them, though it is worth asking when you call. Tell them you are a new patient and describe your symptoms so they can judge how urgent your case is.

### What if the pain becomes severe before my appointment?

If you have severe pain, swelling that is spreading, or difficulty breathing or swallowing, seek urgent medical help. You can call NHS 111 for advice on urgent dental care, and call 999 for life-threatening emergencies.

### Do I need a check-up before treatment?

Almost always. A dentist needs to examine you, and often take X-rays, before recommending treatment.
