## What accreditation means when choosing a vet

Accreditation means a practice or professional has met standards set by a regulator or professional body. It is worth checking both the individual's registration and any practice-level accreditation, as they cover different things.

Ask which accreditations apply, what they require and how you can verify them online. Accreditation is not a guarantee of the outcome, but it does mean there are standards, a code of conduct and a route for complaints.

iComply itself is not an accrediting body. We introduce practices that hold the registration their profession requires, and we encourage you to check it yourself.

## Checking vets are qualified and regulated

Every vet practising in the UK must be registered with the Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons (RCVS), and you can search the RCVS register by name before you book. Veterinary nurses have their own RCVS register too. Many practices also join the voluntary RCVS Practice Standards Scheme, which inspects premises, equipment and clinical processes.

Some vets hold further qualifications such as an RCVS certificate, advanced practitioner status or specialist status in a particular species or discipline. If your animal has a complex or unusual condition, it is reasonable to ask whether the vet treating it holds one of these, or whether they refer to someone who does.

iComply is not a regulator and does not provide the professional service itself. We introduce you to professionals who hold their own registrations, and we encourage you to check them on the public register.
