## What to expect at a first meeting about your portfolio

A first meeting with an accountant about a property portfolio is usually a working session rather than a sales pitch. Expect to talk through each property in turn, how it is held and what records you already keep, so the accountant can see where the gaps are. It helps to bring a simple list of your properties and any lender correspondence you have received.

Much of the conversation tends to centre on the questions that matter for a portfolio rather than a single letting: how accounts by property will be presented, how capital and revenue spending will be separated, and whether a company structure could suit purchases you are considering. If a lender has asked for business plans or cash-flow forecasts, say so early, because those figures are usually built from the same records as your accounts.

Because fees are quoted on application, ask how the accountant would charge for portfolio work and what that covers, so you can compare like with like. It is also fair to ask who in the practice would look after your affairs day to day, and how they prefer to receive information from you.

## What to understand about finding an accountant near you

In the UK, the word 'accountant' on its own is not a protected title, so anyone can use it. Titles such as Chartered Accountant (ICAEW, ICAS or Chartered Accountants Ireland), Chartered Certified Accountant (ACCA) and Chartered Management Accountant (CIMA) are protected, and members are bound by their body's ethics, training and complaints rules. Only registered auditors can sign statutory audit reports.

Every practice offering accountancy services must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by a professional body or by HMRC. It is reasonable to ask which body supervises a firm, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance, and how complaints are handled.

## FAQs: an accountant for portfolio landlords

### Why might a portfolio landlord need an accountant near them?

A portfolio landlord with several properties has more complex accounting needs than a single-property landlord. An accountant near you can provide accounts by property, lender-ready figures and help with longer-term structure planning, and may be able to meet in person to discuss your portfolio.

### What does 'lender-ready figures' mean for a portfolio landlord?

Lenders may ask for business plans and cash-flow forecasts when assessing a portfolio landlord's borrowing. An accountant can prepare these figures from your property accounts, so you can present a clear financial picture to the lender.

### Should I hold my properties in a company?

Whether a company structure suits you depends on your circumstances and future plans. An accountant can discuss the potential advantages and disadvantages, such as tax treatment and administration, and help you decide whether it is right for future purchases.

### How can an accountant help with capital and revenue spending?

Keeping capital and revenue spending separate is important for accurate tax reporting and for understanding your portfolio's performance. An accountant can help you set up a system to track these separately and ensure your records are clear.

### Is an accountant near me better than one further away for a property portfolio?

Proximity can help if you prefer to hand over paperwork or sit down in person, but the deciding factor should be whether the accountant regularly deals with portfolios like yours. Ask how they would produce accounts by property and handle lender requests, then weigh that against the convenience of a local meeting.

### How do I compare accountants for a portfolio when fees are on application?

Ask each one to set out what the fee covers, such as accounts by property, lender-ready figures or advice on structure, so you can compare the same scope rather than headline figures. It is also worth asking which body supervises the practice for anti-money-laundering purposes and whether they hold professional indemnity insurance.

### Do I need to bring anything to a first appointment?

A list of your properties, how each is held and any lender requests you have received will give the accountant a useful starting point. If you already track income and spending, bring a sample so they can see how your records are kept and where help is needed.

### Do I need a chartered accountant?

Not always. For simple Self Assessment a qualified, supervised accountant may be enough. For audits, complex group structures or specialist tax work, a chartered or certified firm is often the safer choice. We can match you to either.
