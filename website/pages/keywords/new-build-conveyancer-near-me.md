## Understanding the conveyancing process for a new-build

New-build conveyancing involves reviewing the developer's contract, the plans, the new home warranty and any estate management charges. Developers often set strict exchange deadlines, so it's important to instruct a conveyancer promptly. The conveyancer will also check that the property is properly registered and that planning permissions are in place.

In England and Wales, conveyancing is a reserved legal activity, so your conveyancer must be a solicitor regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority or a licensed conveyancer regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme is a common accreditation. Check that your chosen firm is on your lender's panel before you instruct them.

## New Build Conveyancer Near Me: FAQs

### Why do I need a conveyancer who specialises in new builds?

New-build conveyancing involves additional documents such as the new home warranty, estate management charges and developer-specific contracts. A conveyancer with experience in this area can help you understand these and meet the developer's deadlines.

### Can I use a conveyancer who is not local to the development?

Yes, you can use a conveyancer anywhere in England or Wales, but many people prefer someone near them for ease of communication. If you search for a new build conveyancer near me, you'll likely find local firms who understand the local area and can meet in person if needed.

### What is a new home warranty and why does it matter?

A new home warranty is an insurance-backed product that covers defects in your new home for a set period. Your conveyancer will check the warranty details to ensure it meets your lender's requirements and gives you adequate protection.
