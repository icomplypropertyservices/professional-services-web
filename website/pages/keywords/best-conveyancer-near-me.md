## Understanding 'best' and 'near me' for conveyancing

There is no official ranking of conveyancers as 'best', so it pays to judge for yourself. Look for a firm regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA) or the Council for Licensed Conveyancers (CLC), and check that it holds professional indemnity insurance. You can also ask whether it is on your lender's panel and whether it has the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme (CQS) accreditation.

Being nearby can be reassuring, but much conveyancing is done by phone, email and post. What matters more is clear communication and a named person who will handle your file. Always verify bank details by phone before sending any money, as fraud is a real risk.

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity. It can be carried out by solicitors regulated by the SRA or by licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers (CLC). Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money. In Scotland, property transactions are handled by solicitors under Scottish law.

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme (CQS) is a common accreditation. Check that your chosen firm is on your lender's panel before you instruct them.

## FAQs: a conveyancer

### How do I judge which conveyancer is best for me?

There is no official 'best' rating, so consider regulation, experience with your type of property, clear communication and a written quote that explains all costs. Ask about lender panel membership and whether the firm has CQS accreditation. Speaking to a conveyancer directly will help you decide.

### Do I need a conveyancer near me?

Not necessarily, as most conveyancing can be done remotely. However, a local firm may be easier to visit if you prefer face-to-face contact or need to drop off documents. What matters most is that you can reach them easily and trust their communication.

### What does a conveyancer do that a solicitor doesn't?

Both can carry out conveyancing, but a licensed conveyancer specialises in property law and is regulated by the CLC, while a solicitor is regulated by the SRA and may cover other areas of law. Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on client money.

### How early should I instruct a conveyancer?

It is wise to instruct as soon as your offer is accepted, or even before you start looking, so there is no delay once a sale is agreed. Early instruction gives time to check the lender's panel and get paperwork in order.

### Should I choose a conveyancer before or after my offer is accepted?

It is sensible to start looking before you commit, so you are ready to instruct as soon as an offer is accepted. That gives you time to check lender panel membership and ask questions without pressure. You can still find a conveyancer after your offer is accepted, but moving quickly then helps avoid delays.

### Can I speak to a conveyancer before I instruct them?

Yes, and it is worth doing. A brief call lets you ask about regulation, insurance, fees and how they handle leasehold or shared ownership matters. It also gives you a feel for how clearly they explain things and whether you would be comfortable working with them.

### What if I am buying in Scotland rather than England or Wales?

Conveyancing is handled differently in Scotland, where property transactions are carried out by solicitors under Scottish law. Tell us where the property is when you enquire, so we can introduce you to someone suited to that system. The rules on regulation and client money differ, so it is worth asking about them directly.

### When should I instruct a conveyancer?

As soon as your offer is accepted, or before you list a property for sale. Instructing early helps you avoid delays.
