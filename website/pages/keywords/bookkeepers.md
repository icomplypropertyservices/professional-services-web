## What people searching for bookkeepers usually need

Most people looking for bookkeepers want their day-to-day numbers kept in order, without the cost or commitment of a full accountancy service.

- You have a shoebox of receipts and bank statements and need someone to record sales, purchases and payments properly.
- You are registered for VAT and want a bookkeeper to prepare your returns on time, without you wrestling with the figures each quarter.
- You run payroll for a small team and need someone to handle the payslips and records alongside the bookkeeping.
- Your bank accounts are not reconciled, and you want clean books so you can see cash-flow problems before they bite.
- You are handing year-end work to an accountant and want tidy, up-to-date records ready for them.

## Comparing several bookkeepers side by side

Searching for bookkeepers in the plural usually means you have decided to bring someone in and now want to weigh up more than one before committing. That is a sensible approach, because bookkeeping arrangements vary widely in what they cover and how they are priced, and the only way to judge is to ask each professional the same questions and compare the answers.

The practical way to compare is to describe your situation identically to each bookkeeper and ask what they would do in the first month. Note whether they would reconcile your bank accounts, prepare VAT returns, run payroll or simply record transactions, and where they would hand over to an accountant. Ask each one to set out what their quote includes, since prices are on application and a quote is only meaningful against a defined scope of work.

It also helps to think about how you would work together month to month. Ask how they prefer to receive documents, how quickly they tend to respond and who you would speak to if something looks wrong. Because bookkeeping is not a regulated title, the supervision and insurance questions matter just as much when you are comparing several as when you are choosing one.

## Questions to ask a bookkeeper before you go ahead

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the bookkeeper is right for you:

- Which body supervises you for anti-money-laundering purposes, and can you confirm your professional indemnity insurance?
- Are you a member of the ICB or the AAT, and what does that mean for how you work?
- How will we share documents each month, and what do you need from me and when?
- Where does your work stop and an accountant's begin, particularly on year-end accounts and tax advice?
- Will you reconcile my bank accounts and prepare my VAT returns as part of the service?
- How do you charge for bookkeeping, and what is included in your quote?

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Bookkeeping is not a regulated title in the UK, but anyone providing bookkeeping services for a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by HMRC or by a professional body. Membership of the Institute of Certified Bookkeepers (ICB) or the Association of Accounting Technicians (AAT) shows a bookkeeper has trained to a recognised standard and agreed to a code of conduct.

Ask whether a bookkeeper holds professional indemnity insurance and which body supervises them. Good bookkeepers are also clear about where their work stops and an accountant's starts, for example on year-end accounts and tax advice.

## Bookkeepers: FAQs

### Do I need a qualified bookkeeper?

Bookkeeping isn't a regulated title in the UK, so there's no single qualification you must look for. Membership of the ICB or AAT shows a bookkeeper has trained to a recognised standard and agreed to a code of conduct, which is a useful signal when you're comparing.

### What is the difference between a bookkeeper and an accountant?

A bookkeeper records sales, purchases, receipts and payments, reconciles bank accounts and often prepares VAT returns and payroll. An accountant typically handles year-end accounts and tax advice, so it's worth asking each professional where their work stops and the other's starts.

### Can a bookkeeper handle my VAT returns and payroll?

Many bookkeepers prepare VAT returns and run payroll alongside the monthly bookkeeping, though not all do. Tell us what you need when you enquire, so we can introduce you to a bookkeeper whose work covers it.

### How do I compare quotes from several bookkeepers?

Ask each one exactly what is included, how often they will work on your books and how they charge, then compare like with like. Since prices are on application, the quote you receive directly from each professional is the figure to judge against the scope of work described.

### Is it worth getting quotes from more than one bookkeeper?

Yes, because bookkeepers structure their work and their charges differently, and comparing several quotes against the same description of your needs shows you what each one actually includes. Tell us you are comparing bookkeepers when you enquire, so we can introduce you to more than one professional to quote.

### How do I compare bookkeepers fairly when prices are on application?

Give each bookkeeper the same information about your business, your records and whether you need VAT returns or payroll, then compare what their quote covers rather than the headline figure alone. A quote for recording transactions is not the same as one that includes bank reconciliation, VAT and payroll, so check the scope before you decide.

### What if my records have fallen behind while I compare bookkeepers?

Say so when you enquire, because catching up on records that have fallen behind is a different job from keeping tidy books up to date each month. A bookkeeper can quote for the catch-up work alongside the ongoing bookkeeping, so you know what each part involves before you go ahead.

### Can a bookkeeper submit my VAT return?

Many can, through Making Tax Digital compatible software, once you authorise them. Check this is included in the scope before you start.
