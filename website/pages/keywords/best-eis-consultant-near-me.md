## How to check you are in safe hands

"Tax adviser" is not a protected title in the UK. The Chartered Institute of Taxation (CTA qualification) and the Association of Taxation Technicians (ATT) are the main specialist bodies, and many accountants and solicitors also advise on tax. Members of these bodies follow Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation, a shared code covering honesty, confidentiality and dealing with HMRC.

Tax advisers who provide services for a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes. HMRC has also been tightening standards for paid tax agents, so it is reasonable to ask about qualifications, supervision and professional indemnity insurance before you go ahead.

"Consultant" is a broad title that anyone can use. Check what qualifications and registrations sit behind it, and whether the work you need is regulated.

## Red flags when choosing an EIS consultant

Most professionals are honest and competent, but it is worth knowing the warning signs:

- Fees linked to a percentage of tax "saved" without clear terms
- No professional body membership or AML supervision
- Advice that relies on keeping information from HMRC
- Schemes marketed as guaranteed or risk-free tax savings

## Best EIS Consultant Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need advance assurance before raising EIS money?

Advance assurance is not compulsory, but it gives investors more confidence that the shares should qualify for EIS reliefs. An EIS consultant can prepare the application and supporting business plan for you. It is worth discussing timing with your adviser early.

### What does an EIS consultant actually do?

They prepare advance assurance applications, compliance statements and investor certificates for growing companies. They also help you check that your use of funds meets the rules. Compliance continues after investment, so their involvement often carries on too.

### How can I judge who is best for my company?

There is no official ranking, so look at qualifications, membership of bodies such as the Chartered Institute of Taxation or the Association of Taxation Technicians, and experience with EIS specifically. Ask about supervision and insurance, and whether they will explain the rules clearly. A short conversation usually tells you a lot.

### Can I work with a consultant who isn't local to me?

Yes. Much of the EIS work is document-based, so many companies work with advisers remotely. If you searched for someone near you, it's still worth asking whether phone and email contact would suit you just as well.

### Should I reply to HMRC before speaking to an adviser?

Note the deadline on the letter, but avoid sending a detailed reply before you have advice if the matter is complex. An adviser can often ask HMRC for more time where it is reasonable.
