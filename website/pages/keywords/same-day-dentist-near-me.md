## What to understand about same-day dental appointments

Same-day dentists keep slots open for urgent problems booked on the day, so it's worth calling early in the morning to secure one. When you call, describe your symptoms clearly — for example, whether the pain is constant or throbbing, if there's swelling, or if a tooth has broken — as this helps the practice prioritise and prepare for your visit.

Private dental practices in England that provide regulated healthcare must be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales, the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales; in Scotland, Healthcare Improvement Scotland; and in Northern Ireland, the RQIA. All dentists must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. If you have a complaint about private dental treatment that isn't resolved by the practice, the Dental Complaints Service can help.

## Questions to ask the dentist

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Do you keep same-day appointments for urgent problems, and what time should I call to get one?
- What information do you need from me when I call to book a same-day appointment?
- Can you tell me about the likely cost of the appointment and any treatment I might need?
- If I need a crown or other restoration, can it be done on the same day?
- What should I do if I can't get an appointment today — do you have any advice for managing the problem until I can be seen?
- Are you registered with the General Dental Council, and is the practice registered with the relevant regulator?

## FAQs: a same-day dentist

### How do I find a same-day dentist near me?

Start by calling private dental practices in your area early in the morning, as same-day slots are often booked quickly. You can also use our free introduction service to tell us what you need and we'll introduce you to a suitable local dentist who offers same-day appointments.

### What should I do if I have severe toothache and can't get a same-day appointment?

If you have severe pain, swelling, or bleeding that won't stop, and you can't get a dental appointment, you can call NHS 111 for advice on urgent care. For life-threatening emergencies, call 999. For less urgent problems, keep trying local practices or contact us for an introduction.

### Can a same-day dentist treat a broken tooth?

Yes, same-day dentists are equipped to handle urgent problems like broken teeth, lost fillings, and dental pain. They will assess the tooth and provide appropriate treatment to relieve pain and restore function, which may include a temporary or permanent repair.

### Is a same-day crown possible?

Some private clinics offer same-day crowns using technology that allows the crown to be designed and fitted in a single visit. It's worth asking the dentist when you call whether they offer this service, as not all practices do.

### Can I see a private dentist and stay with my NHS dentist?

Yes, you can use both. Some patients see a private dentist for specific treatments and keep NHS care for routine check-ups, if their NHS practice agrees.
