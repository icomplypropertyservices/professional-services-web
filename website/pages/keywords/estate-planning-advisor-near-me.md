## One key thing to understand about estate planning advice

Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity in England and Wales, so some will writers are not regulated. Solicitors (regulated by the SRA) and chartered legal executives (regulated by CILEx) are regulated, insured and covered by the Legal Ombudsman. Many specialists are also members of STEP or Solicitors for the Elderly.

When choosing a provider, ask whether they are regulated, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance and where your original will is stored. If you live in Scotland, make sure your adviser practises in the right jurisdiction, as succession rules differ.

## FAQs: an estate planning adviser

### Do I need a solicitor to write a will?

Not necessarily. Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity in England and Wales, so you can use an unregulated will writer. However, solicitors and chartered legal executives are regulated and insured, which may give you more protection.

### Can an estate planning adviser help with probate?

Some estate planning advisers can help with probate, but applying for a grant of probate on someone else's behalf for a fee is a reserved activity. Make sure the person you instruct is authorised to do this work.

### How do I choose an estate planning adviser near me?

Ask about regulation, insurance and experience, and check whether they are members of a professional body such as STEP or Solicitors for the Elderly. It is also worth meeting them to see if you feel comfortable discussing your affairs.

### What should I bring to my first meeting?

Bring details of your assets, any existing will, and information about gifts you have made. It also helps to have a rough idea of what you want to achieve, such as providing for family or reducing inheritance tax.

### Is an estate planning adviser the same as a solicitor?

Not always. Some estate planning advisers work alongside solicitors and financial advisers rather than being solicitors themselves. Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity in England and Wales, so ask whether the person you speak to is regulated and by which body.

### Can I use an adviser near me if I live in Scotland?

You can, but make sure they practise in the right jurisdiction. Scotland has its own rules on succession and confirmation, so an adviser used to the law in England and Wales may not be the right fit.

### What if I only want a simple will?

That is still worth discussing with an adviser, because even a straightforward will can be affected by pensions, gifts and how assets are held. A brief conversation will help you judge whether you need wider estate planning or just a will.

### What happens if I die without a will?

The intestacy rules decide who inherits, which may not reflect your wishes, especially for unmarried partners and stepchildren. A will lets you choose.
