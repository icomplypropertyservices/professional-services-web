## Common misunderstandings about finding the best IR35 adviser near you

A frequent misunderstanding is that a local adviser will automatically know your client's sector or your industry better than someone further away. What actually matters is whether they have handled IR35 questions like yours before, so ask them to talk you through how they would approach your contract and working practices rather than assuming proximity equals insight.

Another common assumption is that the adviser decides your status. For medium and large clients, the client makes the status decision, and your adviser's role is to assess the position, explain the reasoning and help you respond or raise a dispute if you disagree. Knowing this upfront stops you expecting an adviser to overrule a determination on your behalf.

People also often think a single read of the contract settles the question. Working practices matter as much as the contract, so a good adviser will want to talk about how you actually do the work, who directs it and how your engagement runs day to day before giving a view.

## Qualifications and registration to look for

"Tax adviser" is not a protected title in the UK. The Chartered Institute of Taxation (CTA qualification) and the Association of Taxation Technicians (ATT) are the main specialist bodies, and many accountants and solicitors also advise on tax. Members of these bodies follow Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation, a shared code covering honesty, confidentiality and dealing with HMRC.

Tax advisers who provide services for a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes. HMRC has also been tightening standards for paid tax agents, so it is reasonable to ask about qualifications, supervision and professional indemnity insurance before you go ahead.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## FAQs about best IR35 adviser near me

### What does an IR35 adviser actually do?

An IR35 adviser looks at your contract and working practices to assess employment status, and helps contractors and clients respond to status determinations. They can also help you prepare for or challenge a determination.

### Do I need a local adviser for IR35?

Not necessarily. Many advisers work remotely, but if you prefer face-to-face meetings, searching 'near me' can help you find someone local. What matters most is their specific IR35 experience.

### Who makes the IR35 status decision?

For medium and large clients, the client makes the status decision. As a contractor, you can dispute a determination if you believe it's wrong, and an adviser can help you with that.

### How can I judge if an adviser is the best for me?

There's no official ranking, so ask about their experience with IR35, their qualifications, and whether they'll look at both your contract and working practices. A good adviser will explain their reasoning clearly and answer your questions.

### Will a local IR35 adviser know my client's business?

Not necessarily, and that is not the main thing to check. Ask instead about their experience of IR35 questions in situations like yours and how they would review both your contract and your working practices.

### Can an adviser change my client's status determination?

No. For medium and large clients the client makes the decision. An adviser can assess whether it looks right, explain why, and help you raise a dispute with the client if you believe it is wrong.

### Is the contract on its own enough for an IR35 review?

Usually not. Working practices matter as much as the contract, so expect questions about how you actually carry out the work, who controls it and how the engagement operates in practice.

### Should I reply to HMRC before speaking to an adviser?

Note the deadline on the letter, but avoid sending a detailed reply before you have advice if the matter is complex. An adviser can often ask HMRC for more time where it is reasonable.
