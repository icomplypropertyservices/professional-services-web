## How to check you are in safe hands

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

"Lawyer" is a general term. In England and Wales the person handling your matter will usually be a solicitor, chartered legal executive or barrister, each regulated by their own body.

## One key thing to understand about debt recovery

In England and Wales, most debts are subject to a six-year limitation period, which generally runs from the date the money became due. If you leave it too long, you may lose the right to recover the debt through the courts, so it is worth taking advice early. Statutory interest can sometimes be added to business debts, but the rules are specific, and a solicitor can tell you whether it applies to your situation.

Before you sue, it is sensible to check whether the debtor has the means to pay. A judgment in your favour is only useful if the debtor can actually pay, and enforcement can add cost and delay. A solicitor can help you weigh up the likelihood of recovery against the expense of pursuing the claim.

## FAQs about debt recovery lawyers near me

### Can I recover a debt myself without a solicitor?

Yes, you can often pursue a debt through the county court without legal representation, especially for smaller amounts. However, a solicitor can advise on the strength of your case, the correct procedure, and enforcement, which may save you time and avoid mistakes.

### What is a letter before action and do I need one?

A letter before action is a formal demand for payment that sets out your intention to start court proceedings if the debt is not paid within a specified period. It is often required before issuing a claim, and a solicitor can draft one that complies with the relevant rules.

### How do I check if a solicitor is regulated?

In England and Wales, solicitors are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland, check with the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland with the Law Society of Northern Ireland.

### What if the debtor cannot pay?

If the debtor has no assets or income, recovering the money may be difficult or impossible, even with a judgment. A solicitor can help you assess the debtor's financial position and advise whether pursuing the claim is worthwhile.

### Should I choose a debt recovery lawyer who is close to me or close to the debtor?

For most debt recovery work the court process is handled in writing or online, so the debtor's location matters less than you might expect. A solicitor near you is convenient for meetings and for discussing documents, but do not rule out one further away if they clearly handle this kind of claim regularly.

### Can I speak to more than one debt recovery lawyer before deciding?

Yes, and it is sensible when you are comparing several. Give each the same outline of the debt, what you have already tried, and the documents you hold, then compare what they say about the route, the likely stages and how their charges work.

### What should I have ready for a first conversation with a debt recovery lawyer?

Have the invoice or agreement, any terms setting out payment dates, your reminders and the debtor's replies to hand. Details of when the money fell due help the solicitor check the limitation position and advise on the most proportionate next step.

### Can I get legal aid?

Legal aid is still available for some criminal, family (especially where there is domestic abuse), housing and immigration matters, subject to means and merits tests. A matched firm can tell you whether you are likely to qualify.
