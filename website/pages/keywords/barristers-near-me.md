## Comparing several barristers near you

Searching for barristers near me in the plural suggests you are weighing up more than one option rather than settling on the first name you find. That is sensible, because barristers specialise in different areas of law, and two chambers on the same street may have very different strengths. Location tells you who is practically reachable, but it says little about whether someone handles the kind of matter you are facing.

A useful way to compare is to ask each barrister the same set of questions and note the answers side by side. Ask what proportion of their work is in your area of law, whether they have appeared in the court or tribunal your case involves, and whether they accept direct instructions under the Public Access scheme. You can then check each name on the Barristers' Register to confirm their practising certificate and direct access authorisation before deciding.

Fees are quoted on application, so compare what each quote actually covers rather than the headline figure alone. One barrister may quote for a hearing only, while another includes preparation and a written advice, so ask for the scope in writing. Availability matters too, especially if a hearing date is fixed, and the clerk at each chambers can tell you who could take the work in time.

## Understanding how barristers work and how to choose one

Barristers usually work from chambers and are regulated by the Bar Standards Board in England and Wales; in Scotland, the equivalent professionals are advocates regulated through the Faculty of Advocates. You can check the Barristers' Register to confirm whether someone holds a current practising certificate and whether they are authorised to accept instructions directly from the public. Many barristers can be instructed through a solicitor in the traditional way, while those registered under the Public Access scheme can be instructed directly for suitable matters.

Fees are usually agreed for a defined piece of work, so it's important to clarify what is included. When searching for barristers near you, consider the barrister's specialism and experience in your area of law rather than just location, as the right expertise matters most.

## FAQs: a barrister

### Can I instruct a barrister directly without going through a solicitor?

Yes, if the barrister is authorised under the Public Access scheme, you can instruct them directly for suitable matters. You should check the Barristers' Register to confirm their authorisation, and the barrister will assess whether your case is appropriate for direct instruction.

### How do I check if a barrister is regulated?

Barristers in England and Wales are regulated by the Bar Standards Board.

### What does 'price on application' mean for barrister fees?

It means the barrister will provide a quote based on the specifics of your case, as fees vary depending on complexity and the work involved. You should ask for a clear breakdown of what the fee covers before instructing them.

### How do I choose the right barrister for my case?

Look for a barrister with expertise in your area of law and experience in the relevant court or tribunal. You can ask about their case history and check their details on the Barristers' Register, and iComply can introduce you to a suitable professional based on your needs.

### Should I contact several barristers near me at once?

It is reasonable to approach more than one so you can compare specialism, approach and quoted fees. Be clear with each about your timescales, and once you have instructed someone, let the others know so they are not holding time for you.

### Does a barrister near me cost more or less than one further away?

Distance alone does not determine fees, which are quoted on application and depend on the work involved. A barrister closer to your court may be more convenient for hearings, but the right specialism for your matter matters more than the postcode.

### How do clerks fit into choosing between barristers?

Clerks manage barristers' diaries and fees, and they are usually your first point of contact at chambers. They can tell you who is available, what a quote covers and how to send papers, so it is worth speaking to them directly.

### Can I go straight to a barrister without a solicitor?

Often yes, through the Public Access scheme, if the barrister is registered and the matter is suitable. Some cases still need a solicitor, and the barrister will tell you if so.
