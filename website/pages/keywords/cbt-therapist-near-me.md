## Checking counsellors are qualified and regulated

"Counsellor" and "psychotherapist" are not protected titles in the UK. To protect yourself, choose a practitioner on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS. Practitioner psychologists, who use titles such as clinical or counselling psychologist, must be registered with the HCPC.

Accredited registers require training, supervision, a code of ethics and a complaints procedure. A good therapist will explain their approach, confidentiality and its limits in the first session.

"Therapist" covers many approaches. Ask about training, which register they are on and the type of therapy they offer.

## FAQs: a CBT therapist

### What is the difference between a CBT therapist and a counsellor?

CBT therapists focus specifically on cognitive behavioural therapy, which is structured and goal-oriented, while counsellors may use a range of talking approaches. Many counsellors also train in CBT, so it's worth asking about their specific CBT training and accreditation.

### Do I need a referral from my GP to see a CBT therapist?

No, you can contact a private CBT therapist directly without a GP referral. However, if you're accessing CBT through the NHS, a referral may be needed. It's fine to see a private therapist while also speaking to your GP about your options.

### How do I check if a CBT therapist is properly qualified?

Look for accreditation with BABCP, which shows CBT training, and check that the therapist is on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS. You can also ask about their training, supervision and code of ethics.

### Can I see a CBT therapist near me if I have mobility difficulties?

Yes, many therapists offer ground-floor rooms or step-free access, but it's best to ask when you enquire. If travelling is difficult, online CBT sessions may be an option, so mention this to iComply when you get in touch.

### What is the difference between counselling and psychotherapy?

The terms overlap. Counselling often focuses on a specific problem over a shorter period, while psychotherapy may explore deeper patterns over a longer time. A good practitioner will explain their approach.
