## Choosing the best executor service for your situation

The word best means different things to different families. For one person it is clear fees explained up front; for another it is someone who will take the time to explain each step and keep the family informed. When you speak to a professional, ask how their fees are structured and what is and is not included, so you can compare like with like.

Applying for a grant of probate on someone else's behalf for a fee is a reserved legal activity, so the person doing it should be properly regulated. Solicitors are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority and chartered legal executives by CILEx, and both are covered by the Legal Ombudsman. Many specialists are also members of STEP or Solicitors for the Elderly. It is sensible to ask whether the person you speak to is regulated, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance, and how long they expect the estate to take. If the estate is in Scotland, make sure the adviser practises in the right jurisdiction, as succession and confirmation follow different rules there.

## How to check you are in safe hands

Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity in England and Wales, so some will writers are not regulated. Solicitors (SRA) and chartered legal executives (CILEx) are regulated, insured and covered by the Legal Ombudsman. Many specialists are also members of STEP (the Society of Trust and Estate Practitioners) or Solicitors for the Elderly. Applying for a grant of probate on someone else's behalf for a fee is a reserved activity.

When choosing a provider, ask whether they are regulated, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance and where your original will is stored. Scotland has its own rules on succession and confirmation, so make sure your adviser practises in the right jurisdiction.

## Best Executor Services Near Me: FAQs

### Can a solicitor act as executor instead of me?

Yes. A solicitor can be appointed as executor in the will, or can take on the role if the named executors decide not to act, subject to the terms of the will. Many families ask a professional to act so that the administration is handled by someone with experience of the process. The professional will explain what this involves and how their fees work before you decide.

### What does an executor actually have to do?

An executor collects in the assets of the estate, pays any debts and taxes, applies for the grant of probate where needed, and then distributes what remains to the beneficiaries named in the will. It also involves a good deal of correspondence and record keeping. A wills and probate specialist can either do this for you or guide you through each stage.

### Is the cheapest executor service the right choice?

Price matters, but the lowest quote is not always the best value, because what is included can vary a great deal between providers. Ask for a clear breakdown of fees and any expenses that will be added, and check what happens if the estate turns out to be more complicated than expected. Comparing on that basis tells you more than comparing headline figures alone.

### What if the family cannot agree on how to proceed?

Disagreements between executors or beneficiaries are not unusual, and a professional can often help by explaining the legal position clearly and keeping communication formal. If the dispute becomes serious, specialist advice becomes more important. It is worth raising any tension in the family when you first speak to a professional, so they know what they are taking on.

### Do I need a solicitor for probate?

Not always. Executors can apply for probate themselves for simpler estates. Many choose professional help where there is property, inheritance tax, overseas assets or family tension.
