## FAQs: an estate planning solicitor

### Do I need a solicitor to write a will?

You can write a will yourself, but a solicitor can help make sure it is clear and valid. Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity, so some will writers are not regulated, whereas solicitors are regulated and insured.

### What is the difference between a will and lasting powers of attorney?

A will sets out what happens to your assets after you die, while lasting powers of attorney let you appoint someone to make decisions about your health and money during your lifetime if you lose capacity. Estate planning solicitors can advise on both together.

### How can I check if an estate planning solicitor is regulated?

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and chartered legal executives by CILEx. You can ask the professional directly about their regulation and insurance, and check the relevant register.

### Does it matter where the solicitor is based?

For most estate planning work, the law that applies depends on where you live, not where the solicitor is based. If you live in Scotland, make sure your adviser practises in Scottish succession rules, as they differ from England and Wales.

### Should I bring anything to the first appointment?

Bring a list of what you own and owe, details of your family, and any existing will or lasting powers of attorney. If you have made significant gifts or own property abroad, note those down too, as they affect the advice you will be given.

### Will the solicitor decide everything at the first meeting?

No. The first appointment is usually for taking instructions and explaining your options, and you can take time to consider them. You should feel free to ask questions and to leave without committing until you are ready.

### How do I compare quotes for estate planning work?

Ask each professional what their quote covers, whether it includes lasting powers of attorney as well as the will, and how they charge for any extra work. Comparing the scope alongside the price helps you judge which option suits you, rather than simply choosing the lowest figure.

### What happens if I die without a will?

The intestacy rules decide who inherits, which may not reflect your wishes, especially for unmarried partners and stepchildren. A will lets you choose.
