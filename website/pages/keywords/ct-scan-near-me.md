## What to understand about getting a CT scan near you

A CT scan is a type of imaging that uses X-rays to create detailed pictures of the inside of your body. It must be clinically justified, meaning a qualified practitioner needs to refer you. Private clinics that provide CT scans are regulated, and in England they must be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and rates them. In Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland, different regulators apply. You can check a clinic's registration and rating online.

If you have private medical insurance, your insurer may require a GP referral and may only cover scans at certain hospitals or clinics. It's worth checking with your insurer before you book. Also, CT scans involve radiation, so the benefits should outweigh the risks; your referring practitioner will consider this. Sometimes a contrast dye is used to improve the images, and you may need to prepare for that.

## Checking private consultants are qualified and regulated

Hospital consultants should appear on the GMC's specialist register for their field, which you can check online. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA. Private hospitals in England may also share outcomes data through the Private Healthcare Information Network (PHIN).

Many private specialists also work in the NHS. If you have private medical insurance, your insurer may require a GP referral and may limit you to certain consultants or hospitals, so check before you book.

## CT Scan Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need a referral for a private CT scan?

Yes, a CT scan must be referred by a qualified practitioner, such as a GP or consultant. They will assess whether the scan is clinically justified. If you don't have a referral, iComply can introduce you to a private consultant who can assess you and refer you if appropriate.

### Can I get a CT scan on the NHS?

Yes, CT scans are available on the NHS when clinically indicated, but waiting times can vary. A private CT scan may be an option if you want to be seen sooner or at a location of your choice. You would need a referral from a qualified practitioner.

### Is a CT scan safe?

CT scans use X-rays, which involve radiation. The benefits are generally considered to outweigh the risks when the scan is clinically justified. Your referring practitioner will discuss this with you. If contrast dye is needed, they will also check for any allergies or kidney problems.

### How do I find a reputable private clinic for a CT scan near me?

In England, private clinics providing regulated healthcare must be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which publishes inspection ratings. You can check a clinic's registration and rating with the CQC. It's also worth asking about the consultant's specialist registration with the General Medical Council (GMC).

### Do I need a GP referral to see a private consultant?

Self-paying patients can sometimes book directly, but many consultants prefer a referral, and insurers usually require one. A private GP can provide one quickly.
