## Qualifications and registration to look for

Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity. It can be carried out by solicitors regulated by the SRA or by licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers (CLC). Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money. In Scotland, property transactions are handled by solicitors under Scottish law.

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme (CQS) is a common accreditation. Check that your chosen firm is on your lender's panel before you instruct them.

## What happens once you instruct a commercial conveyancer

Once you have chosen someone and told them about your deal, a commercial conveyancer usually starts by checking the title to the property and raising enquiries with the seller's side. They will also look at matters that rarely arise with a home purchase, such as whether VAT applies, what the lease says if there is one, and whether there are any restrictions on how the premises can be used.

You should expect to be asked for documents early on — the heads of terms if a lease is being negotiated, any planning or environmental information you hold, and details of your lender if you are borrowing. Because commercial due diligence is wider than residential, the enquiries stage often takes longer and raises more questions, so keep in touch with your conveyancer about anything that could affect your plans for the premises.

A useful first conversation is to ask how they will keep you informed and who your main point of contact will be. It also helps to ask what they need from you and by when, so you can gather paperwork without holding up the transaction. If a lease is involved, ask them to explain the repairing and insurance obligations clearly before you sign anything.

### Does it matter whether my conveyancer is a solicitor or a licensed conveyancer?

Both can carry out conveyancing in England and Wales — solicitors are regulated by the SRA and licensed conveyancers by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. Either way they must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money.

### Will a commercial conveyancer also advise me on the business side of the deal?

Conveyancers deal with the legal side of the property — the title, the lease and the due diligence. They do not usually advise on whether the deal makes commercial sense for your business, so you may want separate advice on that.

### Can I use the same conveyancer for a commercial purchase and a related lease?

Often the same firm can handle both parts if they are connected, but you should tell them about all the arrangements from the start. They will explain what they can and cannot act on for you, and quote you directly.

### What should I bring to a first meeting with a commercial conveyancer?

Bring whatever you have about the property — the heads of terms, any draft lease, planning information and your lender's details if you are borrowing. The more they can see early, the clearer their quote and advice will be.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a licensed conveyancer?

Both are regulated and insured. Licensed conveyancers specialise in property transactions, while solicitors can also advise on wider legal issues. Either can handle a standard purchase or sale.
