## Checking financial advisers are qualified and regulated

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications.

## Costs and quotes for a chartered financial planner

Advisers often charge for initial advice, then an ongoing fee for reviews, which may be fixed or a percentage of the money they look after. Firms must set out charges clearly before advising you. The firm quotes you, POA.

Ask for charges in pounds as well as percentages, and check what the ongoing service includes. The FCA's Consumer Duty expects firms to provide fair value.

## Chartered Financial Planner Near Me: FAQs

### What does 'chartered' mean for a financial planner?

Chartered financial planners hold advanced qualifications from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and chartered firms meet further professional standards. This means they have demonstrated a high level of knowledge and commitment to ethical practice.

### How do I check that a financial planner is properly authorised?

You can check the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register to confirm that the adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm. This gives you protection, including access to the Financial Ombudsman Service and potentially the Financial Services Compensation Scheme.

### Will a chartered financial planner near me charge for an initial meeting?

Charging structures vary, and advisers must tell you how they charge before you commit. Some may offer a free initial consultation, while others charge a fee, so it's best to ask upfront.

### What should I prepare before speaking to a financial planner?

It helps to gather details of your income, savings, investments, pensions, and any debts, along with your financial goals. Having this information ready will allow the planner to give you more focused advice.

### Is the first meeting free?

Many firms offer a free initial consultation to explain their service and charges. Formal advice is then charged as agreed in writing.
