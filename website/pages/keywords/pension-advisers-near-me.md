## What people searching for pension advisers near me usually need

Most people want clear, local help with a pension decision that feels complicated or urgent.

- You have several workplace pensions from different employers and want to know whether consolidating them makes sense for you.

- You're unsure how to take money from your pension tax-efficiently while keeping enough for later life.

## Red flags when choosing a pension adviser

Be cautious if you notice any of the following:

- Pressure to transfer quickly
- Overseas investments or unusual schemes
- Firms not on the FCA register
- Offers to access a pension before age 55 (rising to 57 from 2028)

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Some pension decisions carry special rules. Advice on transferring out of a defined benefit (final salary) scheme above a set value must come from an adviser with pension transfer specialist permissions, and safeguarded benefits deserve particular care. Free, impartial guidance is available from Pension Wise for people over 50 with defined contribution pensions.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Questions to ask the pension adviser

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority for the type of pension advice I need?
- Do you have pension transfer specialist permissions if I'm considering transferring a defined benefit scheme?
- How will you be paid, and what will the total cost be for the advice you provide?
- Will you look at my existing pensions and explain the charges I'm currently paying?
- What happens if I change my mind or if the advice turns out to be unsuitable?
- Can you explain how taking money from my pension will affect my tax and any state benefits?

## FAQs about pension advisers near me

### Do I need a pension adviser who is local to me?

Not necessarily. Many advisers work remotely by phone or video, but some people prefer to meet in person. When you search for pension advisers near me, you can ask iComply to introduce you to an adviser who offers the type of contact you prefer.

### What is Pension Wise and do I need to use it?

Pension Wise is a free and impartial government service that gives guidance to people over the age of fifty with defined contribution pensions. It is not regulated advice, but it can help you understand your options before you speak to an adviser.

### How do I check that a pension adviser is properly authorised?

You can check the Financial Conduct Authority's register to see if an adviser or firm is authorised or is an appointed representative. This is a key step before you agree to any advice.

### What should I do if I have a problem with a pension adviser?

First, complain to the firm directly. If you're not satisfied, you can take your complaint to the Financial Ombudsman Service. The Financial Services Compensation Scheme may also help if an authorised firm fails.

### Is it better to speak to several pension advisers before deciding?

It can be, because comparing how advisers explain things, what they charge and what they specialise in helps you judge who suits you. You are not obliged to go with the first one you speak to, and quotes are on application so you can weigh them up.

### How can I tell whether an adviser is right for my pension decision?

Ask about their experience with your particular type of pension, such as a defined benefit scheme or an SIPP, and check their entry on the Financial Conduct Authority register. A clear explanation of fees and of what they will and will not do is a good sign.

### Do all pension advisers near me cover the same work?

No. Some focus on retirement income and annuities, others on transfers or on reviewing existing pots, and only those with pension transfer specialist permissions can advise on transferring a defined benefit scheme above a set value. ### Should I combine my pensions?

Sometimes it makes life simpler and cheaper, but some older pensions have valuable guarantees or low charges. An adviser checks before recommending a transfer.
