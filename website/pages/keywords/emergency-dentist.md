## Checking private dentists are qualified and regulated

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Specialist titles such as orthodontist or endodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process. The Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

## Typical timescales

Many private practices can see new patients within days, and urgent pain can often be seen the same or next day. Longer courses of treatment such as implants or aligners take months. For swelling that affects breathing or swallowing, seek emergency care at once.

## What to expect at an emergency dental appointment

An emergency appointment is usually about settling the immediate problem rather than completing a full course of treatment. The dentist will ask what happened and when, look at the painful or damaged tooth, and may take a X-ray to see what is going on beneath the surface. From there they can explain what needs doing to get you comfortable.

You may find the dentist offers to stabilise the tooth there and then, for example by removing a loose fragment, re-cementing a crown or draining an abscess, and then asks you to come back for the permanent repair. Sometimes the emergency visit is mainly about diagnosis and pain relief, with definitive treatment planned for a later appointment. It helps to ask what is being done today and what is being left for next time.

Because emergency appointments are often shorter than routine ones, it is worth arriving with a clear idea of your symptoms and any medication you take. If you are anxious about dental treatment, say so when you phone, as the practice can advise on how the appointment will run. The aim is to deal with the urgent problem safely and leave you knowing the next step.

## Emergency Dentist: FAQs

### Can I get an emergency dentist appointment on a weekend?

Some private dental practices offer emergency appointments at weekends, but availability varies. Phone first to check, as practices often have arrangements for out-of-hours care.

### What should I do if I have severe facial swelling?

If you have facial swelling that affects your breathing or swallowing, you should go to A&E immediately. For other swelling, contact NHS 111 or an emergency dentist for advice.

### Will an emergency dentist see me if I'm not registered with them?

Many private emergency dentists will see new patients for urgent problems, but you should phone first to confirm. They may ask for details about your symptoms to prioritise your appointment.

### How can I check if a dentist is properly registered?

You can check the General Dental Council (GDC) register online to confirm a dentist is registered. In England, private clinics providing regulated healthcare must also be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which publishes inspection ratings.

### Is an emergency appointment the same as a full dental examination?

Not usually. An emergency appointment focuses on the urgent problem, such as pain, swelling or a damaged tooth, and may be shorter than a routine check-up. The dentist will tell you if a fuller examination is needed once the immediate issue is settled.

### Can an emergency dentist help if the problem turns out to need specialist care?

Yes. The dentist can assess what is happening and explain whether your problem needs a specialist, such as an oral surgeon or endodontist. Specialist titles can only be used by people on a General Dental Council specialist list, so you can check the register if a referral is suggested.

### What if I am not sure whether my problem counts as an emergency?

If you are in severe pain, have swelling or have damaged a tooth, it is reasonable to phone a practice and describe what has happened. They can tell you whether you need an urgent appointment or whether it can wait. If your breathing or swallowing is affected by swelling, go to A&E instead.

### Do I need a check-up before treatment?

Almost always. A dentist needs to examine you, and often take X-rays, before recommending treatment.
