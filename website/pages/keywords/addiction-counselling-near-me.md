## Understanding who you are choosing

"Counsellor" and "psychotherapist" are not protected titles in the UK, so anyone can use them. To protect yourself, look for a practitioner on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS. Practitioner psychologists, who use titles such as clinical or counselling psychologist, must be registered with the HCPC.

Accredited registers require training, supervision, a code of ethics and a complaints procedure. A good therapist will explain their approach, confidentiality and its limits in the first session, so you know where you stand before you commit.

## FAQs: addiction counselling

### Do I need to be sure I have an addiction before I enquire?

No. Many people get in touch when they are worried about their drinking, drug use or gambling but are not sure how serious it is. A counsellor can talk it through with you and help you work out what kind of support makes sense.

### Can counselling help if I also need medical support?

Counselling can sit alongside medical help. For alcohol in particular, a medical detox may be needed, and a counsellor should be able to discuss this with you and encourage you to seek appropriate medical advice. Groups such as AA can also complement therapy.

### What if the person who needs help is a family member, not me?

You can still enquire on their behalf and ask about family support, which often helps. The counsellor can explain what is possible and whether the person themselves would need to be involved.

### How do I know a counsellor is properly trained?

Because "counsellor" is not a protected title, check whether the practitioner is on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS. Accredited registers require training, supervision, a code of ethics and a complaints procedure, so it is worth asking before you begin.

### Can I get therapy on the NHS?

Yes. In England you can refer yourself to NHS Talking Therapies. Waiting times vary by area, which is why some people choose private therapy.
