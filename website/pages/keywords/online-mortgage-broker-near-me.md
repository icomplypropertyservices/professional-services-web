## Checking mortgage brokers are qualified and regulated

Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and you can check this on the FCA register. Advisers typically hold a qualification such as CeMAP.

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders, and how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

A broker works on your behalf to find suitable products from providers. Ask how many providers they compare and how they are paid.

## FAQs: a mortgage broker

### Can I get a mortgage entirely online?

Many brokers now offer online services, including video calls and secure document upload, so you can complete the process remotely. However, some lenders may still require certain documents to be signed in person or checked by a professional. The broker will explain what is needed.

### How do I check if an online mortgage broker is authorised?

You can check the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register to see if a broker is authorised or an appointed representative of an authorised firm. The broker should also tell you their FCA registration number. You can also ask about their qualifications, such as CeMAP.

### What fees should I expect from an online mortgage broker?

Fees vary, and brokers must disclose their charges upfront. They may charge a flat fee, a percentage of the loan amount, or receive commission from the lender. You should always ask for a written quote and understand what is included.

### How long does it take to get a mortgage offer through an online broker?

The time can vary depending on the lender and the complexity of your case, but it often takes several weeks from application to offer. Your broker can give you an idea based on your circumstances, but they cannot promise a timeframe.

### If the broker is online, does it matter where they are based?

It can. A broker familiar with your area will understand local property values and the kinds of homes you're considering, which helps when checking affordability. Ask early on whether they regularly work with buyers in your part of the UK.

### How can I send my documents safely to an online broker?

Ask them to explain how they handle personal and financial information before you send anything. A firm authorised by the FCA must take care with your data, and they should be able to tell you clearly which method they prefer and why.

### Can an online broker still help if my situation is complicated?

Possibly, but say so at the start. Self-employment, a credit blip or an unusual property can narrow which lenders will consider you, and a broker should tell you honestly whether your case is one they handle or whether you'd be better served elsewhere.

### Is a broker better than going to my bank?

A broker can compare many lenders and may find options your bank does not offer. Your bank can only offer its own products. Either can suit you; a broker gives you a wider view.

### Can everything be done online?

Often much of it can. Some steps may need a visit, depending on the service. The practice will tell you what can be done remotely.
