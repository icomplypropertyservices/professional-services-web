## Checking immigration advisers are qualified and regulated

It is a criminal offence to give immigration advice in the UK unless you are qualified and regulated. Advisers must be registered with the Immigration Advice Authority (IAA, formerly the OISC) at the right level for your matter, or be a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive authorised by their own regulator. Check the IAA's adviser finder or the SRA register before you share documents or pay.

IAA advisers are registered at levels that limit what they can do, from basic applications to appeals and representation. Ask what level an adviser holds and whether it covers your type of case.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Visa Advisor Near Me: FAQs

### Can anyone call themselves a visa advisor and charge for help?

No. Giving immigration advice in the UK is regulated, and it is a criminal offence to do so unless you are qualified and registered with the Immigration Advice Authority or authorised by your own regulator as a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive. Always check the register before sharing documents or paying.

### How do I check an adviser is registered?

Use the Immigration Advice Authority's adviser finder, or the Solicitors Regulation Authority register if they are a solicitor. Check that their registration level covers your type of case, not just that they appear on the list.

### Do I need to see an adviser in person?

Not necessarily, but many people prefer it for document-heavy applications. If you search for a visa advisor near me, tell iComply how far you are willing to travel so the introduction suits you.

### What should I have ready before I speak to an adviser?

Gather your passport, any previous applications or refusals, and correspondence from the Home Office. Having these to hand helps the adviser understand your situation and give useful guidance from the first conversation.

### Should I choose the adviser closest to me, or the one who knows my visa type best?

The right visa experience matters more than distance, though a nearby adviser can make document handling and meetings easier. Use location as one factor among several, and ask each adviser directly whether your type of application is regular work for them.

### Can I get help from a visa adviser if I have already applied?

Yes, advisers can often help with an application that is already underway, including responding to requests from the Home Office. Tell them exactly where things stand and share any correspondence you have received, so they can see what is needed next.

### What if I am not sure which visa route I need?

That is a common reason to speak to an adviser, and choosing the right route is usually the first step. Describe your circumstances rather than a visa name, and a regulated adviser can explain which routes may fit and what evidence each one requires.

### Can an adviser speed up my application?

An adviser cannot control Home Office processing times, but a complete, well-prepared application avoids avoidable delays. Some routes have paid priority services.
