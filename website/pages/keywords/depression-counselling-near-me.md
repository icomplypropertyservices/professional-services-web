## Questions to ask the counsellor

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the counsellor is right for you:

- What is your approach to working with depression, and do you use behavioural activation?
- Are you on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS?
- How do you handle confidentiality, and what are its limits?
- How many sessions might I need, and how will we check progress together?
- Do you work alongside GP care, and how do you coordinate with my doctor?
- What should I do if I feel unsafe between sessions?

## How to check you are in safe hands

"Counsellor" and "psychotherapist" are not protected titles in the UK. To protect yourself, choose a practitioner on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS. Practitioner psychologists, who use titles such as clinical or counselling psychologist, must be registered with the HCPC.

Accredited registers require training, supervision, a code of ethics and a complaints procedure. A good therapist will explain their approach, confidentiality and its limits in the first session.

iComply is not a regulator and does not provide the professional service itself. We introduce you to professionals who hold their own registrations, and we encourage you to check them on the public register.

## Before you choose depression counselling

A few points come up again and again with depression counselling:

- Talk to your GP about medication options.

Raise any of these with the counsellor you are introduced to; they should be happy to explain.

## Choosing between local counselling options for depression

Searching for depression counselling near me often means you want someone you could travel to easily, perhaps because leaving the house feels hard at the moment. It's worth thinking about what 'near' means for you: a short walk, a bus ride, or somewhere you can reach without too much planning on a low day. If getting out is difficult, ask each counsellor whether they also offer sessions by phone or online, so distance doesn't become the reason you put it off.

When you compare counsellors for depression, ask how they work with low mood specifically, rather than assuming all counselling is the same. Some use behavioural activation, which focuses on gentle, planned activity to lift mood, while others take a more exploratory approach; neither is automatically right for you. A brief phone call before booking can tell you whether their way of working matches what you're hoping for.

It can help to ask how they would work alongside your GP, especially if you're already receiving care for depression. A counsellor should be comfortable talking about this and clear about what they can and cannot offer. If you ever feel unsafe or in crisis, contact NHS 111 or 999, or your GP surgery, rather than waiting for a counselling appointment.

## Red flags when choosing depression counselling

Be cautious if you notice any of the following:

## FAQs: depression counselling

### What is depression counselling and how can it help?

It gives you a regular space to talk through what you're experiencing with a trained professional.

### How do I find a qualified counsellor near me?

Look for a counsellor on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority, such as BACP, UKCP or NCPS. These registers require training, supervision, a code of ethics and a complaints procedure.

### Should I see my GP before starting counselling?

It's a good idea to talk to your GP about how you're feeling, especially if you're considering medication or have physical symptoms. They can also tell you about local NHS support.

### What if I feel unsafe or in crisis?

If you feel unsafe or are in crisis, seek urgent help. You can call NHS 111 or 999, or contact your GP surgery for urgent advice.

### How do I choose between counsellors who all seem similar?

Ask each one how they work with depression, whether they use approaches such as behavioural activation, and how they handle confidentiality. A short introductory call can help you sense whether you'd feel comfortable talking to them, and you are free to choose another if it doesn't feel right.

### Can I have counselling for depression if I'm already seeing my GP?

Yes, many people have counselling alongside GP care, and it's worth telling both about each other. Your GP can discuss medication options with you, and a counsellor can explain how their sessions fit around that.

### What if I can't face travelling to appointments?

Tell us when you enquire whether you'd prefer sessions by phone or online, as well as in person. Being clear about what you can manage on a difficult day helps us introduce you to a counsellor whose arrangements suit you.

### Can I get therapy on the NHS?

Yes. In England you can refer yourself to NHS Talking Therapies. Waiting times vary by area, which is why some people choose private therapy.
