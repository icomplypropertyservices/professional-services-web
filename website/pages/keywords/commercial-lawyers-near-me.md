## Checking who you are dealing with

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so it is worth confirming that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive or another authorised professional. Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register; in Scotland the Law Society of Scotland regulates solicitors, and in Northern Ireland the Law Society of Northern Ireland.

Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure. If a service complaint is not resolved that way, the Legal Ombudsman can look at it in England and Wales. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation can only be carried out by authorised people, so ask early who will actually handle your work.

## Comparing several commercial solicitors before you choose

Searching for commercial lawyers near me, in the plural, suggests you are weighing up more than one option rather than looking for a single name. That is sensible for business work, because two firms can quote very differently for the same piece of contract drafting or review. Ask each one what they would actually do on your matter, so you are comparing like with like.

A useful way to judge is to ask who would handle your file day to day and whether that person is a solicitor or another authorised professional. Then ask how they would approach your particular agreement, whether that is a distribution deal, licensing terms or a supplier contract that has gone wrong. If they cannot explain their approach plainly, that tells you something.

Bear in mind that a commercial solicitor is not the same as a general practice solicitor who occasionally sees business clients. Ask how much of their work is commercial contracts and trading relationships, and whether they have dealt with your kind of agreement before. The first conversation is also your chance to see how quickly they respond and whether they listen to what your business actually does.

## Costs and quotes for a commercial solicitor

Legal fees depend on the type of matter, its complexity and who does the work. Firms commonly charge hourly rates, fixed fees for defined stages, or (in some claims) conditional fee arrangements. Regulated firms in England and Wales must publish pricing information for certain services and give you clear costs information at the outset. The firm will give you a POA quote.

Ask for a written estimate and for an explanation of what is not included, such as court fees, barristers' fees or experts. Ask too whether legal expenses insurance or legal aid might cover some or all of the cost.

## Commercial Lawyers Near Me: FAQs

### How do I judge which commercial solicitor is right for my business?

Look at whether they regularly deal with the kind of agreement or disagreement you have, ask who will handle the work, and compare how clearly they explain their charges. A short conversation usually tells you whether you can work together.

### Can iComply tell me what my contract means?

No. iComply is an introduction service and does not give professional advice or provide the service itself. The solicitor you are introduced to will advise you directly and quote you for the work.

### Why does it help to find a solicitor near me?

Commercial matters often involve documents and conversations that are easier face to face, and a local firm may know the business landscape around you. That said, many matters can be handled remotely, so say what suits you.

### What should I have ready before I speak to a solicitor?

Gather the draft or signed agreement, any relevant correspondence and a clear summary of what you want to achieve. Having these to hand helps the solicitor quote accurately and get to the point faster.

### Should I contact several commercial solicitors at once?

It is reasonable to speak to more than one before committing, especially for a contract review or a negotiation where approach matters as much as price. Just be clear with each about what you need so their quotes are comparable.

### Is a commercial solicitor the same as a business solicitor?

The terms are often used loosely, but what matters is whether the person regularly handles contracts such as supply, distribution, licensing and outsourcing agreements, or commercial disputes. Ask about their day-to-day work rather than relying on the label.

### What if my business is small and the matter feels minor?

Smaller businesses still sign supply and licensing terms that can carry real obligations, so it is worth asking a solicitor whether a short review is enough. Explain your size and budget openly and let them tell you what level of help fits.

### Can I get legal aid?

Legal aid is still available for some criminal, family (especially where there is domestic abuse), housing and immigration matters, subject to means and merits tests. A matched firm can tell you whether you are likely to qualify.
