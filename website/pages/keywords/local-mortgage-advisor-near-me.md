## FAQs: a mortgage adviser

### How do I check that a mortgage advisor is properly authorised?

You can check the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register to see if a broker or adviser is authorised or an appointed representative of an authorised firm. The FCA regulates mortgage advice, so it's important to verify their status before proceeding.

### Will a local mortgage advisor come to my home?

Many mortgage advisors offer home visits, but this varies. When you speak to the advisor, ask whether they can meet you at home or if they work remotely by phone or video call.

### What is the difference between a mortgage advisor and a mortgage broker?

The terms are often used interchangeably. A mortgage advisor recommends suitable mortgages, while a broker may also handle the application. Both should be authorised by the FCA and explain whether they search the whole market or a limited panel.

### Do I need to pay a fee for mortgage advice?

Some advisors charge a fee, while others are paid by lender commission, or a combination of both. Brokers must tell you how they are paid before you commit, so ask for a clear explanation of any fees.

### Is a local mortgage advisor better than one I find online?

Not necessarily. What matters is whether the adviser is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority, searches a suitable range of lenders for your circumstances and explains their fees clearly. Being local can make meetings easier, but it isn't a substitute for checking those points.

### Can I use a mortgage advisor outside my area?

Yes. Many advisers work with clients across the UK by phone, email and video call, so distance needn't rule someone out. If meeting in person matters to you, ask upfront whether they can offer that.

### Will speaking to a broker affect my credit score?

An initial conversation does not. Formal applications usually involve a credit search, and the broker should tell you before that happens.
