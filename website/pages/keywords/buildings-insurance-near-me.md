## Understanding rebuild cost and subsidence history

Buildings insurance should be based on the rebuild cost of your property, not its market value. The rebuild cost is what it would take to rebuild your home from scratch, including materials and labour, and it can be very different from the price you paid for the property.

If your property is in an area prone to subsidence, or has had subsidence in the past, it's important to check whether the policy covers it. A broker can help you understand the exclusions and conditions around subsidence, and what you need to disclose when applying.

## Buildings Insurance Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need buildings insurance if I own a flat?

If you own a flat, the building itself is often covered by a communal policy arranged by the freeholder or management company. However, you may still need contents insurance, and it's worth checking what the communal policy covers.

### What is the difference between buildings insurance and contents insurance?

Buildings insurance covers the structure of your property, such as the walls, roof and fixtures. Contents insurance covers the items inside your home, like furniture and electronics.

### How do I check if an insurance broker is authorised?

You can check whether a broker is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) on the FCA register. Many brokers also belong to the British Insurance Brokers' Association (BIBA).

### What can I do if I'm unhappy with the service from my broker?

If you have a complaint that the broker cannot resolve, you can take it to the Financial Ombudsman Service. They provide a free and independent service for resolving disputes.

### Why does my broker need to know about subsidence history?

Subsidence is a common exclusion or condition in buildings policies, and insurers will want to know whether the property has suffered movement before. Telling your broker upfront helps them approach the right insurers and explain what will and will not be covered.

### Can I arrange buildings insurance through a broker if my lender has strict requirements?

Yes. Lenders require buildings cover, and a broker can check that a proposed policy meets your lender's conditions before you commit. Tell your broker what your lender has asked for so they can match the cover accordingly.

### Is a broker more expensive than buying direct?

Not necessarily. Brokers can access insurers and schemes not available directly, and help with claims. The right choice depends on how complex your needs are.
