## Qualifications and registration to look for

Many surveyors are members of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), and RICS-regulated firms must follow professional standards, hold insurance and offer a complaints process. Chartered surveyors use the letters MRICS or FRICS. Some specialist roles have their own bodies, such as the Faculty of Party Wall Surveyors or the Property Care Association for damp and timber specialists.

Check the RICS directory, ask which report type you are getting and confirm whether the surveyor is independent of the sale. For asbestos work, look for organisations accredited by UKAS for surveying.

## Homebuyer Surveyor Near Me: FAQs

### What is a HomeBuyer survey and who is it for?

A HomeBuyer survey, also known as a Level Two survey, is a report on the condition of a conventional home in reasonable condition. It highlights urgent issues and often includes a valuation. It's suitable for most modern homes and older properties that haven't been extensively altered.

### How do I find a homebuyer surveyor near me?

You can search the RICS directory for surveyors in your area, or tell iComply what you need and we'll introduce you to a suitable local surveyor. Always check that the surveyor is independent of the sale and ask which report type you'll receive.

### Does a HomeBuyer survey include a valuation?

Many HomeBuyer surveys include a valuation, but not all do. You should confirm with the surveyor whether a valuation is included in the quote, especially if you need it for your mortgage lender.

### What should I do if the survey finds problems?

If the survey flags issues, discuss them with the surveyor to understand their severity. You may need to get further specialist reports or renegotiate the price before you exchange contracts. Always follow up on concerns before you commit to the purchase.

### Which survey do I need when buying a home?

For a conventional property in reasonable condition, an RICS Level 2 survey is often enough. Older, larger or altered properties often justify a Level 3 building survey. A surveyor can recommend the right level.
