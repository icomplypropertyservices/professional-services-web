## Checking solicitors are qualified and regulated

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

iComply is not a regulator and does not provide the professional service itself. We introduce you to professionals who hold their own registrations, and we encourage you to check them on the public register.

## FAQs about child arrangements solicitor near me

### Do I have to go to mediation before I can apply to court about child arrangements?

In most cases, you'll need to attend a mediation information and assessment meeting (MIAM) before making a court application, unless an exemption applies. The meeting is a chance to find out whether mediation could help you reach an agreement without going to court.

### How can I check that a solicitor is properly regulated?

In England and Wales, you can check a solicitor or firm on the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA) register. In Scotland, check with the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland with the Law Society of Northern Ireland. This helps you confirm they are authorised to carry out reserved legal work.

### What does the court consider when deciding child arrangements?

The court's main concern is the welfare of the child. It will look at factors such as the child's wishes and feelings, their physical and emotional needs, and the ability of each parent to meet those needs. A solicitor can explain how these factors might apply in your case.

### Can a solicitor help me communicate better with my ex-partner about the children?

Yes, a solicitor can help you set up clear, child-focused communication and may suggest ways to reduce conflict, such as using a parenting plan or mediation. They can also correspond with the other parent on your behalf if that helps.

### Does it matter if the child arrangements solicitor is not on my doorstep?

Not necessarily. Many child arrangements matters are handled largely by phone, email and video, and court papers can be dealt with remotely. What matters most is that the solicitor understands your situation and that you can communicate with them comfortably.

### What should I expect at a first appointment about child arrangements?

The solicitor will usually ask about your children, where they live now, what you and the other parent have already tried, and whether there are any safety concerns. They should then explain your options, including mediation and the court process, and set out how their fees work. It helps to bring any relevant letters or court forms with you.

### Can I speak to more than one solicitor before deciding?

Yes, and it can be sensible to do so, since fees and approach vary between firms. We can introduce you to a suitable solicitor and they will quote you directly, so you can weigh up the options before committing to anyone.

### Can I get legal aid?

Legal aid is still available for some criminal, family (especially where there is domestic abuse), housing and immigration matters, subject to means and merits tests. A matched firm can tell you whether you are likely to qualify.
