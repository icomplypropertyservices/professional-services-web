## Checking solicitors are qualified and regulated

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

iComply is not a regulator and does not provide the professional service itself. We introduce you to professionals who hold their own registrations, and we encourage you to check them on the public register.

## What a business solicitor is likely to cost (POA)

Legal fees depend on the type of matter, its complexity and who does the work. Firms commonly charge hourly rates, fixed fees for defined stages, or (in some claims) conditional fee arrangements. Regulated firms in England and Wales must publish pricing information for certain services and give you clear costs information at the outset. The firm will give you a POA quote.

## FAQs: a business solicitor

### Do I need a solicitor who is local to my business?

Not necessarily. Many business matters can be handled by phone, email and video call, but meeting in person can help with complex documents or disputes. If you searched for a solicitor near you, tell us how far you are willing to travel and we can introduce you to someone suitable.

### Can a business solicitor help with a shareholder agreement?

Yes. Business solicitors commonly draft and prepare shareholder agreements, which set out how a company is run and what happens if shareholders disagree or one wants to leave. Getting this right early can prevent costly disputes later.

### How do I check that a solicitor is properly regulated?

In England and Wales you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland, check with the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland with the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must also hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure.

### What should I have ready before I speak to a business solicitor?

Gather any relevant contracts, correspondence, company documents and a clear summary of what you need help with. Knowing your key dates and what outcome you want helps the solicitor quote accurately. You can include these details in your enquiry to us.

### Should I choose a business solicitor in my own town?

It depends on the matter. Company formation, contract drafting and employment documents are usually handled comfortably by phone and email, while a lease, a dispute or a sale of the business may benefit from meeting in person. Tell us how far you are willing to travel and we can take that into account when introducing you to a solicitor.

### What is the difference between a business solicitor and a general practice solicitor?

A general practice solicitor may take on a wide range of personal and commercial work, whereas a business solicitor concentrates on companies, contracts, disputes, employment and commercial property. Ask what proportion of their work is commercial, and whether they have dealt with an issue like yours before, so you can judge the fit for yourself.

### Will the first conversation cost me anything?

That varies between firms, so ask before you book. Some solicitors offer a short initial conversation at no charge, while others charge for their time from the outset. Whatever the answer, get it in writing along with an estimate of the likely cost of the whole matter before you instruct anyone.

### Can I get legal aid?

Legal aid is still available for some criminal, family (especially where there is domestic abuse), housing and immigration matters, subject to means and merits tests. A matched firm can tell you whether you are likely to qualify.
