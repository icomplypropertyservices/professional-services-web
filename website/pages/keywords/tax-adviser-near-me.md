## Checking tax advisers are qualified and regulated

"Tax adviser" is not a protected title in the UK. The Chartered Institute of Taxation (CTA qualification) and the Association of Taxation Technicians (ATT) are the main specialist bodies, and many accountants and solicitors also advise on tax. Members of these bodies follow Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation, a shared code covering honesty, confidentiality and dealing with HMRC.

Tax advisers who provide services for a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes. HMRC has also been tightening standards for paid tax agents, so it is reasonable to ask about qualifications, supervision and professional indemnity insurance before you go ahead.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## FAQs: a tax adviser

### Do I need a tax adviser near me, or can I use one further away?

Many tax matters can be handled remotely by phone, email or video, so location may not be critical. However, if you prefer face-to-face meetings or have a complex local issue, a nearby adviser could be more convenient. We can introduce you to a suitable professional based on your preferences.

### How do I check if a tax adviser is qualified?

You can ask about their qualifications and professional memberships, such as CTA or ATT, and check the relevant body's published register to confirm they are a member. It is also reasonable to ask about their experience and whether they hold professional indemnity insurance.

### What information should I have ready before I speak to a tax adviser?

Gather any relevant documents, such as recent tax returns, correspondence from HMRC, and details of the income, expenses or transactions involved. Being clear about your situation and what you want to achieve will help the adviser give you useful advice.

### Can a tax adviser help if I have already made a mistake on my tax return?

Yes, a tax adviser can help you correct errors and communicate with HMRC about them. It is best to seek advice as soon as you realise there may be a problem, as early action can often simplify matters.

### Should I choose a tax adviser who is closest to me?

Proximity is convenient but says nothing about suitability for your particular tax issue. It is worth comparing what each nearby adviser actually deals with day to day, and whether they are happy to meet in person or work remotely.

### Is a local accountant the same as a tax adviser?

Not necessarily. Many accountants advise on tax, and some go on to specialise, but tax adviser is not a protected title, so the label alone tells you little. Ask about their qualifications and the kind of tax work they handle most often.

### What should I bring to a first meeting with a tax adviser near me?

Bring anything that explains your position, such as recent returns, letters from HMRC and details of the income, expenses or transaction involved. Being clear about what you want to achieve helps the adviser tell you whether they can help.

### Is tax planning legal?

Using reliefs and allowances as Parliament intended is normal and legal. Aggressive avoidance schemes are a different matter and can carry serious risks. A qualified adviser will explain the difference clearly.
