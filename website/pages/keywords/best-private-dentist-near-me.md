## Qualifications and registration to look for

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Specialist titles such as orthodontist or endodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process. The Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

## Narrowing down the right practice on your doorstep

Searching for the best private dentist near me usually means you want someone you can actually get to — on foot, by bus, or with easy parking — rather than a practice that looks good on paper but is awkward to reach for a check-up or an urgent visit. Before you compare anything else, it helps to draw a small circle around your home or workplace and look only at practices inside it, then weigh the rest from there.

Two practices side by side can feel very different once you walk in. It's worth asking each one how they handle nervous patients, whether they see children, what their arrangements are for out-of-hours problems, and whether the same dentist will see you each time. A practice that offers longer appointments and explains each stage slowly may suit you far better than one that simply offers more treatments.

Remember that 'best' for a friend or a neighbour may not be best for you, because it depends on your teeth, your budget and how you like to be treated. Reading a written treatment plan from more than one practice, and comparing what is included rather than just the headline figure, is the most reliable way to judge for yourself. ## FAQs about best private dentist near me

### How do I check if a private dentist is properly qualified?

You can check the General Dental Council (GDC) register online to confirm that the dentist and other dental professionals are registered. If the practice is in England, you can also check whether it is registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC).

### What should I do if I'm unhappy with private dental treatment?

Start by raising your concern with the practice's complaints process. If it isn't resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### Will I get a treatment plan and costs upfront?

Private dentists should provide a written treatment plan with itemised costs before you agree to treatment. It's always worth asking for this so you know exactly what you'll be paying.

### Should I choose a practice that is close to home or close to work?

It depends on when you're most likely to need appointments. If you'd rather visit on the way to or from work, a practice near your workplace may suit you better, but check that its opening hours fit around your working day.

### Is it worth visiting a practice before booking treatment?

Many practices are happy for you to look round or speak to the reception team before you commit to anything. It's a good way to get a feel for the place and ask about appointment lengths, out-of-hours arrangements and how treatment plans are set out.

### What if I need help urgently and can't get an appointment straight away?

Contact the practices near you and explain that you're in pain or have a problem that can't wait, as many keep space for urgent cases. If you're struggling to get help and feel unwell, NHS 111 can advise you on what to do next.

### Can I see a private dentist and stay with my NHS dentist?

Yes, you can use both. Some patients see a private dentist for specific treatments and keep NHS care for routine check-ups, if their NHS practice agrees.
