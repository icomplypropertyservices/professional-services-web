## Check regulation before you share anything

In the UK, it is a criminal offence to give immigration advice unless you are qualified and regulated. Advisers must be registered with the Immigration Advice Authority (IAA, formerly the OISC) at the right level for your matter, or be a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive authorised by their own regulator. The title 'consultant' is not protected, so always check the IAA's adviser finder or the SRA register before you share documents or pay.

IAA advisers are registered at different levels that limit what they can do, from basic applications to appeals and representation. Ask what level an adviser holds and whether it covers your type of case. Also ask for fees in writing so you know what you will be charged before you commit.

## FAQs: an immigration consultant

### Is it legal for just anyone to call themselves an immigration consultant?

Yes, the title 'consultant' is not protected, so anyone can use it. However, it is a criminal offence to give immigration advice unless they are regulated by the Immigration Advice Authority or authorised by a professional regulator such as the SRA.

### How can I check that an adviser is properly regulated?

You can check the Immigration Advice Authority's adviser finder or the SRA register online. Always do this before you share personal documents or pay any money.

### What does 'regulated at the right level' mean?

IAA advisers are registered at different levels that limit what they can do, from basic applications to appeals and representation. Ask what level an adviser holds and whether it covers your type of case.

### Do employers need a different kind of adviser?

Employers often need advice on sponsor licences and compliance duties, which may require a different level of registration or a solicitor. Tell iComply that you are an employer so we can introduce you to someone suitable.

### Does an adviser have to be close by to help me?

No. Immigration advice can be given remotely, and many people deal with an adviser by phone, email or video. If you specifically want to attend in person, check where meetings actually take place before you book.

### What should I bring to a first meeting with an immigration adviser?

Bring any letters from the Home Office, your passport or other identity documents, and anything relevant to your application or sponsor licence. Ask the adviser beforehand what they want to see, so you do not share more than necessary at the first meeting.

### Can I speak to more than one adviser before deciding?

Yes. It is reasonable to contact a few and ask about their registration, the level they hold and their fees in writing. That way you can compare how they respond before you commit to anyone.

### Can an adviser speed up my application?

An adviser cannot control Home Office processing times, but a complete, well-prepared application avoids avoidable delays. Some routes have paid priority services.
