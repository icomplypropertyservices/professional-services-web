## One key thing to understand about urgent eye care

In many areas of the UK, community optometrists provide urgent eye care services for problems like red eyes, sudden vision changes, flashes, floaters and minor eye injuries. These services can assess your condition quickly and either treat you or refer you on if needed.

However, some eye problems need immediate hospital care. If you have a chemical injury to your eye, rinse it immediately with water and go to A&E. If you have sudden vision loss, you should seek same-day care from an optician or hospital eye service.

## Checking opticians are qualified and regulated

Optometrists and dispensing opticians must be registered with the General Optical Council (GOC), which you can check online. Optometrists carry out eye examinations; dispensing opticians fit and supply glasses and, with extra training, contact lenses.

Some optometrists hold extra qualifications that let them manage more eye conditions or prescribe certain medicines. In many areas of the UK, community optometrists provide minor eye condition and urgent eye care services.

## FAQs about emergency eye care near me

### What should I do if I get a chemical in my eye?

Rinse your eye immediately with clean water for at least ten to fifteen minutes and then go straight to A&E. Do not wait for an optician appointment.

### Can an optician help with sudden vision loss?

Yes, an optician can assess sudden vision changes and may refer you urgently to a hospital eye service if needed. You should seek same-day care.

### Are urgent eye care services available on the high street?

Many areas have opticians who provide urgent eye care services for minor eye conditions and injuries. iComply can introduce you to a suitable professional near you.

### Do I need a referral to see an optician for an urgent eye problem?

### Do I have to buy glasses where I have my eye test?

No. You are entitled to a copy of your prescription and can buy glasses anywhere.
