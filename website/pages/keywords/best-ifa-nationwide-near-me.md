## Qualifications and registration to look for

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications.

## How fees usually work (POA)

Advisers often charge for initial advice, then an ongoing fee for reviews, which may be fixed or a percentage of the money they look after. Firms must set out charges clearly before advising you. The firm quotes you, POA.

## FAQs: an IFA

### How do I check that an IFA is authorised?

You can check the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register to see if an adviser or firm is authorised or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm. This is a key step to ensure you're protected.

### What does 'independent' mean for an IFA?

An independent financial adviser must consider products from across the market when making recommendations. They should clearly explain their independent status and how they are able to offer whole-of-market advice.

### Can I get advice if I'm not near the adviser?

Yes, many advisers offer remote consultations by phone or video call, so you can work with someone nationwide. You should confirm how they handle meetings and paperwork remotely.

### What qualifications should I look for in an IFA?

Look for advanced qualifications such as Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, or certifications like CFP. These show a high level of expertise and commitment to professional standards.

### Is an adviser further away any less suitable than a local one?

Not necessarily. What matters is that the firm is authorised on the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register, that the advice is genuinely independent if that's what you want, and that you're comfortable with how meetings will work. Many people are happy with phone or video appointments, but you should confirm this with the adviser before going ahead.

### How can I compare advisers when they're spread across the country?

Ask each one the same questions: whether their advice is independent or restricted, what qualifications they hold, and how they charge, with figures in pounds. Comparing like with like on those points tells you more than any ranking, and you can then judge which adviser suits your circumstances.

### What should I have ready before the first appointment?

It helps to gather details of your pensions, investments and any protection cover, along with a rough idea of your goals and when you'd like to reach them. You don't need everything in perfect order, but the more the adviser understands, the more useful the first conversation will be.

### What is the difference between independent and restricted advice?

Independent advisers consider products from across the market. Restricted advisers focus on certain products or providers. Both can give good advice; they must tell you which they offer.
