If your situation is not listed, that is fine. Describe it in your own words and we will work out which kind of planning consultant fits best.

## When to think twice

Be cautious if you notice any of the following:

- Pressure to submit before the scheme is ready
- Vague answers about local policy
- No written scope
- Guaranteed planning permission

## How to check you are in safe hands

Many planning consultants are chartered members of the Royal Town Planning Institute (RTPI), shown by the letters MRTPI. Chartered members follow the RTPI's code of professional conduct. You can check the RTPI directory before appointing anyone.

Planning consultants work alongside architects, surveyors and specialist consultants such as heritage, ecology and highways experts. A good consultant will tell you early whether a scheme is realistic under local policy.

"Consultant" is a broad title that anyone can use. Check what qualifications and registrations sit behind it, and whether the work you need is regulated.

## What to ask before you instruct anyone

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the planning consultant is right for you:

- Should we seek pre-application advice?
- What are the main planning risks for my scheme?
- What happens if the application is refused?
- Are you an RTPI chartered member?
- What other reports will the council need?
- How long will the process take?

## FAQs about planning consultant near me

### Do I need planning permission?

It depends on the work, the property and its location. Some changes fall under permitted development; a consultant can advise or apply for a lawful development certificate.

### Can I appeal a refusal?

Usually yes, to the Planning Inspectorate in England, within strict deadlines that depend on the type of application.
