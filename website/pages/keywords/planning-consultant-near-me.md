## Understanding local planning policy

Local planning authorities decide applications based on their local plan and national policy. A consultant familiar with your area can help you interpret these policies and avoid common pitfalls. Pre-application advice from the council can also reduce risk by clarifying what's likely to be acceptable.

Many planning consultants are chartered members of the Royal Town Planning Institute (RTPI), shown by the letters MRTPI. Chartered members follow the RTPI's code of professional conduct, and you can check the RTPI directory before appointing anyone. A good consultant will tell you early whether a scheme is realistic under local policy.

## FAQs: a planning consultant

### Do I need a planning consultant for a small home extension?

Not always, but a consultant can help if your project is complex or if you're unsure about local policy. They can advise on whether permission is needed and help prepare a strong application.

### How do I find a planning consultant near me?

You can search the RTPI directory for chartered members in your area, or ask for recommendations. iComply can also introduce you to a suitable consultant based on your needs.

### What should I prepare before contacting a planning consultant?

Gather basic details about your property, any plans or drawings you have, and information about previous applications. This will help the consultant understand your situation quickly.

### Can a planning consultant help with an appeal?

Yes, planning consultants often prepare and manage appeals, including writing statements and negotiating with the council. They can also advise on appeal deadlines, which are strict.

### Can I appeal a refusal?

Usually yes, to the Planning Inspectorate in England, within strict deadlines that depend on the type of application.
