## What to understand about land surveys

Accurate surveys underpin design, so it is worth asking about the methods they use, such as drone and laser scanning, and checking the deliverable formats they will provide.

Many surveyors are members of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), and RICS-regulated firms must follow professional standards, hold insurance and offer a complaints process. Chartered surveyors use the letters MRICS or FRICS. You can check the RICS directory to confirm a surveyor's membership and ask which report type you are getting and confirm whether the surveyor is independent of any sale.

## FAQs: a land surveyor

### Do I need a land surveyor for a house extension?

A land surveyor can provide a topographical survey that shows the levels and features of your land, which is often required by architects and planners before design work. It helps ensure your extension is designed accurately and meets planning requirements.

### What is the difference between a land surveyor and a building surveyor?

A land surveyor measures and maps land and its features, while a building surveyor assesses the condition of a property. For land-related projects, you need a land surveyor; for building defects or condition reports, you need a building surveyor.

### Can a land surveyor help with a boundary dispute?

Yes, a land surveyor can produce a measured survey that shows the physical features of your land and may help clarify boundaries. However, they cannot give legal advice, so you may also need a solicitor to interpret the findings in relation to your title deeds.

### How do I check if a land surveyor is qualified?

Many land surveyors are members of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS) and use the letters MRICS or FRICS. You can check the RICS directory to confirm their membership and whether their firm is regulated by RICS.

### Which survey do I need when buying a home?

For a conventional property in reasonable condition, an RICS Level 2 survey is often enough. Older, larger or altered properties often justify a Level 3 building survey. A surveyor can recommend the right level.
