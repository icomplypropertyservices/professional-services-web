## Making the most of a first private GP appointment

Searching for top private GPs near you often means you have something specific you want sorted out in one visit, so it helps to arrive prepared. Write down your symptoms, when they started and anything that makes them better or worse, along with a list of medicines you take and any questions you want answered. That way the longer appointment time works in your favour rather than being spent trying to remember details.

Because private appointments commonly run from fifteen to thirty minutes, a first consultation can cover several concerns that would normally need separate visits. Bring any relevant records you already hold, such as recent test results or a letter from a specialist, and be ready to say what outcome you're hoping for, whether that's a referral, a test, a certificate or simply reassurance. If you think you may need a video consultation instead, mention this when you enquire so it can be arranged.

It's also worth remembering that a private GP works alongside your NHS GP rather than replacing them, so consenting to share notes keeps your records joined up and helps prescriptions stay safe. If your concern turns out to need urgent NHS care, the private GP can point you in the right direction, and for urgent medical help you can always contact NHS 111 or, in an emergency, 999.

## Checking private GPs are qualified and regulated

Every doctor practising in the UK must be registered and hold a licence to practise with the General Medical Council (GMC). GPs also appear on the GMC's GP register. You can search the GMC's online register by name. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

A private GP does not replace your NHS GP. Good private clinics will ask for consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice, so your records stay complete and medication is prescribed safely.

## Top Private GPs Near Me: FAQs

### How do I check if a private GP is properly qualified?

Every doctor practising in the UK must be registered with the General Medical Council (GMC) and hold a licence to practise. GPs also appear on the GMC's GP register, which you can search online by name. It's also worth checking that the clinic is registered with the relevant regulator, such as the Care Quality Commission in England.

### Will a private GP share information with my NHS GP?

A private GP does not replace your NHS GP, and good private clinics will ask for your consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice. This helps keep your records complete and ensures medication is prescribed safely. You can ask the clinic about their policy when you enquire.

### What can a private GP help with compared to my NHS GP?

A private GP offers longer, faster appointments, often the same or next day, for the same range of everyday medical problems an NHS GP sees. They can also provide referrals, tests and certificates. The main difference is the speed and length of appointments, and you pay for the service.

### How do I choose the best private GP for me?

Since 'top' is subjective, it's best to judge for yourself by checking the doctor's GMC registration and the clinic's regulatory registration. Consider factors like location, appointment length, availability and whether they offer video consultations. You can also ask about fees and what's included before booking.

### Can I see a private GP for more than one problem in a single appointment?

Yes, the longer appointment times private GPs typically offer make it practical to discuss several concerns in one visit. It helps to list them in advance and mention at the start that you have more than one thing to cover, so the doctor can pace the consultation.

### What should I bring to my first appointment with a private GP near me?

Bring a note of your symptoms and how long you've had them, a list of any medicines you take, and any recent test results or specialist letters you hold. If you're seeking a certificate or referral, say so early so the GP knows what you need.

### Does a private GP near me have access to my NHS records?

A private GP won't automatically see your NHS records, which is why good clinics ask for your consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice afterwards. You can also bring relevant information yourself or ask your NHS GP to pass on details.

### Can a private GP prescribe medication?

Yes, a GMC-registered doctor can issue private prescriptions. You pay the clinic's prescription fee and the pharmacy's price for the medicine.
