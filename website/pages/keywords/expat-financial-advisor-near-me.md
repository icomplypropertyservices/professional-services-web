## Understanding regulation for expat financial advice

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). You should check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. This matters especially for expat advice, where the adviser may also be subject to rules in your country of residence.

Overseas pension transfers carry risk, and currency movements can affect the value of your savings. Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent or restricted, and how they charge. Advanced qualifications such as Chartered status or CFP can indicate expertise, but you should always confirm the scope of their advice for your situation.

## Questions to ask the professional

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the financial adviser is right for you:

- Are you authorised by the FCA to advise on expat matters, and can you provide your firm reference number?
- Do you have experience advising clients in my country of residence, and are you regulated there as well?
- Will you advise on an independent or restricted basis, and what does that mean for my options?
- How do you charge for your services, and will you provide a clear fee quote in advance?
- What are the specific risks of transferring my pension overseas, and how would you mitigate them?
- How do you account for currency risk in your recommendations?

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.
