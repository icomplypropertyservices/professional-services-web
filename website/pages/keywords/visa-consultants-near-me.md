## Comparing several visa consultants near you

The word "consultants" is looser than it sounds, and the people you find under it may not all be qualified to give immigration advice. In the UK that advice can only come from someone registered with the Immigration Advice Authority at the right level, or from a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive authorised by their own regulator, so a friendly local office is not enough on its own.

When you are weighing up a few names, check each one on the IAA's adviser finder or the SRA register before you go further, then ask what level they hold and whether it covers your type of case. A consultant registered only for basic applications may not be able to help with an appeal or representation, so match the level to what you actually need.

Being near you is a fair thing to want, especially if you would rather hand over documents in person or sit down to explain a complicated family situation. Treat distance as a convenience and regulation and experience with your route as the deciding factors, and ask each consultant the same questions so the answers are easy to compare.

## Checking immigration advisers are qualified and regulated

It is a criminal offence to give immigration advice in the UK unless you are qualified and regulated. Advisers must be registered with the Immigration Advice Authority (IAA, formerly the OISC) at the right level for your matter, or be a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive authorised by their own regulator. Check the IAA's adviser finder or the SRA register before you share documents or pay.

IAA advisers are registered at levels that limit what they can do, from basic applications to appeals and representation. Ask what level an adviser holds and whether it covers your type of case.

"Consultant" is a broad title that anyone can use. Check what qualifications and registrations sit behind it, and whether the work you need is regulated.

## FAQs: a visa consultant

### How do I check that a visa consultant is allowed to advise me?

Look the adviser up on the Immigration Advice Authority's adviser finder, or on the SRA register if they are a solicitor. This is worth doing before you share documents or pay anything.

### Does it matter if the consultant is not close to where I live?

Regulation and experience with your route matter most, and much can be handled remotely. Someone reasonably near you can still be convenient for meetings, so it is fine to balance both.

### Can iComply tell me whether I will get a visa?

No. We are not regulated and do not give immigration advice, and we cannot promise any outcome. We simply introduce you to a suitable professional, who will assess your situation and quote you directly.

### What should I have ready before I speak to an adviser?

It helps to know your visa route, your key dates and what documents you already hold. Having this clear means the adviser can get to the useful detail sooner.

### Is a visa consultant the same as an immigration adviser?

Not necessarily. "Consultant" is a general word anyone can use, while immigration adviser usually points to someone registered with the Immigration Advice Authority or authorised by a legal regulator. Always check the individual, not the job title, before sharing documents or paying.

### Several consultants near me offer a free chat — is that a good way to choose?

A short initial conversation can help you hear how someone explains your route and whether they seem to understand it, but a free chat is not a substitute for checking their registration. Use it to ask about their level and experience, then confirm the details on the IAA's adviser finder or the SRA register.

### What happens at a first appointment with a visa consultant?

Expect questions about your route, your circumstances and any dates you are working around, along with a list of documents they will need. This is also your chance to ask what level they are registered at, whether it covers your case, and how they will keep you updated.

### Can an adviser speed up my application?

An adviser cannot control Home Office processing times, but a complete, well-prepared application avoids avoidable delays. Some routes have paid priority services.
