## What to understand about business wills and regulation

Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity in England and Wales, so some will writers are not regulated. Solicitors (regulated by the SRA) and chartered legal executives (regulated by CILEx) are regulated, insured and covered by the Legal Ombudsman. Many specialists are also members of STEP or Solicitors for the Elderly.

When choosing a provider, ask whether they are regulated, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance and where your original will is stored. Scotland has its own rules on succession and confirmation, so make sure your adviser practises in the right jurisdiction.

## FAQs: a business will

### Do I need a separate business will if I already have a personal will?

It depends on what your personal will says about your business assets. A separate business will can deal specifically with your shares, partnership interest or business property, and can be drafted to work alongside your existing will and any agreements. A specialist can look at your current arrangements and advise.

### How does a business will interact with a shareholder agreement?

A shareholder agreement often sets out what happens to shares when a shareholder dies, such as whether the remaining shareholders have the option to buy them. A business will should be drafted to complement those terms, not contradict them. Your adviser will check both documents together.

### What are the changes to business relief I keep hearing about?

Business relief rules are changing, and the details can affect how business property is treated for inheritance tax. Because the rules are evolving, it is important to get advice based on your own circumstances. A wills and probate specialist can explain the current position and how it might apply to you.

### Is a business will legally binding in Scotland?

Scotland has its own rules on succession and confirmation, so a will made under English law may not be effective for Scottish business assets. If you have business interests in Scotland, you should make sure your adviser practises in the right jurisdiction. iComply can introduce you to a specialist who works in the relevant area.

### Can one professional deal with both my personal will and my business will?

Often yes, and there can be advantages in having one adviser see the whole picture so the two documents do not pull against each other. Ask at the outset whether they will look at your articles of association or partnership agreement as part of the work.

### What should I bring to a first meeting about a business will?

Bring your existing will if you have one, your articles of association or partnership agreement, and a note of what shares, partnership interest or business property you hold. It also helps to say who you want to benefit and who else is involved in the business.

### Does it matter if my business is in Scotland?

Yes. Scotland has its own rules on succession and confirmation, so an adviser who practises in the right jurisdiction matters more than a short journey. Tell us where the business and its assets are based when you enquire.

### Do I need a solicitor for probate?

Not always. Executors can apply for probate themselves for simpler estates. Many choose professional help where there is property, inheritance tax, overseas assets or family tension.
