- Refusal to provide a written estimate or prescription when asked

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Every vet practising in the UK must be registered with the Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons (RCVS), and you can search the RCVS register by name before you book. Veterinary nurses have their own RCVS register too. Many practices also join the voluntary RCVS Practice Standards Scheme, which inspects premises, equipment and clinical processes.

Some vets hold further qualifications such as an RCVS certificate, advanced practitioner status or specialist status in a particular species or discipline. If your animal has a complex or unusual condition, it is reasonable to ask whether the vet treating it holds one of these, or whether they refer to someone who does.

## FAQs: a cat-friendly vet

### How do I check that a vet is properly registered?

Every vet practising in the UK must be registered with the Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons, and you can search the RCVS register by name before you book. Veterinary nurses have their own RCVS register too, so you can check them in the same way.

### What does Cat Friendly Clinic accreditation mean?

It is a recognition that a practice has taken steps to reduce stress for cats, such as separate waiting areas, gentle handling and quieter consulting rooms. It is worth asking a practice directly whether it holds this accreditation.

### My cat hates the carrier. What can help?

A covered carrier and a familiar blanket help reduce stress, and it is worth asking the practice whether it can offer a quieter appointment time. Cats hide illness well, so regular checks matter as they age, even when your cat seems fine.
