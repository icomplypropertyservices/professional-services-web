## What to understand about aesthetic doctors near you

Aesthetic doctors are registered with the General Medical Council, which means they meet certain standards of training and conduct. They can prescribe prescription-only medicines such as botulinum toxin for anti-wrinkle injections, and they are trained to recognise and manage complications. Dermal fillers are devices rather than medicines, and the government has set out plans for a licensing scheme for non-surgical cosmetic procedures in England.

Until licensing is in force, choose a clinic carefully. Practitioners on registers such as Save Face or the JCCP, and doctors and nurses registered with the GMC or NMC, offer extra reassurance. Clinics run by regulated healthcare providers may also be registered with the Care Quality Commission. It's worth checking these details before you book.

## Aesthetic Doctor Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need a referral to see an aesthetic doctor?

No, you can usually book a consultation directly with an aesthetic doctor. However, for prescription-only medicines like botulinum toxin, the doctor must assess you face to face before prescribing.

### What is the difference between an aesthetic doctor and an aesthetic practitioner?

An aesthetic doctor is a GMC-registered doctor with medical training, while an aesthetic practitioner may have different qualifications. Doctors can prescribe medicines and manage complications, which some people prefer.

### How can I check if an aesthetic doctor is properly qualified?

You can check their registration on the General Medical Council's online register. You can also ask if they are on a register such as Save Face or the JCCP, and whether the clinic is registered with the Care Quality Commission.

### Are dermal fillers regulated in the same way as anti-wrinkle injections?

No, dermal fillers are devices rather than medicines, so they are not subject to the same prescription rules. The government has set out plans for a licensing scheme for non-surgical cosmetic procedures in England, but until then it's important to choose a clinic carefully.

### Are under-18s allowed to have these treatments?

In England it is illegal to give Botox-type injections or dermal fillers for cosmetic purposes to under-18s.
