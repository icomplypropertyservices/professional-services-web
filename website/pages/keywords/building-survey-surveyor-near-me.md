## Understanding the building survey and who carries it out

A building survey is the most detailed inspection of a property's condition and is particularly suited to older, larger or altered homes. The report explains defects and the repairs they may need, which helps you decide how to proceed before exchange.

Many surveyors are members of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), and RICS-regulated firms must follow professional standards, hold insurance and offer a complaints process. Chartered surveyors use the letters MRICS or FRICS, and you can check the RICS directory to confirm who you are dealing with.

## FAQs: a surveyor for a building survey

### Is a building survey the same as a mortgage valuation?

No. A mortgage valuation is carried out for the lender to confirm the property is worth lending against. A building survey is a much more detailed inspection of the property's condition, suited to older, larger or altered homes.

### When should I book a building survey?

It is best booked before you exchange contracts, so you have the findings in time to act on them. That gives you the chance to discuss any defects and repairs with your surveyor before you are committed.

### How do I check a surveyor's credentials?

Many surveyors are members of RICS, and chartered surveyors use the letters MRICS or FRICS. You can check the RICS directory and ask which report type you are getting and whether the surveyor is independent of the sale.

### What if the property has damp or timber problems?

A building survey will report on defects it finds, including damp and timber issues. For specialist damp and timber work, the Property Care Association represents specialists in that field, so it is worth asking about relevant expertise.

### Is a mortgage valuation the same as a survey?

No. A mortgage valuation is carried out for the lender. A survey is carried out for you and looks at the property's condition.
