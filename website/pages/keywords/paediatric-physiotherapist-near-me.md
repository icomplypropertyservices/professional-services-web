## What families searching for a paediatric physiotherapist usually need

Most parents are looking for practical, local support to help their child move, play and develop with confidence.

- You've noticed your baby isn't rolling, sitting or crawling when other children of the same age are, and you'd like a professional opinion.
- Your child has a diagnosed condition such as cerebral palsy or muscular dystrophy and needs ongoing physiotherapy to stay mobile and comfortable.

- Your child receives physiotherapy through the NHS but you'd like to explore additional private sessions closer to home or with shorter waiting times.

## When to think twice

Most professionals are honest and competent, but it is worth knowing the warning signs:

- No home exercise plan or progress review
- Treatments promised to cure everything
- No referral back when problems do not improve
- No HCPC registration

## What to know before you book

In the UK, 'physiotherapist' is a protected title, so anyone using it must be registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC). You can check the HCPC register online to confirm that the professional you're considering is registered. Many physiotherapists are also members of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy (CSP), which is shown by the letters MCSP after their name.

Paediatric physiotherapists specialise in working with babies and children, and they often use play-based therapy to keep sessions engaging. They should also teach you, as a parent or carer, exercises and techniques to use at home between appointments. When you contact a professional, ask about their experience with your child's specific condition.

## Checking physiotherapists are qualified and regulated

"Physiotherapist" and "physical therapist" are protected titles in the UK. Anyone using them must be registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC), and you can check the HCPC register online. Most physiotherapists are also members of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy (CSP), shown by the letters MCSP.

Sports therapists, massage therapists and personal trainers can be helpful for some needs, but they are not physiotherapists. For an injury, post-surgery rehabilitation or a neurological condition, check that you are booking a HCPC-registered physiotherapist.

## Questions to ask the professional

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- What experience do you have working with children who have my child's condition?
- How do you involve parents in sessions, and what exercises can we do at home?
- What does a typical session look like for a child of my child's age?
- How do you measure progress, and when would we plan to check how things are going?
- Are you registered with the HCPC, and do you hold MCSP membership?
- What are your fees, and how many sessions might my child need?

## Paediatric Physiotherapist Near Me: FAQs

### What does a paediatric physiotherapist do?

Paediatric physiotherapists help babies and children with development, movement, sports injuries and long-term conditions. They use play-based therapy and teach parents exercises to continue at home.

### How can I check a paediatric physiotherapist is qualified?

You can check the HCPC register online, and many physiotherapists are also members of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy (CSP), shown by MCSP.

### Can I see a paediatric physiotherapist privately without a referral?

Yes, you can often book a private paediatric physiotherapist directly without a referral from your GP. However, it's a good idea to let your GP or health visitor know, especially if your child is also under NHS care.

### What should I expect at the first appointment?

The first appointment usually involves an assessment of your child's movement and development, a discussion of your concerns and medical history, and a plan for therapy. The physiotherapist should explain what they recommend and involve you in decisions.

### Can physio help if I have already had a scan?

Yes. Bring the report with you. A physiotherapist will combine it with their own assessment to plan treatment.
