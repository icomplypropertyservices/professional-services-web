## Questions to ask the planning consultant

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- How familiar are you with my local planning authority's local plan policies and this type of proposal?
- What would you recommend as the first step — pre-application advice, a feasibility assessment or preparing an application?
- What have you handled that is similar to my situation, and what did it involve?
- How will you keep me informed about progress and the council's responses?
- What would you need from me, and from my architect or other specialists, to get started?
- If the application is refused, what are the appeal deadlines and how would you approach an appeal?

## FAQs about planning consultants near me

### Do I need a planning consultant or just an architect?

Architects design the scheme, while planning consultants focus on planning policy, applications, appeals and negotiation with the council. For anything beyond straightforward permitted development, a consultant's knowledge of local policy can be valuable, and the two often work together.

### What is pre-application advice and is it worth it?

It is advice sought from the council before you submit a formal application, intended to reduce risk and reveal issues early. Whether it is worthwhile depends on the complexity of your proposal, and a consultant can advise on that for your site.

### How do I judge which planning consultant to choose near me?

Ask about their experience with your local authority and similar proposals, check whether they are an MRTPI chartered member through the RTPI directory, and compare the quotes you receive. Fees are on application, so ask each consultant to set out what is included.

### What happens if my application is refused?

There are routes to challenge a refusal, including appeal, and appeals have deadlines that must be met. A planning consultant can go through the decision notice with you and advise on the options and timing.

### Do I need planning permission?

It depends on the work, the property and its location. Some changes fall under permitted development; a consultant can advise or apply for a lawful development certificate.
