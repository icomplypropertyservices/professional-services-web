## What to understand when choosing a private paediatrician

Every doctor practising in the UK must be registered and hold a licence to practise with the General Medical Council (GMC). You can search the GMC's online register by name to check a paediatrician's registration and licence. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

A private paediatrician does not replace your NHS GP. Good private clinics will ask for consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice, so your child's records stay complete and any medication is prescribed safely. ## FAQs: a private paediatrician

### Do I need a referral from my NHS GP to see a private paediatrician?

A GP referral is often preferred, but many private paediatricians will see children without one. It helps to bring your child's red book and any relevant medical records to the appointment.

### How do I check that a private paediatrician is properly qualified?

You can search the General Medical Council's online register by name to confirm the doctor is registered and holds a licence to practise. You can also check whether the clinic is registered with the relevant regulator, such as the Care Quality Commission in England.

### Will the private paediatrician replace my NHS GP?

No, a private paediatrician does not replace your NHS GP.

### What should I bring to a private paediatric appointment?

Bring your child's red book, any relevant medical records or test results, and a list of current medications. It's also helpful to note down your main concerns and any questions you want to ask.

### Can I find out which private paediatrician is best for my child's particular problem?

There is no official ranking of paediatricians, so the most reliable approach is to ask about a doctor's experience with your child's specific condition, such as asthma, allergies, growth or development. Checking their GMC registration and the clinic's regulator, together with a clear conversation about what the appointment will cover, helps you judge for yourself.

### How close does a private paediatrician need to be?

Close enough that repeat visits are manageable, especially if your child is unwell or the appointment falls on a school day. Ask where consultations and any follow-up take place before you commit, since a clinic that looks near on a map may still involve a difficult journey.

### What if my child needs to be seen urgently?

Private paediatric appointments are arranged directly with the doctor, so ask about availability when you enquire. If your child becomes seriously unwell, contact NHS 111 for advice or 999 in an emergency rather than waiting for a private appointment.

### Can a private GP prescribe medication?

Yes, a GMC-registered doctor can issue private prescriptions. You pay the clinic's prescription fee and the pharmacy's price for the medicine.
