## Why rebuild cost is not the same as market value

That figure covers things like materials, labour and professional fees needed to rebuild, and it can be very different from what the property would sell for. Under-insurance can reduce claims, so getting the figure right matters.

Rebuild costs change over time, so it is wise to check your valuation periodically, especially after alterations or if you have held the same policy for several years. Many surveyors are members of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), and RICS-regulated firms must follow professional standards, hold insurance and offer a complaints process. You can check the RICS directory and ask which report type you are getting.

## How to check you are in safe hands

Chartered surveyors use the letters MRICS or FRICS. Some specialist roles have their own bodies, such as the Faculty of Party Wall Surveyors or the Property Care Association for damp and timber specialists.

Check the RICS directory, ask which report type you are getting and confirm whether the surveyor is independent of the sale. For asbestos work, look for organisations accredited by UKAS for surveying.

## Insurance Valuation Surveyor Near Me: FAQs

### How often should I get an insurance valuation?

Rebuild costs can change over time, so it is sensible to check your valuation periodically, especially after building work or if your policy is several years old. Your insurer may also ask for an updated figure at renewal.

### Is a rebuild cost the same as my home's market value?

No, rebuild cost is what it would take to reconstruct the property from scratch, including materials and labour, while market value is what it would sell for. They are calculated differently and can be very different amounts.

### What happens if I am under-insured?

Under-insurance can reduce claims, meaning your insurer may not pay out the full amount needed to rebuild. A professional valuation helps you set the sum insured correctly.

### Do I need a surveyor to visit my property?

For an accurate insurance valuation, a visit is often needed so the surveyor can assess the property's construction, size and any special features. Some surveyors may offer a desktop valuation, but you should ask what is included.

### Which survey do I need when buying a home?

For a conventional property in reasonable condition, an RICS Level 2 survey is often enough. Older, larger or altered properties often justify a Level 3 building survey. A surveyor can recommend the right level.
