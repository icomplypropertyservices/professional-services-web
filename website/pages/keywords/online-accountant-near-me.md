## Working online without losing the personal touch

Typing "online accountant near me" rather than simply "accountant" suggests you have already decided you are happy to work remotely, but you are not quite ready to hand everything to a firm you will never meet. That is a sensible instinct. An accountant who is a reasonable drive away can still take your records by email or through software day to day, while being on hand if something needs a conversation in person.

When you compare the accountants we introduce you to, ask each one to describe how a typical year would work between you. Who will reply to your emails, how quickly, and will you deal with the same person each time or a different member of staff? Ask how they would want your records sent, and whether they would review them before a deadline rather than after it, so there is time to fix anything that looks wrong.

It also helps to be clear about what you are really buying. An accountant who only files your return is doing one job; one who looks at your figures and tells you what to do differently is doing another, and the two can cost very different amounts. Agree exactly which returns and filings the quote covers, and ask what happens if your circumstances change partway through the year, such as taking on an employee or registering for VAT.

## Qualifications and registration to look for

In the UK, the word "accountant" on its own is not a protected title, so anyone can use it. Titles such as Chartered Accountant (ICAEW, ICAS or Chartered Accountants Ireland), Chartered Certified Accountant (ACCA) and Chartered Management Accountant (CIMA) are protected, and members are bound by their body's ethics, training and complaints rules. Only registered auditors can sign statutory audit reports.

Every practice offering accountancy services must also be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by a professional body or by HMRC. It is reasonable to ask which body supervises a firm, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance, and how complaints are handled.

## Online Accountant Near Me: FAQs

### Can I use an accountant who is not in my town?

Yes. Working online means most accountants can look after clients anywhere in the UK, and many do. Choosing someone reasonably near you is mainly about whether you value occasional face-to-face meetings.

### How do I check an accountant's qualifications?

Ask which professional body they belong to, such as ICAEW, ICAS, Chartered Accountants Ireland, ACCA or CIMA, and confirm membership with that body. These titles are protected, unlike the word "accountant" on its own.

### What should I have ready before I enquire?

Gather a summary of your income sources, your latest accounts or tax return if you have one, and a note of any deadlines. Saying how your records are kept helps the accountant quote accurately.

### Will the accountant help with Making Tax Digital?

Most will, but it is worth asking directly how they handle digital records and submissions. Agree at the outset which returns and filings the fee covers so there are no surprises.

### Should I choose an accountant near me or one further away who works online?

There is no single right answer. Distance matters most if you would value occasional face-to-face meetings or want someone who knows your local area; otherwise, working online means an accountant further away can serve you just as well, so judge them on how they communicate and what their quote covers.

### What happens at a first meeting with an online accountant?

Usually a conversation by phone or video, where they ask about your income sources, how you keep records and any deadlines coming up. It is a chance for you to ask which returns their quote covers and how they handle Making Tax Digital, so treat it as a two-way conversation rather than an interview.

### Can an online accountant deal with HMRC and Companies House for me?

Accountants commonly prepare accounts and tax returns and file them with HMRC and Companies House on your behalf, but the exact scope varies between practices. Ask directly which filings are included in the quote and which would be charged separately, so you know what you are agreeing to before you start.

### Do I need a chartered accountant?

Not always. For simple Self Assessment a qualified, supervised accountant may be enough. For audits, complex group structures or specialist tax work, a chartered or certified firm is often the safer choice. We can match you to either.
