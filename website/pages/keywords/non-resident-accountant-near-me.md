## What to understand about non-resident tax affairs

The UK tax system treats non-residents differently from residents, especially when it comes to property income and gains. The Non-Resident Landlord Scheme allows you to receive rent without UK tax being deducted, provided you register with HMRC. If you sell a UK property, you must report the disposal to HMRC within a short window, even if you have no tax to pay.

The word "accountant" is not a protected title in the UK, so anyone can call themselves an accountant. Every practice offering accountancy services must also be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by a professional body or by HMRC. It is reasonable to ask which body supervises a firm, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance, and how complaints are handled.

## Non Resident Accountant Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need to register for the Non-Resident Landlord Scheme?

If you are a non-resident landlord and want to receive rent without UK tax being deducted, you need to register with HMRC. Once registered, your letting agent or tenant can pay you rent gross. An accountant can help you with the application.

### What is the deadline for reporting a UK property sale if I am non-resident?

You must report the disposal of UK property to HMRC within a short deadline after completion, even if you have no tax to pay. The exact deadline depends on the date of sale, so it is important to act quickly. An accountant can handle the report for you.

### Can I claim treaty relief on my UK income?

The UK has tax treaties with many countries that may reduce or eliminate UK tax on certain types of income. Whether you can claim relief depends on your country of residence and the specific income. An accountant can advise you on how to claim any relief you are entitled to.

### What is the difference between a Chartered Accountant and someone who just calls themselves an accountant?

In the UK, "accountant" is not a protected title, so anyone can use it. Titles like Chartered Accountant, Chartered Certified Accountant and Chartered Management Accountant are protected, meaning the person has met specific training and ethical standards set by a professional body. It is reasonable to ask which body supervises any accountant you consider.
