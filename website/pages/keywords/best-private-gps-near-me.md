## Checking private GPs are qualified and regulated

Every doctor practising in the UK must be registered and hold a licence to practise with the General Medical Council (GMC). GPs also appear on the GMC's GP register. You can search the GMC's online register by name. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

A private GP does not replace your NHS GP. Good private clinics will ask for consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice, so your records stay complete and medication is prescribed safely.

## Getting the most from your first private GP appointment

When you're weighing up the best private GPs near you, it helps to think about what you'll actually do in the appointment itself. A private GP consultation often runs longer than an NHS slot, which gives you room to raise more than one concern, talk through a symptom properly, and leave with a clear plan rather than a hurried exchange. Knowing this in advance means you can arrive prepared instead of trying to compress everything into a few minutes.

Before you book, jot down what you want to cover: how long a problem has been going on, any medication you take, and what you're hoping to get from the visit, whether that's reassurance, a test, a referral or a certificate. If you'd find it easier, ask whether the clinic offers a video consultation, which suits many everyday problems and saves you travelling to be seen. It's also worth asking how the clinic shares notes with your NHS practice, so your records stay joined up.

One common misunderstanding is that paying for a private GP means you're opting out of NHS care, or that the private doctor will automatically take over your prescriptions and ongoing treatment. In practice a private GP works alongside your NHS GP, and good clinics ask your consent to pass notes back so nothing is missed. Treat the private appointment as an addition to your usual care, not a replacement for it.

## Questions to ask a private GP

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you registered on the GMC's GP register, and what is your GMC number?
- Is the clinic registered with the CQC (or the relevant regulator in my nation), and what does its latest inspection report say?
- How long are your appointments, and can I book a video consultation if that suits me?
- Will you share my consultation notes with my NHS GP, and how do you get my consent?
- What are the fees for a consultation, and are there extra charges for tests or referrals?
- How quickly can I usually get an appointment, and how do I contact you if I have a follow-up question?

## FAQs: a private GP

### How do I check if a private GP is properly qualified?

All doctors practising in the UK must be registered and hold a licence to practise with the General Medical Council (GMC), and GPs also appear on the GMC's GP register. You can search the GMC's online register by name to confirm a doctor's registration and licence.

### Is a private GP clinic regulated?

In England, private clinics providing regulated healthcare must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings.

### Will a private GP tell my NHS GP about my visit?

A good private clinic will ask for your consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice, so your records stay complete and medication is prescribed safely. You can ask the clinic how they handle this before you book.

### Can I see a private GP for a second opinion?

Yes, many people see a private GP for a second opinion or to discuss a problem in more depth. It's worth telling the GP about any treatment you're already having and asking them to share notes with your NHS GP.

### How should I prepare for a private GP appointment?

Write down your symptoms, how long they've lasted, any medication you take, and the questions you want answered. If you're hoping for a referral, a test or a certificate, say so at the start so the GP can plan the appointment around it.

### Can a private GP prescribe medication or arrange tests?

A private GP can discuss treatment and arrange tests as part of your consultation, and will explain any charges for these before you go ahead. Ask the clinic to share notes with your NHS practice so your records stay complete and any prescribing is safe.

### Does seeing a private GP affect my NHS care?

No, a private GP doesn't replace your NHS GP, and you can carry on using both. Good clinics will ask your consent to send consultation notes to your NHS practice, which helps keep your records and medication joined up.

### Will a private GP share my notes with my NHS GP?

With your consent, most do, and it is safer for you. Joined-up records help avoid duplicate tests and medication clashes.
