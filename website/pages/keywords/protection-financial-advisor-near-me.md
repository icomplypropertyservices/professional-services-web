## What people searching for protection advice usually need

Most people looking for a protection adviser want to protect their family or their income from the unexpected.

- You have a mortgage or rent to pay and want to make sure your family could keep up the payments if you died or became too ill to work.
- You have children and want to ensure there would be money to cover everyday living costs if you were no longer around.
- You are self-employed or your employer does not offer sick pay, so you need income protection to cover your bills if illness or injury stopped you working.
- You have a health condition or a family history of illness and want to understand how that might affect your cover and what an insurer would need to know.

Protection advice rarely needs someone to visit your home, so 'near me' is often more about finding an adviser you feel comfortable speaking to than about distance. Many advisers work by phone or video as well as in person, so someone a little further away may still suit you well.

## Checking financial advisers are qualified and regulated

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Protection Financial Advisor Near Me: FAQs

### What is the difference between independent and restricted protection advice?

Independent advisers consider the whole market when recommending protection products, while restricted advisers are limited to certain products or providers. Advisers must tell you which type of advice they offer before you proceed, so you can decide what suits you.

### Why does my health matter for protection cover?

Insurers assess the risk you present, so your health and medical history can affect whether you are offered cover and on what terms. Underwriting is a normal part of the process, and an adviser can help you understand what information you will need to provide.

### Should I put my protection policy in trust?

Writing a policy in trust can help ensure that a payout goes to the people you intend without delay, and it may have inheritance tax benefits. An adviser can explain whether a trust is suitable for your situation.

### How often should I look at my protection cover again?

It is a good idea to go over your cover after major life changes such as moving house, having a child, changing jobs or getting married. An adviser can help you check whether your existing cover still meets your needs.

### Do I need to meet a protection adviser in person?

Not necessarily. Many people are happy to discuss life insurance, income protection and critical illness cover by phone or video, and some advisers work this way as standard. If meeting face to face matters to you, mention it when you enquire so we can take it into account.
