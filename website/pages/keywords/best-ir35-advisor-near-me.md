## Checking tax advisers are qualified and regulated

"Tax adviser" is not a protected title in the UK. The Chartered Institute of Taxation (CTA qualification) and the Association of Taxation Technicians (ATT) are the main specialist bodies, and many accountants and solicitors also advise on tax. Members of these bodies follow Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation, a shared code covering honesty, confidentiality and dealing with HMRC.

Tax advisers who provide services for a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes. HMRC has also been tightening standards for paid tax agents, so it is reasonable to ask about qualifications, supervision and professional indemnity insurance before you go ahead.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## What to expect at a first IR35 appointment

When you first speak to an IR35 adviser, they will usually start by asking for your contract and a description of how you actually work. This is because IR35 status depends on both the written terms and the reality of the engagement, and an adviser needs to see whether the two match. They may also ask whether a status determination has already been made and by whom, since for medium and large clients it is the client who decides.

The adviser will then explain how the key tests — such as control, substitution and mutuality of obligation — apply to your situation. They will not give you a simple yes or no without understanding the details, because IR35 is rarely clear-cut. Expect a conversation about the risks and the practical steps you can take, rather than a quick verdict.

If you are the client making the determination, the adviser will focus on your obligations and how to document your decision properly. If you are a contractor who disagrees with a determination, they will explain how a dispute can be raised with the client. Either way, the first meeting is about gathering facts and setting out your options, not about signing anything on the spot.

## FAQs: an IR35 adviser

### Can you tell me who the best IR35 advisor is?

We cannot say who is best, and nobody honestly could without knowing your situation. Instead, ask about qualifications, supervision and insurance, and judge for yourself whether their explanation of your contract and working practices makes sense.

### Do I need an advisor near me, or can this be done remotely?

IR35 advice is largely about documents and conversations, so location matters less than you might think. Being nearby can help if you prefer to meet in person, but what counts most is whether the adviser understands your engagement.

### Who decides whether my contract is inside or outside IR35?

For medium and large clients, the client makes the status determination. If you are the contractor and you disagree, you can raise a dispute with the client, and an adviser can help you prepare for that.

### What should I have ready before I speak to an adviser?

Have your contract, a clear picture of how you actually work day to day, and any status determination you have received. That lets the adviser compare the paperwork with the reality.

### Will the adviser tell me definitively if I am inside or outside IR35?

A good adviser will give you a clear view based on your contract and working practices, but they cannot guarantee an outcome. IR35 depends on the specific facts, and HMRC could take a different view. What they can do is explain the risks and help you present your position clearly.

### How is an IR35 adviser different from a general accountant?

Many accountants handle tax returns and general compliance, but IR35 advice requires a detailed understanding of employment status tests and how they apply to contractors and clients. Some tax advisers specialise in this area, so it is worth asking about their experience with IR35 specifically.

### Should I reply to HMRC before speaking to an adviser?

Note the deadline on the letter, but avoid sending a detailed reply before you have advice if the matter is complex. An adviser can often ask HMRC for more time where it is reasonable.
