## Checking financial advisers are qualified and regulated

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Understanding independent versus restricted advice

Independent financial advisers must consider the whole market and are not tied to particular providers, so they can recommend products from any firm. Restricted advisers, on the other hand, focus on a limited range of products or providers. Neither is necessarily better, but independent advice gives you the widest choice.

All financial advisers in the UK must be authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm. You can check the FCA register to confirm their status. Advisers must also tell you whether their advice is independent or restricted and explain how they charge.

## FAQs: an independent financial adviser

### How do I check that an adviser is authorised?

You can check the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register online or by contacting the FCA. It will show whether the adviser or firm is authorised or an appointed representative. Always do this before agreeing to any advice.

### What is the difference between independent and restricted advice?

Independent advisers must consider the whole market and are not tied to particular providers. Restricted advisers focus on a limited range of products or providers. Both must be authorised by the FCA and must tell you which type of advice they offer.

### Will I have to pay for the initial meeting?

Many advisers offer a free initial consultation, but not all do. You should ask upfront whether there is any charge for the first meeting and how fees are structured.

### What should I have ready for my first meeting?

It helps to gather any relevant paperwork, such as pension statements, investment valuations, and details of existing policies. Also think about your goals and any concerns you have, so you can discuss them clearly.

### Does 'near me' mean I have to choose an adviser in my own town?

Not necessarily. Many people prefer a local adviser for face-to-face meetings, but others are happy to work with an adviser further away by phone or video. What matters most is that the adviser is independent if that is what you want, and that they are authorised by the FCA.

### How can I tell if a local adviser is truly independent?

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent or restricted. You can also check the FCA register to confirm the firm's status.

### Is the first meeting free?

Many firms offer a free initial consultation to explain their service and charges. Formal advice is then charged as agreed in writing.
