## One key point about probate applications

Applying for a grant of probate on someone else's behalf for a fee is a reserved legal activity, which means the person or firm doing it must be regulated. Solicitors are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority and chartered legal executives by CILEx; both are insured and covered by the Legal Ombudsman. Many specialists are also members of STEP or Solicitors for the Elderly.

Most probate applications are now made online, but inheritance tax forms may need to be filed first and processing times can vary. Before instructing anyone, ask whether they are regulated, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance and where any original will is stored.

## FAQs about probate application near me

### Do I need a professional to apply for probate?

You can apply for probate yourself if you are an executor, but many people choose a regulated professional to avoid mistakes, especially if the estate is complex or inheritance tax forms are needed.

### What is the difference between probate and confirmation?

Probate is the grant used in England and Wales, while confirmation is the equivalent in Scotland. The rules and process differ, so make sure your adviser practises in the right jurisdiction.

### How long does a probate application take?

Processing times vary depending on the court and whether inheritance tax forms are required. Your professional can give you an idea based on your circumstances.

### Can I get help with inheritance tax forms?

Yes, a wills and probate specialist can help you complete the necessary inheritance tax forms, which often need to be filed before the probate application is made.

### Will a local specialist need to visit the property?

Not usually. A probate application is mostly paperwork and can often be handled remotely, but a local professional may be able to help if the estate includes a property that needs valuing or securing.

### Can I start the application myself and switch to a professional later?

In many cases yes, but it is worth asking a specialist early on, as inheritance tax forms often need to be filed before the application and mistakes can cause delays.

### What should I bring to a first appointment?

Bring the original will if you have it, a list of the estate's assets and debts, and any inheritance tax paperwork already completed. This helps the professional give you a clearer picture of what the application involves.

### Do I need a solicitor for probate?

Not always. Executors can apply for probate themselves for simpler estates. Many choose professional help where there is property, inheritance tax, overseas assets or family tension.
