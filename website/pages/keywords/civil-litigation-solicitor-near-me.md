## Checking who can handle your litigation

The word lawyer is not protected on its own, so it is worth confirming that the person handling your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation can only be carried out by authorised people. Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA register; in Scotland it is the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the Law Society of Northern Ireland.

Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure. If a service complaint is not resolved that way, the Legal Ombudsman can look at it in England and Wales. Because searching near me often reflects a wish to meet in person or deal with your local court, ask the solicitor which courts they cover and how they would handle hearings that fall elsewhere.

### What does a civil litigation solicitor actually do?

They can also advise on settling before a hearing, which is often quicker and less costly than going all the way.

### How do I check that someone is a solicitor?

In England and Wales you can check an individual or firm on the Solicitors Regulation Authority register. In Scotland check with the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland with the Law Society of Northern Ireland.

### Why does limitation matter in my dispute?

Limitation periods apply to civil claims, and if one expires you may lose the right to bring the claim at all. Tell the solicitor when the dispute arose so they can advise on timing.

### What should I ask about funding?

Ask about funding options early, because civil litigation can be paid for in different ways. Also ask what the costs consequences would be if you make or refuse a settlement offer.

### Can I get legal aid?

Legal aid is still available for some criminal, family (especially where there is domestic abuse), housing and immigration matters, subject to means and merits tests. A matched firm can tell you whether you are likely to qualify.
