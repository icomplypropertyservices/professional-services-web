## The legal side to understand

Employers' liability insurance is legally required for most UK employers, covering claims from employees who are injured or made ill at work. The minimum level of cover is set by law, and you should display your certificate where staff can see it. Fines can apply if you employ people without the required cover.

Insurance brokers and intermediaries in the UK must be authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority, or be appointed representatives of an authorised firm, so you can check them on the FCA register. Many brokers belong to the British Insurance Brokers' Association, and some hold Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute. A broker must understand your demands and needs, explain key policy features and exclusions, and tell you how they are paid.

Most UK employers are legally required to hold employers' liability insurance, and the minimum level of cover is set by law. There are limited exceptions, so it is worth asking a broker whether they apply to your situation.

Fines can apply if you employ people without the required employers' liability cover. Getting cover in place before staff start work avoids that risk.
