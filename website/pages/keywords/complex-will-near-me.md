## Why the word complex changes what you should look for

A straightforward will often turns on simple choices: who gets the house, who looks after the children, who acts as executor. When you search for a complex will near me, you are signalling that your estate has features that interact with each other, such as a trust that must sit alongside a business interest, or an overseas property that must not contradict your wishes at home. That interaction is where a specialist earns their keep, so look for someone who asks detailed questions rather than reaching for a standard form.

It also helps to think about who will actually do the work. A firm may take your instructions but pass the drafting to a colleague, so ask at the first conversation who will prepare the will and whether you can speak to them directly. Ask, too, how they will keep your wishes up to date if your circumstances change, since a complex will may need revisiting after a marriage, a sale of a business or a move abroad.

Finally, be wary of treating complexity as a reason to delay. The longer a blended family or business arrangement is left without a will that reflects it, the more room there is for disagreement later, and a specialist can talk you through the options without pressure. ## Qualifications and registration to look for

Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity in England and Wales, so some will writers are not regulated. Solicitors (SRA) and chartered legal executives (CILEx) are regulated, insured and covered by the Legal Ombudsman. Many specialists are also members of STEP (the Society of Trust and Estate Practitioners) or Solicitors for the Elderly. Applying for a grant of probate on someone else's behalf for a fee is a reserved activity.

When choosing a provider, ask whether they are regulated, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance and where your original will is stored. Scotland has its own rules on succession and confirmation, so make sure your adviser practises in the right jurisdiction.

## FAQs: a complex will

### Is a complex will something a specialist near me can quote for directly?

Yes. We introduce you to a suitable UK wills and probate specialist, who will discuss your situation and quote you directly. Quotes are price on application, so the figure comes from the professional rather than from us.

### Do I need a solicitor rather than an unregulated will writer for a complex will?

Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity, so unregulated will writers can prepare one. For blended families, business assets, overseas property or trusts, a solicitor regulated by the SRA or a chartered legal executive regulated by CILEx gives you the protection of regulation, insurance and the Legal Ombudsman.

### Can one will cover my overseas property as well as my UK estate?

Not always. Property abroad may need a local will, and the two documents need to work together rather than contradict each other. A specialist can explain how the jurisdictions interact, and for Scotland the rules on succession and confirmation are different again.

### What should I have ready before I speak to a specialist about a complex will?

It helps to have a clear picture of your family, your assets including any business interests and overseas property, and who you want to benefit. Mentioning any trusts or inheritance tax concerns early means the quote reflects the real work involved.

### My situation feels unusual. Will a specialist near me have seen anything like it before?

Complex estates are more common than people expect, and wills and probate specialists regularly deal with blended families, business assets, overseas property and trusts. Describe your circumstances plainly when you enquire, and the professional can tell you whether it falls within their experience.

### Can I start with a complex will and add trusts later?

Sometimes, but it is usually better to decide the overall shape of your estate before the will is drafted, because a trust added afterwards may mean rewriting parts of it. Raise any trust idea at the first appointment so the will and the trust are designed to work together.

### What if my family is spread across the UK and abroad?

Tell the specialist where each family member lives and where your assets sit, since different jurisdictions may each have a say. For Scotland in particular, succession and confirmation follow their own rules, so make sure your adviser practises in the right jurisdiction.

### What happens if I die without a will?

The intestacy rules decide who inherits, which may not reflect your wishes, especially for unmarried partners and stepchildren. A will lets you choose.
