## Checking independent status and accreditation

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). You can check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register, and that check is the most reliable starting point. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent, meaning they consider the whole market, or restricted to certain products or providers, and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications, so ask what accreditation actually means in each case. On best, no one can promise that for you, so compare what each firm offers and judge for yourself.

## Checking accreditation before you choose

Accreditation means a practice or professional has met standards set by a regulator or professional body. It is worth checking both the individual's registration and any practice-level accreditation, as they cover different things.

Ask which accreditations apply, what they require and how you can verify them online. Accreditation is not a guarantee of the outcome, but it does mean there are standards, a code of conduct and a route for complaints.

### How can I tell whether an IFA is properly accredited?

Check the adviser or firm on the FCA register to confirm they are authorised or an appointed representative of an authorised firm. Then ask what any accreditation means and who issued it, as Chartered status and certifications such as CFP are signs of advanced qualifications.

### What does independent mean for an IFA?

An independent financial adviser considers products from across the market when recommending pensions, investments and protection. Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent or restricted, so ask them to confirm it clearly.

### Will I know the cost before I go ahead?

Advisers must explain how they charge, and you can ask for the likely cost in pounds before any work begins. Getting that in writing helps you compare firms and decide what suits you.

### What can I do if something goes wrong with the advice?

You can complain to the firm first, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service. The Financial Services Compensation Scheme may also protect you if an authorised firm fails.

### Should I bring anything to a first meeting with an IFA?

Bring details of any pensions, investments or protection policies you already hold, along with a rough idea of your goals and what you can afford to set aside. Having this to hand helps the adviser understand your position and explain what they can do.

Yes. Nothing obliges you to go ahead, and taking time to check the firm on the FCA register and weigh up the charges is sensible. If you do proceed, ask for the arrangement to be confirmed in writing.
