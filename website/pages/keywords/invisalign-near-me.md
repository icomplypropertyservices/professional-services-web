## What to understand about Invisalign before you choose a practice

Invisalign trays are removable, so they only work if you wear them most of the day, every day, and take them out to eat and clean your teeth. Your dentist plans the movement digitally and will usually see you at intervals to check progress, with refinements if your teeth don't move exactly as planned. Retainers afterwards are what keep the result, so factor that into your thinking from the start.

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Only people on a GDC specialist list can call themselves an orthodontist, so if a practice uses that title, it's worth checking. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales it's Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

## FAQs: Invisalign

### Is Invisalign suitable for everyone?

No. Clear aligners work well for many people, but complex bite problems or heavily restored teeth may need a different approach. A dentist needs to examine your teeth and discuss your options before you commit.

### Do I need to wear the trays all the time?

Aligners must be worn most of the day to move teeth as planned, and you take them out to eat and clean your teeth. If you don't wear them enough, treatment can stall or the result may not be what was planned.

### What happens after my Invisalign treatment finishes?

You'll usually need retainers to keep your teeth in their new position, because teeth can drift back over time. Ask how retainers are provided and whether they're included in your quote.

### How do I check a dentist is properly registered?

You can check the General Dental Council's online register for any dentist, hygienist, therapist or nurse. If a practice calls someone an orthodontist, you can also check that they're on a GDC specialist list.

### Can I see a private dentist and stay with my NHS dentist?

Yes, you can use both. Some patients see a private dentist for specific treatments and keep NHS care for routine check-ups, if their NHS practice agrees.
