## Qualifications and registration to look for

Barristers in England and Wales are regulated by the Bar Standards Board (BSB). You can check the Barristers' Register to see whether someone holds a current practising certificate and whether they are authorised to accept instructions directly from the public. In Scotland the equivalent professionals are advocates, regulated through the Faculty of Advocates.

Barristers usually work from chambers, with clerks who manage diaries and fees. Many barristers can be instructed through a solicitor in the traditional way, and those registered under the Public Access scheme can be instructed directly by members of the public for suitable matters.

## FAQs: a barrister

### Can I instruct a barrister directly without a solicitor?

Yes, if the barrister is registered under the Public Access scheme and the matter is suitable. You can check the Barristers' Register to see whether someone is authorised to accept instructions directly from the public.

### How do I judge which barrister package is best for me?

Look at the barrister's specialism, what the package covers, and whether the fee is agreed in writing for a defined piece of work. Comparing those details tells you more than any label about who is best suited to your situation.

### What does a barrister package usually cover?

It depends on the work, but packages often cover preparation, advocacy at a hearing, or drafting and advice. Ask for the scope in writing so you know what is included and what would be charged separately.

### Can I find a barrister near me?

Barristers work from chambers across the UK, and many can act for you even if the chambers is not on your doorstep. Tell us how near you would like the barrister to be and whether you need to meet in person, and we can take that into account.

### Is a package always cheaper than paying for the work in stages?

Not necessarily. A package fixes the price for a defined piece of work, which can help you budget, but it is only good value if it covers what your matter actually needs. Compare the scope against the work you expect and ask what would be charged separately.

### Can I speak to the barrister before agreeing a package?

Often yes, though clerks usually handle the first enquiry and the diary. Ask whether a short conversation about the matter is possible before you commit, so you can judge the barrister's grasp of your area of law.

### Does near me matter if the hearing is elsewhere?

It can, because travel to court and meeting in person may affect both cost and convenience. Tell us where any hearing is listed as well as where you are, and we can take both into account when making an introduction.

### Can I go straight to a barrister without a solicitor?

Often yes, through the Public Access scheme, if the barrister is registered and the matter is suitable. Some cases still need a solicitor, and the barrister will tell you if so.
