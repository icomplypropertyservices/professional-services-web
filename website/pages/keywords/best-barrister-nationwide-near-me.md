## Checking barristers are qualified and regulated

Barristers in England and Wales are regulated by the Bar Standards Board (BSB). You can check the Barristers' Register to see whether someone holds a current practising certificate and whether they are authorised to accept instructions directly from the public. In Scotland the equivalent professionals are advocates, regulated through the Faculty of Advocates.

Barristers usually work from chambers, with clerks who manage diaries and fees. Many barristers can be instructed through a solicitor in the traditional way, and those registered under the Public Access scheme can be instructed directly by members of the public for suitable matters.

## Balancing nationwide choice with the right specialism

Searching nationwide widens the field, but it also means you need a clearer way to narrow it down. Start with the area of law your matter falls under, because a barrister who is excellent in one field may not practise in yours at all. The word best is personal here: it means the right specialism, the right approach and a fee arrangement you are comfortable with, not a ranking anyone can look up.

Nationwide also raises practical questions worth settling early. Ask whether the barrister can travel to your hearing or appear remotely, and how that affects preparation and cost. If your case spans England and Wales, the Bar Standards Board and the Barristers' Register are your reference points; in Scotland, advocates are regulated through the Faculty of Advocates, so the route differs.

Comparing options fairly means asking each barrister the same things: their experience in cases like yours, who will actually handle the work, how fees will be agreed for a defined piece of work, and how they will keep you informed. That gives you a like-for-like picture rather than a guess based on distance or a bold claim.

## Questions to ask a barrister before instructing

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you authorised to accept instructions directly from the public under the Public Access scheme?
- What is your experience in cases like mine, and can you provide an outline of your approach?
- How will you handle my case if you are based far from me—will you travel or appear remotely?
- What are your fees and how will they be agreed for the work I need?
- Will you be the person handling my case, or might it be passed to another barrister in chambers?
- How do you keep clients informed about progress and what is the best way to contact you?
