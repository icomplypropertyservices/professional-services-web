## What to understand about contractor accountants

In the UK, 'accountant' on its own is not a protected title, so anyone can call themselves an accountant. However, titles like Chartered Accountant (ICAEW, ICAS or Chartered Accountants Ireland), Chartered Certified Accountant (ACCA) and Chartered Management Accountant (CIMA) are protected, and members are bound by their body's ethics, training and complaints rules. It's reasonable to ask which professional body a contractor accountant belongs to and how they handle complaints.

Every accountancy practice must also be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by a professional body or by HMRC. When you speak to a contractor accountant, you can ask which body supervises them and whether they hold professional indemnity insurance. This helps you judge the firm's accountability, especially since contractor work involves complex rules like IR35 and expenses.

## FAQs: a contractor accountant

### Do I need a specialist contractor accountant rather than a general accountant?

A general accountant may not be familiar with the specific rules that apply to contractors, such as IR35 and the expenses rules for travel and subsistence. A contractor accountant focuses on these areas and can help you avoid common pitfalls.

### What is IR35 and why does it matter for contractors?

IR35 is the set of rules that determines whether a contractor working through a limited company is effectively an employee for tax purposes. Getting it wrong can lead to unexpected tax bills, so it's worth asking whether contract reviews are included.

### Can a contractor accountant help if I use an umbrella company?

Yes, many contractor accountants can advise on umbrella arrangements, including checking that your expenses and tax deductions are correct. They can also help you compare umbrella working with a limited company setup.

### How can I check that a contractor accountant is properly qualified?

Look for protected titles such as Chartered Accountant, Chartered Certified Accountant or Chartered Management Accountant, and ask which professional body they belong to. You can also ask which body supervises them for anti-money-laundering purposes and whether they hold professional indemnity insurance.

### Should I choose a contractor accountant I can visit in person?

That depends on how you like to work. A local practice may suit you if you prefer face-to-face meetings, while one you deal with remotely can be easier when contracts take you elsewhere. What matters most is that they understand contractor work and answer your questions clearly.

### How do I compare contractor accountants when fees are on application?

Ask each practice to explain what its quote covers, such as company accounts, payroll, dividend planning and IR35 contract reviews, and whether any of those cost extra. Comparing the scope of what is included is more useful than comparing a single figure. You can also ask how they handle questions between appointments.

### Does it matter whether I run a limited company or use an umbrella company?

Yes, because the work involved is different. A contractor accountant advising a limited company handles company accounts, payroll and dividend planning, while umbrella arrangements raise different questions about expenses and deductions. Tell the practice which setup you use, or if you are weighing up both.

### Do I need a chartered accountant?

Not always. For simple Self Assessment a qualified, supervised accountant may be enough. For audits, complex group structures or specialist tax work, a chartered or certified firm is often the safer choice. We can match you to either.
