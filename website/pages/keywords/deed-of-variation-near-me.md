## The two-year window and the need for agreement

A deed of variation must normally be made within two years of the person's death, so timing matters. All affected beneficiaries usually need to agree to the change, and the deed cannot be used to make a payment to someone for agreeing.

The tax effect of a variation can be significant, and statements may need to be included for inheritance tax or capital gains tax purposes. Because of this, it is important to take advice from someone who understands both the legal and tax sides.

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity in England and Wales, so some will writers are not regulated. Solicitors (SRA) and chartered legal executives (CILEx) are regulated, insured and covered by the Legal Ombudsman. Many specialists are also members of STEP (the Society of Trust and Estate Practitioners) or Solicitors for the Elderly. Applying for a grant of probate on someone else's behalf for a fee is a reserved activity.

When choosing a provider, ask whether they are regulated, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance and where your original will is stored. Scotland has its own rules on succession and confirmation, so make sure your adviser practises in the right jurisdiction.

## FAQs about deed of variation near me

### Can a deed of variation be made after the two-year period?

Generally, a deed of variation must be made within two years of the death. After that, it may not be possible without special arrangements, so it is best to act promptly.

### Do all beneficiaries need to agree to a deed of variation?

Yes, all affected beneficiaries usually need to agree to the change. If someone does not agree, the variation cannot proceed in the way you might want.

### Can a deed of variation be used to reduce inheritance tax?

It can be, depending on the circumstances. A specialist can explain how the variation might affect the tax position and what statements are needed.

### Is a deed of variation only for wills?

No, it can also apply where someone died without a will, under the intestacy rules. The same two-year window and need for agreement apply.

### What happens if I die without a will?

The intestacy rules decide who inherits, which may not reflect your wishes, especially for unmarried partners and stepchildren. A will lets you choose.
