## What to understand about bookkeeping locally

Bookkeeping is not a regulated title in the UK, but anyone charging a fee for bookkeeping services must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by HMRC or by a professional body. Membership of the Institute of Certified Bookkeepers or the Association of Accounting Technicians shows a bookkeeper has trained to a recognised standard and agreed to a code of conduct.

A local bookkeeper is useful when you want to hand over paperwork in person or have someone who understands your area, but distance matters less than a clear working arrangement. Ask how you will share documents each month, and be clear about where the bookkeeper's work stops and an accountant's begins, for example on year-end accounts and tax advice.

## Working with a bookkeeper who is close by

Choosing someone near you often comes down to how you like to hand things over. If your receipts and bank statements are paper, a bookkeeper you can drop them to in person may suit you better than posting them or scanning everything, and a short journey can make a monthly routine easier to keep up. If your records are already digital, being close by matters less than a clear arrangement for sharing documents each month.

At a first meeting, expect to talk through what your business does, how you currently record sales and purchases, and how far behind things are. A good bookkeeper will look at a sample of your records, explain what they would do and how often, and be upfront about where their work stops and an accountant's begins on year-end accounts and tax advice. Take your bank statements, invoices and receipts with you so the conversation is grounded in your actual paperwork.

When you compare local bookkeepers, ask each one the same questions so the answers are easy to weigh up. Ask which body supervises them for anti-money-laundering purposes, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance, and whether VAT returns and payroll are included or quoted separately. Clear, organised answers tell you more about how they will handle your books than any claim about being the best in your area.

## FAQs: a bookkeeper

### Do I need a bookkeeper who is based close to me?

Not necessarily. Many bookkeepers work remotely and share documents online, which can suit businesses that rarely handle paper. A local bookkeeper helps if you prefer to drop off paperwork or meet face to face, so weigh that against how you like to work.

### Can a bookkeeper handle my VAT returns and payroll?

Many bookkeepers prepare VAT returns and run payroll as part of their service, alongside recording sales, purchases, receipts and payments. Ask exactly what is included in the quote so you know what you are getting.

### How do I judge a bookkeeper when I am comparing quotes?

Ask about their training, which body supervises them, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance, and how clearly they explain where their work stops and an accountant's starts. A clear, organised answer tells you more than any claim about being the best.

### What records should I gather before a bookkeeper starts?

Collect your bank statements, sales invoices, purchase receipts and any payroll details, and note whether you are VAT registered. The bookkeeper can then tell you what else is needed and how to share it each month.

### Is it better to choose a bookkeeper in my own town?

It depends on how you handle paperwork. A bookkeeper nearby is convenient if you prefer to drop off receipts or meet face to face, but many people share documents online and never need to visit. Weigh convenience against how clearly they explain their service and what the quote covers.

### What should I bring to a first appointment with a local bookkeeper?

Bring your bank statements, sales invoices, purchase receipts and any payroll details, along with a note of whether you are VAT registered. Having these to hand lets the bookkeeper see the scale of the work and give you a realistic quote.

### Does meeting a bookkeeper in person mean they are better qualified?

Not necessarily. Bookkeeping is not a regulated title in the UK, so what matters is whether they are supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance, and whether their training is with a recognised body such as the Institute of Certified Bookkeepers or the Association of Accounting Technicians. Ask these questions whether you meet in person or work remotely.

### Can a bookkeeper submit my VAT return?

Many can, through Making Tax Digital compatible software, once you authorise them. Check this is included in the scope before you start.
