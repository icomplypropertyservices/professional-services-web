## Costs and quotes for a barrister

Barristers usually agree a fee for a defined piece of work, such as a written advice, a conference or attendance at a hearing, often negotiated through their clerk. Fees reflect seniority, specialism and the time needed. The chambers will quote you, POA.

Ask clerks to confirm the fee in writing, what it covers and what happens if the hearing runs longer or is adjourned. Under public access, you may need to handle some administrative tasks a solicitor would normally do.

## Understanding how barristers work and how to choose one

Barristers in England and Wales are regulated by the Bar Standards Board, and you can check the Barristers' Register to see whether someone holds a current practising certificate and whether they are authorised to accept instructions directly from the public. In Scotland the equivalent professionals are advocates, regulated through the Faculty of Advocates. Barristers usually work from chambers, where clerks manage diaries and fees, and many can be instructed through a solicitor in the traditional way.

Those registered under the Public Access scheme can be instructed directly by members of the public for suitable matters, and fees are usually agreed for a defined piece of work. The most important thing is the right specialism: a barrister who handles your kind of matter routinely will know the courts, the procedure and the arguments that matter. Distance is worth weighing alongside that, since much work can be done remotely but hearings may need attendance in person.

## FAQs: a barrister

### Can I instruct a barrister directly without a solicitor?

Yes, if the barrister is registered under the Public Access scheme and the matter is suitable for direct instruction. You can check the Barristers' Register to see whether someone is authorised to accept instructions directly from the public. For some matters a solicitor is still the better route, and a barrister can tell you which applies to you.

### How do I know a barrister is properly regulated?

Barristers in England and Wales are regulated by the Bar Standards Board, and the Barristers' Register shows whether someone holds a current practising certificate. In Scotland, advocates are regulated through the Faculty of Advocates. Checking the register is a sensible step before agreeing any work.

### Does it matter whether the barrister is near me?

It can help if you want to meet in person or if hearings are at a local court, but the right specialism matters more than distance. Much preparatory work can be done by phone or in writing. Tell us how far you are willing to travel and we will take that into account.

### How are a barrister's fees agreed?

Fees are usually agreed for a defined piece of work, such as advice, a written opinion or representation at a hearing. The barrister quotes you directly, so you can see what is covered before deciding. Ask for the quote in writing and check what happens if the work turns out to be more involved than expected.

### What if the nearest barrister does not practise in my area of law?

That is common, and it is worth widening the search rather than settling for the wrong specialism. A barrister who handles your kind of matter routinely will understand the procedure and the arguments, which matters more than a short journey. Tell us the area of law and we will look for a suitable fit.

### Can a barrister near me take my case at short notice?

Availability depends on the individual barrister's diary, which is managed by chambers clerks, so it is worth asking early. A barrister further away who is free sooner may serve you better than a local one who is fully booked. Mention any hearing date when you enquire so the introduction can reflect it.

### Do I have to meet a barrister in person before they act for me?

No, much of the preparatory work can be done by phone, video or in writing, and many people never visit chambers. A face-to-face meeting can still help if your matter is complex or emotional. Say what you would prefer and it can be taken into account.

### Can I go straight to a barrister without a solicitor?

Often yes, through the Public Access scheme, if the barrister is registered and the matter is suitable. Some cases still need a solicitor, and the barrister will tell you if so.
