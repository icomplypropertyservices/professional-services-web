## Questions to ask the mortgage broker

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the mortgage broker is right for you:

- Do you search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders?
- How are you paid, and will I pay a fee directly or is it covered by lender commission?
- What is your process for getting a decision in principle, and how long does it usually take?
- Can you explain the different types of mortgages available for my situation, such as fixed or variable rates?
- What happens if my application is declined, and will you help me find an alternative?
- Are you authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority, and what is your firm's reference number?

## FAQs about mortgage broker near me

### How do I check if a mortgage broker is authorised?

You can check on the FCA register to see if the broker or their firm is authorised or an appointed representative. The broker should also be able to tell you their firm's reference number.

### Will a mortgage broker near me charge a fee?

Brokers must tell you how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. Fees vary, so ask for a clear explanation before you proceed.

### What is a decision in principle and do I need one?

A decision in principle is an indication from a lender of how much they might lend you, based on an initial check. It can help when making an offer on a property, and brokers often recommend getting one early.

### Can a mortgage broker help if I'm self-employed?

Yes, many brokers specialise in helping self-employed clients and understand how to present income evidence to lenders. Tell the broker about your income history and any accounts you have.

### Does a mortgage broker need to be near me to help?

Not necessarily. Much of the work can be done by phone, email or video, so being nearby is convenient but not essential. What matters more is that the broker is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority and is set up to handle your situation.

### Can I visit a local broker in person?

Many brokers offer face-to-face appointments, though some work mainly remotely, so it's worth asking when you first get in touch. If meeting in person matters to you, say so early so you're introduced to someone who works that way.

### What should I bring to a first meeting with a local broker?

Bring proof of income such as payslips or accounts if you're self-employed, details of your deposit and any existing mortgage, and information about regular outgoings. The broker will use these to check affordability and explain your options.

### Is a broker better than going to my bank?

A broker can compare many lenders and may find options your bank does not offer. Your bank can only offer its own products. Either can suit you; a broker gives you a wider view.
