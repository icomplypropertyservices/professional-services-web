## Questions to ask the mortgage adviser

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority, and can you provide your firm's reference number?
- Do you search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders?
- How are you paid – by lender commission, a fee from me, or both?
- What qualifications do you hold, such as CeMAP?
- Will you also discuss protection products like life insurance or income protection?
- What is the process if I want to make a complaint?

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and you can check this on the FCA register. Advisers typically hold a qualification such as CeMAP.

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders, and how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Best Mortgage Advisors Near Me: FAQs

### How can I check if a mortgage adviser is properly authorised?

You can check on the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register to see if the adviser or their firm is authorised. The adviser should be able to give you their firm reference number. This is a key step to ensure you are dealing with a regulated professional.

### What does 'whole of market' mean for a mortgage adviser?

A whole-of-market adviser can recommend mortgages from across the market, while a limited panel adviser only considers a selection of lenders. Advisers must tell you which service they offer. This affects the range of deals available to you.

### Will using a mortgage adviser cost me anything?

Advisers must explain how they are paid – whether by lender commission, a fee from you, or both. Some may not charge a direct fee, but it's important to ask upfront. The cost should be clear before you proceed.

### Can a mortgage adviser help if I have bad credit?

Yes, some advisers specialise in helping people with credit issues. They can assess your situation and identify lenders that may consider your application. It's worth discussing any credit problems openly so the adviser can find suitable options.

### Should I speak to several mortgage advisers before deciding?

It can help, because comparing how each one explains their service, their lender range and their charges gives you a clearer picture. Ask each the same questions so the differences are easy to see. You are not obliged to go with any particular one.

### Does 'near me' mean I have to meet my adviser in person?

Not necessarily. Many people search for someone nearby because they would prefer a face-to-face meeting, but advice can often be given by phone or online as well. It is worth asking each adviser how they usually work with clients before you choose.

### What should I have ready for a first conversation with an adviser?

It helps to know roughly what the property is worth, how much you want to borrow, and whether you are buying, remortgaging or moving. Details of your income and employment, including any irregular or self-employed income, let the adviser assess your situation properly. Mentioning any credit issues you know about means the advice can reflect them from the start.

### Is a broker better than going to my bank?

A broker can compare many lenders and may find options your bank does not offer. Your bank can only offer its own products. Either can suit you; a broker gives you a wider view.
