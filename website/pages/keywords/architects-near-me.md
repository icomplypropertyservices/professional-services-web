## Qualifications and registration to look for

"Architect" is a protected title in the UK. Only people on the Architects Registration Board (ARB) register can call themselves architects, and they must follow the ARB code of conduct and hold professional indemnity insurance. Many practices are also RIBA Chartered Practices. Architectural designers and technologists can do excellent work too, but they are not registered architects, so it is worth knowing which you are hiring.

Before you appoint anyone, check the ARB register and ask for a written appointment that sets out services, fees and responsibilities. Building regulations approval, planning permission and party wall matters are separate processes that your architect can help you navigate.

## How fees usually work (POA)

Architects commonly charge a fixed fee per stage, a percentage of the build cost, or an hourly rate for smaller pieces of work. The scope, from initial sketches to full contract administration, makes a big difference. The practice will give you a POA proposal.

Ask for a written fee proposal linked to the RIBA stages, and check what is excluded, such as planning fees, surveys and other consultants.

## FAQs about architects near me

### Do I need an architect for a house extension?

Not always, but an architect can prepare drawings for planning permission and building regulations and help you avoid problems later. Some alterations are permitted development, while others need a formal application, so it is worth checking before you start.

### What is the difference between an architect and an architectural designer?

An architect is on the ARB register and must follow its code of conduct and hold professional indemnity insurance. Architectural designers and technologists are not registered architects, though they can do excellent work, so it is worth knowing which you are hiring.

### How do I check someone is a registered architect?

You can check the ARB register before you appoint anyone. Ask for a written appointment that sets out services, fees and responsibilities, and confirm whether the practice is an RIBA Chartered Practice.

### Can an architect help with planning permission and building regulations?

Yes, an architect can prepare drawings for planning and building regulations and can help you navigate those separate processes. Party wall matters are also separate, and your architect can advise on when they apply.

### Should I meet more than one architect before deciding?

Meeting a few practices is a good way to compare approaches, fees and how well you communicate with each other. Ask each one what they would do for your project and what their written appointment would cover.

### How do I compare quotes from different architects fairly?

Look at what each quote includes rather than the total alone, since one may cover planning and building regulations drawings while another treats them as extra. Ask for the scope to be set out by RIBA stage so the comparison is like for like.

### Will a local architect know my area's planning authority?

A practice that regularly works in your area will usually be familiar with the local planning authority and the kinds of applications it deals with. It is still worth asking directly about their experience with your type of property and project.

### Do I need an architect for an extension?

Not legally, but a registered architect brings design skill, planning experience and accountability. For simple work, an architectural technologist or designer may be enough. We can match you to the right level of help.
