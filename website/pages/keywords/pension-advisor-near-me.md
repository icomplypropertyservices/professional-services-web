## Checking who can advise you on your pension

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Before you share any details, check that the adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Some pension decisions carry special rules. Advice on transferring out of a defined benefit scheme above a set value must come from an adviser with pension transfer specialist permissions, and safeguarded benefits deserve particular care. If you are over the age of fifty with a defined contribution pension, free and impartial guidance is also available from Pension Wise, which can help you understand your options before you take advice.

## What a first pension appointment near you usually covers

A first meeting with a local pension adviser tends to be about gathering facts rather than making decisions. Expect to talk through each pension you hold, when you started it, who runs it now and whether it is a workplace scheme, a personal pension or a final salary arrangement. Bringing recent statements and any letters from your scheme makes that conversation far more useful.

The adviser will usually want to understand what you are aiming for, whether that is retiring earlier, taking a regular income, or simply knowing where you stand. They should explain how they charge before any work begins, so you can decide whether to go ahead. Nothing should be signed or transferred at that first meeting, and you are free to take time to think it over.

Because you searched for someone near you, it is worth asking whether the first meeting can be face to face and whether later reviews would be in person or by phone. Some people find it easier to hand over paperwork in person, while others are happy with remote appointments once they know the adviser. ## Pension Advisor Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need a pension adviser who is local to me?

Not necessarily. Many pension matters can be handled by phone or online, but meeting in person suits some people better, especially when discussing retirement plans. Searching for a pension advisor near me is a reasonable way to find someone you can sit down with.

### Can I get free guidance before paying for advice?

Yes. Pension Wise offers free, impartial guidance to people over the age of fifty with defined contribution pensions, and it can help you understand your options. It does not replace regulated financial advice, but it can prepare you for the conversation.

### How do I check that a pension adviser is properly authorised?

Look up the adviser or firm on the FCA register to confirm they are authorised or an appointed representative of an authorised firm. This matters because the Financial Ombudsman Service and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may only be able to help you if an authorised firm is involved.

### What if my pension is a final salary scheme?

Advice on transferring out of a defined benefit scheme above a set value must come from an adviser with pension transfer specialist permissions. Tell us about the scheme when you enquire so we can introduce you to someone suited to that kind of work.

### Should I bring anything to a first meeting with a pension adviser?

Bring recent statements for each pension, any letters from your scheme, and your state pension forecast if you have one. If you have received a transfer offer or paperwork about safeguarded benefits, take that too, as it shapes the advice you need.

### Will the adviser want to transfer my pensions straight away?

A good first meeting is about understanding your situation, not rushing a transfer. You should expect an explanation of the options and the costs before you decide anything, and you can always take time to think it over.

### Is it worth meeting in person rather than by phone?

That depends on how you prefer to work through paperwork and ask questions. Meeting locally can make it easier to go through statements together, but many pension matters can also be handled by phone or online once you are comfortable with the adviser.

### What is Pension Wise?

A free, impartial government-backed service offering guidance to people over 50 about their options for defined contribution pensions. It is guidance, not personal advice.
