## Checking financial advisers are qualified and regulated

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications.

## What "comparison" really involves

A comparison site for financial advice does not work like a price comparison for car insurance, because the advice you receive is built around your circumstances rather than being a standard product on a shelf. What you can compare is the adviser's status, the way they charge and their qualifications — the things that stay constant whichever firm you approach.

One common misunderstanding is that the cheapest headline fee means the best value. An adviser who charges less but only considers a narrow range of providers may not suit a pension transfer or a complex investment, while a higher fee may reflect more detailed work. Ask each adviser what the fee covers and whether it is a fixed sum or a percentage, so you are comparing the same thing.

It also helps to compare how the first meeting works. Many advisers offer an initial conversation at no cost, which lets you ask about independence, charges and qualifications before you commit to anything. Use that conversation to see whether the adviser listens to what you actually need, rather than steering you towards a particular product.

## Best IFA Comparison Near Me: FAQs

### How can I compare independent financial advisers fairly?

Compare on things you can check: whether the adviser is independent or restricted, how they charge in pounds, and their qualifications. Checking the FCA register confirms the adviser or firm is authorised, which helps you compare like for like.

### Does independent advice cost more than restricted advice?

Not necessarily — charges vary by firm and by the work involved, so ask each adviser to set out their fees in pounds. Comparing those figures directly is more reliable than assuming one type of advice is cheaper.

### What does "near me" mean for financial advice?

Many people prefer a local adviser they can meet face to face, but advice can often be given by phone or remotely too. Tell us your preference and we will introduce a suitable UK adviser who quotes you directly.

### What protection do I have if I use an authorised adviser?

If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service. The Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

### Can I compare independent financial advisers without paying anything?

Many advisers offer an initial conversation before any chargeable work begins, so you can ask about independence, charges and qualifications first. Confirm with each adviser what, if anything, the first meeting costs before you book.

### Is a local adviser better than one further away?

Not necessarily — what matters is that the adviser is authorised, independent if that is what you want, and clear about charges. A local adviser may suit you if you prefer face to face meetings, but phone and remote advice can work just as well for many people.

### How do I compare advisers when fees are quoted as POA?

Ask each adviser to set out their charges in pounds, whether fixed or a percentage, and what those charges cover. With figures in front of you, you can weigh up the cost against the scope of advice and the adviser's qualifications.

### What is the difference between independent and restricted advice?

Independent advisers consider products from across the market. Restricted advisers focus on certain products or providers. Both can give good advice; they must tell you which they offer.
