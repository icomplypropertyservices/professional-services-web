## What to understand when comparing private dentists

Private dentists must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. If you are considering a specialist treatment, such as orthodontics or endodontics, check that the dentist is on the relevant GDC specialist list. In England, private clinics providing regulated healthcare must also be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland different regulators apply.

When comparing practices, ask for an itemised treatment plan so you can see what each part of the treatment costs. Membership plans can help spread the cost of routine care, but check what is included. If something goes wrong with private treatment, the practice's complaints process is the first step, and the Dental Complaints Service can help if the complaint is not resolved.

## FAQs about best private dentists near me

### How can I check if a private dentist is properly registered?

You can check the General Dental Council (GDC) register online to confirm that the dentist, hygienist, therapist or nurse is registered. For specialists, you can also check the GDC specialist lists. In England, you can check the Care Quality Commission (CQC) register for the practice's registration and ratings.

### What should I do if I have a problem with private dental treatment?

Start by raising your concern with the practice's complaints process. If it is not resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints. They offer a free service to help resolve issues between patients and private dental practices.

### What is a membership plan and how does it work?

A membership plan is a way to spread the cost of routine dental care, usually covering check-ups and hygiene visits for a regular payment. Plans vary, so ask what is included and whether there are any exclusions. It can help you budget for preventive care.

### Can I get private dental treatment if I am normally an NHS patient?

Yes, you can choose to see a private dentist for some or all of your care. Some people mix NHS and private treatment, but you should check with the practice about what is available. Private treatment often offers more choice and quicker access.

### Can anyone tell me which private dentist is the best near me?

No, because 'best' depends on what matters to you, such as location, appointment availability, treatment range or how a dentist works with nervous patients. What you can do is compare local practices on the same points, check GDC registration, and ask for itemised treatment plans so the quotes you receive are comparable.

### What should I compare when I have quotes from several private dentists?

Look at what each treatment plan actually includes rather than only the headline figure, since materials, follow-up appointments and laboratory work can differ between practices. Check whether a membership plan is part of the quote and what it covers, and confirm who would carry out each stage of the treatment.

### What happens at a first appointment with a private dentist?

A first appointment usually involves a check-up and a conversation about your dental history, any concerns and what you would like to change. Private appointments often allow more time for this discussion, and you can ask for a written, itemised treatment plan before deciding whether to go ahead.

### Do I need a check-up before treatment?

Almost always. A dentist needs to examine you, and often take X-rays, before recommending treatment.
