## Getting help quickly without a regular dentist

Searching for emergency dental near me often means you do not have a regular dentist to call, or your usual practice is closed and you cannot wait until it reopens. That is a common position, and it helps to say so plainly when you enquire, because the dentist will want to know whether anyone already holds your dental records. If you do have a regular dentist, mention them too, as they may be able to see you sooner than you expect.

Before you travel, think about what you can safely do at home. A cold compress held against the outside of your cheek can ease swelling, and a pharmacist can advise on pain relief that is suitable for you. If your face or jaw is swelling and you feel unwell, or you have trouble breathing or swallowing, seek urgent medical help rather than waiting for a dental appointment, and call 999 if it is an emergency.

When you compare practices for urgent care, ask two practical questions: whether they keep time aside for people in pain, and what the emergency appointment covers. An urgent visit often deals with the immediate problem and plans the permanent treatment as a separate step, so ask what happens next and what each stage involves. Price is on application, so ask for the cost of the urgent visit and any follow-up before you agree to be seen.

## FAQs: emergency dental care

### What should I do if I knock out an adult tooth?

Keep the tooth in milk and seek dental help promptly. Do not try to put a baby tooth back in.

### Can a private dentist help with severe toothache?

Yes, a private dentist can assess the cause of severe toothache and provide urgent treatment. Pain relief may come first, with permanent treatment planned afterwards.

### How do I know the dentist is properly registered?

You can check the General Dental Council (GDC) register online to confirm the dentist is registered. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must also register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings.

### What if I am not happy with the private dental treatment I receive?

Start with the practice's complaints process. If the complaint is not resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### I do not have a regular dentist — can I still be seen for an emergency?

Yes. Private practices can see people who are not registered with them, though you should say when you enquire that you do not have a regular dentist. It also helps to mention any treatment you have had recently and any medication you take.

### When is a dental problem too urgent to wait for a dental appointment?

If you have swelling around your face or jaw that is spreading, or you are having difficulty breathing or swallowing, treat it as a medical emergency and call 999. For advice when it is not life-threatening, NHS 111 can guide you on where to go.

### Will the emergency appointment fix the problem for good?

Not always. Emergency dental care treats pain, infection and trauma quickly, then plans follow-up treatment, so pain relief may come first and the permanent repair may happen at a later visit. Ask the dentist to explain what has been done and what still needs doing.

### Do I need a check-up before treatment?

Almost always. A dentist needs to examine you, and often take X-rays, before recommending treatment.
