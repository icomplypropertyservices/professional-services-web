## How to check you are in safe hands

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Specialist titles such as orthodontist or endodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process. The Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

## What to expect at a crown consultation

A first appointment for a crown is usually about assessment rather than treatment. The dentist will look at the tooth, may take a X-ray to check the root and surrounding bone, and will talk through whether a crown is the right way to protect it. This is your chance to ask about materials and see shade guides against your own teeth.

If you go ahead, the tooth is usually prepared and a temporary crown fitted while the permanent one is made at a dental lab. The fit and bite are checked when it is cemented, and a review appointment lets the dentist adjust anything that feels high or rough. Ask in advance how many visits this will take so you can plan around work or caring commitments.

Because 'near me' matters for crowns, think about how easy it is to get back to the practice if the crown feels uncomfortable after fitting. A dentist slightly further away who explains things clearly may suit you better than the closest one, and that is a judgement only you can make once you have spoken to them.

## FAQs about best private crown near me

### How do I find the best private dentist for a crown near me?

Start by checking that the dentist is registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and in England that the practice is registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC). Then ask about their experience with the type of crown you need and whether they can explain the material and lab choices clearly.

### Is a private crown better than an NHS one?

Private crowns offer a wider choice of materials and labs, including highly aesthetic ceramics, but 'better' depends on your tooth and your priorities. Discuss the options with your dentist so you can weigh up appearance, strength and cost.

### What should I do if I have a problem with a private crown?

First speak to the practice where you had the crown fitted and follow their complaints process. If the issue is not resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### Can I check if a dentist is qualified to fit crowns?

Yes, all dentists must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the register online. If the dentist uses a specialist title, such as prosthodontist, they must also be on a GDC specialist list.

### Will I need X-rays before a crown?

Often yes, because the dentist needs to see the health of the root and the bone around the tooth before deciding on a crown. Ask whether any X-ray charge is included in the quote you receive.

### Can I choose the colour of my crown?

You can discuss shade with your dentist, and they will usually match it to your neighbouring teeth rather than to the brightest white. If the tooth is near the front, ask to see the shade in different lighting before it is finalised.

### What if the crown feels wrong once it is fitted?

Tell the practice promptly so they can check the bite and adjust or remake it if needed. If you are not satisfied after their complaints process, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### Can I see a private dentist and stay with my NHS dentist?

Yes, you can use both. Some patients see a private dentist for specific treatments and keep NHS care for routine check-ups, if their NHS practice agrees.
