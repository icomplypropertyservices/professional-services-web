## Qualifications and registration to look for

Every vet practising in the UK must be registered with the Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons (RCVS), and you can search the RCVS register by name before you book. Veterinary nurses have their own RCVS register too. Many practices also join the voluntary RCVS Practice Standards Scheme, which inspects premises, equipment and clinical processes.

Some vets hold further qualifications such as an RCVS certificate, advanced practitioner status or specialist status in a particular species or discipline. If your animal has a complex or unusual condition, it is reasonable to ask whether the vet treating it holds one of these, or whether they refer to someone who does.

## FAQs about exotic vet near me

### How do I know if a vet is qualified to treat exotic pets?

All vets in the UK must be registered with the Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons (RCVS), and you can check the register by name. Some vets hold further qualifications in zoological medicine or a particular species group, so it is reasonable to ask about their experience and qualifications before booking.

### What should I bring to an appointment with an exotic vet?

Bring photos of your pet's enclosure, including lighting and heating, as many problems relate to environment and diet. Also bring any records of feeding, cleaning and previous treatments, and a fresh sample of droppings if relevant.

### Are exotic vets more expensive than regular vets?

Fees vary between practices and depend on the treatment needed. Exotic pets may require special equipment or medicines, which can affect the cost. You can ask for a written estimate before treatment begins, and it is wise to compare quotes.
