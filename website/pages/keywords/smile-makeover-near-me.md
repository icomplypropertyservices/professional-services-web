## What to understand about smile makeovers

A smile makeover is not a single treatment but a plan that combines procedures such as aligners, whitening, bonding and veneers to reach an agreed result. Planning comes first, and the sequence matters because some treatments work best when done before others. A good dentist will discuss realistic expectations with you rather than promising a perfect outcome.

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA. If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process, and the Dental Complaints Service can help with complaints that are not resolved.

## Smile Makeover Near Me: FAQs

### What is a smile makeover?

A smile makeover is a combination of treatments such as aligners, whitening, bonding and veneers planned together to improve the appearance of your smile. It is tailored to your individual needs and agreed with your dentist.

### How do I choose a dentist for a smile makeover?

Check that the dentist is registered with the General Dental Council (GDC) and, in England, that the practice is registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC). Ask about their experience with the treatments you are considering and whether they can show you examples of their work.

### Is a smile makeover available on the NHS?

Smile makeovers are generally considered cosmetic and are usually provided privately. Some treatments may be available on the NHS if there is a clinical need, but this depends on your individual circumstances and local NHS rules.

### What should I do if I am unhappy with private dental treatment?

Start with the practice's complaints process. If the issue is not resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### Do I need a check-up before treatment?

Almost always. A dentist needs to examine you, and often take X-rays, before recommending treatment.
