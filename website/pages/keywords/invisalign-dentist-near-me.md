## What to understand about Invisalign and finding a local dentist

Invisalign is a brand of clear aligner treatment, and dentists who offer it are trained to plan and monitor aligner treatment, but their experience levels vary. It is not a specialist title, so any registered dentist can provide it, and the quality of the result depends on careful planning and monitoring. You can check that your dentist is registered with the General Dental Council (GDC) on their online register, and in England you can also check whether the practice is registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC).

Private dental treatment is not covered by the NHS in the same way, so costs and what is included will vary between practices. If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process, and the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved. It's sensible to ask about monitoring arrangements and what happens if you need extra aligners or appointments.

## Questions to ask the dentist

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the private dentist is right for you:

- How many Invisalign cases do you treat in a typical month, and how often do you use aligners for cases like mine?
- What monitoring arrangements do you provide during treatment, such as how often I will be seen and who I should contact if I have a problem?
- Does your quote include retainers, follow-up appointments and any additional aligners if my treatment needs adjusting?
- What happens if my teeth do not move as planned, and are there additional costs for extra aligners or appointments?
- Are you registered with the General Dental Council, and is the practice registered with the Care Quality Commission?
- Can you explain the risks and alternatives to Invisalign, including fixed braces or doing nothing, so I can make an informed choice?

## What happens at a first Invisalign consultation

At a first appointment, expect the dentist to look at your teeth and gums, ask what you would like to change, and discuss whether clear aligners are a realistic option for you. They may take photographs, impressions or a scan so they can plan treatment properly, and they should talk you through the alternatives as well as the aligners themselves.

This is also your chance to see how the practice feels day to day, since aligner treatment means returning for monitoring over a period rather than a single visit. Ask who you would see at each appointment and what you should do if an aligner does not fit or you lose one between visits.

You do not have to decide at the first appointment, and it is reasonable to take the quote away and compare it with another local practice. A good consultation should leave you clear about what is involved, what is included and what the total cost is likely to be.

## How to check you are in safe hands

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Specialist titles such as orthodontist or endodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process. The Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

## Invisalign Dentist Near Me: FAQs

### Is Invisalign suitable for everyone?

No, Invisalign is not suitable for everyone. Suitability depends on your dental health, the complexity of your case and how well you can commit to wearing the aligners. A dentist will need to assess your teeth and gums before recommending treatment.

### How do I check if a dentist is qualified to provide Invisalign?

All dentists must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the register online. In England, practices providing regulated healthcare must also be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC). You can ask the practice about the dentist's training and experience with aligner treatment.

### Will I need to wear a retainer after Invisalign?

Yes, teeth can move back after any orthodontic treatment, so retainers are usually needed to hold the new position. Your dentist should explain the retainer options and any costs as part of your treatment plan.

### What should I do if I am unhappy with private dental treatment?

Start by raising your concern with the practice's complaints process. If it is not resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### Do I need a referral from my own dentist to see an Invisalign dentist?

Not usually. Many private practices accept patients directly for an aligner consultation, though it helps to tell them about your dental history and any recent treatment. If you have gum problems or untreated decay, they may ask you to have those dealt with first.

### Can I have Invisalign if I have crowns, veneers or a bridge?

Sometimes, but it depends on the individual case and how the teeth and restorations are positioned. The dentist will need to examine you and may take scans before saying whether aligners are suitable. Be ready to tell them about any existing dental work.

### Can I see a private dentist and stay with my NHS dentist?

Yes, you can use both. Some patients see a private dentist for specific treatments and keep NHS care for routine check-ups, if their NHS practice agrees.
