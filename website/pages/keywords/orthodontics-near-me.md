## What to understand before you choose an orthodontic provider

Orthodontics is a regulated dental specialty. Only dentists and other dental professionals registered with the General Dental Council can practise, and the title orthodontist can only be used by someone on the GDC's specialist list. You can check any professional's registration and specialist status on the GDC register online. In England, private clinics providing regulated healthcare must also be registered with the Care Quality Commission, which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

NHS orthodontic treatment is limited to children with a clear clinical need, so many adults and some children will be seen privately. Retainers after treatment are essential to keep teeth in their new position, and this is an ongoing commitment. If something goes wrong with private treatment, start with the practice's complaints process; the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

## Orthodontics Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need a referral from my dentist to see an orthodontist?

You can often approach an orthodontic practice directly, but some practices prefer a referral from your general dentist. It is worth asking when you enquire. If you are seeking NHS treatment, a referral from your dentist is usually the starting point.

### Is orthodontic treatment available on the NHS?

NHS orthodontic treatment is generally limited to children under a certain age who have a clear clinical need, assessed by a dentist. Adults usually need to pay privately. Your dentist can advise on eligibility.

### What is the difference between an orthodontist and a dentist?

An orthodontist is a dentist who has completed further specialist training in straightening teeth and correcting bites, and is on the GDC specialist list. A general dentist may offer some orthodontic treatments, but only a specialist can call themselves an orthodontist.

### How can I check that a professional is properly registered?

You can check the GDC register online for any dentist or orthodontist. In England, you can also check whether the clinic is registered with the Care Quality Commission; in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland the relevant regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, Healthcare Improvement Scotland and the RQIA respectively.

### Do I need a check-up before treatment?

Almost always. A dentist needs to examine you, and often take X-rays, before recommending treatment.
