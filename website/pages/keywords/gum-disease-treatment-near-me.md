## Understanding gum disease treatment and who provides it

Gum disease is common, and bleeding gums are often an early sign. Smoking worsens gum disease, so stopping can make a real difference to how well treatment works. Treatment can range from a thorough clean by a hygienist to more involved periodontal therapy, and in advanced cases you may be referred to a periodontist, who is a GDC specialist.

All dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

## FAQs about gum disease treatment near me

### Do I need a referral to see a private dentist for gum disease?

No, you can usually contact a private dental practice directly for an assessment. Some practices may ask for details of your dental history, but a referral is not normally required.

### What is the difference between a regular cleaning and periodontal therapy?

A regular cleaning removes plaque and tartar from above the gum line, while periodontal therapy, such as deep cleaning, goes below the gum line to treat infection and pockets around the teeth. The right approach depends on how advanced your gum disease is.

### Can smoking affect my gum disease treatment?

Yes, smoking worsens gum disease and can make treatment less effective because it affects blood flow to the gums and healing. Stopping smoking can improve your gum health and the outcome of treatment.

### How do I check if a dental professional is qualified?

You can check whether a dentist, dental hygienist, dental therapist or dental nurse is registered with the General Dental Council (GDC) by using the GDC register online. Specialist titles such as periodontist can only be used by those on a GDC specialist list.

### Do I need a check-up before treatment?

Almost always. A dentist needs to examine you, and often take X-rays, before recommending treatment.
