## Questions to ask the vet

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Will I usually see the same vet each time I visit, or might I see different vets depending on the day?
- What are your out-of-hours arrangements, and who provides cover when the practice is closed?
- Are you registered with the RCVS, and do you hold any further qualifications in my animal's species or condition?
- Is the practice part of the RCVS Practice Standards Scheme, and what does that mean for the care you provide?
- How do you handle referrals if my animal needs specialist treatment?
- Can you explain the likely costs of treatment upfront, so I know what to expect before we proceed?

## Costs and quotes for an independent private vet

Veterinary fees vary a great deal between practices, regions and ownership groups, and the CMA has looked closely at transparency in this market. A good practice will explain the consultation charge, any likely tests and the range of treatment options before you commit. ## How to check you are in safe hands

Every vet practising in the UK must be registered with the Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons (RCVS), and you can search the RCVS register by name before you book. Veterinary nurses have their own RCVS register too. Many practices also join the voluntary RCVS Practice Standards Scheme, which inspects premises, equipment and clinical processes.

Some vets hold further qualifications such as an RCVS certificate, advanced practitioner status or specialist status in a particular species or discipline. If your animal has a complex or unusual condition, it is reasonable to ask whether the vet treating it holds one of these, or whether they refer to someone who does.

## FAQs: an independent private vet

### How do I check if a vet is properly qualified?

Every vet practising in the UK must be registered with the Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons (RCVS). You can search the RCVS register by name before you book to confirm their registration. Veterinary nurses also have their own RCVS register.

### What does it mean if a practice is independent?

Independent practices are owned by the vets who work there rather than by a large group. This can mean you see the same vet each visit and build a personal relationship with them, which some owners prefer for continuity of care.

### Can I ask for a vet with a special interest in my animal?

Yes, it's reasonable to ask whether the vet holds further qualifications such as an RCVS certificate or specialist status in a particular species or discipline. If your animal has a complex condition, you can also ask whether they refer to someone with that expertise.

### How do I find out about out-of-hours care?

Out-of-hours arrangements vary between practices and may be shared with other local practices. It's worth asking directly how emergencies are handled when the practice is closed, so you know what to do if your animal needs urgent help.

### Do I need to register with a vet before my pet is ill?

It is sensible to register in advance. Registration means the practice has your details and history on file, which saves time if something goes wrong.
