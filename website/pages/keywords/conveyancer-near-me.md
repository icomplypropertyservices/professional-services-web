## Checking conveyancers are qualified and regulated

Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity. It can be carried out by solicitors regulated by the SRA or by licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers (CLC). Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money. In Scotland, property transactions are handled by solicitors under Scottish law.

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme (CQS) is a common accreditation. Check that your chosen firm is on your lender's panel before you instruct them.

## Conveyancer Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need a conveyancer who is local to the property?

Not necessarily, as much of the work can be done remotely. However, a local conveyancer may have useful knowledge of the area and local searches, so it can be worth considering.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a licensed conveyancer?

Both can carry out conveyancing in England and Wales, but solicitors are regulated by the SRA and licensed conveyancers by the CLC. Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow rules on handling client money.

### How do I check if a conveyancer is on my lender's panel?

You can ask the conveyancer directly, or check with your mortgage lender. Many lenders publish their panel lists or can confirm over the phone.

### When should I instruct a conveyancer?

It's best to instruct a conveyancer as early as possible, ideally once your offer is accepted, to avoid delays. They can start work on the legal checks while other parts of the process are ongoing.

### Is it better to choose a conveyancer who is close to the property or close to me?

It depends on your priorities. Some people prefer a conveyancer near their own home for convenience, while others prefer one near the property for local knowledge. The most important factors are regulation, lender panel membership and experience with your type of property.

### Can I use a conveyancer who is not local to the property?

Yes, there is no requirement for a conveyancer to be local to the property. Much of the work is done remotely, and what matters is that they are properly regulated and on your lender's panel if you have a mortgage.
