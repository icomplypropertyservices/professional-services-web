## What happens when you first meet a local accountant

A first meeting with a local accountant is usually a getting-to-know-you conversation rather than a paperwork drop-off. Expect them to ask what you do, how your business or income is structured, and what you already have in place for records and filings. It's your chance to see whether you'd be comfortable sitting down with this person regularly, which is often the real reason people search for someone local.

Bring what you already have, even if it's incomplete: recent bank statements, any correspondence from HMRC, and notes on how you currently keep records. The accountant can then tell you what they'd need from you and which returns and filings their quote would cover. Ask them to set that out clearly, so you can compare like for like with anyone else you speak to.

It also helps to ask how they prefer to work day to day. Some people want a fixed yearly rhythm of accounts and tax returns, while others want someone to call when a decision comes up, such as taking on an employee or changing how the business is set up. A good accountant should help you plan, not only file, so use the first meeting to judge whether that's how they work.

## Qualifications and registration to look for

In the UK, the word "accountant" on its own is not a protected title, so anyone can use it. Titles such as Chartered Accountant (ICAEW, ICAS or Chartered Accountants Ireland), Chartered Certified Accountant (ACCA) and Chartered Management Accountant (CIMA) are protected, and members are bound by their body's ethics, training and complaints rules. Only registered auditors can sign statutory audit reports.

Every practice offering accountancy services must also be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by a professional body or by HMRC. It is reasonable to ask which body supervises a firm, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance, and how complaints are handled.

## Local Accountant Near Me: FAQs

### Why does it matter that an accountant is local?

A local accountant can meet you face to face, understands your area and is easier to visit when you need to go through paperwork together. It also means you can build a working relationship in person if that suits you.

### Is the word "accountant" protected in the UK?

No, on its own it isn't a protected title, so anyone can call themselves an accountant. Protected titles include Chartered Accountant, Chartered Certified Accountant and Chartered Management Accountant, and members are bound by their body's rules.

### What should I have ready before I enquire?

It helps to know what kind of business you run, what you need help with and roughly where you're based. You don't need exact figures to start the conversation.

### How do I compare quotes from local accountants?

Ask exactly which returns and filings each quote covers, so you're comparing like for like. Quotes are price on application, and it's worth checking what's included before you decide.

### Should I choose an accountant before or after I start trading?

Many people speak to an accountant before they start, because decisions about how to set up as a sole trader or limited company affect what's involved later. It's also fine to seek one out after you've begun, if you want help catching up with registration and records.

### Can a local accountant take over from my current one?

Yes, changing accountant is common, and the new practice usually handles the handover of your records and correspondence with HMRC. Ask at the first meeting what they need from you and from your previous accountant to make the switch smooth.

### What if I only need help with one thing, such as a tax return?

That's perfectly reasonable, and it's worth saying so clearly when you enquire. Ask exactly which returns and filings the quote covers, so you know whether it's a one-off piece of work or an ongoing arrangement.

Not always. For simple Self Assessment a qualified, supervised accountant may be enough. For audits, complex group structures or specialist tax work, a chartered or certified firm is often the safer choice. We can match you to either.
