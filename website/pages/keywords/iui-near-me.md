## What to understand about IUI and clinic licensing

IUI involves placing prepared sperm directly into the womb around the time of ovulation. It is less invasive than IVF, but success rates per cycle are generally lower. It is commonly used with donor sperm or for mild fertility issues.

Clinics that provide IUI using donor sperm, or that carry out treatments involving eggs, sperm or embryos, must be licensed by the Human Fertilisation and Embryology Authority (HFEA). The HFEA's Choose a Fertility Clinic tool allows you to check a clinic's inspection ratings, success rates and patient feedback. Simple tests such as blood hormone checks may be offered outside HFEA licensing, but any treatment involving eggs, sperm or embryos should always be at a licensed centre.

## FAQs about IUI near me

### Do I need a referral from my GP to get IUI?

Some clinics accept self-referrals for IUI, while others may require a referral from your GP. It's best to ask the clinic directly when you enquire.

### Is IUI available on the NHS?

IUI may be available on the NHS in some areas, but eligibility criteria vary. You can check with your GP or local integrated care board for details.

### How is IUI different from IVF?

IUI is less invasive than IVF because it involves placing sperm into the womb without egg retrieval or embryo transfer. However, success rates per cycle are generally lower with IUI.

### Can I have IUI if I'm using donor sperm?

Yes, IUI is commonly used with donor sperm. The clinic must be licensed by the HFEA for donor treatment, so always check this before proceeding.

### Am I eligible for NHS fertility treatment?

Eligibility depends on local commissioning rules, including age, how long you have been trying and previous children. Ask your GP; a private clinic can also explain options.
