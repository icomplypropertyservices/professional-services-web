## FAQs: a barrister

### How do I check if a barrister is accredited?

You can check the Bar Standards Board's Barristers' Register to see if a barrister holds a current practising certificate and any specific authorisations. Accreditation for direct public access is shown there, and you can also ask the barrister's chambers directly.

### What does 'accredited' mean for a barrister?

Accreditation usually means a barrister has met additional standards set by a relevant body, often for direct public access or a specialist area. It is not a promise of quality, but it shows the barrister has taken steps to be accountable.

### Can I instruct a barrister directly without a solicitor?

Yes, if the barrister is registered under the Public Access scheme, you can instruct them directly for suitable matters. Not all barristers offer this, so you should check the Barristers' Register or ask the chambers.

### How do I find a barrister near me?

You can search the Bar Standards Board's Barristers' Register by location, or use the Bar Council's directory. Many chambers have online listings of their barristers and their areas of practice. iComply can also introduce you to a suitable barrister based on your needs.

### Does accreditation mean a barrister is the best choice for my case?

No. Accreditation shows a barrister has met specific requirements, often for direct public access, but it is not a ranking or a promise about outcomes. The better guide is whether their specialism matches your legal issue and whether you understand how they will approach it.

### How can I compare barristers if I cannot rely on ratings?

Ask each barrister about their experience in your area of law, their accreditation, how they will communicate with you, and how fees will be agreed for the work. You can also check the Bar Standards Board's Barristers' Register for their practising certificate and direct access authorisation. Comparing those answers gives you a clearer picture than any single label.

### Will I have to travel to see an accredited barrister?

Not necessarily. Barristers work from chambers, and much of the preparatory work can be done by phone, email or video, though court hearings happen wherever your case is listed. It is worth asking each barrister how they usually work with clients before deciding how near they need to be.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a barrister?

Solicitors usually manage a case from start to finish and deal with clients day to day. Barristers specialise in advocacy and specialist advice, and are typically brought in for hearings or difficult legal questions.
