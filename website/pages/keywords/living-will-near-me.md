## Qualifications and registration to look for

Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity in England and Wales, so some will writers are not regulated. Solicitors (SRA) and chartered legal executives (CILEx) are regulated, insured and covered by the Legal Ombudsman. Many specialists are also members of STEP (the Society of Trust and Estate Practitioners) or Solicitors for the Elderly. Applying for a grant of probate on someone else's behalf for a fee is a reserved activity.

When choosing a provider, ask whether they are regulated, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance and where your original will is stored. Scotland has its own rules on succession and confirmation, so make sure your adviser practises in the right jurisdiction.

## Getting a living will right

A living will is often called an advance decision. If it refuses life-sustaining treatment, it has to meet strict legal rules to be valid, so the wording and the way it is signed and witnessed matter a great deal. A specialist can make sure yours is set out in a way that doctors will recognise and follow.

Many people make an advance decision alongside a health and welfare lasting power of attorney, which lets someone you choose make decisions on your behalf when you cannot. These are separate documents doing different jobs, and it is worth discussing both. It also helps to share your advance decision with your GP so it becomes part of your medical record.

## Living Will Near Me: FAQs

### Is a living will the same as an advance decision?

Yes, living will is the everyday name for what the law calls an advance decision to refuse treatment. It sets out treatments you would refuse in future if you lost capacity. If it refuses life-sustaining treatment, it must meet strict rules to be valid.

### Do I need a lasting power of attorney as well?

An advance decision only covers the treatments you refuse, while a health and welfare lasting power of attorney lets someone you choose make wider decisions on your behalf. Many people put both in place. A specialist can explain how they work together in your circumstances.

### Should I tell my GP about my living will?

It is sensible to share a copy with your GP so it can be added to your medical record and seen by those treating you. It also helps to talk it through with the people close to you, so they know what you have decided and why.

### Can I change my mind after making one?

Yes, you can change or cancel an advance decision while you still have capacity, but it needs to be done clearly and the old document should be withdrawn. Ask the specialist how to record any change properly and who should be told.

### What happens if I die without a will?

The intestacy rules decide who inherits, which may not reflect your wishes, especially for unmarried partners and stepchildren. A will lets you choose.
