## Haulage Insurance Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need separate goods in transit cover if I already have vehicle insurance?

Yes, goods in transit cover is usually separate from vehicle insurance. Vehicle insurance typically covers the vehicle itself and third-party liabilities, while goods in transit covers the loads you carry. A broker can confirm what your existing policy includes.

### What is an O-licence and why does it matter for insurance?

An O-licence is the operator licence required for goods vehicles over a certain weight. Insurers need to know you hold one because cover must meet the conditions attached to it. A broker will ask about your licence when arranging haulage insurance.

### Will my drivers' history affect the quote?

Yes, driver history matters for haulage insurance. Points, convictions and claims can affect the premium and the terms offered. It helps to have details of all drivers ready when you speak to a broker.

### How do I check that a broker is properly authorised?

You can check on the FCA register to see if a broker is authorised or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm. Many brokers also belong to BIBA, and some hold Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute.

### Is it better to use a local haulage insurance broker than one further away?

A local broker can visit your premises, see your vehicles and discuss your operation face to face, which can be helpful for complex haulage risks. Distance alone does not decide quality, though, so weigh up their haulage experience and how clearly they explain cover alongside how near they are.

### What should I have ready before I speak to a broker about haulage cover?

Have details of your vehicles, your O-licence, the goods you carry and the driving histories of everyone who will be driving. It also helps to know your current cover and renewal date so the broker can compare like for like. The more you can tell them up front, the more useful the first conversation will be.

### Is a broker more expensive than buying direct?

Not necessarily. Brokers can access insurers and schemes not available directly, and help with claims. The right choice depends on how complex your needs are.
