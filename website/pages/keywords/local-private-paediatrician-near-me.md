## What to understand about private paediatric care

A private paediatrician is a children's doctor who assesses and treats conditions such as allergies, growth, development, asthma and recurring infections. Every doctor practising in the UK must be registered and hold a licence to practise with the General Medical Council (GMC), and you can search the GMC's online register by name. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

A private paediatrician does not replace your NHS GP. A GP referral is often preferred, so it's worth checking whether you need one before you book. Good private clinics will ask for consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice, so your child's records stay complete and any medication is prescribed safely. It's also wise to ask about follow-up arrangements, as some children need a further appointment.

## FAQs about local private paediatrician near me

### Do I need a referral to see a private paediatrician?

A GP referral is often preferred, but some private paediatricians will see children without one. It's best to ask the paediatrician's clinic directly when you enquire, and to check whether they need any information from your NHS GP beforehand.

### Will the private paediatrician tell my NHS GP about the appointment?

Good private clinics will ask for your consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice, so your child's records stay complete. You can ask about this when you book and mention if you'd like your GP to be kept informed.

### What should I bring to a private paediatric appointment?

It's helpful to bring your child's red book, any relevant medical reports or test results, and a list of medications or allergies. This gives the paediatrician a fuller picture and helps them make the most of the consultation.
