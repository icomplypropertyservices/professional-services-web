## Qualifications and registration to look for

"Tax adviser" is not a protected title in the UK. The Chartered Institute of Taxation (CTA qualification) and the Association of Taxation Technicians (ATT) are the main specialist bodies, and many accountants and solicitors also advise on tax. Members of these bodies follow Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation, a shared code covering honesty, confidentiality and dealing with HMRC.

Tax advisers who provide services for a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes. HMRC has also been tightening standards for paid tax agents, so it is reasonable to ask about qualifications, supervision and professional indemnity insurance before you go ahead.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## FAQs: an adviser for HMRC matters

### Can a tax adviser speak to HMRC for me?

Yes. Advisers who deal with HMRC routinely handle letters, compliance checks, penalties and disclosures on a client's behalf, and can negotiate time to pay. You would normally give them authority to act for you.

### Does it matter if my adviser is not near me?

Much of the work with HMRC can be done by phone, post or online, so distance is often not a barrier. Meeting in person can still help if your affairs are complicated or you would prefer to talk things through face to face.

### Will I pay less penalty if I come forward voluntarily?

A voluntary disclosure usually leads to lower penalties than waiting for HMRC to find the problem. The adviser can explain how this applies to your circumstances and help you make the disclosure properly.

### What should I have ready before I speak to an adviser?

Gather the HMRC letters you have received, any reference numbers, and records of the income, expenses or tax concerned. Knowing your deadlines helps the adviser assess how urgently the matter needs attention.

### Should I choose an adviser in my own town or is further afield fine?

For most HMRC matters, correspondence, calls and online dealings mean location is not a barrier. Choose on relevant experience and clear communication first, then use closeness as a tiebreaker if you would prefer to meet in person.

### Can I visit an adviser in person about an HMRC letter?

Many advisers are happy to meet, though arrangements vary between practices. When you enquire, say that you would like a face-to-face appointment and ask where and when that could happen.

### What happens at a first appointment about an HMRC matter?

Expect to go through the letters you have received, explain what has happened so far, and hear how the adviser would approach it. They should set out what they need from you and how their charges will work before you decide whether to go ahead.

### Should I reply to HMRC before speaking to an adviser?

Note the deadline on the letter, but avoid sending a detailed reply before you have advice if the matter is complex. An adviser can often ask HMRC for more time where it is reasonable.
