## Checking mortgage brokers are qualified and regulated

Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and you can check this on the FCA register.

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders, and how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## What happens once you've found your remortgage adviser

The first conversation is usually a fact-find rather than a sales pitch: the adviser will ask about your current lender, your outstanding balance, the rate you're on and when your deal ends, so have your latest mortgage statement to hand. They'll also want to understand your income, your plans for the property and how long you intend to stay on the new deal, because these shape which options are worth comparing.

From there, a good adviser will set out your current lender's product transfer alongside remortgage deals elsewhere, and talk you through the costs on each side — arrangement fees, valuation fees and any early repayment charge that would apply if you leave your existing deal before it ends. Ask them to show the comparison in writing so you can see what you're weighing up rather than relying on a verbal summary.

If you decide to proceed, the adviser handles the application and liaises with the lender and, where needed, a solicitor or conveyancer on the legal side of switching. A remortgage isn't the same as moving home: there's no property purchase, but there is still paperwork to sign and a completion date to agree, so knowing roughly what the process involves helps you plan around it.

## FAQs about best remortgage advisor near me

### How do I check a remortgage adviser is properly authorised?

You can check on the FCA register to see if the adviser or their firm is authorised or an appointed representative. It's a quick step that helps you feel confident before you share your details.

### What's the difference between a product transfer and a remortgage?

A product transfer is a new deal with your current lender, which can be simpler and may not require a new application. A remortgage means switching to a different lender, which might offer a better rate but could involve fees and early repayment charges.

### Will a remortgage adviser near me always offer the cheapest deal?

No, and it's worth being cautious if anyone claims to be the cheapest. The right adviser will explain the options available to you and how they are paid, so you can judge for yourself which deal suits your circumstances.

### When should I start looking for a remortgage adviser?

It's usually sensible to start a few months before your current deal ends, so there's time to compare options and complete any paperwork. Starting early also gives you a chance to ask about early repayment charges and product transfers.

### Do I need to have my remortgage adviser nearby to get a good service?

Not necessarily. Many people prefer a local office for face-to-face appointments, while others are happy with phone and video conversations. What matters more is that the adviser explains your options clearly and is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority, which you can check on the FCA register.

### Can I remortgage before my current deal ends without paying a penalty?

It depends on the terms of your current mortgage. Some lenders let you lock in a new deal a few months ahead without triggering an early repayment charge, but others don't, so it's worth asking your adviser to check the small print before you commit to anything.

### What if I want to borrow more when I remortgage?

Remortgaging can sometimes be used to release equity as well as switch deals, for example to fund home improvements. Tell your adviser this early on, because it changes which lenders and deals are worth comparing and may affect how your affordability is assessed.

### Will speaking to a broker affect my credit score?

An initial conversation does not. Formal applications usually involve a credit search, and the broker should tell you before that happens.
