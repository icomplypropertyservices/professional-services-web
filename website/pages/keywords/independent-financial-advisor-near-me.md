## Qualifications and registration to look for

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Understanding independent versus restricted advice

Independent financial advisers must consider the whole market and are not tied to particular providers. Restricted advisers, on the other hand, focus on a limited range of products or providers. Both can offer good advice, but independent advice gives you access to a wider choice.

You can check that any adviser or firm is authorised on the FCA register. Advisers must also tell you whether their advice is independent or restricted and explain how they charge.

## Choosing an adviser who is genuinely near you

Searching for an independent financial advisor near me often means you would rather sit down face to face than work entirely by phone or online. Local knowledge can be useful, but the more important question is whether the adviser is authorised and independent, which you can check on the FCA register before you contact them.

Distance matters most for the first meeting and any regular reviews. Ask how the adviser usually works with clients who live nearby: whether they visit you at home, whether you go to their office, and whether they offer video or phone appointments for convenience. You should also ask whether they cover your specific need, such as retirement planning or investment advice, since some firms focus on particular areas.

When you speak to more than one local adviser, compare how clearly each explains their independence, their charges and what you will receive in writing. A nearby office is no substitute for a clear explanation of how they are paid, so ask for a written breakdown before you commit. If something goes wrong later, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

## How fees usually work (POA)

Advisers often charge for initial advice, then an ongoing fee for reviews, which may be fixed or a percentage of the money they look after. Firms must set out charges clearly before advising you. The firm quotes you, POA.

Ask for charges in pounds as well as percentages, and check what the ongoing service includes. The FCA's Consumer Duty expects firms to provide fair value.

## When to think twice

Be cautious if you notice any of the following:

- Unclear or hidden charges
- Guaranteed high returns
- Unregulated investments presented as safe
- Not on the FCA register, or a clone firm using a real firm's name

## Independent Financial Advisor Near Me: FAQs

### How do I check that an adviser is authorised?

You can check the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register to see if an adviser or firm is authorised. The register will show whether they are independent or restricted and what permissions they have.

### What is the difference between independent and restricted advice?

Independent advisers must consider the whole market and are not tied to particular providers. Restricted advisers focus on a limited range of products or providers.

### Will I have to pay for the initial meeting?

Some advisers offer a free initial consultation, while others charge for their time from the start. It is best to ask about fees upfront so you know what to expect.

### Can I complain if I am unhappy with the advice?

Yes, you can complain to the firm first. If you are not satisfied with their response, you can take your complaint to the Financial Ombudsman Service. The Financial Services Compensation Scheme may also protect you if an authorised firm fails.

### Does an adviser need to be based in my town to be truly local?

Not necessarily. Some advisers cover a wider area but still meet clients face to face, while others work mainly by phone or video. What matters is that you can reach them easily and that they understand your circumstances, so ask how they usually arrange meetings with clients near you.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent or restricted, and you can also check the FCA register. Independent advisers must consider the whole market and are not tied to particular providers, while restricted advisers focus on a limited range of products or providers.

### What should I have ready for a first meeting with a local adviser?

It helps to gather details of any pensions, investments or policies you already hold, along with a clear idea of what you want to achieve. Write down your questions about charges and independence so you can compare answers from different advisers.
