## Costs and quotes for a life insurance adviser

Brokers are often paid commission by the insurer and sometimes charge a broker fee; they must disclose how they are paid if you ask. Premiums are set by insurers based on risk. The broker quotes you, POA.

Compare cover, excesses and exclusions, not just the premium. The cheapest policy is not always the best value when you need to claim.

## What happens at a first meeting with a local life insurance adviser

A first meeting with a life insurance adviser is mostly a conversation about your circumstances rather than a sales pitch. Expect them to ask about your mortgage and other debts, who depends on your income, and what you would want any payout to cover. They should also explain how they are paid before you go any further, so you can decide whether to carry on.

The adviser will then talk you through the main types of cover — term, decreasing and whole-of-life — and how each might line up with your debts and needs. This is the moment to ask about writing the policy in trust, because that decision affects who receives the money and how quickly. Take your time, ask for anything unclear to be repeated, and do not feel pressured to decide on the day.

Before the meeting ends, ask what happens next and whether you will receive anything in writing setting out the recommendation and the costs. It also helps to ask when you should come back for a review, particularly after a big change such as a new mortgage, a separation or a new child. A good adviser will welcome those questions rather than brush them aside.

## Checking who you are dealing with

Insurance brokers and intermediaries in the UK must be authorised by the FCA, or be appointed representatives of an authorised firm, and you can check them on the FCA register. Many brokers belong to the British Insurance Brokers' Association, and some hold Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute.

A good adviser will take time to understand your demands and needs, explain the key features and exclusions of any policy, and tell you how they are paid. ## FAQs about life insurance advisor near me

### What does a life insurance advisor actually do?

A life insurance adviser recommends term, decreasing or whole-of-life cover to protect your dependants. They should look at your debts and needs, explain the key features and exclusions, and tell you how they are paid.

### How do I check that an advisor is properly authorised?

You can check them on the FCA register before you go ahead, and many also belong to the British Insurance Brokers' Association.

### Why might a policy be written in trust?

Writing a policy in trust can help any payout reach the people you intend without unnecessary delay. Ask your adviser to explain how it would work for your circumstances and whether it suits the cover you choose.

### What should I do if I am unhappy with the advice?

Raise the complaint with the firm first, as they should have a clear process for handling it. If it is not resolved to your satisfaction, you can take the matter to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

### Do I need to have my mortgage details ready before I speak to a local adviser?

It helps to know the rough size of your mortgage and any other significant debts, because the adviser will use that to match cover to your needs. You do not need exact figures to start the conversation, but having the paperwork nearby makes the first meeting more useful.

### Is a local life insurance adviser different from a general insurance broker?

A general insurance broker may deal with home, car or business cover, while a life insurance adviser focuses on protection for your dependants through term, decreasing or whole-of-life policies.
