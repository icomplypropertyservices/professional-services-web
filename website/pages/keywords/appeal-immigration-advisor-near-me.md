## One key thing to understand about immigration appeals

Not every refusal comes with a right of appeal. The type of decision and your circumstances determine whether you can appeal to the First-tier Tribunal, request an administrative review or seek a judicial review. Deadlines are very short, so it's important to act quickly.

It is a criminal offence to give immigration advice in the UK unless you are qualified and regulated. Advisers must be registered with the Immigration Advice Authority (IAA, formerly the OISC) at the right level for your matter, or be a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive authorised by their own regulator. Always check the IAA's adviser finder or the SRA register before sharing documents or paying.

## How to check you are in safe hands

Check the IAA's adviser finder or the SRA register before you share documents or pay.

IAA advisers are registered at levels that limit what they can do, from basic applications to appeals and representation. Ask what level an adviser holds and whether it covers your type of case.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## FAQs about appeal immigration advisor near me

### Do I always have a right of appeal against an immigration refusal?

No, not every refusal carries a right of appeal. The refusal letter should state whether you can appeal, and if not, whether other options like administrative review or judicial review are available. An adviser can help you understand your specific case.

### How quickly do I need to act after a refusal?

Deadlines for appeals and reviews are very short, often just days or a few weeks. It's important to seek advice as soon as possible so you don't miss the deadline.

### Can I introduce new evidence in my appeal?

Fresh evidence may help your appeal, but there are rules about when and how it can be considered. An adviser can tell you what evidence is relevant and how to present it.

### How do I check that an immigration adviser is properly regulated?

You can check the Immigration Advice Authority's adviser finder or the Solicitors Regulation Authority register. Always do this before sharing documents or paying any money.

### Can I switch advisers if I've already started an appeal?

Yes, you can change advisers, but it's important to do so quickly so the new adviser has time to review your case before any deadline. You should inform your current adviser and request your file, and check whether any fees are outstanding.

### What should I bring to my first appointment with an appeals adviser?

Bring your refusal letter, any other correspondence from the Home Office, and any evidence you think is relevant, such as new documents that weren't considered before. It also helps to bring a note of your immigration history and any deadlines you're aware of.

### Can an adviser speed up my application?

An adviser cannot control Home Office processing times, but a complete, well-prepared application avoids avoidable delays. Some routes have paid priority services.
