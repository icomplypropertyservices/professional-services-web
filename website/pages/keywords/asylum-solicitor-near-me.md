## Checking that an adviser can legally help with asylum

In the UK it is a criminal offence to give immigration advice unless the person is qualified and regulated. That means they must be registered with the Immigration Advice Authority (formerly the OISC) at the right level for your matter, or be a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive authorised by their own regulator. You can check the IAA's adviser finder or the Solicitors Regulation Authority register before you share documents or pay anything.

IAA advisers are registered at levels that limit what they can do, from basic applications up to appeals and representation, so it is worth asking what level they hold and whether it covers your type of case. Legal aid often covers asylum work, so ask early whether it applies to you. Interviews are key moments in a claim, and appeals have short deadlines, so raise anything time-sensitive straight away.

## Asylum Solicitor Near Me: FAQs

### Can anyone help me with an asylum claim?

No. Giving immigration advice in the UK without being qualified and regulated is a criminal offence. The person helping you should be registered with the Immigration Advice Authority at the right level, or be a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive authorised by their own regulator.

### How do I check that an asylum solicitor near me is properly regulated?

You can use the Immigration Advice Authority's adviser finder, or the Solicitors Regulation Authority register if they are a solicitor. It is sensible to check before you share documents or pay anything.

### Does legal aid cover asylum work?

Legal aid often covers asylum cases, though whether it applies depends on your circumstances and the work involved. Ask the professional early on whether legal aid may be available to you.

### What should I do if I have an interview or appeal deadline coming up?

Interviews are key moments in a claim and appeals have short deadlines, so tell iComply and the professional about any date you have been given straight away. Being clear about timing helps them judge whether they can help in time.

### Does an asylum solicitor near me have to be in the same town?

Not necessarily, but it helps if they are close enough that you can attend appointments in person without difficulty. Ask how they handle meetings and whether they can see you face to face when it counts, such as before an interview or hearing.

### Can I change professionals partway through my asylum claim?

It is possible to move to a different regulated professional, but it can take time and your file has to be transferred. If you are thinking about it, raise it early and be clear about any interview or appeal date already set, so nothing is missed.

### What happens at a first appointment about asylum?

Expect to go through your history and the timeline of your claim, and to be asked what evidence you already have. The professional should explain what they can do, whether legal aid may apply, and what they need from you next — and you should feel able to ask about their regulation and level of registration.

### Can an adviser speed up my application?

An adviser cannot control Home Office processing times, but a complete, well-prepared application avoids avoidable delays. Some routes have paid priority services.
