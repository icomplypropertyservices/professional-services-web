## Costs and quotes for a family solicitor

Legal fees depend on the type of matter, its complexity and who does the work. Firms commonly charge hourly rates, fixed fees for defined stages, or (in some claims) conditional fee arrangements. Regulated firms in England and Wales must publish pricing information for certain services and give you clear costs information at the outset. The firm will give you a POA quote.

Ask for a written estimate and for an explanation of what is not included, such as court fees, barristers' fees or experts. Ask too whether legal expenses insurance or legal aid might cover some or all of the cost.

## What happens at a first meeting with a local family solicitor

A first appointment is usually a chance to explain your situation in your own words while the solicitor listens and asks questions about what has happened so far, including any arrangements already in place for children, money or housing. They can then outline the options open to you and what each might involve, so you leave with a clearer picture rather than a decision forced on you. It helps to bring any relevant paperwork, such as letters, court forms or existing agreements, so the discussion is grounded in your actual circumstances.

You can also use that meeting to ask how the solicitor prefers to work. Some family solicitors encourage a non-confrontational approach, and Resolution members commit to this, which may suit you if you hope to avoid a drawn-out dispute. Ask whether they would suggest mediation or another way of reaching agreement before going to court, and how they would keep you informed as things progress.

Because quotes are on application, ask at the first meeting how the solicitor charges and what that means for your matter, including whether legal aid may be available where there is domestic abuse. A good first meeting should leave you understanding both your legal position and what the next step would be, without pressure to instruct on the spot.

## One key thing to understand when looking for a family solicitor near me

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland, solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people. ## Family Solicitor Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need a family solicitor near me, or can I use one further away?

You can use a family solicitor who is not geographically close, especially if you are comfortable with remote meetings. However, some people prefer a local solicitor for face-to-face meetings and familiarity with the local courts. When searching for a family solicitor near me, consider what matters most to you in terms of convenience and accessibility.

### What is the difference between a family solicitor and a general solicitor?

A family solicitor specialises in family law matters such as divorce, separation, children, and domestic abuse, while a general solicitor may handle a wider range of legal issues. For family matters, it is usually better to choose a solicitor who focuses on family law. You can ask about their experience and specialisation when you speak to them.

### How can I check if a family solicitor is properly regulated?

In England and Wales, you can check the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA) register online to confirm that a solicitor or firm is regulated. In Scotland, check with the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland with the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Always verify regulation before instructing a solicitor.

### Will I be able to get legal aid for my family law matter?

Legal aid may be available for certain family law matters, particularly where there is domestic abuse, but eligibility depends on your circumstances and the type of case. You should ask the solicitor directly about legal aid eligibility and any other funding options. They can advise you on what support may be available.

### Should I choose a family solicitor near me or one further away?

A local solicitor can be convenient for face-to-face meetings and may know the courts in your area, but distance matters less if you are comfortable with phone or video appointments. Think about how you would prefer to meet and how often you may need to attend in person before deciding.

### Can I bring someone with me to the first appointment?

Many family solicitors are happy for you to bring a friend or family member for support, though it is worth asking beforehand. If the matter involves domestic abuse, tell the firm when you book so they can discuss what arrangements would help you feel safe.

### What if my former partner wants to use the same solicitor?

A solicitor cannot act for both of you where your interests conflict, which is usually the case in family matters. If you mention that your former partner has already approached a particular firm, they can tell you whether they are able to take your matter on.

### Can I get legal aid?

Legal aid is still available for some criminal, family (especially where there is domestic abuse), housing and immigration matters, subject to means and merits tests. A matched firm can tell you whether you are likely to qualify.
