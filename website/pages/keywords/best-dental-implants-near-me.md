## Checking private dentists are qualified and regulated

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Specialist titles such as orthodontist or endodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process. The Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

iComply is not a regulator and does not provide the professional service itself. We introduce you to professionals who hold their own registrations, and we encourage you to check them on the public register.

## FAQs about best dental implants near me

### How do I judge which dentist is best for dental implants near me?

Check that the dentist is registered with the General Dental Council (GDC) and, in England, that the practice is registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC). Ask about the implant system used, the dentist's experience, and the aftercare provided. A thorough assessment and clear explanation of your options are good signs of quality care.

### What should I expect during the implant process?

Implant treatment usually takes several months. It begins with an assessment of your bone quality and suitability, followed by placement of the implant, a healing period, and then fitting of the crown, bridge or denture. Your dentist will explain each stage and the timeline for your case.

### Will my bone quality affect whether I can have implants?

Yes, bone quality is an important factor in implant suitability. If your bone is thin or soft, you may need a bone graft or other procedure before implants can be placed. Your dentist will assess this using scans and discuss your options.

### What if I have a problem with private dental treatment?

If something goes wrong, start with the practice's complaints process. If the issue is not resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### Should I choose the nearest practice or travel further for implants?

Convenience matters because implant treatment involves several appointments over a number of months, including aftercare. A practice that is easy for you to reach makes it simpler to keep those appointments. Balance this against how confident you feel about the dentist's assessment and explanation of your options.

### How can I compare implant quotes fairly?

Ask each practice what the quote covers, including the implant system, any scans or extra procedures, the crown, bridge or denture on top, and follow-up appointments. A lower headline figure may not include everything you need. Having the details in writing lets you compare the full picture rather than just the total.

### What happens at a first implant consultation?

The dentist will usually examine your mouth, ask about your general health and any missing teeth, and may take scans to assess your bone. They should then explain whether implants are suitable for you and what the treatment would involve. It is a good moment to ask about the implant system used and the costs before deciding.

### Can I see a private dentist and stay with my NHS dentist?

Yes, you can use both. Some patients see a private dentist for specific treatments and keep NHS care for routine check-ups, if their NHS practice agrees.
