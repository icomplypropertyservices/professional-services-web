## What to understand about expat mortgage advice

Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and you can check this on the FCA register. Advisers typically hold a qualification such as CeMAP.

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders, and how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

## Expat Mortgage Broker Near Me: FAQs

### Can I get a mortgage in the UK if I live abroad?

Yes, many lenders offer mortgages to expats, but the criteria vary. A broker can help you understand which lenders are likely to accept your situation.

### What is a remote ID check and why is it needed?

Remote ID checks allow brokers and lenders to verify your identity without you being physically present in the UK. This is a standard part of the application process for expats.

### How does currency affect my mortgage application?

Lenders may assess your income in the currency you earn and convert it to sterling, which can affect how much you can borrow. They may also consider exchange rate fluctuations.

### Will I need to pay a fee to the broker?

Brokers must tell you how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. You should ask for this in writing before proceeding.

### Does the broker need to be near my UK property or near where I live abroad?

Neither is essential, because expat applications are handled remotely. What matters is that the broker is authorised in the UK and experienced with applicants living in your country of residence.

### Can I speak to a broker at a time that suits my time zone?

Many brokers working with expat clients will arrange calls outside standard UK office hours. Ask about this early so you know how communication will work before you commit.
