## What people searching for executor assistance usually need

Most people looking for executor assistance want practical help with the paperwork and decisions that follow a death, without giving up control of the estate.

- You have been named as an executor in a will and want to understand what the role actually involves before you start.
- The estate includes property, bank accounts or investments and you want help with valuations and the paperwork probate requires.
- You are unsure about inheritance tax and want someone to explain what has to be reported and paid, and when.
- You would like a professional to take on some or all of the work of applying for the grant of probate, while you stay informed.
- You are acting alongside other executors, or you live far from the deceased, and the practical side of gathering information is proving difficult.

## Checking wills and probate specialists are qualified and regulated

Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity in England and Wales, so some will writers are not regulated. Solicitors (SRA) and chartered legal executives (CILEx) are regulated, insured and covered by the Legal Ombudsman. Many specialists are also members of STEP (the Society of Trust and Estate Practitioners) or Solicitors for the Elderly. Applying for a grant of probate on someone else's behalf for a fee is a reserved activity.

When choosing a provider, ask whether they are regulated, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance and where your original will is stored. Scotland has its own rules on succession and confirmation, so make sure your adviser practises in the right jurisdiction.

## FAQs about executor assistance near me

### Can I get help with only part of the executor work?

Yes. You can choose how much help you need, from a one-off conversation about the forms to handing over the whole administration. A specialist can explain which parts they can take on and which decisions stay with you as executor.

### Can I step back as an executor if I no longer want the role?

Executors can step back before acting, but once you have started dealing with the estate it is more complicated. Speak to a specialist promptly if you are unsure, because the options depend on what you have already done.

### Do I need to keep records of what I spend on the estate?

Yes. Keep receipts for expenses, as they may need to be accounted for when the estate is finalised. A specialist can tell you which costs can properly be met from the estate.

### Does it matter where the professional is based if I want help near me?

It can, because the estate is usually dealt with where the deceased lived and where the assets are. Ask about jurisdiction as well as location, particularly if the deceased lived in Scotland, which has its own rules on succession and confirmation.

### What happens if I die without a will?

The intestacy rules decide who inherits, which may not reflect your wishes, especially for unmarried partners and stepchildren. A will lets you choose.
