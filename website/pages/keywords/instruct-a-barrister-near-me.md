## How fees usually work (POA)

Barristers usually agree a fee for a defined piece of work, such as a written advice, a conference or attendance at a hearing, often negotiated through their clerk. Fees reflect seniority, specialism and the time needed. The chambers will quote you, POA.

Ask clerks to confirm the fee in writing, what it covers and what happens if the hearing runs longer or is adjourned. Under public access, you may need to handle some administrative tasks a solicitor would normally do.

## FAQs: how to instruct a barrister

### Can I instruct a barrister directly without a solicitor?

Yes, if the barrister is authorised under the Public Access scheme and your matter is suitable. You can check the Barristers' Register to see if a barrister is registered for direct access.

### How do I find a barrister near me?

You can search online for barristers' chambers in your area or use the Barristers' Register to find barristers by location and specialism. iComply can also introduce you to a suitable barrister based on your needs.

### What should I do before contacting a barrister?

Organise your documents chronologically and be clear about what you need. Write a brief summary of your case and any key dates, such as hearings or deadlines.

### Are barristers regulated?

Yes, barristers in England and Wales are regulated by the Bar Standards Board (BSB). You can check the Barristers' Register to confirm a barrister's practising status and authorisation.

### Does a barrister have to be near me to take my case?

Not necessarily. Barristers can often work remotely or travel, and what matters most is their experience in your area of law. Ask the clerks where the barrister is based and how they would handle meetings, hearings or paperwork in your situation.

### Should I choose a barrister or a solicitor for a local matter?

Solicitors usually handle the day-to-day running of a case and can instruct a barrister on your behalf, while barristers specialise in advocacy and written opinions. For suitable matters you may be able to instruct a barrister directly under the Public Access scheme, so it helps to be clear about what you need before you approach anyone.

### What happens at a first meeting with a barrister?

The barrister will usually want to hear your account, read your documents and discuss the strengths and risks of your position. Come prepared with a clear summary, key dates and any correspondence, and ask what the next steps and likely fees will be.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a barrister?

Solicitors usually manage a case from start to finish and deal with clients day to day. Barristers specialise in advocacy and specialist advice, and are typically brought in for hearings or difficult legal questions.
