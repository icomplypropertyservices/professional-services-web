## Questions to ask the professional

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the recruitment agency is right for you:

- How do you usually find candidates for roles like mine, and what is your approach to challenging the brief?
- Can you provide market salary data for similar roles in my area?
- How often will you update me on progress, and what is your preferred way to communicate?
- What are your terms of business, including your fees and any charges I should be aware of?
- Are you a member of the Recruitment and Employment Confederation or APSCo, and what does that mean for how you work?
- For temporary roles, how do you handle pay, holiday and right-to-work checks?

## How to check you are in safe hands

Employment agencies and businesses in the UK must follow the Conduct of Employment Agencies and Employment Businesses Regulations, enforced by the Employment Agency Standards Inspectorate. Agencies cannot usually charge work-seekers for finding them work. Membership of the Recruitment and Employment Confederation (REC) or APSCo shows commitment to an industry code.

Agencies supplying temporary workers have obligations on pay, holiday and right-to-work checks. Clear terms of business for employers, and clear contracts for workers, are essential.

"Consultant" is a broad title that anyone can use. Check what qualifications and registrations sit behind it, and whether the work you need is regulated.

## FAQs: a recruitment consultant

### Do I have to pay a recruitment consultant if I'm looking for work?

Employment agencies in the UK cannot usually charge work-seekers for finding them work, so as a candidate you should not expect to pay a fee. However, it's always wise to check the terms before you sign anything.

### What should I look for in a recruitment consultant near me?

Look for someone who understands your local job market and takes the time to understand your needs. A good consultant will challenge your brief and provide market salary data, and you can ask about their communication style and industry memberships.

### How do I know if a recruitment agency is reputable?

Check whether the agency is a member of a recognised trade body such as the Recruitment and Employment Confederation (REC) or APSCo, which shows a commitment to an industry code. You can also ask about their terms of business and how they handle pay and right-to-work checks for temporary workers.

### Can iComply help me find a recruitment consultant for temporary staff?

Yes, iComply can introduce you to a suitable UK recruitment professional who deals with temporary placements. They will quote you directly, with prices on application, and you can discuss your specific needs with them.

### What does "near me" really mean for a recruitment consultant?

It usually means you want someone familiar with your local job market, and often someone you can meet in person. Ask the consultant how much of their work is in your area and whether they visit employers and candidates locally.

### Should I choose the consultant who quotes the lowest fee?

Not necessarily, because the cheapest quote may not reflect the level of service or the specialist knowledge your role needs. Compare what each consultant offers, how they communicate and their terms of business before deciding.

### Can I speak to more than one consultant before deciding?

Yes, and it can help you compare approaches and quotes. Just be upfront with each one about what you are doing so nobody duplicates work on your behalf.

### What is a rebate period?

An agreed period after a permanent hire during which the agency refunds part of its fee or finds a replacement if the person leaves.
