## Health comes before cosmetics

Cosmetic dentistry is still dentistry, so your general oral health matters first. A good cosmetic dentist will check your teeth and gums before discussing whitening, veneers or aligners, because healthy teeth and gums give the best and safest results.

Whitening must be done by a dental professional — dentists, dental hygienists and dental therapists are registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales it is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

## What happens at a cosmetic consultation

A cosmetic consultation is a conversation before any treatment. The dentist will look at your teeth and gums, ask what you would like to change and talk through which options could suit you, such as whitening, bonding, veneers, aligners or crowns. It is a chance to ask questions rather than a commitment to go ahead.

You may be offered a check-up or X-rays first, and the dentist should explain the risks, the aftercare and what the treatment involves. Ask to see examples of their own cosmetic cases so you can judge the results for yourself, and ask who will carry out each stage of the work.

Before you book, compare a few local practices on what they offer and how they explain things, not just on price. Quotes are price on application, so ask what the total includes, such as follow-up visits, and get it in writing. If you are unhappy with private treatment later, start with the practice's complaints process, and the Dental Complaints Service can help if it is not resolved.

## Warning signs to watch for

Be cautious if you notice any of the following:

- Unclear arrangements if something fails
- No discussion of risks or alternatives
- No written treatment plan or costs
- A dentist you cannot find on the GDC register

## Cosmetic Dentist Near Me: FAQs

### Is teeth whitening safe if I do it at home?

Whitening must be done by a dental professional, so talk to a dentist before using any whitening product. They can check your teeth and gums and advise on the safest option for you.

### Do I need a check-up before cosmetic treatment?

Yes, a cosmetic dentist will usually want to check your teeth and gums first. Health comes before cosmetics, and healthy teeth and gums give the best results.

### What can I do if I am unhappy with private dental treatment?

Start with the practice's complaints process. If it is not resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### How do I check a cosmetic dentist's registration?

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC). You can check the GDC register online, and specialist titles such as orthodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list.

### Will the dentist pressure me to have treatment at the consultation?

A consultation should be for information and assessment, not a sales pitch. You can take time to think about the options and ask for a written quote before deciding anything.

### Can I have cosmetic work if I haven't seen a dentist in a while?

Yes, but expect the dentist to check your teeth and gums first. Health comes before cosmetics, so any problems may need treating before whitening, veneers or aligners are discussed.

### What should I bring to a first appointment?

Bring a note of what you would like to change about your teeth, any current sensitivity or gum problems, and details of past dental treatment. Mention any preferences, such as a practice that is easy to reach from home or work.

### Can I see a private dentist and stay with my NHS dentist?

Yes, you can use both. Some patients see a private dentist for specific treatments and keep NHS care for routine check-ups, if their NHS practice agrees.
