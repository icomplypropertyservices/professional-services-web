## How to check you are in safe hands

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Specialist titles such as orthodontist or endodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

## What happens at a first private appointment

A first appointment with a private dentist is usually longer than an NHS check-up, giving you time to explain what has prompted your search and to ask about anything you have been putting off. The dentist will examine your teeth and gums and may take x-rays or photographs so you can see what they are describing. You should leave with a clear picture of your mouth and what, if anything, needs doing.

If treatment is needed, ask for the plan in writing and itemised, so each stage and its cost is set out before you agree to anything. This is also the moment to ask whether part of the care could be provided on the NHS and what the difference in cost and timing would be. Nothing should be carried out until you have had the chance to think it over and ask questions.

It helps to bring a list of any medicines you take, the name of your previous practice if you have one, and any concerns about pain or anxiety. If you are nervous, say so at the start so the appointment can be paced around you. Being open about what you can afford also lets the dentist suggest options in an order that suits you.

## FAQs about private dentist near me

### How do I check that a private dentist is properly registered?

You can search the General Dental Council register online for any dentist, hygienist, therapist or nurse.

### What should a treatment plan from a private dentist include?

Ask for an itemised plan so you can see each stage of treatment and its cost before you commit. This makes it easier to compare what different practices are proposing and to ask questions.

### What can I do if I am unhappy with private dental treatment?

Start with the practice's own complaints process. If that does not resolve things, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### Is a membership plan worth considering?

Membership plans spread the cost of routine care such as check-ups and hygiene visits across the year. Ask exactly what is covered and whether any treatment is charged separately before you decide.

### Will a private dentist see me if I am already registered with an NHS dentist elsewhere?

Yes, you can see a private dentist while remaining registered with an NHS practice, though it is worth telling both practices so your records stay clear. Ask the private practice how they will share information with your existing dentist if treatment is needed.

### Do I have to accept everything on the treatment plan?

No. An itemised plan is there so you can consider each part and decide what to go ahead with and when. You can ask for the most urgent work to be done first and leave the rest for later.

### Can I see a private dentist and stay with my NHS dentist?

Yes, you can use both. Some patients see a private dentist for specific treatments and keep NHS care for routine check-ups, if their NHS practice agrees.
