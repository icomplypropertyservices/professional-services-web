## How to check you are in safe hands

It is a criminal offence to give immigration advice in the UK unless you are qualified and regulated. Advisers must be registered with the Immigration Advice Authority (IAA, formerly the OISC) at the right level for your matter, or be a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive authorised by their own regulator. Check the IAA's adviser finder or the SRA register before you share documents or pay.

IAA advisers are registered at levels that limit what they can do, from basic applications to appeals and representation. Ask what level an adviser holds and whether it covers your type of case.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Choosing between local and remote business visa advice

Searching for a business visa advisor near me suggests you would rather deal with someone you could meet face to face, perhaps because your plans involve documents, signatures or a sponsor who is also local. That instinct is reasonable, but it is worth knowing that many people are advised perfectly well by professionals based elsewhere in the UK, by phone, email or video. The important question is not distance but whether the adviser is regulated for the type of business visa matter you have.

When you compare options, ask each adviser what share of their work involves business visits, sponsored work, global business mobility or founder routes, and whether they regularly deal with employers as well as individuals. If your case involves a sponsor licence, check that the adviser is comfortable advising the employer side too, since the duties fall on the business rather than on you alone. On cost, quotes are price on application, so ask what the fee covers and whether it includes preparing documents, corresponding with the Home Office and any follow-up after a decision.

If meeting in person matters to you, say so when you enquire and give the town or city you can reasonably reach, rather than assuming the nearest adviser is the right fit. A short first conversation usually covers your purpose in the UK, your immigration history, any sponsor or endorsement involved and the documents you already hold. From there the adviser should explain the route that fits, what they would need from you and how they will keep you updated, so you can decide whether to go ahead.

## Business Visa Advisor Near Me: FAQs

### Can I work in the UK on a business visit?

No, business visitors cannot work in the UK. A business visit allows certain activities like meetings or conferences, but not employment. If you plan to work, you will need a different visa route, and an adviser can explain the options.

### Do I need endorsement for a founder route?

Yes, founder routes need endorsement from an approved body before you can apply. An adviser can help you understand the endorsement process and prepare your application.

### Does my UK employer need a sponsor licence?

Yes, if you are coming to the UK for sponsored work, your employer must hold a valid sponsor licence. The employer must apply for the licence and meet certain duties, and an adviser can guide them through this.

### How can I check that an adviser is properly regulated?

You can check the Immigration Advice Authority's adviser finder or the SRA register for solicitors. Always confirm the adviser's registration and level before sharing documents or paying any fees.

### Do I have to use an adviser who is physically near me?

No. What matters legally is that the adviser is properly regulated for your type of case, not how close they are. Many business visa matters are handled remotely, though you may prefer to meet in person for documents or a sponsor meeting.

### Can one adviser help both me and my UK employer with a sponsor licence?

Some advisers work with businesses as well as individuals, so it is worth asking whether they can advise on both sides. The sponsor licence duties sit with the employer, so clarity about who is being advised and on what is important from the start.

### Can an adviser speed up my application?

An adviser cannot control Home Office processing times, but a complete, well-prepared application avoids avoidable delays. Some routes have paid priority services.
