## What to understand when choosing a dentist near you

All dentists in the UK must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the register online. If you're in England and considering a private clinic that provides regulated healthcare, you can also check whether it is registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, different regulators apply.

Private dental treatment is not covered by the NHS, so you'll pay for your care. It's important to ask for a written treatment plan before you start, so you know what will be done and what it will cost. If something goes wrong, the practice's complaints process is the first step, and the Dental Complaints Service can help with unresolved private dental complaints.

## How to check you are in safe hands

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Specialist titles such as orthodontist or endodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process. The Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

## Dentist Near Me: FAQs

### How do I check if a dentist is properly qualified?

You can check the General Dental Council (GDC) register online to confirm that a dentist is registered. If you're considering a private clinic in England, you can also check whether it is registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC).

### What should I do if I have a dental emergency?

If you have severe pain, bleeding, or swelling, contact a dentist as soon as possible. For urgent medical advice, you can call NHS 111, and in a life-threatening emergency, call 999.

### Will I know the cost before treatment starts?

A private dentist should provide a written treatment plan with costs before you agree to treatment. Prices are on application, so ask for a clear quote.

### What can I do if I'm unhappy with private dental treatment?

Start by raising your concerns with the practice's complaints process. If the issue isn't resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### Do I need a check-up before treatment?

Almost always. A dentist needs to examine you, and often take X-rays, before recommending treatment.
