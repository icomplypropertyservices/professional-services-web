## Comparing dental clinics fairly before you decide

A clinic that calls itself a centre or a practice tells you little on its own, so it helps to compare like with like. A single-dentist practice and a larger clinic with hygienists, specialists and in-house imaging can both be right for different needs, and the size of the team matters more than any label when you have a particular treatment in mind.

Rather than ranking clinics by reputation, judge each one on what you can check for yourself: whether the dentists and hygienists are on the General Dental Council register, whether the clinic is registered with the relevant regulator for your nation, and whether the opening hours and emergency arrangements fit your life. These are concrete things you can confirm before you book.

It is also worth asking how a clinic handles referrals. If your treatment needs a specialist such as an orthodontist or endodontist, ask whether that care is available in-house or whether you would be referred on, and check that anyone using a specialist title appears on a GDC specialist list. That way you know what to expect from the first visit.

## Typical timescales

Many private practices can see new patients within days, and urgent pain can often be seen the same or next day. Longer courses of treatment such as implants or aligners take months. For swelling that affects breathing or swallowing, seek emergency care at once.

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Specialist titles such as orthodontist or endodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process. The Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

Clinics vary from single-practitioner rooms to multi-specialist centres. Ask who will see you, their registration and what facilities are on site.

## Best Dental Clinic Near Me: FAQs

### How do I check that a dental clinic is properly regulated?

You can check that dentists, hygienists, therapists and nurses are registered with the General Dental Council (GDC) on their online register. In England, private clinics providing regulated healthcare must also be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which publishes inspection reports and ratings.

### What should I do if I have a problem with private dental treatment?

Start by raising it with the practice through their complaints process. If it is not resolved, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints.

### Can I get emergency dental care from a private clinic?

Many private clinics keep slots for urgent problems, but availability varies. It is worth asking when you contact them, and if you need urgent help outside opening hours, you can call NHS 111 for advice.

### Will I know the cost before treatment starts?

A reputable private clinic should give you a written treatment plan and estimate before you commit. Ask for this at your consultation so you understand the fees.

### Does a bigger dental clinic mean better care?

Not necessarily. Larger clinics may offer specialists and imaging under one roof, which can be convenient, but a smaller practice may suit you better if you value continuity with one dentist. Judge each clinic on the treatment you need and how comfortable you feel there.

### How can I compare clinics without relying on rankings?

Look at practical things: opening hours, whether emergency slots are kept, how costs and treatment plans are explained, and whether the clinicians are on the GDC register. You can also ask about the practice's complaints process before you commit.

### What if my treatment needs a specialist?

Ask the clinic whether specialist care is available in-house or whether you would be referred. Specialist titles such as orthodontist or endodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list, so you can check that yourself.

### Can I see a private dentist and stay with my NHS dentist?

Yes, you can use both. Some patients see a private dentist for specific treatments and keep NHS care for routine check-ups, if their NHS practice agrees.
