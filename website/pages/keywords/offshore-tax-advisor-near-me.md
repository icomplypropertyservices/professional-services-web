## How to check you are in safe hands

"Tax adviser" is not a protected title in the UK. The Chartered Institute of Taxation (CTA qualification) and the Association of Taxation Technicians (ATT) are the main specialist bodies, and many accountants and solicitors also advise on tax. Members of these bodies follow Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation, a shared code covering honesty, confidentiality and dealing with HMRC.

Tax advisers who provide services for a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes. HMRC has also been tightening standards for paid tax agents, so it is reasonable to ask about qualifications, supervision and professional indemnity insurance before you go ahead.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Understanding offshore tax and disclosure

HMRC receives information about offshore accounts and income through international agreements, so it is increasingly aware of overseas assets. If you have unreported offshore income, using a disclosure facility can reduce penalties compared with waiting for HMRC to contact you. Trusts abroad have complex UK rules, so specialist advice is often needed.

The Chartered Institute of Taxation and the Association of Taxation Technicians are the main specialist bodies, and their members follow Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation. Tax advisers who charge a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, so it is reasonable to ask about qualifications, supervision and professional indemnity insurance before you go ahead.

## Red flags when choosing an offshore tax adviser

Be cautious if you notice any of the following:

- Fees linked to a percentage of tax "saved" without clear terms
- Schemes marketed as guaranteed or risk-free tax savings
- Advice that relies on keeping information from HMRC
- Pressure to act quickly without written advice

## FAQs: an offshore tax adviser

### Do I need an offshore tax advisor if I have a small amount of foreign income?

It depends on your circumstances. Even small amounts of foreign income may need to be declared, and a tax adviser can tell you whether you have a reporting obligation. They can also help you avoid mistakes that could lead to penalties.

### What is a disclosure facility and how can it help?

A disclosure facility is a way of telling HMRC about previously unreported income or gains. Using one can reduce penalties compared with waiting for HMRC to contact you. An offshore tax adviser can explain which facility suits your situation.

### Can I use a local tax adviser for offshore matters?

Yes, many tax advisers handle offshore matters and you can choose someone near you if you prefer face-to-face meetings. However, offshore tax can be complex, so it is worth checking that the adviser has relevant experience. iComply introduces you to a suitable professional based on your needs.

### How do I know if a tax adviser is properly qualified?

Tax adviser is not a protected title, so qualifications vary. Look for membership of a recognised body such as the Chartered Institute of Taxation or the Association of Taxation Technicians, and ask about their experience with offshore matters. It is also reasonable to ask about supervision for anti-money-laundering purposes and professional indemnity insurance.

### Is tax planning legal?

Using reliefs and allowances as Parliament intended is normal and legal. Aggressive avoidance schemes are a different matter and can carry serious risks. A qualified adviser will explain the difference clearly.
