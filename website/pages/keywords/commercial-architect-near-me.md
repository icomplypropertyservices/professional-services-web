## What people searching for a commercial architect usually need

Commercial work has its own pressures, and the brief is usually shaped by how the building has to earn its keep.

- You are fitting out a retail unit or hospitality space and need the layout to work for customers, staff and deliveries.
- You are refitting offices and want the space to suit how your team actually works now.
- You have an industrial or warehouse scheme and need the shell and servicing thought through properly.
- You are changing the use of a building and need help with planning permission and building regulations approval.
- You need fire safety provisions designed in from the start, because the rules for commercial buildings are strict.

## How fees usually work (POA)

Architects commonly charge a fixed fee per stage, a percentage of the build cost, or an hourly rate for smaller pieces of work. The scope, from initial sketches to full contract administration, makes a big difference. The practice will give you a POA proposal.

Ask for a written fee proposal linked to the RIBA stages, and check what is excluded, such as planning fees, surveys and other consultants.

## How to check you are in safe hands

"Architect" is a protected title in the UK. Only people on the Architects Registration Board (ARB) register can call themselves architects, and they must follow the ARB code of conduct and hold professional indemnity insurance. Many practices are also RIBA Chartered Practices. Architectural designers and technologists can do excellent work too, but they are not registered architects, so it is worth knowing which you are hiring.

Before you appoint anyone, check the ARB register and ask for a written appointment that sets out services, fees and responsibilities. Building regulations approval, planning permission and party wall matters are separate processes that your architect can help you navigate.

## FAQs: a commercial architect

### Do I need a registered architect for a commercial fit-out?

Not always, but "architect" is a protected title, so anyone using it must be on the Architects Registration Board register. Architectural designers and technologists can also do good work, so it is worth asking which you are hiring and checking the register.

### Can an architect help with planning permission and building regulations?

Yes. Planning permission and building regulations approval are separate processes, and your architect can help you navigate both. Ask for this to be set out in the written appointment.

### Why does fire safety matter so much for commercial buildings?

Fire safety rules are strict for commercial premises, and they affect layout, escape routes and materials. Raise this early with the practice so it is designed in rather than added later.

### What is the difference between fit-out and shell work?

Fit-out covers the interior of a unit, such as partitions, finishes and services, while shell work concerns the structure and envelope of the building. Tell us which you need so we introduce you to a practice suited to it.

### Does a commercial architect need to be local?

It is not a requirement, but a practice near your premises can visit the site more easily and will often know the local planning authority. That can help during design and while the work is on site.

### How do I compare commercial architects near me?

Ask each practice about its experience with your type of building, how it approaches fire safety and whether it will handle planning permission and building regulations approval. Ask for a written appointment setting out services, fees and responsibilities so you can compare like for like.

### What should I have ready for a first appointment?

Bring whatever you have on the property, such as lease plans, existing drawings or a survey, and be clear about how the building needs to work for your business. It also helps to say whether you are fitting out an interior, altering the shell, or both.

### Will an architect guarantee planning permission?

No one can guarantee planning permission. A good architect will explain local policy, the risks and how to give your application the best chance.
