## Private care sits alongside the NHS

Private healthcare covers GP appointments, specialist consultations, diagnostics and treatment that you or your insurer pay for, and it runs alongside NHS care rather than replacing it. A private GP does not replace your NHS GP, and a good clinic will ask for your consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice so your records stay complete and medication is prescribed safely.

## Working out what private healthcare near you actually covers

The phrase private healthcare near me is broader than private GP near me. It can lead you to a clinic offering GP appointments, a specialist consultation, scans or tests, and those are priced and arranged in quite different ways. Before you ring round, it helps to be clear which of these you are actually after, because a clinic that suits a quick GP appointment may not be the right place for a specialist opinion.

When you compare options, ask each clinic what happens after the first appointment. Some will see you, advise you and hand you back to your NHS GP, while others can arrange tests and onward referral within the same setting. Neither approach is automatically better, but knowing which you need stops you paying for a consultation that cannot take you further. It is also worth asking whether the clinician you will see is the one who will handle any follow-up.

Location matters more than it first appears. A clinic that is close on the map can still be awkward to reach by public transport or at the times you are free, so check opening hours and travel before you commit. If you are using insurance, confirm with the insurer that the specific clinic and clinician are covered before you book, as the insured and self-pay routes work differently.

## Questions to ask the professional

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- What does a first consultation cost, and is it a fixed price or charged by time?
- Are you registered with the General Medical Council and on the GP register?
- Is the clinic registered with the relevant regulator for its nation, and where can I see that?
- Will you share consultation notes with my NHS GP, and how does that work?
- Do you work with my insurer, and what would I need to arrange before the appointment?
- What happens if my concern turns out to need tests or a specialist referral?

## FAQs: private healthcare

### Will a private GP replace my NHS GP?

No. A private GP does not replace your NHS GP, and private care runs alongside NHS care. A good clinic will ask for consent to share notes with your NHS practice so your records stay complete.

### Can I use my health insurance for a private GP appointment near me?

Some insurers cover private GP appointments and some do not, and the self-pay and insured routes differ. It is worth checking with your insurer before you book so you know what is covered.

### How do I check that a clinic near me is properly regulated?

You can search the GMC's online register by name to check any doctor's registration and licence to practise. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission, and each nation has its own regulator.

### What should I do in an emergency?

Emergency care stays with the NHS. For urgent advice use NHS 111, and for a life-threatening emergency call 999.

### Is private healthcare near me the same as seeing a private GP?

Not quite. Private healthcare is the wider category, covering GP appointments, specialist consultations, diagnostics and treatment, while a private GP is one way into it. If you are unsure which you need, describe your concern when you enquire and the professional can tell you whether they are the right starting point.

### Can I be seen privately for something my NHS GP is already treating?

Yes, private care runs alongside NHS care rather than replacing it. A good clinic will ask your consent to share notes with your NHS practice so your records stay complete and any medication is prescribed safely.

### What if my concern turns out to be urgent?

For urgent advice use NHS 111, and for a life-threatening emergency call 999 rather than waiting for a private appointment.

### Will a private GP share my notes with my NHS GP?

With your consent, most do, and it is safer for you. Joined-up records help avoid duplicate tests and medication clashes.
