## What people searching for a retirement planner near them usually need

Most people looking for a retirement planner near them want clear, local advice on turning their pension savings into a reliable income.

- You want to know how much income your pension pot could realistically provide and whether it will last as long as you need.
- You are approaching retirement and need help deciding when to start drawing on your pension and how to take it tax-efficiently.
- You have several pension pots from different jobs and want to understand whether consolidating them makes sense for you.
- You are unsure how inflation will affect your retirement income and want a plan that takes rising costs into account.
- You have a defined benefit or final salary pension and need to understand your options, including whether transferring is ever appropriate.

## Choosing between local retirement planners

Searching for a retirement planner near you suggests you would value meeting someone in person, perhaps at an office you can reach without a long journey. It is worth asking each planner you consider how they would hold meetings, since some work mainly by phone or video even when their office is local to you. Think about what 'near' means in practice for you, whether that is a short drive, a train ride or somewhere you can reach on foot.

When you compare retirement planners, ask what they would actually do for you: how they would map your goals, model your income and account for inflation over the years ahead. Ask too how they charge, since some work on a fixed fee and others on a percentage, and a clear quote before any work begins helps you judge whether the cost feels fair for the work involved. You can check each firm's entry on the FCA register to confirm it is authorised for pension advice.

It also helps to think about what you want from the first meeting. A retirement planner will usually want to understand your pension pots, your target income and when you hope to stop working, so gathering those details beforehand makes the conversation more useful. If you hold a final salary scheme or safeguarded benefits, mention this early, as those decisions carry special rules and may need an adviser with pension transfer specialist permissions.

## Checking pension advisers are qualified and regulated

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Some pension decisions carry special rules. Advice on transferring out of a defined benefit (final salary) scheme above a set value must come from an adviser with pension transfer specialist permissions, and safeguarded benefits deserve particular care. Free, impartial guidance is available from Pension Wise for people over 50 with defined contribution pensions.

## Questions to ask the professional

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you and your firm authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority, and what permissions do you hold for pension advice?
- How do you charge for retirement planning, and will I receive a clear quote before any work begins?
- What experience do you have with the type of pension I hold, especially if it is a defined benefit or final salary scheme?
- How will you model my retirement income and take inflation into account over the long term?
- What ongoing service do you provide after the initial plan, and how is that paid for?
- Will you put your recommendations in writing, including the risks and alternatives I should consider?

## How fees usually work (POA)

Pension advice is usually charged as an initial fee, sometimes with an ongoing advice fee. Specialist transfer advice is often charged separately. The firm quotes you, POA.

Ask whether fees can be taken from the pension, and how that affects its value.

## When to think twice

Most professionals are honest and competent, but it is worth knowing the warning signs:

- Offers to access a pension before age 55 (rising to 57 from 2028)
- Overseas investments or unusual schemes
- Firms not on the FCA register
- Pressure to transfer quickly

## FAQs: a retirement planner

### Do I need a retirement planner near me, or can I use someone further away?

Many people prefer a local retirement planner for face-to-face meetings, but advice can also be given by phone or online. What matters most is that the adviser is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority and that you feel comfortable with how they work.

### What is the difference between a retirement planner and a pension adviser?

The terms are often used interchangeably for professionals who help you plan your retirement income and make pension decisions. iComply introduces you to a suitable UK pension adviser who will quote you directly, with fees on application.

### Can a retirement planner help if I have a final salary pension?

Yes, but advice on transferring out of a defined benefit (final salary) scheme above a set value must come from an adviser with pension transfer specialist permissions. Make sure you tell iComply if you hold this type of pension so they can introduce you appropriately.

### What free guidance is available before I see a retirement planner?

Pension Wise offers free, impartial guidance for people who have reached the age when it applies and have defined contribution pensions. It can help you understand your options before you speak to a financial adviser.

### How can I judge whether a local retirement planner is right for me?

There is no single best choice, so it helps to compare how each planner explains your options, how they charge and whether their way of working suits you. Check that the firm is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority, and ask for recommendations and risks in writing before you decide.

### Should I see a retirement planner before or after taking free guidance?

Many people find it useful to speak to Pension Wise first if they have defined contribution pensions and have reached the age when it applies, as the free, impartial guidance can help you understand your options. You can then bring what you have learned to a retirement planner, who can give personal advice on your circumstances.

### Should I combine my pensions?

Sometimes it makes life simpler and cheaper, but some older pensions have valuable guarantees or low charges. An adviser checks before recommending a transfer.
