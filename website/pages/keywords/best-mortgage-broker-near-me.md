## What 'best' means when choosing a mortgage broker

There's no official ranking of mortgage brokers, so 'best' really depends on your needs. Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), and any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm. You can check this on the FCA register, and advisers typically hold a qualification such as CeMAP.

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders, and how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. That transparency helps you judge who is right for you. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

## FAQs about best mortgage broker near me

### How do I check if a mortgage broker is authorised?

You can check on the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register to see if a broker or adviser is authorised or an appointed representative. The register will show the firm's status and any restrictions. Always ask for their firm reference number.

### Will a mortgage broker near me charge a fee?

Brokers must tell you how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. Fees vary, so ask for a clear explanation before you start. iComply does not charge you for the introduction, but the broker will quote you directly.

### What is a decision in principle and do I need one?

A decision in principle is an indication from a lender of how much they might lend you, based on a preliminary check. It can show estate agents you're a serious buyer. A broker can help you get one early in the process.

### Can a broker help if I have bad credit?

Some brokers specialise in helping people with less-than-perfect credit. They can compare lenders who may be more flexible. Tell the broker about your circumstances so they can search appropriately.

### Does a mortgage broker near me have to cover my local area?

Not necessarily. Many brokers work with clients across the UK by phone and email, so being nearby is often about convenience rather than necessity. If you want to meet in person, ask the broker whether they offer face-to-face appointments in your area.

### Should I choose a broker who searches the whole market?

A whole-of-market broker can compare mortgages from a wider range of lenders, while a limited-panel broker only looks at certain lenders. Neither is automatically better for your situation, but the broker must tell you which approach they take so you can decide. Ask how their panel might affect the deals available to you.

### What happens at a first appointment with a local broker?

The broker will usually ask about your income, outgoings, deposit and the property you want to buy or remortgage. They should explain how they are paid and whether they search the whole market or a limited panel. You can then decide whether to go ahead, and you should receive a written fee agreement before any chargeable work begins.

### Is a broker better than going to my bank?

A broker can compare many lenders and may find options your bank does not offer. Your bank can only offer its own products. Either can suit you; a broker gives you a wider view.
