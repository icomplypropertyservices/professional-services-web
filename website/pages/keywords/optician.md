## What people searching for an optician usually need

Most people looking for an optician want clear, practical help with their eyes or vision.

- You've noticed your vision has changed and you want a proper eye examination to see whether you need glasses or a new prescription.
- You already have a prescription and want to choose frames or lenses that suit your face and your budget.
- You wear contact lenses and need a check-up, a supply of lenses, or advice on looking after them safely.
- You have a specific concern, such as dry eyes, headaches when reading, or a family history of eye problems, and want to speak to someone who can assess it.
- You're due for a regular eye health check and want to find a local optician who can see you at a convenient time.

## Questions to ask the optician

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the optician is right for you:

- What does the eye examination include, and how long will it take?
- Will I get a copy of my prescription after the test?
- Do you offer OCT scans, and would they be useful for me?
- What options do I have for glasses or contact lenses, and what might they cost?
- Can you explain the difference between an optometrist and a dispensing optician in your practice?
- If you find something that needs further attention, what happens next?

## How to check you are in safe hands

Optometrists and dispensing opticians must be registered with the General Optical Council (GOC), which you can check online. Optometrists carry out eye examinations; dispensing opticians fit and supply glasses and, with extra training, contact lenses.

Some optometrists hold extra qualifications that let them manage more eye conditions or prescribe certain medicines. In many areas of the UK, community optometrists provide minor eye condition and urgent eye care services.

## Understanding the word optician and what it covers

When you type the single word optician, it can mean several things at once. You might be after an eye examination, a new pair of glasses, a contact lens check, or advice about a symptom that has been bothering you. Knowing which of these you actually need helps you ask the right questions from the start.

A common misunderstanding is that everyone in an opticians practice does the same job. In fact the eye examination itself is carried out by an optometrist, while dispensing opticians fit and supply glasses and, with extra training, contact lenses. Both must be registered with the General Optical Council, which you can check online, so you can confirm who you are seeing and what they are qualified to do.

It also helps to know that some optometrists hold extra qualifications allowing them to manage more eye conditions or prescribe certain medicines, and in many areas of the UK community optometrists provide minor eye condition and urgent eye care services. If your reason for searching is a specific worry rather than a routine check, say so when you enquire so you are introduced to someone suited to it.

## Who does what at an optician's practice

At an optician's practice, the eye examination itself is carried out by an optometrist, while dispensing opticians fit and supply glasses and, with extra training, contact lenses. ## FAQs about optician

### How often should I have an eye test?

Most people are advised to have an eye test every two years, though your optician may suggest a different interval depending on your age, health, and family history. Regular tests check eye health as well as vision.

### What is an OCT scan and do I need one?

An OCT scan is a detailed scan of the back of your eye that can help detect certain eye conditions early. Not everyone needs one, so ask your optician whether it would be useful for you.

### Can I get a copy of my prescription?

Yes, you are entitled to a copy of your prescription after an eye examination. It's useful to keep it so you can compare prices or order glasses elsewhere if you wish.

### What should I do if I have an urgent eye problem?

If you have sudden vision loss, severe eye pain, or an injury, seek urgent help. You can contact NHS 111 for advice or visit an urgent care service; in an emergency, call 999.

### Is an optician the same as an optometrist?

Not quite. The word optician is often used loosely to describe the whole practice, but the eye examination is carried out by an optometrist, while a dispensing optician fits and supplies glasses and, with extra training, contact lenses. Both are registered with the General Optical Council, and you can check their registration online.

### Should I ask for an optometrist or a dispensing optician?

If you need your eyes examined or have a concern about your eye health, you want an optometrist. If your prescription is up to date and you need help choosing and fitting glasses, a dispensing optician is the right person. Telling us which you need helps us introduce you to a suitable professional.

### Can an optician help with a minor eye problem rather than just a test?

In many areas of the UK, community optometrists provide minor eye condition and urgent eye care services, so it is worth asking what is available locally. For sudden vision loss, severe eye pain or an injury, seek urgent help instead: contact NHS 111 for advice, or call 999 in an emergency.

### Do I have to buy glasses where I have my eye test?

No. You are entitled to a copy of your prescription and can buy glasses anywhere.
