## What to understand about finding a tax adviser near you

Tax adviser is not a protected title in the UK, so anyone can call themselves one. The main specialist bodies are the Chartered Institute of Taxation (for the CTA qualification) and the Association of Taxation Technicians (for the ATT qualification). Many accountants and solicitors also advise on tax, and members of these bodies follow a shared code called Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation.

Tax advisers who charge a fee must be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, and HMRC has been tightening standards for paid tax agents. ## FAQs: a tax adviser

### Do I need a tax adviser who is local to me?

Not necessarily. Many tax advisers work remotely and can help you by phone, email or video call. However, if you prefer face-to-face meetings, searching for a tax advisor near me can help you find someone in your area.

### What is the difference between a tax adviser and an accountant?

A tax adviser focuses specifically on tax matters, while an accountant may offer a broader range of services including bookkeeping and financial reporting. Many accountants also advise on tax, and some tax advisers are qualified accountants or solicitors.

### How do I check a tax adviser's credentials?

You can ask whether they hold qualifications such as ATT or CTA, or are a member of a professional body like the Chartered Institute of Taxation. You can also ask about their supervision for anti-money-laundering purposes and whether they have professional indemnity insurance.

### Can iComply recommend a specific tax adviser?

### Should I choose a tax adviser who is close to where I live?

Proximity can be convenient if you prefer face-to-face meetings, but it isn't essential. Many advisers work remotely and can serve you just as well by phone or video call, so weigh up what matters most to you.

### How can I compare tax advisers when fees are price on application?

Ask each adviser to explain their charging structure and provide a written quote for the work you need. That lets you compare like for like, rather than guessing from a headline figure.

### What should I bring to a first meeting with a tax adviser?

Bring any relevant paperwork, such as correspondence from HMRC, details of your income or business, and a list of questions. It also helps to have a clear idea of what you want to achieve, so the adviser can focus their advice.

### Should I reply to HMRC before speaking to an adviser?

Note the deadline on the letter, but avoid sending a detailed reply before you have advice if the matter is complex. An adviser can often ask HMRC for more time where it is reasonable.
