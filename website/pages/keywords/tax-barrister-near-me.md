## Understanding how tax barristers work

Tax barristers usually work from chambers and are regulated by the Bar Standards Board in England and Wales. You can check the Barristers' Register to confirm that a barrister holds a current practising certificate and is authorised to accept instructions directly from the public under the Public Access scheme. In Scotland, the equivalent professionals are advocates, regulated through the Faculty of Advocates.

Many tax barristers can be instructed through a solicitor in the traditional way, but those registered for Public Access can be instructed directly by members of the public for suitable matters. Accountants and tax advisers can often instruct barristers directly as well.

### Can I instruct a tax barrister directly without a solicitor?

Yes, if the barrister is registered under the Public Access scheme, you can instruct them directly for suitable matters. Otherwise, you may need to go through a solicitor or your accountant.

### How do I check if a tax barrister is regulated?

You can check the Barristers' Register to see if someone holds a current practising certificate and is authorised to accept direct instructions. In Scotland, advocates are regulated by the Faculty of Advocates.

### What information should I have ready before contacting a tax barrister?

It helps to have a clear summary of your tax issue, any correspondence from HMRC, relevant dates, and an idea of what you want to achieve. This will help the barrister assess your case quickly.

### Will a tax barrister near me be more expensive?

Fees vary depending on the barrister's experience and the complexity of your case. It's best to ask for a quote and discuss your budget upfront; location may not be the main factor as many barristers work remotely.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a barrister?

Solicitors usually manage a case from start to finish and deal with clients day to day. Barristers specialise in advocacy and specialist advice, and are typically brought in for hearings or difficult legal questions.
