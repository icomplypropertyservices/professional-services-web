## Red flags when choosing a mortgage adviser

Be cautious if you notice any of the following:

- A firm or adviser not on the FCA register
- Pressure to borrow more than you are comfortable with
- Fees requested up front with no written terms
- No explanation of how the broker is paid

## What the first appointment with a mortgage advisor is like

When you meet a mortgage advisor near you for the first time, expect a conversation rather than a sales pitch. They will ask about your income, your outgoings, your deposit and what you want the mortgage to do for you, whether that is buying, remortgaging or moving. Much of the first appointment is about gathering the facts they need before they can recommend anything suitable.

You do not need to have every document to hand for that first chat, but it helps to bring proof of income, details of any existing mortgage, and an idea of the property or price range you have in mind. The advisor should explain whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders, and how they are paid, before you go any further. If anything about their answers feels unclear, ask again, as you are entitled to understand how the advice works.

After that first meeting, the advisor will usually go away and research mortgages that fit your circumstances, then come back with a recommendation and an explanation of why it suits you. Advice must be suitable for you, so take the time to read what is proposed and ask questions about anything you do not follow. ## Questions to ask the mortgage advisor

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Do you search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders?
- How are you paid for your advice, and will I pay a fee?
- Are you authorised by the FCA, and can you give me your firm's reference number?
- What qualification do you hold, such as CeMAP?
- Will you also discuss protection products with me?
- What happens if I want to complain about your advice?

## How to check you are in safe hands

Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and you can check this on the FCA register. Advisers typically hold a qualification such as CeMAP.

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders, and how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Best Mortgage Advisor Near Me: FAQs

### How do I check if a mortgage advisor is authorised?

You can check on the FCA register to see if the advisor or their firm is authorised or an appointed representative. It is a good idea to do this before you share your financial details.

### Will using a mortgage advisor cost me anything?

Advisors must tell you how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. Any fee will be quoted on application, so you know the cost before you proceed.

### What is the difference between a mortgage advisor and a mortgage broker?

The terms are often used interchangeably. Both can advise on and arrange mortgages, but some work for a single lender while others search the market on your behalf. Always ask which type you are speaking to.

### Can a mortgage advisor help if I have bad credit?

Some advisors specialise in helping people with credit issues, but not all do. It is worth mentioning any credit problems early so they can tell you whether they can help.

### Should I choose a mortgage advisor who is close to where I live?

Being nearby can make it easier to meet in person, but location is only one factor. What matters more is whether the advisor is authorised by the FCA, searches the market you need, and explains their fees clearly.

### Can I speak to more than one mortgage advisor before deciding?

Yes, you are free to speak to more than one advisor and compare how they work. Ask each the same questions about market access, fees and qualifications so you can judge them on the same basis.

### What if I am not ready to apply for a mortgage yet?

Many people speak to an advisor well before they apply, to understand what they could borrow and how to prepare. An advisor can explain what lenders typically look for, though the decision always rests with the lender.

### Will speaking to a broker affect my credit score?

An initial conversation does not. Formal applications usually involve a credit search, and the broker should tell you before that happens.
