## Costs and quotes for a business financial adviser

Advisers often charge for initial advice, then an ongoing fee for reviews, which may be fixed or a percentage of the money they look after. Firms must set out charges clearly before advising you. The firm quotes you, POA.

Ask for charges in pounds as well as percentages, and check what the ongoing service includes. The FCA's Consumer Duty expects firms to provide fair value.

## Checking financial advisers are qualified and regulated

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Questions to ask the financial adviser

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority, and can you provide your firm's reference number so I can check the register?
- Will you be giving independent advice or restricted advice, and what does that mean for the products you recommend?
- How do you charge for your services, and will you give me a clear quote in writing before any work begins?
- What experience do you have with business owners in my situation, particularly around profit extraction and pensions?
- How would you approach shareholder protection if I have business partners?
- What should I be doing now to prepare for a future exit or succession, even if it's several years away?

## FAQs: a business financial adviser

### Do I need a business financial adviser or can my accountant help?

Your accountant can help with tax and accounts, but financial advice on pensions, investments and protection is a separate regulated activity. A business financial adviser can work alongside your accountant to cover those areas.

### What does 'price on application' mean for the adviser's fees?

It means the adviser will quote you directly based on your circumstances and the work involved. You should ask for a clear explanation of how they charge before you commit to anything.

### Can a financial adviser help with shareholder protection?

Yes, business financial advisers can arrange shareholder protection, which is a type of insurance designed to help buy out a shareholder's interest if they die or become seriously ill. It can help keep the business running smoothly.

### How do I check that an adviser is properly regulated?

You can check the Financial Conduct Authority's register to see if an adviser or firm is authorised or is an appointed representative. The adviser should also tell you whether their advice is independent or restricted and how they charge.

### Does my business financial adviser need to be local to me?

Not necessarily. Working with someone nearby can make meetings easier, but what matters most is that they understand businesses like yours and can advise on the areas you need. Ask about how they'd work with you, whether in person or remotely, before you decide.

### Can a business financial adviser help if my company is small?

Yes. Many of the issues business owners face, such as taking profit efficiently, setting up a pension or protecting a shareholding, apply whether the company has a handful of employees or many more. Describe your situation when you enquire so you're introduced to an adviser with relevant experience.

### What is the difference between independent and restricted advice?

Independent advisers consider products from across the market. Restricted advisers focus on certain products or providers. Both can give good advice; they must tell you which they offer.
