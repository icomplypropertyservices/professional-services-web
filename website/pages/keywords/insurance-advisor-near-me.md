## How to check you are in safe hands

Insurance brokers and intermediaries in the UK must be authorised by the FCA, or be appointed representatives of an authorised firm. Check them on the FCA register. Many brokers belong to the British Insurance Brokers' Association (BIBA), and some hold Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute.

Brokers must understand your demands and needs, explain key policy features and exclusions, and tell you how they are paid. Complaints that are not resolved can go to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Choosing between local insurance advisers

Searching for an insurance advisor near me suggests you would rather speak to someone face to face or at least locally, perhaps because you want to sit down and go through paperwork together or have someone close by if a claim gets complicated. Distance is only one factor, though, and a broker slightly further away may still be the right fit if they know your particular type of cover well.

When you compare advisers, ask what kinds of insurance they deal with most often, since a broker who mainly handles business cover may not be the best person for a household policy, and the other way round. Check on the FCA register that each one is authorised or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and ask whether they are a member of BIBA or hold Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute. You can also ask how they are paid and whether they charge a fee as well as commission, so you can weigh up the overall cost rather than just the headline quote.

At a first appointment, expect questions about what you own, who else depends on you and any claims you have made before, because the adviser must understand your demands and needs before recommending anything. Ask them to walk you through the exclusions in plain terms and to explain what happens if you need to claim, including whether they handle claims themselves or pass you to the insurer. Take your existing policy documents along if you have them, as comparing what you already hold with what is being offered is often the quickest way to spot gaps.

## When to think twice

- Unclear fees or commissions

## Insurance Advisor Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need to use an insurance advisor, or can I buy cover directly?

You can buy insurance directly, but an advisor can help you compare options and understand exclusions. They must explain the cover and how they are paid, so you can decide what suits you.

### How do I check that an insurance advisor is properly authorised?

You can check on the FCA register. The advisor should be authorised or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm. You can also ask if they are a member of BIBA or hold Chartered status.

### What information will an insurance advisor need from me?

They will ask about what you want to cover, your circumstances and any previous claims. Being open helps them find suitable cover and avoid problems later.

### Can an insurance advisor help if my claim is refused?

They can explain the policy terms and may help you challenge a decision. If you remain unhappy, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

### Does an insurance advisor near me have to visit my home?

Not usually. Many first conversations happen by phone or in the adviser's office, and a home visit is often only needed for things like a buildings survey or a detailed business review. Ask when you first speak to them what they prefer.

### Is a local insurance advisor better than a national one?

Local can be convenient, especially if you want to drop in documents or discuss a claim in person, but what matters most is that the adviser understands your type of cover and is properly authorised. It is worth comparing a nearby adviser with one further afield before deciding.

### Will an adviser near me only offer policies from local insurers?

No. Advisers generally arrange cover with insurers across the UK, and being nearby does not limit which policies they can access. Ask which insurers they typically use for your kind of cover so you understand the range of options.

### What is non-disclosure?

Failing to tell an insurer something relevant. It can reduce or void a claim, so answer questions fully and ask your broker if unsure.
