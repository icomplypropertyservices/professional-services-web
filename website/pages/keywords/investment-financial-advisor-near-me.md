## Checking financial advisers are qualified and regulated

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## FAQs about investment financial advisor near me

### How do I check that an investment adviser is properly authorised?

You can search the FCA register online for the adviser's name or firm name. The register shows whether they are authorised or an appointed representative of an authorised firm. It is a good idea to check before you share any personal or financial details.

### What is the difference between independent and restricted advice?

Independent advisers consider the whole market when recommending investments. Restricted advisers are limited to certain products or providers. Advisers must tell you which type of advice they offer before you engage them, so you can decide what suits you.

### Will I have to pay for an initial meeting?

Many advisers offer a free initial consultation, but some charge for their time from the start. You should ask about any fees before you meet, and get a clear explanation of how charges are structured. iComply does not set or take any fees; the adviser quotes you directly.

### Can I get investment advice if I only have a small amount to invest?

Some advisers have minimum investment levels, while others may offer advice on smaller sums or a one-off piece of work. It is worth asking about minimums when you enquire. iComply can introduce you to an adviser who may be able to help, but we cannot ensure they will take on every case.

### Does my investment adviser have to be local?

No. Advice can be given by phone, video or post as well as face to face, and many people never visit their adviser's office. If meeting in person matters to you, say so when you enquire so we can look for someone in your area.

### Should I bring anything to a first meeting about investing?

It helps to bring recent statements for any savings, pensions or investments you already hold, along with a rough idea of what you are investing for. Having these to hand lets the adviser give you a more useful conversation rather than a general chat.

### How can I compare two local advisers fairly?

Ask each the same questions: whether their advice is independent or restricted, how they charge, and what experience they have with investments like yours. Comparing their answers side by side tells you more than distance alone, and you can check both on the FCA register before deciding.
