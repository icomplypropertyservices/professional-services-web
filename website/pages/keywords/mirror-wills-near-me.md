## One key thing to understand about mirror wills

Mirror wills are two separate wills with almost identical terms, usually made by a couple. Each will typically leaves everything to the other, and then to the same beneficiaries. It's important to know that either person can change their will later, so mirror wills are not set in stone.

## FAQs about mirror wills near me

### What are mirror wills?

Mirror wills are two separate wills, usually made by a couple, with almost identical terms. They typically leave everything to each other and then to the same beneficiaries.

### Can we change our mirror wills later?

Yes, either person can change their will later. Mirror wills are not legally binding on each other in the way that mutual wills can be.

### Are mirror wills suitable if we have children?

Mirror wills can include arrangements for children, such as appointing guardians and setting up trusts. A specialist can help you consider the best way to protect your children's interests.

### Do we need a solicitor to make mirror wills?

Writing a will is not a reserved legal activity in England and Wales, so you can use a regulated solicitor or an unregulated will writer. Using a regulated professional gives you the reassurance of insurance and access to the Legal Ombudsman if something goes wrong.

### Do mirror wills have to be made at the same time?

Not necessarily. Mirror wills are two separate wills, so they can be made at different times, though couples often make them together. What matters is that the terms mirror each other, leaving everything to each other and then to the same beneficiaries.

### Can we use mirror wills if we're not married?

Yes, mirror wills can be used by any couple, whether married, in a civil partnership or living together. However, the legal effect can differ, especially for unmarried couples, so it's important to discuss your situation with a specialist.

### What happens if I die without a will?

The intestacy rules decide who inherits, which may not reflect your wishes, especially for unmarried partners and stepchildren. A will lets you choose.
