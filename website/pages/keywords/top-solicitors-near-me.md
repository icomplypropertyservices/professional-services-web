## How to judge who is top for you

Nobody can honestly promise the best solicitor in the country, but you can judge for yourself. Look for someone whose advertised specialism matches your exact matter, ask how much of their work is in that area, and check the firm and the individual on the Solicitors Regulation Authority register if you are in England or Wales, or with the Law Society of Scotland or the Law Society of Northern Ireland if you are elsewhere in the UK.

"Near me" matters most for face-to-face meetings and court attendance, but many solicitors now work by phone, email and video, so a firm slightly further away may still suit you. Ask early what they charge and how they will keep you updated.

## Checking solicitors are qualified and regulated

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

## Questions to ask the solicitor

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- How much of your work is in this specific area of law?
- Will you be handling my matter personally, or will someone else in the firm do the day-to-day work?
- How will you charge me, and can you give me an estimate of the likely costs at the outset?
- What do you need from me to get started, and how quickly can you begin?
- How will you keep me updated as things progress?
- What is your complaints procedure if I am unhappy with the service?

## Comparing several local solicitors fairly

Typing top solicitors near me, plural, suggests you are lining up more than one name and want a sensible way to compare them. That is a good instinct, because the solicitor who suits a neighbour's boundary dispute may be quite wrong for your divorce or your commercial lease. Comparing like with like starts with narrowing the field to firms that genuinely work in your area of law.

Ask each one the same set of questions so the answers sit side by side: how much of their work is in this field, who will handle your file day to day, how costs will be structured, and how they will keep you informed. Be wary of anyone who will not put costs information in writing or who seems vague about specialism. A firm that turns out not to do your kind of work is not being unhelpful by saying so, and it saves you time.

Distance is only one measure of convenience. For many matters, most of the work happens by phone, email and post, with perhaps a couple of meetings, so a firm a little further away that clearly knows your area of law may serve you better than the closest one on the list. Weigh that alongside cost clarity and how comfortable you feel speaking to them, then decide for yourself.

## Costs and quotes for a solicitor

Legal fees depend on the type of matter, its complexity and who does the work. Firms commonly charge hourly rates, fixed fees for defined stages, or (in some claims) conditional fee arrangements. Regulated firms in England and Wales must publish pricing information for certain services and give you clear costs information at the outset. The firm will give you a POA quote.

Ask for a written estimate and for an explanation of what is not included, such as court fees, barristers' fees or experts. Ask too whether legal expenses insurance or legal aid might cover some or all of the cost.
