## Qualifications and registration to look for

Every doctor practising in the UK must be registered and hold a licence to practise with the General Medical Council (GMC). GPs also appear on the GMC's GP register. You can search the GMC's online register by name. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

A private GP does not replace your NHS GP. Good private clinics will ask for consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice, so your records stay complete and medication is prescribed safely.

## What to expect at your first private GP appointment

When you book a private GP appointment after searching for the best private GP near me, you can expect a longer consultation than the standard NHS slot, often lasting fifteen to thirty minutes. This gives you time to describe your symptoms fully and ask questions without feeling rushed. The GP will take a history, examine you if needed, and discuss possible next steps, which may include tests, a referral, or a prescription.

Many private GPs also offer video consultations, which can be convenient if you're unwell or have mobility issues. However, some problems need a physical examination, so it's worth checking whether the clinic offers face-to-face appointments too. If you opt for a video call, make sure you have a private space and a good internet connection.

After your appointment, a good private GP will ask for your consent to share notes with your NHS GP, so your records stay joined up. This is especially important if you're prescribed medication or need ongoing care. It's also worth asking how test results and referrals will be communicated to you and your NHS practice.

## Questions to ask the private GP

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you on the GMC's GP register with a current licence to practise?
- Is this clinic registered with the relevant regulator, and how do I check?
- How long is the appointment, and can I book a video consultation instead?
- Will you share notes with my NHS GP, and how does that work?
- What is the fee, and what does it include or exclude, such as tests or follow-ups?
- If I need a referral or test, how will that be arranged and what might it cost?

## Best Private GP Near Me: FAQs

### Can a private GP see me the same day or next day?

Many private GPs offer same or next day appointments, often with longer slots than an NHS practice. Availability varies by clinic and location, so it's worth saying how soon you need to be seen when you enquire.

### How do I check a private GP is properly registered?

Every doctor practising in the UK must be registered with the General Medical Council and hold a licence to practise, and GPs also appear on the GMC's GP register. You can search the GMC's online register by name, and check the clinic's registration with the CQC in England or the equivalent regulator in Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland.

### Will using a private GP affect my NHS care?

A private GP doesn't replace your NHS GP, and good clinics will ask your consent to share notes with your NHS practice so your records stay complete. This helps keep medication safe and means your NHS GP knows what has been arranged.

### Why are private GP fees quoted on application?

Fees vary between clinics and depend on the length and type of appointment and any tests or referrals. That's why quotes are given on application, so the fee reflects what you actually need rather than a headline figure.

### How long does a private GP appointment usually last?

Private GP appointments often last between fifteen and thirty minutes, giving you more time than a standard NHS consultation. The exact length varies by clinic, so it's worth confirming when you book.

### Can I see a private GP for a second opinion?

Yes, many people see a private GP for a second opinion or to discuss a diagnosis or treatment plan in more detail. The GP can review your history and test results, and may suggest further investigations or refer you to a specialist if needed.

### Will a private GP share my notes with my NHS GP?

With your consent, most do, and it is safer for you. Joined-up records help avoid duplicate tests and medication clashes.
