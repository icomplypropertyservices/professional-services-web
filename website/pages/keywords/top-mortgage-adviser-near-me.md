## FAQs: a mortgage adviser

### How do I check if a mortgage adviser is authorised?

You can check on the FCA register to see if the adviser or their firm is authorised. Any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm.

### What does "whole of market" mean?

It means the adviser searches across the whole mortgage market rather than a limited panel of lenders. Brokers must tell you which service they offer.

### Will a mortgage adviser also help with protection?

Many advisers will discuss protection products such as life insurance or income protection as part of your mortgage planning. You can ask them about this when you speak.

### Can I use a mortgage adviser if I am self-employed?

Yes, mortgage advisers can help if you are self-employed or have an irregular income. They will look at your circumstances and recommend a suitable mortgage.

### Do I have to meet a mortgage adviser in person?

No. Many advisers work by phone or online, so "near me" may not matter as much as how you prefer to communicate. It is worth asking how they usually hold appointments before you book.

### What should I bring to a first mortgage appointment?

Bring identification, proof of income such as payslips or accounts if you are self-employed, and details of any deposit, savings or existing mortgage. Your adviser may ask for more later, but this gives you a useful starting point.

### Is the first meeting with a mortgage adviser free?

That depends on the adviser, so ask before you book. Brokers must tell you how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both, and when any charge applies.

### Will speaking to a broker affect my credit score?

An initial conversation does not. Formal applications usually involve a credit search, and the broker should tell you before that happens.
