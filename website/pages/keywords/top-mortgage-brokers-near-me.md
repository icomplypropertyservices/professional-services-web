## FAQs: a mortgage broker

### How do I check a mortgage broker is properly authorised?

Any broker or adviser should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and you can check this on the FCA register before you proceed.

### What does it mean if a broker says they search the whole market?

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders. If they use a panel, ask which lenders are included and whether that could limit your options.

### Will a broker charge me a fee?

Brokers must explain how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. Ask for this in writing before you start so you understand the arrangement.

### What if I'm unhappy with the service?

You can complain to the firm first, and if that doesn't resolve things you can take your complaint to the Financial Ombudsman Service. It's worth keeping a record of your communications in case you need them.

### Should I speak to more than one mortgage broker?

It's reasonable to speak to a few so you can compare how they work and how they'd approach your situation. Ask each the same questions about market coverage, fees and qualifications so you can judge them side by side. There's no obligation to proceed with any of them.

### How can I tell which broker is right for my circumstances?

Focus on fit rather than labels: whether they've dealt with situations like yours, such as self-employment or a remortgage, and whether their answers are clear. Check they're authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority on the FCA register and that you understand how they're paid. A broker who explains things plainly and listens carefully is often the better sign than one who rushes to a rate.

### Does choosing a broker near me matter?

A local broker can be convenient if you'd prefer to meet in person, but much of the work can be done by phone or online. What matters more is whether they search the whole market or a limited panel and how they're paid. ### Is a broker better than going to my bank?

A broker can compare many lenders and may find options your bank does not offer. Your bank can only offer its own products. Either can suit you; a broker gives you a wider view.
