## Understanding licensing for frozen embryo transfer

Treatment involving eggs, sperm or embryos, including a frozen embryo transfer, should always be carried out at a clinic licensed by the Human Fertilisation and Embryology Authority (HFEA). The HFEA's Choose a Fertility Clinic tool lets you see each licensed clinic's inspection ratings, success rates and patient feedback, which can help you compare local options.

A frozen embryo transfer thaws a stored embryo and places it in the womb, either in a natural cycle timed around your own ovulation or in a medicated cycle. It usually involves less medication than a fresh IVF cycle, and the timing is planned around your cycle. Ask the clinic how they plan transfers and what monitoring is involved.

## Frozen Embryo Transfer Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need a referral from my GP to have a frozen embryo transfer at a clinic?

Many fertility clinics accept self-referrals for frozen embryo transfers, but some may ask for information from your GP or the clinic that stored your embryos. It is worth asking the clinic what they need before you book.

### Can I have a frozen embryo transfer at a clinic that is not near my home?

You can choose any licensed clinic, but a frozen embryo transfer involves several monitoring appointments, so travelling distance can matter in practice. A clinic nearer to you may make the cycle easier to manage alongside work and daily life.

### How long does a frozen embryo transfer cycle take?

The length of a frozen embryo transfer cycle varies depending on whether it is a natural or medicated cycle and on your own cycle. The clinic will explain the likely timeline for you at your consultation.

### Will I need to take medication for a frozen embryo transfer?

A medicated cycle uses medication to prepare the womb lining, while a natural cycle relies on your own hormones and usually involves less medication. The clinic can discuss which approach is suitable for your circumstances.

### Am I eligible for NHS fertility treatment?

Eligibility depends on local commissioning rules, including age, how long you have been trying and previous children. Ask your GP; a private clinic can also explain options.
