## Questions to ask a business solicitor

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the solicitor is right for you:

- Are you regulated by the SRA, the Law Society of Scotland or the Law Society of Northern Ireland, and can you provide your registration number?
- What experience do you have with businesses of my size and in my sector?
- Will you handle my matter personally or will it be passed to a colleague?
- How will you charge for this work, and can you give me an estimate of the likely costs?
- Do you offer fixed-fee packages for start-ups or for the specific work I need?
- What is the likely timescale for resolving my matter, and what do you need from me to get started?

## Checking solicitors are qualified and regulated

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

## Comparing several business solicitors before you choose

Searching for business lawyers near me in the plural suggests you are weighing up more than one option rather than simply taking the first name you find. That is worth doing, because business solicitors vary in the sectors they know and the kind of work they take on, and a firm that suits a dispute may not be the best fit for a lease or a shareholders' agreement. Comparing a few quotes side by side helps you judge both the approach and the price.

When you compare, ask each firm who would actually handle your matter and whether that person specialises in the area you need. Look at how clearly they explain the process and what they need from you, and check how their charges are structured, since some quote a fixed fee for defined work such as a shareholders' agreement while others bill by the hour. A short first conversation often tells you whether their style suits how you like to work.

Location matters less than it once did, but if meeting in person is important to you, ask whether the solicitor you speak to is the one based at the office near you or a colleague elsewhere. Confirm too which part of the UK your business and any contract are governed by, because that decides whether a SRA-regulated solicitor, the Law Society of Scotland or the Law Society of Northern Ireland is the right regulator to check.

## FAQs about business lawyers near me

### How do I check that a business solicitor is properly regulated?

In England and Wales you can check an individual or firm on the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA) register. In Scotland and Northern Ireland, the Law Society of Scotland and the Law Society of Northern Ireland respectively hold the relevant registers. Always confirm the status of the person who will actually handle your matter.

### Can any lawyer deal with my business dispute?

The word lawyer is not protected, so not everyone using that title can carry out reserved activities such as conducting court litigation. Check that you are instructing a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. If your dispute may end up in court, ask about their experience and authorisation to act.

### What should I have ready before I speak to a business solicitor?

Gather any relevant contracts, correspondence, company documents and a clear summary of what you want to achieve. It also helps to know your business structure and your key deadlines. Having this ready can make the first conversation more focused and useful.

### What happens if I am unhappy with the service from a solicitor?

Regulated firms must have a complaints procedure, so start by raising your concern with the firm directly. If that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales. The relevant regulator or law society can also explain the options in other parts of the UK.

### Is it worth getting quotes from more than one business solicitor?

It can be, especially where the work is well defined, such as drafting a contract or setting up a shareholders' agreement. Comparing how each firm proposes to handle the matter and how they charge gives you a clearer basis for deciding than a single quote alone.

### Does my business solicitor need to be nearby?

Not necessarily, since much commercial work can be handled by phone, email and video. A local firm may still appeal if you prefer face-to-face meetings or if your matter involves premises or a court near you, so it is worth saying which you would prefer when you get in touch.

### Can I compare a solicitor with a chartered legal executive for business work?

Yes, and it is worth knowing which you are dealing with. Chartered legal executives (CILEx) are authorised professionals who can handle many commercial matters, but reserved activities such as conducting court litigation can only be carried out by authorised people, so ask about the status of whoever will act for you.

### Is a solicitor the same as a lawyer?

"Lawyer" is a general word that covers solicitors, barristers, chartered legal executives and others. A solicitor is a specific regulated professional who can advise you, run your case and instruct a barrister if needed.
