## When to think twice

Be cautious if you notice any of the following:

- Promises about the outcome of a hearing
- No written client care letter for a public access instruction
- Pressure to instruct without seeing the papers

## Questions to ask the immigration barrister

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Are you authorised to accept instructions directly from the public under the Public Access scheme?
- Have you represented clients at the First-tier or Upper Tribunal in cases like mine?
- How will you and your clerks keep me informed as my case progresses?
- What will you need from me to prepare the evidence bundle, and by when?
- Can you explain how your fees are structured and what they will cover?
- Will you be the barrister attending my hearing, or could someone else represent me on the day?

## Immigration Barrister Near Me: FAQs

### How do I check whether an immigration barrister is regulated?

Barristers in England and Wales are regulated by the Bar Standards Board, and you can use the Barristers' Register to check whether someone holds a current practising certificate and is authorised to accept instructions directly from the public.

### Can I instruct an immigration barrister without going through a solicitor?

Some barristers registered under the Public Access scheme can be instructed directly by members of the public for suitable matters. Others are usually instructed through a solicitor in the traditional way, so it is worth asking which applies to your case.

### Why does the appeal deadline matter so much?

Appeal deadlines in immigration cases are short, and missing one can affect your ability to challenge a decision. That is why it helps to make contact as soon as you receive a refusal or notice and to have your paperwork ready.

### Is legal aid available for immigration cases?

Some immigration cases are legally aided, though not all are, so funding depends on the nature of your matter. Ask the barrister or their clerks about funding early so you understand where you stand.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a barrister?

Solicitors usually manage a case from start to finish and deal with clients day to day. Barristers specialise in advocacy and specialist advice, and are typically brought in for hearings or difficult legal questions.
