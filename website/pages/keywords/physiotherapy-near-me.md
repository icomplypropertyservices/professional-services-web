- What are your fees, and do you charge for an initial assessment separately?

## Getting to your appointments and keeping them going

Searching for physiotherapy near me usually means the journey matters as much as the treatment. Physiotherapy works best as a course of sessions with review along the way, so a clinic you can reach easily on a bad day is more useful than one that is awkward to get to. Think about how you would travel when your symptoms are at their worst, not just on a good day.

Consider practicalities before you commit: parking or public transport, step-free access if you need it, and whether appointment times fit around work or caring responsibilities. Ask whether the physiotherapist offers any remote appointments for reviews or exercise progressions, which can reduce how often you need to travel in. If you are relying on someone else for a lift, check that their availability matches the times offered.

It also helps to ask what happens between sessions. A good plan usually includes exercises you do at home, so the clinic visits become checkpoints rather than the whole of your treatment. Ask how progress is reviewed and what you should do if your symptoms change before the next appointment.

## Checking physiotherapists are qualified and regulated

"Physiotherapist" and "physical therapist" are protected titles in the UK. Anyone using them must be registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC), and you can check the HCPC register online. Most physiotherapists are also members of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy (CSP), shown by the letters MCSP.

Sports therapists, massage therapists and personal trainers can be helpful for some needs, but they are not physiotherapists. For an injury, post-surgery rehabilitation or a neurological condition, check that you are booking a HCPC-registered physiotherapist.

## FAQs: physiotherapy

### How do I know if someone is a qualified physiotherapist?

In the UK, 'physiotherapist' is a protected title, so anyone using it must be registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC). You can check the HCPC register online. Many physiotherapists are also members of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy (CSP) and use the letters MCSP.

### Can I see a physiotherapist without a referral from my GP?

Yes, you can usually book an appointment with a private physiotherapist directly without seeing your GP first. However, if you are claiming on private health insurance, your insurer may require a referral, so it is worth checking your policy.

### What happens during a physiotherapy appointment?

Your physiotherapist will assess your movement and discuss your symptoms and goals. Treatment often includes hands-on techniques, exercises to do at home, and advice on managing your condition. Active treatment works best, so you should expect to be involved in your own recovery.

### How do I choose a physiotherapist near me?

Check that the physiotherapist is HCPC-registered and, if you wish, a member of the CSP. Consider their experience with your specific problem, the location and appointment times, and ask about fees before you book. You can also ask what their approach is and whether they provide home exercises.

### Can I have physiotherapy appointments remotely?

Many physiotherapists offer remote appointments, which can work well for reviews, exercise progression and advice. For an initial assessment of a movement problem, hands-on treatment or certain conditions, being seen in person is often more useful. Ask the physiotherapist what they recommend for your situation.

### What if I have to travel a long way to see a physiotherapist?

If the nearest suitable physiotherapist is some distance away, ask whether some sessions can be done remotely to reduce travel. It is also worth asking how a course of treatment is likely to be spaced out, so you can plan around the journeys. Discuss your circumstances when you enquire so the arrangements can be realistic.

### Do I need to keep going once I feel better?

Often the later sessions focus on building strength and confidence so the problem does not return, and on making sure you can manage your exercises independently. Your physiotherapist should explain why each stage matters and agree with you when sessions can stop. If you are unsure, ask what signs would suggest you should come back.

### Can physio help if I have already had a scan?

Yes. Bring the report with you. A physiotherapist will combine it with their own assessment to plan treatment.
