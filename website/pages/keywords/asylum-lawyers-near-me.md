## How to check you are in safe hands

It is a criminal offence to give immigration advice in the UK unless you are qualified and regulated. Advisers must be registered with the Immigration Advice Authority (IAA, formerly the OISC) at the right level for your matter, or be a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive authorised by their own regulator. Check the IAA's adviser finder or the SRA register before you share documents or pay.

IAA advisers are registered at levels that limit what they can do, from basic applications to appeals and representation. Ask what level an adviser holds and whether it covers your type of case.

"Lawyer" is a general term. In England and Wales the person handling your matter will usually be a solicitor, chartered legal executive or barrister, each regulated by their own body.

No. Asylum help must come from someone registered with the Immigration Advice Authority at the right level, or from a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive authorised by their own regulator.

### Does legal aid cover asylum work?

Legal aid often covers asylum matters, so it is worth asking about eligibility early. The professional you speak to can tell you whether it applies to your situation and what you would pay if it does not.

### Why do appeal deadlines matter so much in asylum cases?

Appeals in asylum cases have short deadlines, so a refusal letter needs attention quickly. Tell us straight away if you have a deadline running so the professional can see how urgent your enquiry is.

### Is it worth speaking to more than one asylum lawyer before deciding?

Many people do, and it can help you judge how clearly each one explains the process and what they would do for you. Ask each the same questions about their registration level, appeals and interview preparation so you can compare fairly.

### Can I bring someone with me to the first appointment?
