## Checking credentials yourself

Most IT consultancy work is not regulated by a single body, so it pays to check credentials yourself before you commit. Relevant memberships include BCS for IT professionals, and you can ask for case studies and references from clients whose situation resembles yours.

Where the work touches regulated areas such as data protection or financial services compliance, check that the consultant is clear about the limits of their role and when you need a solicitor or another regulated adviser. Cyber Essentials can be a good baseline for security, and it is worth asking whether the consultant works with it.

## Questions to ask the consultant

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the consultant is right for you:

- Have you advised businesses similar to mine, and can you share references from them?
- How would you describe the limits of your role if my work touches data protection or another regulated area?
- What would you need from us to assess our systems, and who would be involved?
- How do you approach security, and do you work with Cyber Essentials as a baseline?
- What happens once the project ends, and how would support be arranged?
- How will you document our systems and access so we are not dependent on one person?

An IT consultant advises on systems, cloud migration, cyber security, networks and technology strategy for businesses. The work can range from a one-off assessment to guiding a project through to completion, depending on what you need.
