## Qualifications and registration to look for

It is a criminal offence to give immigration advice in the UK unless you are qualified and regulated. Advisers must be registered with the Immigration Advice Authority (IAA, formerly the OISC) at the right level for your matter, or be a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive authorised by their own regulator. Check the IAA's adviser finder or the SRA register before you share documents or pay.

IAA advisers are registered at levels that limit what they can do, from basic applications to appeals and representation. Ask what level an adviser holds and whether it covers your type of case.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Comparing several local advisers before you choose

Searching for immigration advisors near me, in the plural, suggests you're weighing up more than one option rather than looking for a single name. That's a sensible approach, because fees, waiting times and the level of registration can differ from one practice to another, and a short comparison helps you ask better questions when you make contact.

When you compare, ask each adviser what level they are registered at with the Immigration Advice Authority and whether that level covers your matter, since a basic registration may not extend to appeals or representation. If they are a solicitor, barrister or chartered legal executive, ask which regulator authorises them and check the relevant register yourself. A local adviser who is open about their registration and happy for you to check is easier to deal with than one who is vague.

It also helps to compare how each adviser explains your options and what they would need from you, rather than comparing headline fees alone. Ask for a written breakdown of what is included, whether any Home Office fees are separate, and who will handle your case day to day. Keep copies of everything you send to each adviser, and don't share original documents until you've decided who to instruct.

## Immigration Advisors Near Me: FAQs

### How do I check if an immigration adviser is properly regulated?

You can check the Immigration Advice Authority's adviser finder for IAA-registered advisers, or the Solicitors Regulation Authority register for solicitors. Always do this before sharing documents or paying any money.

### What does 'price on application' mean for immigration advice?

It means the adviser will quote you a fee after they understand your case. Fees can vary depending on the complexity of your matter, so ask for a clear breakdown before you instruct anyone.

### What should I do if I've already received a refusal?

Keep the refusal letter and any other correspondence, and seek advice promptly as there may be deadlines to challenge the decision. A regulated adviser can explain your options, such as an appeal or a fresh application.

### Is a local immigration adviser always better than one further away?

Not necessarily. Being nearby can make face to face appointments easier, especially if you have documents to discuss or need language support. What matters most is that the adviser is regulated for your type of case, so it's fine to consider someone further away if they suit your matter better.

### How do I compare fees between several immigration advisers?

Ask each one for a written breakdown of their fees and what those fees cover, and check whether Home Office fees are separate. Because quotes are on application, the figure will depend on your circumstances, so compare what is included rather than the headline amount alone.

### Can I speak to more than one adviser before deciding?

Yes, and it can be helpful to do so when you're comparing options. Be clear about what you're asking at each stage, and avoid sharing original documents until you've chosen who to instruct.

### Can an adviser speed up my application?

An adviser cannot control Home Office processing times, but a complete, well-prepared application avoids avoidable delays. Some routes have paid priority services.
