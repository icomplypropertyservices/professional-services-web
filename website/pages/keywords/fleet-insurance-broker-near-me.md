## How fleet cover differs from insuring each vehicle separately

With separate motor policies, each vehicle has its own renewal date, its own excess and its own set of terms, which is easy to lose track of once you are running more than a couple of vehicles. A fleet policy brings them together under one arrangement, so there is a single renewal to diarise and one set of documents to keep on top of. That is often the practical reason people start searching for a fleet insurance broker near them rather than simply renewing as before.

The trade-off is that fleet cover is not automatically cheaper or broader. Insurers set their own thresholds for what counts as a fleet, and the driver terms vary considerably, so a policy that suits a business with named drivers may not suit one that lets staff drive pool vehicles. A broker's job is to set out those differences plainly and explain the exclusions that tend to catch people out, such as how vehicles are used or who is permitted to drive them.

It also helps to think about what happens mid-policy, not just at renewal. Adding a vehicle, taking one off the road or changing a regular driver are routine events for a growing business, and how straightforward those changes are depends on the insurer and the broker handling them. Ask at the outset how changes and claims are managed, and who you would speak to.

## Checking insurance brokers are qualified and regulated

Insurance brokers and intermediaries in the UK must be authorised by the FCA, or be appointed representatives of an authorised firm. Check them on the FCA register. Many brokers belong to the British Insurance Brokers' Association (BIBA), and some hold Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute.

Brokers must understand your demands and needs, explain key policy features and exclusions, and tell you how they are paid. Complaints that are not resolved can go to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

A broker works on your behalf to find suitable products from providers. Ask how many providers they compare and how they are paid.

## FAQs: a broker for fleet insurance

### Do I need a broker who is local to me for fleet insurance?

Not necessarily, but a local broker can be convenient for face-to-face meetings and may understand local risks better. Many brokers work remotely effectively, so it depends on your preference.

### What is the difference between fleet insurance and standard motor insurance?

Fleet insurance covers several vehicles under one policy, which can simplify administration and sometimes reduce cost. Standard motor insurance typically covers one vehicle per policy.

### Can I include different types of vehicles in a fleet policy?

Many fleet policies can cover a mix of vehicle types, such as vans and cars, but this depends on the insurer. A broker can check which insurers will cover your specific mix.

### How can I check that a broker is authorised?

You can check on the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) register. Brokers must be authorised by the FCA or be appointed representatives of an authorised firm.

### Will moving to a fleet policy save my business money?

Not necessarily. Bringing vehicles under one policy can reduce administration and may help on cost, but it depends on your vehicles, drivers and claims history. A broker can compare what is available and explain the figures rather than promise a saving.

### Can I add or remove a vehicle during the year?

Usually yes, but the terms differ between insurers, and some changes may affect your premium. It is worth asking how mid-term adjustments are handled before you choose a policy.

### What should I have ready before speaking to a broker?

A list of the vehicles and their registrations, who drives them and how they are used, plus any claims history, will let the conversation get straight to the point. It also helps to say whether you would consider telematics.

### Is a broker more expensive than buying direct?

Not necessarily. Brokers can access insurers and schemes not available directly, and help with claims. The right choice depends on how complex your needs are.
