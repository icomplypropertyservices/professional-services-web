## What happens when a surveyor visits, and how to compare local options

When you search for a local surveyor near me, part of what you are weighing up is convenience: someone who can get to your property without a long journey, and who knows the streets and housing stock around you. At the first appointment the surveyor will usually walk around the property with you if you are there, inspect the fabric inside and out, and ask what has prompted the survey. It helps to have any relevant paperwork to hand, such as plans, warranties or previous reports, and to point out the things that worry you rather than waiting to be asked.

What you receive afterwards depends on the type of survey you have agreed. A condition survey describes what the surveyor found and flags defects and matters needing further investigation; a valuation is a shorter assessment of worth, often for a lender. Before you book, ask which report you are getting, what it covers and what it leaves out, so you are not comparing two quotes that are actually for different pieces of work.

Because quotes are price on application, it is worth speaking to more than one local surveyor and asking each the same questions: what the report includes, whether they are independent of the sale, their RICS status, and when they can visit. Comparing on that basis tells you far more than comparing headline figures alone, and it helps you judge which surveyor suits your situation rather than simply which is nearest.

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Many surveyors are members of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), and RICS-regulated firms must follow professional standards, hold insurance and offer a complaints process. Chartered surveyors use the letters MRICS or FRICS. Some specialist roles have their own bodies, such as the Faculty of Party Wall Surveyors or the Property Care Association for damp and timber specialists.

Check the RICS directory, ask which report type you are getting and confirm whether the surveyor is independent of the sale. For asbestos work, look for organisations accredited by UKAS for surveying.

iComply is not a regulator and does not provide the professional service itself. We introduce you to professionals who hold their own registrations, and we encourage you to check them on the public register.

## Local Surveyor Near Me: FAQs

### How do I know if a surveyor is qualified?

You can check whether a surveyor is a member by asking them directly or looking at the RICS directory.

### What is the difference between a valuation and a survey?

A valuation is an assessment of a property's market value, often required by a lender. A survey is a more detailed inspection of the property's condition, which can help you decide whether to buy and what repairs might be needed.

### Do I need a local surveyor?

A local surveyor will be familiar with the typical issues in your area and can visit your property easily. This can be particularly useful if you need a quick turnaround or have concerns about local conditions.

### How much does a survey cost?

The cost of a survey varies depending on the type of survey, the size and age of the property, and the surveyor's location. It is best to ask for a quote and confirm what the fee includes before you proceed.

### Should I be there when the surveyor visits?

You do not have to be, and many surveys are carried out with keys arranged through an agent. If you can attend, it is a good chance to point out cracks, damp patches or recent alterations and to ask questions on the spot.

### Is a surveyor near me necessarily the best choice?

Proximity helps with scheduling and local familiarity, but it is not the whole picture. A surveyor who works slightly further away but has the right specialism for your property, such as period buildings or damp, may serve you better, so weigh both.

### Will a surveyor tell me whether to buy?

A surveyor reports on condition and flags risks; the decision to buy remains yours. Read the report carefully, ask about anything unclear, and take separate legal advice from your conveyancer on the purchase itself.

### Which survey do I need when buying a home?

For a conventional property in reasonable condition, an RICS Level 2 survey is often enough. Older, larger or altered properties often justify a Level 3 building survey. A surveyor can recommend the right level.
