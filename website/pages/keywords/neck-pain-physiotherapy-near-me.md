## How to check you are in safe hands

"Physiotherapist" and "physical therapist" are protected titles in the UK. Anyone using them must be registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC), and you can check the HCPC register online. Most physiotherapists are also members of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy (CSP), shown by the letters MCSP.

Sports therapists, massage therapists and personal trainers can be helpful for some needs, but they are not physiotherapists. For an injury, post-surgery rehabilitation or a neurological condition, check that you are booking a HCPC-registered physiotherapist.

## Neck Pain Physiotherapy Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need a referral from my GP to see a physiotherapist for neck pain?

In the UK, you can often see a physiotherapist without a GP referral, but some clinics may ask for one. It's worth checking with the physiotherapist you are introduced to. If your neck pain is severe or follows an injury, speak to your GP or call NHS 111 for advice.

### How can I tell if a physiotherapist is properly qualified?

Physiotherapists must be registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) to use the title 'physiotherapist'. You can check the HCPC register online. Many are also members of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy (CSP) and use the letters MCSP.

### What is the difference between a physiotherapist and a sports therapist for neck pain?

Physiotherapists are HCPC-registered healthcare professionals who assess and treat a wide range of conditions, including neck pain. Sports therapists and massage therapists are not physiotherapists and may not have the same training in assessing medical conditions. For neck pain with numbness, weakness or after surgery, choose a HCPC-registered physiotherapist.
