## Questions to ask the family barrister

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the barrister is right for you:

- Are you authorised to accept instructions directly from the public under the Public Access scheme?
- What is your experience with the type of family matter I am facing?
- How do you charge for advice, preparation and representation, and what will I need to pay upfront?
- Will you be the person representing me at the hearing, or might another barrister be instructed?
- How should we communicate between now and the hearing, and how quickly can you respond?
- Can you explain what the court process will involve and what I should prepare?

## Understanding how family barristers work

Family barristers in England and Wales are regulated by the Bar Standards Board. You can check the Barristers' Register to see whether someone holds a current practising certificate and whether they are authorised to accept instructions directly from the public. In Scotland the equivalent professionals are advocates, regulated through the Faculty of Advocates.

Barristers usually work from chambers, with clerks who manage diaries and fees. Many are instructed through a solicitor in the traditional way, while those registered under the Public Access scheme can be instructed directly by members of the public for suitable matters. Courts also encourage settlement, so a barrister may advise on negotiation as well as representation.

## Family Barrister Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need a solicitor to instruct a family barrister?

Not always. Many barristers are instructed through a solicitor in the traditional way, but those registered under the Public Access scheme can be instructed directly by members of the public for suitable matters. ### How do I check that a family barrister is regulated?

Barristers in England and Wales are regulated by the Bar Standards Board. The Barristers' Register shows whether someone holds a current practising certificate and whether they are authorised to accept instructions directly from the public. In Scotland, advocates are regulated through the Faculty of Advocates.

### Will a family barrister near me charge for an initial conversation?

This varies between chambers, and fees are usually quoted on application. Ask the clerk or the barrister directly about any charge for an initial discussion and how fees are structured before you proceed.

### Can a family barrister help me avoid going to court?

Courts encourage settlement, and a barrister can advise on negotiation and help you understand your position before a hearing. They can also represent you if the matter does proceed to court.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a barrister?

Solicitors usually manage a case from start to finish and deal with clients day to day. Barristers specialise in advocacy and specialist advice, and are typically brought in for hearings or difficult legal questions.
