## Questions to ask the asbestos surveyor

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Is your organisation accredited by UKAS for asbestos surveying?
- Which type of survey will you carry out, and what will the report include?
- Will you take samples for laboratory analysis, and is that included in your quote?
- How will you access any areas that are difficult to reach, such as roof voids or sealed spaces?
- What experience do you have with buildings like mine and the materials I am concerned about?
- What happens if asbestos is found during the survey?

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Many surveyors are members of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), and RICS-regulated firms must follow professional standards, hold insurance and offer a complaints process. Chartered surveyors use the letters MRICS or FRICS. Some specialist roles have their own bodies, such as the Faculty of Party Wall Surveyors or the Property Care Association for damp and timber specialists.

Check the RICS directory, ask which report type you are getting and confirm whether the surveyor is independent of the sale. For asbestos work, look for organisations accredited by UKAS for surveying.

## Why UKAS accreditation matters for asbestos surveying

Asbestos surveying is specialist work, and the recognised mark of competence for organisations carrying out asbestos surveys is accreditation by UKAS. Asking whether the organisation is UKAS-accredited for surveying is a straightforward way to check that the work will be done to a recognised standard. Many surveyors are also members of RICS, which sets professional standards for firms it regulates.

There are different types of asbestos survey, and the right one depends on your situation. A management survey is typically for premises that are in use, while a refurbishment and demolition survey is needed before works that will disturb the building. Make sure you know which report type you are being offered before you commission the work.

## FAQs about asbestos surveyor near me

### Do I need an asbestos survey before I refurbish my property?

A refurbishment and demolition survey is normally needed before works that will disturb the building fabric, so that asbestos can be identified and dealt with safely. The surveyor will advise on the right type of survey for your project.

### How do I check that an asbestos surveyor is properly qualified?

Look for organisations accredited by UKAS for asbestos surveying, and ask whether the surveyor is a member of a professional body such as RICS. You can also ask to see examples of similar work they have done.

### What is the difference between a management survey and a refurbishment survey?

A management survey is typically for premises in normal use and helps duty holders manage asbestos, while a refurbishment and demolition survey is more intrusive and is carried out before works that will disturb the building. The right choice depends on your plans.

### Which survey do I need when buying a home?

For a conventional property in reasonable condition, an RICS Level 2 survey is often enough. Older, larger or altered properties often justify a Level 3 building survey. A surveyor can recommend the right level.
