## Best Guarantor Mortgage Near Me: FAQs

### What is a guarantor mortgage?

A guarantor mortgage lets a family member support your borrowing, often by using their savings or property as security or by agreeing to cover payments if you can't. The guarantor takes on real risk, so they should get independent advice before agreeing.

### Do I need a broker near me for a guarantor mortgage?

Not necessarily. Many brokers advise on guarantor mortgages remotely across the UK, so being nearby isn't essential. What matters is that the broker is authorised by the FCA and understands these products.

### Can a guarantor be removed from a mortgage later?

It may be possible to remove a guarantor if your circumstances improve and the lender agrees, but this depends on the lender's criteria and your affordability. A broker can explain the options for your situation.

### What should my guarantor think about before agreeing?

Your guarantor should consider the risk to their own home or savings, and how their circumstances might change. They should get independent legal advice, and a broker can explain how the arrangement works.

### Will my guarantor need their own legal advice?

It's strongly advisable. A guarantor is taking on genuine risk, and independent legal advice helps them understand exactly what they're signing up to. Ask the broker whether the lender will require this, as some do.

### Can a guarantor mortgage help if I'm self-employed?

It can, because the guarantor's support may strengthen your application where your income is harder for lenders to assess. A broker who deals with guarantor mortgages can explain which lenders are more open to self-employed borrowers in your position.

### How is a guarantor mortgage different from a joint mortgage?

With a joint mortgage, everyone named is a borrower and usually owns a share of the property. A guarantor mortgage, or a joint borrower sole proprietor arrangement, lets someone support your borrowing without necessarily being on the title, though their risk is still real.

### Is a broker better than going to my bank?

A broker can compare many lenders and may find options your bank does not offer. Your bank can only offer its own products. Either can suit you; a broker gives you a wider view.
