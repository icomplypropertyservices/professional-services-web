## What a scale and polish involves

A scale and polish removes plaque and tartar from your teeth, which helps prevent gum disease and freshens your mouth. It's usually carried out by a dental hygienist, though a dentist may do it too. How often you need one depends on the health of your gums, so the professional will assess you and advise on the right interval.

It's common to feel some sensitivity after a cleaning, especially if you haven't had one for a while. This usually settles down, but you can ask the professional about it beforehand. ## FAQs: a scale and polish

### Is a scale and polish painful?

Most people find it comfortable, though you may feel some vibration or scraping. If your gums are inflamed or you have a lot of tartar, it can be a little tender, but the professional will go at your pace. Let them know if you feel any discomfort.

### How long does a scale and polish take?

It depends on how much tartar and plaque there is, but a routine clean usually takes a short appointment. The professional will be able to give you an idea when you book.

### Can a hygienist do a scale and polish?

Yes, dental hygienists are trained to carry out scale and polish treatments. They must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the register online. Some practices may have a dentist do it instead.

### What should I do if I'm not happy with the treatment?

If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process. The Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

### Can I see a private dentist and stay with my NHS dentist?

Yes, you can use both. Some patients see a private dentist for specific treatments and keep NHS care for routine check-ups, if their NHS practice agrees.
