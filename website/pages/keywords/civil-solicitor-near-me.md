## Understanding pre-action protocols and costs

Before most civil claims go to court, pre-action protocols apply. These set out the steps you and the other side should take to try to resolve the dispute, including sharing information and considering mediation. A civil solicitor near you can explain how these protocols affect your situation.

Costs risks should be explained early. Civil litigation can be expensive, and if you lose you may have to pay the other side's costs as well as your own. A good solicitor will be upfront about the likely costs and the risks before you commit.

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

iComply is not a regulator and does not provide the professional service itself. We introduce you to professionals who hold their own registrations, and we encourage you to check them on the public register.

## FAQs: a civil solicitor

### Do I need a civil solicitor near me, or can I use one further away?

You can use a solicitor anywhere in England and Wales, but a local one may be more convenient for meetings and may know the local courts. iComply introduces you to a suitable UK civil solicitor based on your needs.

### What is the difference between a civil solicitor and a criminal solicitor?

Civil solicitors deal with non-criminal disputes between people and organisations, such as contracts, debts, property and negligence. Criminal solicitors deal with criminal charges. If your matter is a dispute rather than a criminal allegation, a civil solicitor is the right professional.

### Will I have to go to court?

Not necessarily. Many civil disputes are resolved through negotiation or mediation without going to court. Pre-action protocols encourage parties to exchange information and consider mediation before issuing a claim.

### How can I check that a civil solicitor is regulated?

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA). You can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register.

### Should I choose a civil solicitor based on how close they are?

Proximity can help with meetings and local court familiarity, but it should not be the deciding factor. Relevant experience in your type of dispute and a clear explanation of costs and risks matter more. A solicitor slightly further away may be the better fit for your matter.

### What should I have ready for the first appointment with a civil solicitor?

Bring any contract, correspondence, letter before claim or court papers, and a short timeline of what has happened. It also helps to note what you want to achieve and any deadlines you know about. This lets the solicitor assess your position and explain the pre-action steps and mediation options.

### Can a civil solicitor near me help if the other side has already instructed a solicitor?

Yes. A civil solicitor can act for you whether or not the other side is represented, and pre-action protocols set out how the parties should exchange information. Tell iComply if the other side has a solicitor, as it helps match you with someone suited to your dispute.

### Can I get legal aid?

Legal aid is still available for some criminal, family (especially where there is domestic abuse), housing and immigration matters, subject to means and merits tests. A matched firm can tell you whether you are likely to qualify.
