## Qualifications and registration to look for

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

## How fees usually work (POA)

Legal fees depend on the type of matter, its complexity and who does the work. Firms commonly charge hourly rates, fixed fees for defined stages, or (in some claims) conditional fee arrangements. Regulated firms in England and Wales must publish pricing information for certain services and give you clear costs information at the outset. The firm will give you a POA quote.

Ask for a written estimate and for an explanation of what is not included, such as court fees, barristers' fees or experts. Ask too whether legal expenses insurance or legal aid might cover some or all of the cost.

## Finding the best solicitor: a practical checklist

There is no single best solicitor for everyone. The right choice depends on your situation, how complex it is, where you are and how you like to communicate. A practice that is outstanding for one client can be a poor fit for another.

## What to expect at your first meeting with a solicitor

When you first meet a solicitor near you, they will usually ask you to explain the legal matter in your own words and will then ask questions to fill in the details. They may ask to see any documents you have, such as a contract, a court letter or correspondence with the other side. This helps them understand your position and advise you on your options.

You should expect the solicitor to explain the law in a way you can understand and to outline the possible courses of action, including the risks and likely costs of each. They may not be able to give you a complete answer at the first meeting if they need to review documents or research a point, but they should tell you what happens next and when you will hear from them.

It is also your chance to ask about their experience with cases like yours, who will be handling your matter day to day, and how you will be kept informed. If you feel rushed or unclear about anything, say so. A good solicitor will welcome your questions and make sure you leave with a clear idea of what to do next.

## FAQs about best solicitor near me

### How do I check that a solicitor is properly regulated?

In England and Wales, you can check a solicitor or firm on the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA) register. In Scotland, check with the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland with the Law Society of Northern Ireland.

### Can I ask for a quote before I commit?

Yes, it is sensible to ask for information about fees and what is included before you instruct a solicitor. Many firms will give you an initial indication of costs, though the final amount may depend on how your matter develops.

### What should I do if I have a complaint about my solicitor?

First, use the firm's own complaints procedure. If that does not resolve the issue, in England and Wales you can take service complaints to the Legal Ombudsman.

### Do I need a solicitor who is local to me?

Not necessarily. Many legal matters can be handled remotely, but some people prefer to meet in person. When you enquire, tell us your preference so we can introduce you to a suitable professional.

### Will the first meeting be free?

Not necessarily. Some solicitors offer a free initial consultation, but many charge for their time. Always ask about the cost of the first meeting before you book, and check whether any fee will be deducted from later charges if you go on to instruct them.

### What should I bring to the first appointment?

Bring any documents relevant to your matter, such as contracts, letters, court forms or emails, and a note of key dates and names. It also helps to write down your questions in advance so you do not forget them.

### Can I change solicitors if I am not happy?

Yes, you can change solicitors, but you will need to settle any outstanding fees with the first firm and the new firm will need to obtain your file. It is worth discussing your concerns with your current solicitor first, as many issues can be resolved quickly.

### Is a solicitor the same as a lawyer?

"Lawyer" is a general word that covers solicitors, barristers, chartered legal executives and others. A solicitor is a specific regulated professional who can advise you, run your case and instruct a barrister if needed.
