## How to check you are in safe hands

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

## FAQs: a company and commercial solicitor

### Do I need a company commercial solicitor who is local to me?

Not necessarily, but meeting in person can help with complex or sensitive business matters. A local solicitor may also be more familiar with the local business community, though this is not essential. Many solicitors now work effectively remotely.

### What is the difference between a company commercial solicitor and a general practice solicitor?

Company commercial solicitors focus on business law, including corporate transactions and commercial contracts, while general practice solicitors may handle a wider range of personal and business matters. For specialised work like share sales or investment rounds, a company commercial solicitor is usually more appropriate.

### How can I check that a solicitor is properly regulated?

In England and Wales, you can check the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA) register. In Scotland, check with the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland with the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Always confirm that the person handling your matter is authorised.

### What should I prepare before contacting a company commercial solicitor?

Gather any relevant documents, such as draft contracts, company accounts, or details of the transaction. Think about what you want to achieve and any deadlines. Being clear about your objectives will help the solicitor advise you efficiently.

### Is a solicitor the same as a lawyer?

"Lawyer" is a general word that covers solicitors, barristers, chartered legal executives and others. A solicitor is a specific regulated professional who can advise you, run your case and instruct a barrister if needed.
