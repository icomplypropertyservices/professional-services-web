## Questions to ask the financial adviser

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Financial advice in the UK is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Check that any adviser or firm is authorised, or is an appointed representative of an authorised firm, on the FCA register. If something goes wrong you can complain to the firm, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and the Financial Services Compensation Scheme may protect you if an authorised firm fails.

Advisers must tell you whether their advice is independent (considering the whole market) or restricted (limited to certain products or providers), and how they charge. Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute or the Personal Finance Society, and certifications such as CFP, show advanced qualifications.

Adviser and advisor are simply two spellings of the same word. What matters is whether the person giving advice is qualified and, where the law requires it, regulated for that type of advice.

## Best Financial Advisor Near Me: FAQs

### How can I tell if a financial adviser is properly authorised?

Check the adviser or firm on the FCA register to confirm they are authorised or an appointed representative. You can also ask them directly for their FCA registration number. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

### What is the difference between independent and restricted advice?

Independent advisers consider the whole market when recommending products, while restricted advisers are limited to certain products or providers. Advisers must tell you which type of advice they offer before you proceed. This matters because it affects the range of options available to you.

### Will I have to pay for the first meeting?

Many financial advisers offer an initial meeting at no cost, but this varies. Ask upfront whether there is any charge for the first consultation and how fees are structured. iComply does not set or take fees; the adviser will quote you directly.

### What should I bring to a first meeting with a financial adviser?

It helps to bring details of your income, savings, pensions, investments and any existing policies, along with a note of your goals. You do not need exact figures if you are not comfortable sharing them yet. The adviser will guide you on what they need to know.

### Does my adviser need to be based in my town?

Not necessarily. What matters most is that they are authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority and experienced in the kind of advice you need. If meeting in person matters to you, ask upfront where meetings would take place and whether home visits are possible.

### Can I change my mind after the first appointment?

Yes. A first appointment is a chance to hear how an adviser works and what they would charge before you decide whether to go ahead. You are under no obligation to proceed, and it is sensible to speak to more than one adviser before choosing.

### How do I compare advisers who all say they are the best?

Anyone can describe themselves as the best, so look at what you can actually check: their entry on the FCA register, whether their advice is independent or restricted, their qualifications, and a written explanation of their charges and ongoing service. Comparing those details across advisers tells you far more than a claim on a leaflet.

### Is the first meeting free?

Many firms offer a free initial consultation to explain their service and charges. Formal advice is then charged as agreed in writing.
