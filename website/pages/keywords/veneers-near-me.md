Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- Would you recommend porcelain or composite veneers for my teeth, and why?

## What to understand before you choose a dentist for veneers

Veneers come in two main types: porcelain and composite. Porcelain veneers usually need some enamel removed, which is permanent, while composite veneers are reversible in some cases. Ask any dentist you speak to how long they expect the veneers to last and what aftercare involves.

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA. If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process, and the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

## What happens at a veneer consultation

A first appointment for veneers is usually a conversation as much as an examination. The dentist will look at your front teeth, talk about what you'd like to change, and explain whether porcelain or composite veneers would suit you — or whether something else might be more appropriate.

If you're considering porcelain veneers, ask them to show you what enamel removal would involve and what that means for your teeth in the long term. For composite veneers, ask how they're built up and how they can be maintained or reversed in some cases. Take notes if it helps, and don't feel rushed into deciding on the day.

It's also worth asking what the quote covers before you commit — whether it includes the consultation, any temporary work, and follow-up appointments. Getting this in writing means you can compare options locally on a like-for-like basis rather than on price alone.

## FAQs: veneers

### Do veneers always require my teeth to be drilled?

Not always. Porcelain veneers usually need some enamel removed, but composite veneers are reversible in some cases. A dentist can tell you what's appropriate for your teeth after an examination.

### Can I check whether a dentist is qualified to fit veneers?

Yes. Dentists must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must also register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which publishes inspection ratings.

### What should I do if I'm unhappy with private veneer treatment?

Start with the practice's complaints process. If that doesn't resolve things, the Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

### How do I judge which local dentist to choose for veneers?

Ask about their GDC registration, how they would approach your specific teeth, and what their quote includes. Comparing a few consultations and asking how long they expect veneers to last helps you decide what's right for you.

### Will I be pressured to book veneers at the first appointment?

A good consultation should give you time to think. You're entitled to ask questions, consider the options and come back to a practice once you've decided. If you feel rushed, it's reasonable to say you'd like time to consider.

### Can a dentist tell me whether veneers will suit my teeth before I commit?

Yes — an examination lets the dentist assess your teeth and gums and explain what veneers could achieve for you. They can also tell you whether another treatment might be more suitable for your situation.

### Can I see a private dentist and stay with my NHS dentist?

Yes, you can use both. Some patients see a private dentist for specific treatments and keep NHS care for routine check-ups, if their NHS practice agrees.
