Legal fees depend on the type of matter, its complexity and who does the work. Firms commonly charge hourly rates, fixed fees for defined stages, or (in some claims) conditional fee arrangements. ## Warning signs to watch for

Be cautious if you notice any of the following:

- Requests to send money to an account that changes by email
- Guarantees about the outcome of a dispute
- No clear costs information or client care letter

## Qualifications and registration to look for

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland solicitors are regulated by the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland by the Law Society of Northern Ireland. Regulated firms must hold professional indemnity insurance and have a complaints procedure; if that does not resolve things, the Legal Ombudsman can look at service complaints in England and Wales.

The word "lawyer" is not protected on its own, so check that whoever handles your matter is a solicitor, a chartered legal executive (CILEx) or another authorised professional. Reserved activities such as conducting court litigation and certain probate and conveyancing work can only be carried out by authorised people.

## Employment Solicitor Near Me: FAQs

### Do I need a solicitor who is near me physically?

Not necessarily, as much employment advice can be given by phone or video. But searching for an employment solicitor near you can be useful if you would prefer to meet in person or deal with a firm you can visit.

### How do I check that an employment solicitor is properly regulated?

Solicitors in England and Wales are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and you can check an individual or firm on the SRA's register. In Scotland the regulator is the Law Society of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland it is the Law Society of Northern Ireland.

### I have been asked to sign a settlement agreement. What should I do?

Settlement agreements need independent legal advice before you sign, so it is worth speaking to an employment solicitor promptly. They can explain the terms and what you are agreeing to, and the price will be quoted to you directly.

### How soon should I contact an employment solicitor?

Employment tribunal time limits are short, often three months less a day, and ACAS early conciliation usually has to happen first. Getting advice early gives you more room to consider your options.

### Will the solicitor near me act for me or for my employer?

Employment solicitors can act for either employees or employers, and some firms do both. It is worth asking directly who they usually represent, so you can decide whether they are the right fit for your situation.

### Can I speak to a solicitor near me before deciding whether to instruct them?

Many firms will talk to you briefly about your situation before you commit, though some may charge for a longer appointment. Ask when you enquire what the first conversation involves and how any charge will work, with the price on application.

### What if there is no employment solicitor in my own town?

You may still be able to get advice from a solicitor further away by phone or video, and searching near you can simply be a starting point. If meeting in person matters to you, say so when you enquire and we can take that into account when introducing you.

### Is a solicitor the same as a lawyer?

"Lawyer" is a general word that covers solicitors, barristers, chartered legal executives and others. A solicitor is a specific regulated professional who can advise you, run your case and instruct a barrister if needed.
