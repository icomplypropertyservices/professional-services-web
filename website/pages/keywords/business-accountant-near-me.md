## Qualifications and registration to look for

In the UK, the word "accountant" on its own is not a protected title, so anyone can use it. Titles such as Chartered Accountant (ICAEW, ICAS or Chartered Accountants Ireland), Chartered Certified Accountant (ACCA) and Chartered Management Accountant (CIMA) are protected, and members are bound by their body's ethics, training and complaints rules. Only registered auditors can sign statutory audit reports.

Every practice offering accountancy services must also be supervised for anti-money-laundering purposes, either by a professional body or by HMRC. It is reasonable to ask which body supervises a firm, whether they hold professional indemnity insurance, and how complaints are handled.

Many businesses work with accountants remotely, but a local accountant can be helpful if you prefer face-to-face meetings or need to drop off records. It's a personal choice, and iComply can introduce you to a suitable professional based on your preferences.

It helps to have your business structure, recent financial records, and any HMRC correspondence to hand. The accountant will then be able to give you a clearer picture of what they can do for you.

Accountants typically quote based on the work involved, such as the number of transactions, whether you need payroll or VAT returns, and the complexity of your affairs. Fees are usually agreed in advance, and iComply introduces you to an accountant who will quote you directly.

### Can an accountant help if I'm behind on my tax returns?

Yes, accountants can help you get up to date and deal with HMRC on your behalf. It's best to be upfront about any overdue returns so they can advise you properly.

### Do I need a chartered accountant?

Not always. For simple Self Assessment a qualified, supervised accountant may be enough. For audits, complex group structures or specialist tax work, a chartered or certified firm is often the safer choice. We can match you to either.
