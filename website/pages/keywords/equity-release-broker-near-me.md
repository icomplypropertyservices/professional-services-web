## How to check you are in safe hands

Equity release advice is regulated by the FCA, and advisers must hold a specialist qualification. Check the firm on the FCA register. Most providers and advisers are members of the Equity Release Council, whose standards include a no-negative-equity guarantee and the right to stay in your home for life.

You will also need your own solicitor to explain the legal documents before you sign. Many advisers encourage you to involve family members in the discussion.

A broker works on your behalf to find suitable products from providers. Ask how many providers they compare and how they are paid.

## FAQs: an equity release broker

### Do I need a solicitor as well as an equity release adviser?

Yes. You will need your own solicitor to explain the legal documents before you sign, separately from the adviser who arranges the plan. Many advisers encourage you to involve family members in the discussion too.

### How can I check that an equity release adviser is properly regulated?

You can check the firm on the FCA register before you go ahead, and it's worth asking whether it is a member of the Equity Release Council.

### What does whole-of-market mean when choosing a broker?

It means the adviser can compare plans across lenders rather than only offering products from a limited panel. Because features such as drawdown and voluntary repayments vary between plans, whole-of-market access matters when you're weighing up your options.

### Will I be told about fees before I commit?

Fees must be explained to you, so ask how the adviser charges and when payment would fall due. Quotes are on application, so it's sensible to get this clear early in the conversation.

### Can I lose my home?

Plans meeting Equity Release Council standards give you the right to live in your home for life, as long as you keep to the terms.
