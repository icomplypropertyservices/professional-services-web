## How to check you are in safe hands

Mortgage advice is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Any broker or adviser you use should be authorised, or be an appointed representative of an authorised firm, and you can check this on the FCA register. Advisers typically hold a qualification such as CeMAP.

Brokers must tell you whether they search the whole of the market or a limited panel of lenders, and how they are paid, whether by lender commission, a fee, or both. If something goes wrong, you can complain to the firm and then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

## FAQs about top guarantor mortgage near me

### What is a guarantor mortgage?

A guarantor mortgage allows a family member to support a borrower's application by using their savings or property as extra security. The guarantor agrees to cover repayments if the borrower cannot.

### What does 'joint borrower, sole proprietor' mean?

It's an arrangement where more than one person is named on the mortgage, but only one person is the legal owner of the property. This can help a family member support affordability without becoming a co-owner.

### Should a guarantor get independent advice?

Yes, it's important that anyone acting as guarantor understands the risks and takes independent legal and financial advice. A broker can explain what's involved, but the guarantor should consider separate advice.

### How do I find a top guarantor mortgage broker near me?

Look for a broker who is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority and who specialises in guarantor mortgages. You can check their status on the FCA register and ask about their experience and how they search the market.

### Is there an official list of top guarantor mortgage brokers?

No, there's no official ranking of mortgage brokers. Instead, check that a firm is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority on the FCA register, and ask direct questions about their experience with guarantor mortgages and how they search the market.

### Can I use a broker who isn't local to me?

Yes. Many brokers advise by phone or video, which can be useful if the guarantor lives in a different part of the UK from the borrower. What matters more than distance is that the broker is authorised and can explain the arrangement clearly to everyone involved.

### Will the broker speak to my guarantor as well as me?

A good broker should be willing to include the guarantor in conversations, because the guarantor is taking on real risk. Ask at the outset whether the guarantor can join appointments and whether the broker will recommend that they take independent advice before agreeing.
