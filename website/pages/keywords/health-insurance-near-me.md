## Checking insurance brokers are qualified and regulated

Insurance brokers and intermediaries in the UK must be authorised by the FCA, or be appointed representatives of an authorised firm. Check them on the FCA register. Many brokers belong to the British Insurance Brokers' Association (BIBA), and some hold Chartered status from the Chartered Insurance Institute.

Brokers must understand your demands and needs, explain key policy features and exclusions, and tell you how they are paid. Complaints that are not resolved can go to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

## Health Insurance Near Me: FAQs

### Will private medical insurance cover my existing condition?

Pre-existing conditions are usually excluded from private medical insurance, but some insurers may offer cover with a higher premium or after a waiting period. It is important to disclose your full medical history so the broker can explain what is possible.

### What is the difference between full medical underwriting and moratorium underwriting?

With full medical underwriting, you complete a medical questionnaire and the insurer decides upfront what will and will not be covered. With moratorium underwriting, you do not need to complete a full questionnaire, but conditions you have had in recent years may not be covered until a set period has passed.

### Can I choose which hospital I go to?

Most policies have a list of hospitals you can use, and this can vary between insurers. You should check the hospital lists before you buy to make sure your preferred local hospitals are included.

### How do insurance brokers get paid?

Brokers must tell you how they are paid, which is usually through a commission from the insurer or a fee you pay directly. You should ask for this to be explained clearly before you proceed.

### Is a broker more expensive than buying direct?

Not necessarily. Brokers can access insurers and schemes not available directly, and help with claims. The right choice depends on how complex your needs are.
