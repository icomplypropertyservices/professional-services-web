## Qualifications and registration to look for

Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity. It can be carried out by solicitors regulated by the SRA or by licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers (CLC). Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money. In Scotland, property transactions are handled by solicitors under Scottish law.

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme (CQS) is a common accreditation. Check that your chosen firm is on your lender's panel before you instruct them.

## FAQs about remortgage conveyancer near me

### Do I need a conveyancer for a remortgage?

Yes, a conveyancer is needed to handle the legal work of redeeming your existing mortgage and registering your new lender's charge. Some lenders offer free legal packages, but you can choose your own conveyancer if you prefer.

### Can I use a conveyancer who is not on my lender's panel?

Some lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, so it's important to check before you instruct. If your chosen conveyancer is not on the panel, the lender may not accept them, which could delay your remortgage.

### How early should I start the remortgage process?

It's wise to start well before your current deal ends to avoid any rush. The conveyancing process can take time, especially if there are additional legal matters such as a transfer of equity.

### What is a transfer of equity and does it affect my remortgage?

A transfer of equity is when you change the ownership of a property, for example adding or removing someone from the title. It requires extra legal work, so you should tell your conveyancer if your remortgage includes one.

### Can I use a local conveyancer if my lender has a panel?

Yes, you can use a local conveyancer as long as they are on your lender's panel. Many lenders allow you to choose your own conveyancer, but you should always check first. If your preferred local firm is not on the panel, ask your lender if they will accept them or consider using a panel firm.

### What should I bring to the first appointment?

You'll typically need proof of identity, such as a passport or driving licence, and proof of address. It also helps to have details of your existing mortgage and the new mortgage offer, including the lender's name and account numbers. The conveyancer will let you know exactly what they need beforehand.

### How does a remortgage conveyancer differ from a purchase conveyancer?

A remortgage conveyancer focuses on the legal work of switching your mortgage, which mainly involves redeeming your existing mortgage and registering the new lender's charge. A purchase conveyancer does that plus additional work like checking the title, raising enquiries, and handling searches and contracts. If your remortgage includes a transfer of equity, the work is closer to a purchase.

### What is the difference between a solicitor and a licensed conveyancer?

Both are regulated and insured. Licensed conveyancers specialise in property transactions, while solicitors can also advise on wider legal issues. Either can handle a standard purchase or sale.
