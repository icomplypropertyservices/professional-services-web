## Checking conveyancers are qualified and regulated

Conveyancing in England and Wales is a reserved legal activity. It can be carried out by solicitors regulated by the SRA or by licensed conveyancers regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers (CLC). Both must hold professional indemnity insurance and follow strict rules on handling client money. In Scotland, property transactions are handled by solicitors under Scottish law.

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, and the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme (CQS) is a common accreditation. Check that your chosen firm is on your lender's panel before you instruct them.

## Questions to ask the conveyancer

Once you are introduced, these questions help you decide whether the conveyancer is right for you:

- Are you regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers, and what is your registration number?
- Are you on my lender's panel of approved conveyancers?

- How will you keep me updated on progress, and who will be my main point of contact?
- Do you have experience with the type of property I am buying or selling, such as leasehold or new build?
- What is your process for handling client money, and which bank account will my deposit be held in?

## FAQs: a licensed conveyancer

### What is a licensed conveyancer?

A licensed conveyancer is a property law specialist regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers. They are qualified to handle purchases, sales and remortgages, and must follow strict rules on handling client money.

### How do I check that a conveyancer is licensed?

You can check the Council for Licensed Conveyancers register, which lists all licensed conveyancers and firms. It is a good idea to do this before you instruct anyone.

### Do I need a conveyancer on my lender's panel?

Many lenders only work with conveyancers on their panel, so it is important to check this before you instruct a conveyancer. If your chosen conveyancer is not on the panel, your lender may not accept them.
