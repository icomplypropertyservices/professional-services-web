## Qualifications and registration to look for

Many surveyors are members of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), and RICS-regulated firms must follow professional standards, hold insurance and offer a complaints process. Chartered surveyors use the letters MRICS or FRICS. Some specialist roles have their own bodies, such as the Faculty of Party Wall Surveyors or the Property Care Association for damp and timber specialists.

Check the RICS directory, ask which report type you are getting and confirm whether the surveyor is independent of the sale. For asbestos work, look for organisations accredited by UKAS for surveying.

## Getting the level of detail right

A measured survey can be as simple as outline plans or as detailed as a full set of plans, elevations and sections, and the cost and time follow that choice. Laser scanning can capture a building quickly and in fine detail, which suits complex or irregular spaces, but it is worth agreeing with your architect what is actually needed for the design stage you are at.

## How a measured survey differs from a building survey

People often arrive at "measured surveyor near me" after being told they need a survey, and it is easy to confuse the two. A building survey is an inspection of a property's condition, usually carried out for a buyer before purchase, and it reports on defects and what needs attention. A measured survey is about geometry rather than condition: it records the building as it stands so that accurate plans, elevations and sections can be drawn.

That distinction matters when you are briefing whoever you speak to. If your architect needs existing drawings to design an extension, a measured survey is the job; if you are buying and want to know whether the roof needs work, that is a different service entirely. Saying clearly which one you need saves everyone time and avoids an appointment that does not give you what you were expecting.

It also helps to think about who will use the drawings and for what. A builder pricing a refurbishment may need less detail than an architect submitting a planning application, and the level of detail you agree should reflect that. Being clear about the end use from the start is the simplest way to get drawings that are fit for purpose.

## FAQs: a measured building surveyor

### What does a measured surveyor actually produce?

Measured surveyors produce accurate floor plans, elevations and sections of existing buildings. These become the base drawings an architect designs from, so their accuracy matters for everything that follows.

### Do I need laser scanning for my survey?

Not necessarily. Laser scanning captures a building quickly and in fine detail and suits complex or irregular spaces, but a conventional measured survey may be enough for simpler buildings. Agree the level of detail with your architect first.

### How do I check a surveyor's credentials?

Many surveyors are members of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), and chartered surveyors use MRICS or FRICS after their name. You can check the RICS directory, and RICS-regulated firms must hold insurance and offer a complaints process.

### What should I have ready before the surveyor visits?

It helps to have any existing drawings, plans or deeds you hold, and to know what your architect needs the drawings for. Clear access to all parts of the building, including lofts and outbuildings, lets the surveyor work efficiently.

### Is a measured survey the same as a building survey?

No. A measured survey records the size, shape and layout of a building to produce accurate drawings, while a building survey assesses condition and reports on defects. If you are unsure which you need, explain your plans to the surveyor and they can tell you.

### Can the surveyor work from my existing plans instead of visiting?

Sometimes old drawings can be used as a starting point, but they may not match what is actually there after years of alterations. A visit lets the surveyor check the building as it stands, which is the whole point of a measured survey.

### Is a mortgage valuation the same as a survey?

No. A mortgage valuation is carried out for the lender. A survey is carried out for you and looks at the property's condition.
