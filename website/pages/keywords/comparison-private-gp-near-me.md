## What to understand when comparing private GPs

Private GPs in the UK must be registered with the General Medical Council (GMC) and hold a licence to practise. Clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must also be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings; in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland different regulators apply. You can check a doctor's registration on the GMC's online register.

A private GP does not replace your NHS GP. Good private clinics will ask for your consent to share consultation notes with your NHS practice, so your records stay complete and any medication is prescribed safely. When comparing, ask how each clinic handles this sharing.

## Questions to ask a private GP

Asking a few direct questions early tells you a lot about how a practice works:

- What is the typical length of an appointment, and can I book a longer slot if needed?
- Do you offer video consultations, and for which types of problems are they suitable?
- Will you share consultation notes with my NHS GP, and how do you handle consent?
- What is the process if I need a referral to a specialist or further tests?
- Are there any additional costs for certificates, reports or referrals?
- How do you handle prescriptions, and can they be sent to a pharmacy of my choice?

In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

## FAQs: a private GP

### How do I compare private GPs near me?

Start by checking that the doctor is registered with the GMC and, in England, that the clinic is registered with the CQC. Then compare appointment lengths, availability, consultation types and how they share notes with your NHS GP. You can ask each clinic directly about fees and services.

### Is a private GP consultation covered by the NHS?

No, private GP care is paid for by you or your insurance, not by the NHS. A private GP does not replace your NHS GP, but good clinics will share notes with your NHS practice so your records stay complete.

### Can I see a private GP for a second opinion?

Yes, many people see a private GP for a second opinion or to discuss a problem in more depth. You can ask the GP how they will communicate with your NHS GP about the outcome.

### What should I have ready for my private GP appointment?

It helps to have a list of your current medications, any relevant medical history, and a clear idea of what you want to discuss. If you have a preferred pharmacy, have those details ready too.

### How can I compare private GPs near me if I don't know where to start?

Begin by making a list of what matters to you, such as appointment length, availability, or whether video consultations are offered. Then contact a few local clinics and ask the same questions so you can compare their answers directly. You can also check the GMC register and, in England, the CQC to confirm registration.

### What should I compare when looking at private GP clinics?

Look at appointment length, how soon you can be seen, whether face-to-face or video consultations are available, and how the clinic shares notes with your NHS GP. Also ask about any additional costs for certificates, reports or referrals. Comparing these details helps you find a clinic that suits your needs.

### Can a private GP prescribe medication?

Yes, a GMC-registered doctor can issue private prescriptions. You pay the clinic's prescription fee and the pharmacy's price for the medicine.
