## What happens at the first appointment for a crown

At your first appointment the dentist will examine the tooth and usually take a X-ray to see how much of it is healthy and whether the nerve is affected. They will then talk you through whether a crown is the right option, which materials suit that particular tooth, and what the treatment involves — this is the moment to ask questions and get a written quote before anything goes ahead.

If you decide to proceed, the tooth is usually prepared under local anaesthetic, an impression is taken and a temporary crown is fitted to protect it while the permanent one is made. That means you leave the first appointment with a working tooth, and you come back for the second visit to have the finished crown fitted and checked for fit and colour.

If you have an old crown that has come loose or broken, the first visit may focus on assessing the tooth underneath before any replacement is planned, since the outcome depends on how sound that remaining tooth is. It is worth mentioning any sensitivity, gum tenderness or bite changes when you book, so the practice can allow enough time.

## How to check you are in safe hands

Dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists and dental nurses must be registered with the General Dental Council (GDC), and you can check the GDC register online. Specialist titles such as orthodontist or endodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list. Private clinics providing regulated healthcare in England must register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), which inspects and publishes ratings. In Wales the regulator is Healthcare Inspectorate Wales, in Scotland Healthcare Improvement Scotland, and in Northern Ireland the RQIA.

If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process. The Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

## FAQs: a dental crown

### How do I find a dentist near me for a crown?

You can search for private dentists in your area and check that they are registered with the General Dental Council. iComply can introduce you to a suitable UK professional based on what you tell us, and they will quote you directly.

### What is a crown and why would I need one?

A crown covers a damaged, weakened or root-treated tooth to restore its shape, strength and appearance. It is usually recommended when a tooth cannot be repaired with a filling or when it needs extra protection after root canal treatment.

### How long does it take to get a crown?

A crown is usually fitted over two appointments. The first is to prepare the tooth and take an impression, and the second is to fit the finished crown.

### What should I do if I have a problem with a private crown?

If something goes wrong with private dental treatment, start with the practice's complaints process. The Dental Complaints Service can help with private dental complaints that are not resolved.

### Will I be able to eat normally between the two appointments?

You will have a temporary crown in place, which protects the prepared tooth but is not as strong as the finished one. Most dentists advise avoiding very hard or sticky foods on that side until the permanent crown is fitted.

### What if I grind my teeth — does that affect a crown?

Grinding can put extra strain on a crown, so it is worth telling the dentist if you know you grind or clench, or if you wake with jaw ache. They can discuss whether a night guard or a different material might suit you better.

### Can I see any private dentist for a crown, or do I need a specialist?

Most crowns are provided by general dentists, and specialist titles such as endodontist or prosthodontist can only be used by people on a GDC specialist list. If your tooth is complex, your dentist may suggest a referral, but this is something to discuss at your first appointment.

### Can I see a private dentist and stay with my NHS dentist?

Yes, you can use both. Some patients see a private dentist for specific treatments and keep NHS care for routine check-ups, if their NHS practice agrees.
