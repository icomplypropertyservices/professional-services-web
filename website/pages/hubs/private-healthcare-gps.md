## Appointment types and clinical governance texture

Private GP and private healthcare clinics live or die on **appointment discipline**: triage notes, consent, chaperone offers, safety-netting advice, and clear boundaries between NHS and private pathways. iComply Professional Services structures this hub around appointment types and governance evidence — not a generic professional-services shell copied from accountancy or law.

Membership models, executive health screens, same-day episodic visits, and home visits each need different prep, documentation and follow-up. When those modes share one vague template, notes become inconsistent and complaints become harder to defend. We help clinics describe what good looks like for each mode without drowning clinicians in forms.

## Membership versus episodic care

Subscription patients expect continuity; episodic patients expect speed and clarity of fee discussion before examination. Operational design should separate onboarding, renewal reminders, DNA policies, and how test results are communicated. We facilitate workshops that capture the real sequence your reception and clinicians already use, then simplify it into a maintainable pack.

Pricing for our support work is always **POA**. We never invent clinical fee menus for your patients on this website.

## CQC-aware readiness without overclaiming

Where CQC registration or similar oversight applies, clinics need evidence of training, incidents, medicines management, and premises checks. We do not inspect on behalf of the regulator and we do not practise medicine. We help you organise ownership maps, induction outlines, and sampling methods so your team can show how the clinic runs on an ordinary Tuesday.

Locums and visiting specialists need the same induction themes as employed clinicians: systems access, emergency equipment locations, safeguarding contacts, and documentation standards. Treating locums as permanent exceptions is how gaps appear.

## Reception, triage and the first five minutes

The first five minutes of a private appointment often decide whether the clinical record will be defensible later. Reception scripts should confirm identity, appointment mode, any accompanying person, and whether the visit is membership-covered or fee-paying before the clinician begins. When that conversation is improvised, billing disputes and consent gaps appear together. We help clinics write short reception prompts that match the diary software fields already on screen, so staff are not dual-entering data into a parallel notebook culture.

Triage is not only for urgent red flags. It is also how you decide whether a same-day slot is clinically appropriate, whether a longer slot is needed for a complex new patient, and whether a chaperone should be offered before the patient reaches the room. Documenting those decisions lightly — who decided, what was asked — creates evidence without turning every greeting into a tribunal.

## Medicines, results and follow-up loops

Private clinics frequently manage prescriptions, private bloods, and imaging referrals across multiple providers. Results that arrive when the requesting clinician is on leave need a covering arrangement that is written, not hoped. We facilitate mapping of results inboxes, abnormal-result escalation, and patient notification standards. The aim is a loop that closes: request, receipt, clinical review, patient informed, safety-netting recorded.

Medicines storage, fridge checks, and emergency kit audits belong on a calendar with named owners. Locums must know where the kit is without a scavenger hunt. These are unglamorous controls and exactly where inspections and insurer questions tend to land.

## Corporate and occupational health interfaces

Some private GP services serve employers. Dual loyalties — patient confidentiality versus employer expectations — need clear boundaries in contracts and in staff training. We help you articulate what reports may say, what requires explicit consent, and who signs off corporate work. We do not draft your legal contracts as solicitors; we help the operating conversation stay consistent with the policies you already intend to follow.

## Training rhythm that survives rotas

Annual training matrices that nobody updates are theatre. A workable rhythm ties modules to roles: safeguarding for all clinical staff, information governance for anyone touching records, basic life support with dated evidence, and induction within a fixed number of days for new joiners. We help design the matrix and the chase process so the practice manager is not the only person who remembers.

When clinics expand to a second site, clone-and-drift is the enemy. Template libraries, shared incident taxonomies, and a single complaints owner across sites keep learning portable. Tell us in your enquiry if multi-site consistency is the real reason you are calling — that changes workshop design.
