## Injury pathways as the structural spine

Physiotherapy clinics think in **pathways**: assessment, treatment plan, progression criteria, discharge, and onward referral. iComply Professional Services builds this hub around those pathways — sports injuries, workplace MSK, post-operative rehab, and persistent pain — so the page is not a renamed private-GP shell.

Outcome measures, consent for hands-on treatment, and home-exercise instructions are part of evidence, not optional extras. We help you define when notes must capture red-flag screening, when a second opinion is escalated, and how DNA or late-cancellation policies are applied fairly.

## Sports versus workplace versus post-op programmes

Athletes, office workers, and post-surgical patients arrive with different time pressures and referrer expectations. Separate programme templates reduce copy-paste errors in notes. We facilitate mapping sessions with clinic directors and senior physios to capture what already works, then document it so new joiners do not invent private versions of care.

Our fees for this support are **POA**. We never invent patient treatment prices on this domain.

## Room utilisation, equipment and student placements

Timetabling, equipment checks, and student or apprentice supervision create operational risk if left informal. Who signs off a student's notes? How is electrotherapy equipment logged? What happens when a room runs late and the next patient is waiting? These questions belong in the operating pack beside clinical protocols.

Insurer and medico-legal letter requests need a consistent admin path so clinicians are not improvising under email pressure.

## Assessment notes that future-you can defend

Assessment notes should let another physiotherapist understand what was found, what was ruled out, and why the plan was chosen. That does not require endless prose; it requires consistent headings and red-flag prompts that cannot be skipped on first visits. We help clinics agree a note standard that fits their clinical philosophy — sports-heavy, NHS-overflow private MSK, or mixed — without imposing a single national template fantasy.

Objective measures (range, strength, validated questionnaires) need a rule for when they are repeated. Otherwise outcome conversations become anecdotal. We place those rules in the pathway documents so juniors are not guessing.

## Gym floor, treatment room and telehealth hybrids

Many clinics blend hands-on treatment, rehab gym time, and video follow-ups. Each mode has consent, privacy, and documentation quirks. Telehealth needs identity checks and environment prompts; gym floor work needs eyes-on safety when multiple patients exercise at once. Hybrid models fail when the diary treats every slot as identical. We help you label slot types and attach the right mini-checklist to each.

Equipment faults and cleaning between patients sit in the same operational story as clinical excellence. A broken plinth strap or missing disinfectant wipe is a patient experience and safety issue. Cadence checks with initials beat a dusty laminated poster.

## Referrer relationships and medico-legal letters

GPs, consultants, coaches, and insurers all ask for updates in different formats. A single “write something nice” culture creates uneven letters and missed deadlines. We help define turnaround standards, who may sign what, and when a fee discussion for detailed reports must happen before work starts — process design only; you set your commercial terms. Our support remains **POA**.

## Growth, associates and brand risk

Associate-heavy models need clarity on clinical responsibility, diary ownership, and how complaints are attributed. Brand risk rises when associates invent personal protocols that contradict clinic policy. Induction and periodic file sampling are how you keep one clinic, not a marketplace of private methods under one logo. We design those sampling habits with clinic directors so they are proportionate to volume.
