## Practice areas matrix and risk texture

UK solicitors' firms carry different risk shapes depending on whether the desk is residential conveyancing, private client, litigation, employment, commercial, or a mixed high-street practice. A single generic handbook rarely fits. iComply Professional Services helps firms map **practice areas to controls**: which matters need dual review, which retainers must be rechecked when scope creeps, and how supervision is evidenced when partners are in court or on completions.

We start with how work actually enters the firm. Enquiry handling, conflict checks, source-of-funds questions, and engagement letters are where later complaints often begin. If those steps live only in someone's memory, file reviews become archaeology. We help you write a realistic intake path that fee-earners will follow on busy days, not only on quiet training afternoons.

## Supervision, file ownership and the client journey

Supervision is not a signature stamp. It is a rhythm: who owns the file, who can authorise undertakings, when a matter escalates, and how juniors learn judgment without silent risk. Our workshops walk through a typical client journey — first contact, retainer, matter plan, key milestones, billing conversations, closing and file destruction — and mark where evidence should appear without turning every email into a form.

Many firms already have policies that look complete on paper. The gap is usually between written rules and diary reality. We sample (with your permission and minimised data) how files show advice confirmed in writing, how limitation dates are diarised, and whether complaints acknowledgements meet your published timescales. Findings become a prioritised action list, not a theatrical report.

## SRA-aware operating habits without overclaiming

We do not act as your solicitor or as a substitute COLP or COFA. You remain the authorised firm. Our support is operational: clearer ownership maps, induction packs that cover conduct themes in plain English, file review sampling frameworks, supplier due diligence for outsourced typing or IT, and training outlines partners can deliver. We never invent fixed sterling prices; every engagement is **POA** after scope.

Data protection sits beside professional conduct. Access rights on case management systems, retention schedules, and subject-access readiness belong in the same conversation as file audits. We help you see process gaps; your legal advisers handle legal conclusions.

## What a solicitors engagement can include

- Matter-type control matrix aligned to your departments
- Supervision and escalation maps that name roles, not only job titles
- File review sampling method and scoring sheet
- Induction and refresher outline for fee-earners and support staff
- Complaints and client-care evidence checklist
- Supplier and locum onboarding checks
- Document control habit for policies and templates

Delivery can be remote workshops, on-site sessions by arrangement, or a blend. Nationwide UK firms can enquire regardless of town; we do not invent a branch office on every high street.

New joiners need a first-week path that covers conflicts, undertakings, cyber hygiene, and who to call when unsure. A glossy handbook nobody opens does not meet that need. We favour short, role-based checklists and a named buddy for the first month.

When firms merge or open a second office, template libraries diverge quickly. Version control and a single source of truth for client-care wording prevent two brands of advice living under one letterhead. That is a practical document-control problem we can help structure.


## Undertakings, limitation and diary discipline

Undertakings deserve a visible authority list: who may give them, how they are recorded, and how discharge is evidenced. Limitation and key dates need diary systems with cover when fee-earners are away. We help firms describe those controls in language that matches their case management tool, then sample whether files show the habit under ordinary load. This remains preview-draft guidance for operational support — not a substitute for legal advice to your clients.
